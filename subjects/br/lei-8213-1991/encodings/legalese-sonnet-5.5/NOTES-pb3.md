# NOTES: piece pb3 of the encoding of Lei nº 8.213/1991 (Planos de Benefícios da Previdência Social)

### What this is

This folder encodes one piece of Lei nº 8.213, de 24 de julho de 1991: Título III, Capítulo II, Seção V, Subseções V to IX (arts. 59 to 80: auxílio-doença, salário-família, salário-maternidade, pensão por morte, auxílio-reclusão) and Título IV (arts. 125 to 156: appeals, suits, fine, final and transitional provisions, with the closing lines).
The text is lines 781-1105 and 1540-1766 of `lei-8213-1991.pt.txt`.
The other three pieces (pb1, pb2, pb4) cover the rest of the Law and are written at the same time; this piece reads them only through named inputs (see "Cross-references out of my ranges").
The vocabulary shared by the pieces is `pb-core.l4`, written before the pieces and not edited here.

### Edition, and what is not claimed

The source is a plain-text rendering of Planalto's compilation of the Law, made by a script, with the superseded wording wrapped as `⟦STRUCK: ...⟧` and the amendment notes ("Redação dada pela Lei nº ...") kept inline.
It is a converter rendering and is not the official text: its own last note (line 1765) says that it does not replace the text published in the Diário Oficial da União of 25.7.1991, republished on 11.4.1996 and 14.8.1998.
Nothing here was checked against the official text, against the Planalto page, or by a Brazilian lawyer.
Every quotation in the L4 files (`-- src:N | ...`) is a mechanical copy of a line of the rendering, and `srcrefs.py check --refs` reports 0 problems on all the files.
No regulation, decree, ministerial act, resolution or other law that the text cites was among the inputs: wherever a rule depends on one, its outcome is a plain input and is listed under "Cross-references out of my ranges".

### Method

1. The live lines of both ranges were separated from the struck ones by the keel's script (340 live lines); struck text is never encoded and is named in the note of its row.
2. Layer 1 follows the text's own structure, one module per Subseção or natural group: `pb3-auxilio-doenca.l4` (arts. 59-63), `pb3-salario-familia.l4` (arts. 65-70), `pb3-salario-maternidade.l4` (arts. 71-73-A), `pb3-pensao-por-morte.l4` (arts. 74-78), `pb3-auxilio-reclusao.l4` (art. 80), `pb3-recursos-e-acoes.l4` (arts. 125-133) and `pb3-disposicoes-finais.l4` (arts. 134-156), each with a test file `pb3-tests-<same name>.l4`.
   Every rule carries an `@ref` with `src:` locators and the quotations of the lines it states.
   A rule is named by its citation ("art. 60, § 3º — ..."), and every input is a plain `GIVEN` on each rule that reads it.
3. Layer 2 is `pb3-seat.l4` and `pb3-cases-seat.l4` (see "Seat").
4. The tests were written from the text, rule by rule: every branch, and the values on both sides of every threshold.
   The expected values were written down before each run and were not edited to match the output.
   For the files written last (arts. 74 to 156 and the seat layer) every assertion was satisfied on its first run; to see that the binary reports a wrong assertion, a throwaway file with three deliberately wrong assertions was run and gave three errors.
5. Where the text does not answer, the rule returns `NOTHING` (or a constructor that says so) and a case asserts it.

### Dates

The date of the Law (24 July 1991) and the date it enters into force are the core's constants.
The only commencement provision in my ranges is art. 155 ("Esta Lei entra em vigor na data de sua publicação"); `art. 155 — data de entrada em vigor da Lei nº 8.213` cites the core constant for it, and the core holds that date (25 July 1991) from the footer of the rendering, which is Planalto's note and not the Law's text (the core's fork F-1).
Arts. 137 and 138 ("até a entrada em vigor desta Lei") and arts. 153 and 154 (periods "a partir da data da sua publicação") use the same constant.
Art. 142 names 24 July 1991 itself and the date is written out (`YMD 1991 7 24`) with its `@ref`.
There is no other commencement provision in the ranges: no order is awaited and no date is left to a decree.

## Seat

The seat is a person insured under the Regime Geral de Previdência Social, or a dependant of one, who asks whether they qualify for a benefit, how much it is, from when and for how long, and what they must do or declare.
It was ruled for Commons on 2026-10-18 (the individual to whom the law is applied, never the regulator, the court or the department) and is restated in the brief of this piece.
It is one seat for both persons because the Law writes the benefits of the insured (arts. 59 to 73-A) and of the dependants (arts. 74 to 80) side by side, and a family asks the two sets together.
The persons the text puts duties on next to the insured (the empresa, the empregador doméstico, the sindicato, the INSS, the Conselho de Recursos, the court) are not seats: their duties appear as what the insured is owed, with the facts about them as plain inputs ("O salário-maternidade já foi concedido?", "O INSS é parte na ação?"), as the brief asks.
Further seat layers, one per party (the empresa, for example), would sit beside this one as `pb3-seat-<party>.l4`; none is written in this pass.

Defaults of CLAUDE.md (ruled 2026-10-18 and 2026-10-21) followed:

- the Law alone, with the instruments that set its numbers brought in as inputs (none of them was among the inputs);
- the actors the text names;
- a judgment the Law hands to someone else answered conditionally, as an input that says whose conclusion it is ("A perícia médica do INSS concluiu que ...?");
- the Law's own definitions worked out from facts, borrowed terms (arts. 11 and 16, pb1) asked as facts;
- one case as the natural unit (one insured, one benefit, one imprisonment, one death);
- the Law's own terms, each with a plain gloss in a comment;
- every operative provision in scope, and the machinery (appeals machinery, powers of the Minister, internal conditions on the INSS's procurador) encoded in layer 1 for what it decides and marked in the coverage table where it has no question.

Departures and choices:

- The records the long modules expect (`Segurado`, `Dependente`, `Valores de referência`) are built inside each question from plain inputs by three constructors at the head of `pb3-seat.l4`, and the same module rule is then called; no rule is restated.
  A field the called rule never reads gets a fixed placeholder, and a comment above the question names it.
- An input that the other pieces also need keeps the keel's exact name (`o segurado cumpriu a carência exigida (arts. 24 a 27-A)` and the others of its table), so that a form over the merged pieces asks it once.
  The rest keep the name layer 1 gives them, in the Law's third person, and carry an `@desc` worded as the person is asked.
- The figures the Law leaves to the Executive or to the indices (the salário mínimo, the limits of the salário-de-contribuição, the figures of art. 66, the limit of art. 128, the low-income limit of art. 80 § 3) are asked as facts and not assumed.
  The ages of art. 77 § 2, V, c, are the Law's own and are not asked: an act of the Minister under § 2-B would change the answers of the questions about the length of the pension (fork F-P3-26).
- A layer-1 deontic rule (`PARTY ... MUST ...`) answers with an obligation that a form cannot show.
  Where it is the only statement of a duty the person asks about, a BOOLEAN rule that states its condition once is paired with it in layer 1 and the deontic calls it (arts. 68, 69, 74 § 5, 77 § 7, 78 § 2, 80 § 1, 126 § 4, 129-A §§ 1 and 3); elsewhere the BOOLEAN or the value rule already existed (arts. 60 §§ 3 and 4, 62, 63, 72, 73-A).
  The question calls the BOOLEAN or the value.
- The questions are in Portuguese, in the first person, grouped under `§` headings in the text's order.
  The headings "Se estou preso", "O que a empresa me deve", "Se adotei ou se sou o sobrevivente", "Se o segurado desapareceu" and "O litígio de acidente do trabalho ..." arise only in the situation they name, and the form may ask "Isto aconteceu com você?" before showing them.
- Questions are not asked for the provisions that bind only the INSS, the Conselho, the court, the Minister or the legislator and decide nothing the person could ask; the rows of the coverage table say which and why (art. 80 § 2, art. 125, art. 125-A, arts. 131 and 132, arts. 153 and 154, the retention of vouchers in art. 68 § 1 and art. 72 § 2).

## Jural relations

One row per provision that creates a relation, from the Law's own words: the party who holds the claim, privilege, power or immunity, the party who bears the correlative duty, no-right, liability or disability, the section, and the L4 rule that encodes it.
Hohfeld's pairs are used as the brief states them: a claim has a duty opposite it, a privilege a no-right, a power a liability, an immunity a disability.
A rule is stated once, as a relation between two parties, and named neutrally (it is not repeated from the other side); the seat layer asks the person's side of it, with the other party's facts as inputs.
A provision that creates no such relation (a measure, a definition, a counting rule) is listed once, with `none` where it has no party.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Measured on the stable build named in the brief (l4 1.6.98, target `main`), or recorded as it was met:

- Accented letters in backticked names, in `@export` and `@desc` text, in `§` headings and in comments compile and run on every file, and `srcrefs.py` accepts them in the quotations (0 problems).
- A time-limit helper with a bare number as an argument is not read: `prazo em dias 90` without backticks gives "could not find a definition for prazo"; the call is `` (`prazo em dias` 90) ``.
- A call of a rule on several lines needs its argument lines indented further than the rule's name; with the arguments at or left of the name's start inside `AND (NOT ...)` the parse stops at "unexpected AND".
  The form that works is `(NOT` on its own line, the rule's name on the next, the arguments further right.
- `OTHERWISE JUST (Date (Day x PLUS n)` with the arithmetic continued on a line that starts left of the expression is a parse error; the rule keeps the arithmetic on one line in a helper with `WHERE`.
- A `CONSIDER ... WHEN` inside a bracket followed by `OR` on a line less indented than its `WHEN`s reads as the left side of the `OR` (probed in the scratch with `NOTHING` and `TRUE`).
- A `DEONTIC` result is not an `#ASSERT` value on this build; the deontic rules are run with `#TRACE`, and the outcome is read off the output, because `check.sh` counts only assertions and a trace moves no count.
  The outcomes of every trace were read: FULFILLED where the duty was done or did not apply; the residual obligation (`PARTY ... MUST ...`) where it applies and nothing was done yet (modules 1 to 3 have these on purpose); and one BREACH, written to end so (art. 126 § 4, the INSS silent at day 31).
- `@export` of a function that returns a `DEONTIC` is accepted by `l4 check`, and `l4 batch -e <DECIDE name>` answers `<obligation>`, which a form cannot show; that is why the seat's questions call BOOLEAN rules.
  `l4 batch` selects the function by its `DECIDE` name, not by the `@export` text.
- A `MAYBE` answer is returned bare by `l4 batch` when it is `JUST` (a date as `2024-03-01`), and an enumeration with a payload as an object (`{"por prazo certo":[{"Prazo":[15,"em anos"]}]}`).
- `add months` and `add years` keep the day of the month and stop at the last day of a shorter month (31 August plus 6 months is 28 February: asserted for art. 78 caput); that is the library's rule and not the Law's (the core's fork F-2).
- Decimals (`0.5`, `5180.25`, `1.1`) and percentages (`91%`) are accepted; `5698.275` is the exact result of `5180.25 TIMES 1.1`.
- The target is `main`, so `L4_TARGET=unstable` only tells `check.sh` not to run a second binary.
