# Lei nº 8.213/1991, piece pb4: arts. 81 to 124-G (src:1106-1539)

This folder holds piece pb4 of the encoding of the Social Security Benefits Plans Law (Lei nº 8.213, de 24 de julho de 1991): Título III, Capítulo II, Seção V Subseções X to XII (arts. 81 to 87), and Seções VI, VII and VIII (arts. 88 to 124-G).
It was written by one agent in one context from `lei-8213-1991.pt.txt`, against the shared core `pb-core.l4` of the keel agent.
No independent test author read it, and no domain expert has read it against the official text.

**The source.**
`lei-8213-1991.pt.txt` is a plain-text rendering, made by a converter script, of the compilation that Planalto publishes at `https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8213cons.htm` (retrieved 2026-10-21, 1766 lines, sha256 of the text file `27b818e2…eb95b3`).
It is not the official text, it is not marked up, and nothing here is verified against the official text.
Planalto's own footer says that only the text published in the Diário Oficial da União is official.
Superseded wording that Planalto strikes through is kept in the file as `⟦STRUCK: … ⟧` and is never encoded; the amendment notes (`Redação dada pela …`) are part of the line, not provisions.
My range has 259 live lines (the keel's script, lines 1106-1539), many of them headings and bare notes such as "Vigência encerrada".

**What is encoded.**
Every article of the range, 54 of them (81 to 87, 88 to 124, and 103-A, 110-A, 117-A, 124-A to 124-G), has at least one row in the coverage table below, 159 rows in all.
Layer 1 is eight modules, `pb4-tipos.l4` (nouns only) and seven rule modules in the order of the Law; layer 2 is `pb4-seat.l4` with 100 questions and `pb4-cases-seat.l4` with 229 worked cases.
Seven test files, `pb4-tests-*.l4`, test layer 1.
Thirty-one rows are `repealed or blank in this text`: whole articles (81 to 85, 87, 95, 100, 123, 124-E, 124-F) and parts of articles (93 V and § 4, 101 § 3 and § 7, 106 I and III, 113 sole paragraph, 115 V, VII, §§ 6 and 7, § 11, 117 I to III and sole paragraph, 118 sole paragraph, 124-B I, 86 §§ 1-A, 5 and 6, the former § 4 of art. 86, and the former art. 122 with its sole paragraph).
Ten rows are `inert`: eight are division titles (a title decides nothing), and two are art. 115 § 5 and art. 117-A § 2 (a regulation-making power and an act-making power).

**Method.**
The coverage table was drafted first from the live-lines script and the section index; the nouns module was written second; each module was then written section by section and tested before the next.
Every rule carries `@ref` with `src:` locators, and its quotation is copied from the raw file by `srcrefs.py quote` (a template expander read the raw file; nothing was retyped, except the one STRING constant of art. 119, which was compared with its source line by a script and is identical).
The tests were written from the text before they were run, and the only failures met on the way were mistakes of mine, fixed in the code or the test generator, never by editing an expected value (finding 26 records the one that mattered).

**Dates.**
My range contains no date of enactment or making of the Law and no commencement provision (the title block and art. 155 belong to the keel and to piece pb3), so nothing was done about them.
The text fixes two other dates, and both are carried as values with an `@ref`: 1 April 2003 (art. 96, V) and the date of Emenda Constitucional nº 20, 15 December 1998 (art. 96, § 1).

**Language.**
Identifiers, `@export`, `@desc` and `§` headings are Brazilian Portuguese with accents; comments, this file and PROGRESS.md are English, with a gloss beside each Portuguese phrase quoted.

## Seat

**The seat** is the one the brief fixes: a person insured under Brazil's Regime Geral de Previdência Social, or a dependant of one, asking whether they qualify for a benefit (such as a retirement, disability, sickness, maternity or death benefit), how much it is, from when and for how long, and what they must do or declare.
The range suits it: Seção VI gives the person services, Seção VII the counting of their time, and Seção VIII says how the benefit is paid, what may be deducted, when it ends, when a right lapses and what the person owes the INSS.
The dependant is in the seat for the questions of the pensioner (art. 101 for the invalid pensioner; arts. 110, 112 and 124, VI).
There is no seat for an official: the INSS, the Serviço Social, the servant who analyses a request, the Ministries and the federal administration appear only as what the person is owed or exposed to, in the Hohfeld rows below and in the regulative rules.
Another party the range binds is the firm (art. 93 reserve of posts; art. 118 guarantee of the contract), and the entities that may file for or pay a benefit (arts. 117 and 117-A).
A seat layer for the employer, `pb4-seat-empresa.l4`, was not written: the Commons rule makes it welcome, not required, and the brief asked for one layer.
It would reuse the same layer-1 rules (art. 93 caput and § 1, art. 118, art. 117, art. 117-A) with the employer's own inputs.

Each question has `@export` and `@desc`, takes plain inputs (yes or no, a number, a date, or a choice from the Law's own lists), and builds the records of layer 1 inside its `WHERE`; the same layer-1 rule is then called, and no rule is restated.
A record field that the called rule never reads is given a fixed placeholder, with a comment saying so.
The groups follow the Law's order; a group that arises only in a situation the person may not be in says so in its comment (rehabilitation, deductions, work accident, rural proof).
Where the layer-1 answer is a `MAYBE`, the question returns the `MAYBE` (`NOTHING` means the provision does not decide the case).
Questions with no seat counterpart are listed in the header of `pb4-seat.l4` and in the coverage table (they bind a body and ask nothing of the person).
The regulative rules of layer 1 (`PARTY ... MUST/MAY`) are exercised by `#TRACE` in the test files; the seat exports the BOOLEAN rules beside them, because a deontic cannot be asserted and the Interact form wants a value.

## Jural relations

Hohfeld's pairs: a claim is the other party's duty; a privilege is the other party's no-right; a power is the other party's liability (subjection); an immunity is the other party's disability.
Each relation is stated once, as a relation between two parties, and named neutrally in layer 1.
`none` rows are provisions that create no relation (a counting rule, a proof rule, a repealed text).

## Coverage table

One row per provision of the range: one per article, and one per paragraph, inciso or alínea wherever they differ in disposition or in the rule they state.
The range has 54 articles (81 to 124-G) and the table has 159 rows: 96 `encoded`, 22 `fork`, 10 `inert` (8 division titles and 2 powers) and 31 `repealed or blank in this text`.
Each division heading of the range (Subseções X, XI and XII of Seção V; Seção VI and its Subseções I and II; Seções VII and VIII) has an `inert` row for its title and subtitle, because a title decides nothing; the struck variants of a provision are listed in the `source lines` of its own row.
`fork` rows are encoded: the row names the reading taken in the fork register, or the explicit not-covered value returned.
`where` lists every layer-1 rule that encodes the row, by its name; the seat's questions for it are in `pb4-seat.l4` under the same article in their names.
Struck variants of a provision are named in the note of its row.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Measured on 1.6.98 (`l4 --version` for the stable build named in the brief; sha256 `82ea44ad…0263e`) on 2026-10-24, in the course of writing this piece.

- **Accents compile** everywhere I used them: in backticked names, `§` headings, `@ref` text, `@export` and `@desc` lines, and `-- src:` quotation lines. `srcrefs.py` accepted them all. Nothing was rejected for an accent.
- **`NOT (x) OR y` on one line is read as `NOT ((x) OR y)`** with no diagnostic. Write `(NOT (x)) OR y`, or put `NOT` and the connective on separate lines with the connective less indented, or use `AND NOT (x)`. This is the dialects.md row for `NOT a AND b`, extended to a bracketed first operand.
- **`JUST 2%` is read as `(JUST 2)%`** and is a type error. Write `JUST (2%)`.
- **`EXPONENT` is floating point**: `EXPONENT 1.06 2` is `1.1236000000000002`, not `1.1236`. The art. 96 IV interest factor is computed by recursion, which is exact in rationals.
- **A call whose last argument sits on the next line inside brackets is a parse error** (`unexpected (`): `f x (g y)` split as `f x` then a line of `(g y)` is refused. Write the call on one line or name the pieces in `WHERE`. The same layout in a `WHERE` definition is refused unless the body starts on the line after `MEANS`.
- **An unused `GIVEN` parameter gives no warning**, so a rule may keep an input it does not read (arts. 86 § 3, 105, 107, 115 § 8, 124-G), and a `DECIDE` of the seat form (`GIVEN ... @desc`, `DECIDE name IS ...`) is accepted without listing its parameters after the name.
- **A `MAY` with no `WITHIN` and no event leaves a pending obligation**, not `FULFILLED`: `#TRACE` prints the residual `PARTY ... MAY ...`. The one such trace in the tests (art. 124-A § 2) is meant to show that.
- **`srcrefs.py check --strict` reports 21 problems** on this folder, and `--refs` reports 0. The 21 are locators on later rules that cite a quotation run in an earlier block, which is what strict mode asks for in its own block. The quotations themselves pass R1 and every locator passes R2 and R3 under `--refs`.
