## Seat

**The seat is a person insured under Brazil's Regime Geral de Previdência Social (RGPS), or a dependant of one, asking whether they qualify for a benefit (a retirement, disability, sickness, maternity or death benefit), how much it is, from when and for how long, and what they must do or declare.**
That is the seat the brief names, and the keel's.
My part of the Law is the front of the plan of benefits: who is an insured (arts. 10 to 14, 11 in particular), when an insured stops being one (art. 15), who is a dependant (art. 16) and how they inscribe themselves (art. 17), which benefits and services exist (art. 18), what an accident at work is and who must report it (arts. 19 to 23), and how many monthly contributions each benefit asks for (the carência, arts. 24 to 27-A).
So the seat's questions are the ones that come before "how much" (which is pb2's) and "for how long" (which is pb3's): "Sou segurado? De que categoria? Ainda sou segurado? Sou dependente? Tenho direito à prestação? Cumpri a carência?"

Why this seat and no other: the Law is applied to the insured and the dependant.
The employer (`empresa`, `empregador doméstico`), the INSS, the Previdência Social and the officials (the registrar of art. 18 § 4, the perícia médica of art. 21-A, the Conselho de Recursos) are the other side of the relations; they are never a seat, and where a provision lays a duty on one of them it appears as what the insured is owed (art. 19 §§ 1-3, art. 22, art. 18 § 4) or exposed to.
The brief gives the individual's seat only for this piece; no second seat (employer, firm) was written, although arts. 11 (the firm's side of the employment), 14, 19 and 22 are where an employer's seat would begin.

How the layer is built (as the brief and `ENCODING-GUIDE.md` §3 require):

- `pb1-seat.l4` imports the layer-1 modules and the core and calls their rules; it never restates or copies a rule, and no layer-1 module changed to suit it.
- Every question is `@export` with a first-person question in Portuguese ending in `?`, takes plain inputs (yes/no, a number, a date, a choice from a list), one for each fact the person can state, each with an `@desc` worded as that person would be asked.
- The records the modules expect (`Trabalho como empregado (art. 11, I)`, `Situação do segurado especial (art. 11, VII)`, `Dependente`, `Circunstâncias do dependente (art. 16)`, `Fatos da carência (arts. 25 a 27-A)`, ...) are built inside each question, in its `WHERE`, from those answers. A field that the rule being called never reads is given a fixed placeholder (`FALSE` or `0`) with a comment saying so.
- Questions are grouped under `§§` headings in the Law's order; where a group arises only in a situation the person may not be in (an acidente do trabalho; a representation on the CNPS; being a special insured; a retiree back at work; a union leader in office), the comment above the question says so, so the form can ask "Isto aconteceu com você?" first.
- A question whose rule says "x não descaracteriza ..." is worded "Posso x sem perder ...?" so that `TRUE` answers the question as asked; a rule that decides a status or a bar is worded to match (`A inscrição depois da morte do segurado é vedada`).
- Judgments the Law hands to someone else are conditional facts, never guessed: the finding of the perícia médica do INSS (art. 21-A), whether the Previdência Social finds that a disease resulted from the work (art. 20 § 2), what the Regulamento says (arts. 11 VI, 16 § 5), the date the Plano de Custeio fixes (art. 15 § 4), the list of diseases of the Ministries (arts. 20, 26 II).
- A number the Law leaves to a regulation is asked of the person (the salário mínimo, the menor benefício, the first business day), never invented.
- Each question names the provision it decides at the end of its text, in parentheses, so that the form can cite it.

Defaults of `COMMONS-CLAUDE.md` / `ENCODING-GUIDE.md` §4 followed: the Act alone (no regulation is brought in: none is among the inputs); the actors the text names; judgments left to someone else answered conditionally; the Act's own definitions worked out from facts (the categories of insured, the classes of dependants, acidente do trabalho, carência) and borrowed terms asked as facts (módulo fiscal, empresa de trabalho temporário); one case as the unit (one person, one activity, one benefit); the Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss in a comment; the operative sections in scope, the machinery (the CNPS and its competence, the Ouvidoria, powers to make regulations) out.
Departures: none known.
One choice that is **assumed, not ruled**: the question wording `Posso ... sem perder ...?` for the "não descaracteriza" paragraphs of art. 11 § 8 and § 12 (it could equally be asked as "Isto me tira a condição de segurado especial?" with the answer inverted); a change of wording would not change a rule.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Nothing in my range was rejected for an accent: accents compile in rule names, type names, constructor names, field names, `§` headings, `@ref` text, `@desc` and `-- src:` quote lines, and `srcrefs.py` accepts them (the whole folder runs clean and `srcrefs.py check --refs` prints 0 problems).
I needed no construct that the stable binary refuses, other than those the keel already lists.
Measured on 1.6.98 unless a line says otherwise, as the pieces' authors may wish to know:

- **Loud.** A `GIVEN` name that the head of the rule does not repeat is an error ("The names in a type signature must match those in the definition"; the keel recorded it, I did not re-run it), and so is an argument count that does not match the rule (a seat case that gave 14 of 15 inputs was refused when I ran it).
- **Silent: a positional record (by construction, not re-measured).** `OF` with 22 BOOLEANs builds a record in field order, with no check of the names; the fixtures of `pb1-tests-art-11-i-ii-vi` are generated from the field list for that reason, and a swap of two fields of one type would give a wrong answer with no error.
- **Silent: a sum with an `IF` as its right operand.** `IF TRUE THEN 24 ELSE 12 PLUS IF TRUE THEN 12 ELSE 0` reads as `IF TRUE THEN 24 ELSE (12 PLUS IF ...)` and evaluates to 24, not to 24 plus 12; with the operands bracketed it is 36 (both measured).
  The test at 121 contributions and a proved unemployment (expected 36 months) caught it in art. 15, and the rule now names the two terms as `WHERE` helpers.
- **Silent: `Date d 13 y`.** An impossible date rolls over (`Date 1 13 2026` is 1 January 2027); art. 11 § 10 II relies on it for December.
- **Accepted** (measured here, used by the folder): a two-line `#ASSERT` (the brief and the keel ask for one line and every file keeps to it; the stable binary did not refuse the two-line form in a throwaway probe, which has been deleted); `PARTY` followed by an input name rather than a constructor; `WITHIN (Day ...
