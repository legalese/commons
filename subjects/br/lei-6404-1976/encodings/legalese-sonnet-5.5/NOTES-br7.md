## Seat

The seat is the brief's: **um acionista, inclusive o acionista minoritário, de uma companhia (sociedade anônima), perguntando o que a Lei lhe garante, o que lhe exige, e o que a companhia, os administradores e o acionista controlador podem fazer em relação a ele.**
In English: a shareholder, minority shareholders included, of a company (sociedade anônima), asking what the Law gives them, what it requires of them, and what the company, its managers and its controlling shareholder may do in relation to them.

Why this seat suits this range, and what it leaves to layer 1.
The range is the part of the Law that stands around the company: groups of companies, consortia, the comandita por ações, prescription, general provisions (publications, the CVM's powers, small and smaller companies) and the transitional provisions.
A shareholder meets it as a person whose company joins a group (and who may dissent, be treated as a minority, ask for a conselho fiscal, sue for the group's abuses), whose claims prescribe, who is told what the company must publish and in what form, and who, if the company already existed in 1976, lives under the transition rules.
Everything else in the range (what a convention must contain, who may represent whom, what a publication must carry) is still encoded in layer 1, because Meng ruled on 2026-10-22 that the whole range is encoded and that nothing is left out because of the seat.

What layer 2 is.
`br7-seat.l4` holds 120 questions in 14 `§` groups in text order, each calling one rule of the six layer-1 modules and restating none.
`br7-cases-seat.l4` holds 509 assertions, at least one per question and, where a question has branches, one for each.
Every question has `@export` (the question in the first person, ending in `?`, with the citation in it) and every input has `@desc` (one sentence to the shareholder).
The rule names begin with a capital and carry no citation, as the keel's naming rule 5 says.

Plain inputs.
Every input is a yes/no, a number, a date, or one of the modules' own lists of choices (a `Tipo de companhia`, an action of art. 287, a matter of art. 266 and so on).
A layer-1 rule that takes a record has the record built inside the question, in its `WHERE`, from the shareholder's answers, and the same rule is called.
A layer-1 rule that takes a `MAYBE` (a filing date that may not exist, a percentage the CVM may not have fixed) is asked as a yes/no and a value; the `@desc` of the value says to give any value when the answer is no, because it is then not read.
The facts the Law takes from another piece (quorum of art. 136, who dissents under art. 137, whether a company is controlled under art. 243) are asked as plain facts, with the article in the `@desc`.

A seat for an official is never written.
The provisions that address only an official or a body (the CVM's powers in arts. 291 to 294-B, the conselho fiscal's request for information, the managers of the group) appear in layer 2 only as what they mean to the shareholder: what may be done to me, what I am owed, and by when.

The comma-less one-line `WITH` is the one that stopped me while I wrote the seat's cases; the others I probed from the brief and my notes.

| the construct | what the binary does | what I write instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CONSIDER c WHEN a THEN 1 WHEN b THEN 2` on one line | parse error | one `WHEN` per line, each under the `CONSIDER` |
| `g 5% 3`, a percentage literal followed by another argument | parse error | `g (5%) 3` |
| `R WITH f IS 1 h IS 2` on one line | parse error | commas, `R WITH f IS 1, h IS 2`, or one field per line with no commas |
| `R OF 1 2` | parse error | `R OF 1, 2` |
| an `#ASSERT` continued on the next line | parse error | the whole assertion on one line |

Two further points that are not refusals.
`check.sh` counts assertion lines and does not count a `#TRACE` outcome, so the four traces of art. 296 (`FULFILLED`, `FULFILLED`, `BREACH`, `FULFILLED`) are read by running the file and are not in the totals.
The brief lists other constructs the stable binary refuses (`REFUSE`, `@lang`, `@nlg:xx`, `TYPICALLY` on a record field, `WAIT UNTIL`); I did not write any of them and did not probe them, so I report nothing about them.

## Before this is worth relying on

What the numbers in this folder do and do not show.

1. **The text is a rendering.**
   `lei-6404-1976.pt.txt` is a plain-text conversion of the compiled text on the Planalto site; its last lines say "Este texto não substitui o publicado no DOU de 17.12.1976 (suplemento)" (src:2592).
   Superseded wording sits in `⟦STRUCK: ... ⟧` spans, which I did not encode.
   No one has set my quoted lines against the Diário Oficial or the site; `srcrefs.py` shows only that each quotation equals the line of this file.
2. **Forks that want a Brazilian lawyer before anyone relies on them.**
   B4 (control shared by a Brazilian and a foreign controller: not "brasileiro"), B6 (a group is constituted on the latest of three filings), B13 (the 5% of art. 277 is of each class, not of the two together), B23 (art. 287, II, g names no event), B25 (art. 288 is a bar on completion, not a suspension), B35 and B38 (the date of publication, the count of 60 days and of one year, and the 15 February 1978 that is two dates at once), B42 (endorsable and book-entry shares are not "nominativas" in art. 298, IV) and B43 (the reimbursement is pro rata by shares).
   Each is a reading of the words; the register gives the other reading and the text that licenses each.
3. **Nineteen inputs are other pieces' facts.**
   They are plain yes/no, number or date inputs (listed under the cross-references heading), each commented `-- out of range: section N (piece X)`.
   I have not run my modules against the other pieces' rules; whoever merges the pieces should replace an input by a call only where the owning piece answers the same question.
4. **Some figures are not the Law's.**
   The CVM's scale under art. 291 and its regulation under art. 294-A are inputs; the Law fixes neither.
   Art. 294's R$ 78.000.000,00 and art. 294-B's R$ 500.000.000,00 are the text's figures, and the CVM may update the second.
5. **Money.**
   Arts. 297 and 298 speak in cruzeiros of 1977; a sum is named `em cruzeiros` and nothing converts it to reais.
6. **Most of arts. 295 to 300 is spent.**
   The periods ran out between 1978 and 1982 and the 2008 balances of arts. 299-A and 299-B are accounting rules of that year.
   The questions are for a company or a dispute of that time, not for a company today.
7. **The tests are mine.**
   The expected values were written by hand from the text and not copied from the encoding's output, and a deliberately false assertion was run to show that an assertion can fail; but the author is the same, so the 1089 satisfied assertions show that my reading and my code agree, not that either is right.
   Nothing was run against a second reader's cases.
8. **The deontic rule is a device.**
   Art. 296, caput states a duty and no sanction on the company; `LEST BREACH` is how the encoding gives the duty a deadline (finding 30), and its four traces are read, not counted.
9. **The reading ends at the Law's text.**
   I read no case law, doctrine, CVM instruction or later amendment outside the compiled text, so a rule that the Law's text leaves to such a source answers NOTHING or takes an input, as the fork register says.
