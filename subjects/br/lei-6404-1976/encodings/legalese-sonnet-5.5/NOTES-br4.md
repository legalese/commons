## Seat

**The seat.**
Um acionista, inclusive o acionista minoritário, de uma companhia (sociedade anônima), perguntando o que a Lei lhe garante, o que lhe exige, e o que a companhia, os administradores e o acionista controlador podem fazer em relação a ele.
(A shareholder, including a minority shareholder, of a company, asking what the Law gives them, what it requires of them, and what the company, its managers and its controlling shareholder may do in relation to them.)
The brief fixes this seat for all seven pieces; the Commons ruling of 2026-10-18 (the individual to whom the law is applied) and the ruling of 2026-10-21 (a longer encoding keeps its modules and gets a seat layer in the same folder) are what make `br4-seat.l4` a layer over the eight layer-1 modules and not a replacement for them.
There is no seat for the conselho fiscal, the diretoria, the CVM or any other organ: what they must do appears only as what the shareholder is owed or exposed to.

**Why this seat suits this range.**
Chapters XII to XIV are mostly about organs, but they give the shareholder some of the most used rights of the Law, and several of them belong to a minority: to require the voto múltiplo (art. 141, 10% of the voting capital), to elect a conselheiro and a conselheiro fiscal in a separate vote (art. 141, § 4º; art. 161, § 4º), to require disclosure at the ordinary meeting (art. 157, § 1º, 5%), to bring the action against an administrator the meeting would not bring (art. 159, §§ 3º and 4º), to ask the conselho fiscal for information (art. 163, § 6º), to ask for its installation (art. 161, § 2º), to subscribe in proportion to the shares held (art. 171), and to be paid for the fractions of a capitalisation (art. 169, § 3º).
Each threshold in those articles is a plain input of the seat, so that a minority holder can state what the group holds and read the answer.

**What the layer is.**
`br4-seat.l4` holds 215 questions, each a `DECIDE` with `@export` (the question in the first person, ending in `?`, with the citation in it) and an `@desc` on every input (one sentence worded to the shareholder, with the unit where there is one), grouped under eight `§§` headings in the order of the text: the administration of the company (arts. 138 and 139, 7 questions), the conselho de administração (arts. 140 to 142, 25), the diretoria (arts. 143 and 144, 7), who may be an administrador, the investiture, the resignation and the pay (arts. 145 to 152, 33), the administrators' duties and liability (arts. 153 to 160, 47), the conselho fiscal (arts. 161 to 165-A, 42), the increase of the capital (arts. 166 to 172, 45) and the reduction of the capital (arts. 173 and 174, 9).
`br4-cases-seat.l4` has 669 assertions, at least one per question, written from the text before they were run: the boundary on each side of every threshold the question exposes (10%, 15%, 5%, 1/3, 3/4, 48 hours, 30 days, 60 days, three months, 10 days before a deadline), each branch of a rule, and a not-covered case (`NOTHING`) wherever a question has one.
Every rule name of the seat is a capitalised Portuguese question with no citation, so that it cannot collide with the rules of the layer-1 modules or with those of the other pieces' seats.

**How a question is built.**
It takes plain inputs and calls the layer-1 rule; it never restates a rule.
Where the layer-1 rule wants a record (`Perfil da companhia`, `Estatuto quanto ao conselho de administração (art. 140)`, `Conduta do administrador quanto à lealdade (art. 155)`, and so on) the question builds it in its `WHERE` from the inputs, and a field the rule never reads gets a fixed placeholder with a comment saying so (each of the nine `Perfil da companhia` records the seat builds marks every placeholder field).
Where a rule is built from two or three layer-1 rules, the seat calls the one that composes them (for example `a vedação de acumular os cargos alcança a pessoa (art. 138, §§ 3º e 4º)`, which already uses the two limbs), and the components are asked only through it: arts. 138 §§ 3º and 4º, 140 incisos I to IV, 143 incisos I to IV, 147 § 1º, 155 incisos I to III and 168 § 1º alíneas a to d (18 layer-1 rules in all) have no question of their own for that reason.
Every other one of the 235 layer-1 rules is called by a question.
The facts several pieces ask keep the keel's canonical name and `@desc` as they stand: `o tipo da companhia`, `a companhia tem conselho de administração`, `a companhia tem conselho fiscal permanente`, `a companhia tem capital autorizado`, `a quantidade de ações do acionista` and `o total de votos que podem ser dados`.
Where the Law hands a fact to someone else's judgment (whether the administrator acted with the care of an active and honest man, whether a gratuitous act is reasonable) the seat takes the conclusion as a fact the shareholder states, as the layer-1 rules do; it never guesses.

**Paired questions.**
Where the Law gives the shareholder a claim, a privilege or a power, the questions ask for the facts about the other side on which the text makes it depend: whether the company has fixed the term, whether it has proposed the action in time, whether the administrators have notified the exchange, whether the issue is inside the authorised limit.
Where the Law also puts a duty or a liability on the shareholder, the paired question is there: what I answer for if I use what the administrators revealed outside the company's legitimate interest (art. 157, § 3º), whether I am forbidden to use inside information (art. 155, § 4º), whether I answer jointly with an administrator if I helped an unlawful act for gain (art. 158, § 5º), whether I must carry out the urgent acts when the diretoria is vacant and I hold the most shares (art. 150, § 2º), and whether my rights are suspended until I present the share certificates (art. 173, § 2º).

**What a CVM or ministerial act would change.**
The Law leaves the following to the CVM, and the seat takes each as an input, never as a constant: the act that exempts smaller companies from the ban on one person holding two offices (art. 138, § 4º, with the size test of art. 294-B), the terms and deadlines of the independent conselheiros (art. 140, § 2º), the terms of the conselheiro's declaration (art. 147, § 4º), and the content, form and time of the information on shareholdings (arts. 157, § 6º and 165-A, where the seat asks only whether the duty exists).
A new CVM act would change those answers; no fixed percentage or date stands in for them.
The percentages and fractions the Law itself states (10%, 15%, 5%, 50%, 1%, 1/3, 3/4, 25%) are constants of the layer-1 rules, and the seat takes the facts they are measured against.

**Situations.**
Several groups arise only in a situation the shareholder may not be in; the comment after each `§§` heading says so, so the form can ask "has this happened to you?" first: the duties and liability questions (a shareholder who thinks an administrator has failed), the questions on being elected (arts. 146 and 147, only if the shareholder is or may be elected), the increase of the capital (only when the company is raising capital or capitalising reserves), and the reduction of the capital (only when the meeting is to reduce it; the creditor's questions only for a shareholder who is also a creditor, and the debenture question only for one who also holds debentures).

**Not covered.**
Where the text answers only part of a question the answer is a `MAYBE` and the case that the text does not reach is asserted as `NOTHING`: the voto múltiplo vacancy with a substitute (art. 141, § 3º), the preference over shares themselves under art. 171, § 3º, a special meeting where only the ordinary and extraordinary are named (arts. 159, § 1º; 161, § 3º; 163, inciso V), an independence test before the CVM has defined it (art. 140, § 2º), a company with no shares at all (arts. 169 and 171), and the reductions of arts. 45 and 107 under art. 174.

## Jural relations

Each relation is encoded once, in layer 1, under a neutral name, and the seat layer asks about it from the shareholder's side.
"Not named" in the first column means that the text creates the duty but does not say who may demand it.

## Needs from the core

- **A time limit in hours.** Art. 141, § 1º ("até 48 (quarenta e oito) horas antes da assembléia-geral") is the only limit of this Law counted in hours that this piece meets, and the core's `Prazo` has days, months and years only.
  This piece takes `a quantidade de horas entre o requerimento e a assembleia-geral` as a NUMBER and tests it AT LEAST 48.
  If the lead promotes an hours constructor to `Prazo`, `o requerimento do voto múltiplo foi feito no prazo (art. 141, § 1º)` is the one rule to change.
- **Canonical names for facts that several seat questions ask.**
  The keel's canonical table has nine facts; this piece's seat asks five more under its own names, and another piece that asks the same fact under another name would make the form ask it twice.
  Proposed canonical names, each used in more than one question here: `a espécie de assembleia` (`Espécie de assembleia`, "Que espécie de assembleia vai deliberar?"), `a data de referência` (DATE, "Em que dia (dia, mês e ano) você faz a pergunta?"), `o conselho fiscal está em funcionamento` (BOOLEAN, "O conselho fiscal da sua companhia está em funcionamento?"), `o total de ações com direito a voto` (NUMBER) and `o valor do capital social, em reais` (NUMBER).
  Nothing in this piece needs them to exist: the seat defines its own parameters and passes them positionally.
- **A date input is a plain DATE.**
  Every date the seat asks (art. 141, § 6º; 149, § 1º; 150; 159, § 3º; 163; 166, § 1º; 171, § 5º; 174) is a `DATE` input with "dia, mês e ano" in the `@desc`; the core's `YMD` shim is used only in the cases.

Numbered findings. Each ends "Asserted." where a case in `br4-tests-*.l4` or `br4-cases-seat.l4` asserts it.

Asserted.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Failures sorted by how they announce themselves.

**Silent (exit 0, a wrong answer):**

- `NOT` swallows everything to its right on the same line, with or without parentheses around its operand. `NOT a AND b` and `NOT (a) AND b` both mean `NOT (a AND b)`, and `NOT (a) OR b` means `NOT (a OR b)`. Measured with `l4 run` on a two-input probe: for a = TRUE, b = TRUE, `NOT (a) OR b` evaluates to FALSE and `(NOT a) OR b` to TRUE. Only the parenthesis around the whole `NOT` expression is safe, so every `NOT` in this piece's rules is written `(NOT x)`. A layout line-break before the `AND` or `OR` also separates them, which is why two early rules happened to come out right; the first rule where it went wrong was `quem convoca a assembleia-geral por vacância de todo um órgão (art. 150, §§ 1º e 2º)`, three failed assertions, found by the tests.
- A `BRANCH IF … THEN … IF … THEN … OTHERWISE` chain gives the same answers as nested `IF/ELSE IF/ELSE` once the `NOT` is parenthesised (probed on a three-input function).

**Loud (a parse or type error):**

- `25%` as an argument in a call (`f 25% 150 2000`) is a parse error, "unexpected 150"; written `(25%)` it parses. The percent sign is accepted inside an expression (`x AT LEAST 10% TIMES y`) and as the only argument of a call.
- `ELSE IF c THEN x ELSE y` on one line followed by `ELSE NOTHING` at a lesser indent is a layout error, "incorrect indentation (got 10, should be greater than 10)"; breaking before `THEN` and `ELSE` fixes it.
- `IMPORT` of a sibling module resolves only beside the importing file; a probe in a subfolder fails with "Module not found: br-core", which is a warning rather than an error, so it reads as a clean run unless one greps for it.
- **Seat layer, measured on 1.6.98 while writing `br4-seat.l4`.**
  Loud: none new.
  A `DECIDE` with no `GIVEN` compiles and can be asserted (an assertion of the form ``#ASSERT `Q` EQUALS TRUE``); the empty list is `EMPTY`, not `LIST` alone; an `@desc` with `?`, parentheses, `§` and accents is accepted; a record built in a `WHERE` with `--` comment lines between its fields is accepted; importing `br-core` and all eight layer-1 modules into one file works (no "multiple definitions", because every name of this piece ends with a citation and every question has none).
  Silent: none new; the `NOT` trap above does not arise in the seat, which has no `NOT`.
  **Positive control.** After the cases passed, two deliberately wrong assertions (that the shareholder cannot assign the right of preference, and that 200 new shares are `JUST 201`) were appended to a copy of the cases file and both reported `assertion failed`, so the 669 satisfied assertions are counted by an instrument that does fail.

## Before this is worth relying on

- **The text is not the official text.**
  The source is a converter's plain-text rendering of Planalto's compiled version, retrieved on 2026-10-21; every `src:N` locator and every quoted line was checked against that file by `srcrefs.py` (0 problems in each of the eight modules), but nothing was checked against the Diário Oficial.
- **No lawyer has read it.**
  The 19 `fork` rows of the coverage table (F4 to F18) are readings an engineer took where the text is open: who is a "diretor" for art. 138, § 1º, whether the 60th day of art. 174 is inside the period, whether the preference window of art. 171, § 5º is open until the end of the period, and so on.
  Each is stated in the Fork register with the other reading, so that a lawyer can overrule it in one place, but until one has, the answers that depend on them are the engineer's.
- **No independent test pass was run.**
  The expected values of both layers were written from the text by the same agent that wrote the rules, before each run; the assertions never failed against the code, so no case here caught a rule that was wrong, and a reader who wants evidence that the cases bite has the positive control recorded in `## What the stable binary does not accept`.
  A second reader, writing cases from the text without seeing these rules, is the test that is still owed.
- **The seat has not been deployed or put in front of a shareholder.**
  Whether the form can ask the inputs that are lists (`LIST OF NUMBER`, `LIST OF Condenação do candidato (art. 147, § 1º)`), optional (`MAYBE DATE`, `MAYBE NUMBER`) or a choice among many constructors (`Parte`, with 21) is not known, and a `MAYBE` answer (`NOTHING` for a case the Law does not reach) is printed by the form as whatever it prints for an empty value.
  The 215 questions are many for one chapter; the order and the `§§` situations are the only guide the form has to ask fewer of them.
- **Inputs that other pieces will answer.**
  This range takes 12 facts decided outside it as plain inputs, each marked `-- out of range` in the module (see the cross-reference table): arts. 8, 18, 45 and 107 (one input), 71, 82 (two inputs), 98, 132, 182 § 2º, 190, 202 and 294-B; and the CVM-delegated terms above, which belong to no piece.
  Until the merged encoding links them, the shareholder is asked each one directly.
- **What an amendment would change.**
  The text carries amendments from 2001 (Lei nº 10.303), 2013 (Lei nº 12.838), 2021 (Leis nº 14.195 and Complementar nº 182) and others; only the live wording is encoded, and a later amendment to a threshold (10%, 15%, 5%, the 30 and 60 days) changes one constant in one layer-1 rule, which the tests will then fail on, by design.
