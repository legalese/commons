## Seat

The seat is the one the brief fixes: "um acionista, inclusive o acionista minoritário, de uma companhia (sociedade anônima), perguntando o que a Lei lhe garante, o que lhe exige, e o que a companhia, os administradores e o acionista controlador podem fazer em relação a ele".
In English: a shareholder, including a minority shareholder, of a company (sociedade anônima), asking what the Law gives them, what it requires of them, and what the company, its managers and its controlling shareholder may do in relation to them.
Chapters XVII to XX are written for the company, its organs, its creditors and the authorities as much as for the shareholder, so the seat asks of each provision only what it means for the shareholder: what it gives them (a claim, a privilege, a power, an immunity), what it requires of them or exposes them to (a duty, a liability), and what it decides about their company.
The text is a converter's plain-text rendering of a compiled text of the Law, not the official text; nothing here claims to have been checked against the official text.

Layer 2 is two files.
`br6-seat.l4` holds 111 questions, with 326 plain inputs, in 14 groups under `§§` headings in the text's order, each heading carrying in a comment the situation in which the group is asked (for instance, only if the company is in liquidation).
`br6-cases-seat.l4` holds 414 assertions, at least one for every question, and for every question that can return NOTHING a case that asserts it.
Each question calls one layer-1 rule and builds the records that rule expects in its `WHERE` from the plain inputs, so no rule is restated.
Each plain input has an `@desc` worded as the shareholder would be asked, each question has an `@export` in the first person with the citation in the text, and the names of the questions carry no citation.

How the seat was drawn.
- The facts that several pieces ask (the type of the company, whether it is of economia mista, the species of the shares, the number of shares, whether they are paid up, the votes) use the canonical names and `@desc` of `KEEL-NOTES.md`.
- The canonical votes question ("Quantos votos estavam presentes, a favor, contra e em branco?") is split into one question per field, and a question asks only the fields its rule reads: Arts. 215, § 1º and 252, § 2º ask the total and the votes in favour.
- A field of a record that the called rule never reads is given a fixed placeholder, with a comment saying so; a field it reads is always asked.
- Where the text gives the shareholder a claim, a privilege, a power or an immunity, the other party's side is asked as facts (what the liquidator, the majority, the company or the controller has done or must have done), and many questions ask whether a document the Law owes the shareholder was received (the protocol, the justification, the instrument of the offer, the notes), which is how a claim is asked.
- Where the text puts a duty or a liability on the shareholder, the paired question is asked beside the claim: what the liquidator may demand of the shareholder (Art. 210, V); what the majority may impose (Art. 215, § 1º); what a creditor may demand and what the shareholder may recover from the others (Art. 218); the 5% and the security of the suit (Arts. 206, II, b and 246, § 1º); whether the shareholder's duties subsist (Art. 217).
- Facts that the Law takes from another range (the time limit and conditions of Art. 137, the CVM's norms, the conditions of Art. 30) are plain inputs, with the hint of the text where it has one.
- A rule that the Law leaves open for a case returns a MAYBE, and the cases assert the NOTHING.

## Jural relations

One row for each relation that a provision creates, and one `none` row for a provision that creates none (a definition, a status, a counting rule, or what is inert or repealed).
Holder and bearer are named in Hohfeld's terms: a claim has a duty as its correlative; a privilege, a no-right; a power, a liability; an immunity, a disability.
Where the text states a duty and names no one who may demand it, the holder cell says so, and no holder is invented.
The `L4 rule` column is the layer-1 rule that encodes the provision (from the coverage table), and the `seat question` column is the question of `br6-seat.l4` that asks the relation, or `not asked:` and the reason.
Each relation is stated once, in one row, from the side the text states it; the seat asks its other side as the paired question where the shareholder is burdened.
A provision whose sentence has two clauses that the encoding separates has one row for each clause (Art. 213, § 1º).
Six rows name a group of coverage rows, because one question carries the group: art. 243, § 1º, § 4º and § 5º; art. 244, caput and § 1º; art. 251, caput, § 1º and § 2º; art. 252, §§ 1º and 2º; art. 257, §§ 2º and 3º; art. 264, §§ 4º and 5º.
Each member of a group has its own coverage row, and the renderer fails if a row names a provision that has none.
There are 177 rows for 161 coverage rows: 115 name a seat question, 62 say why none is asked, and 38 rows are `none` rows (a definition, a status, or an inert or repealed provision; a status may still be asked as a question).

## What the stable binary does not accept

Measured on the stable `l4` 1.6.98 (`1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`) on 2026-10-22, with `JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` unset.

- **Method decision, not a refusal by the binary: no `PARTY ... MUST` or `MAY` rule is written.**
  The stable binary accepts regulative rules, but an `#ASSERT` cannot compare a `DEONTIC` value (`Trying to check equality on types that do not support it`: `source-patterns` entry 5.2, which I read and did not re-measure), and duties are exercised with `#TRACE`, which counts nothing in `check.sh`.
  So every duty and power of this range is a predicate over facts (a BOOLEAN, a NUMBER, a DATE or a MAYBE), which can be asserted case by case, and no deontic wrapper and no `#TRACE` test exists.
  A reader who needs "who must do what by when" as an obligation value will not find one in this piece (see "Before this is worth relying on").

- **An unparenthesised field access as an argument of a rule application does not parse.**
  Measured on 2026-10-22 with the stable `l4` 1.6.98 (`1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`, sha256 beginning 82ea44ad): `` `quatro` r's f r's g `` gives `unexpected 's` at the second `'s`; `` `g` r's f `` with one argument gives `You are trying to apply r ... (which is not a function) to 1 argument here`.
  Inside a parenthesised operand after an `OR` the same mistake is reported at the `OR` of that operand (`unexpected OR`, `expecting %, ;, WHERE, end of input, or space token`), not at the `'s`, which sent me looking at the layout.
  Written as `` `quatro` (r's f) (r's g) `` each of them checks.

- **A multi-line parenthesised first operand followed by an `OR` is not the cause of that error.**
  I first read the `unexpected OR` as a layout rule and wrote helper rules (one small rule per alínea of Art. 206, II) to avoid it.
  Eight minimal files I wrote with that layout all check (a multi-line parenthesised `AND` first operand followed by an `OR`, with and without spaces after the opening parenthesis, with plain names, with `r's f` fields, with backticked names, and with two and three operands).
  Two further files with the same layout fail, and in both the only cause is an unparenthesised field access as an argument; adding the parentheses makes each of them check, with the layout unchanged, so I could not reproduce a layout failure; the helper rules remain because they follow the text's alíneas, not because the binary needs them.

- **What I used that works, measured on this binary:** `maximum (LIST a, b)`; `MAYBE` with `JUST` and `NOTHING`, and `#ASSERT (rule args) EQUALS NOTHING`; `IS A MAYBE DATE` as an input type; an enumeration as an input type; `§§` sub-headings; a seat that imports the prelude, the core and eleven modules without a name clash (the check of the whole folder shows it); records built with `` `Type` WITH `f` IS v ``; `CONSIDER` with `WHEN` and `OTHERWISE`; fractions with `%` and thresholds tested by multiplication.

- **What I kept out because the keel lists it as rejected by the stable binary, or as degrading silently (`@nlg:xx`):** `REFUSE` and `BECAUSE` (except `LEST BREACH BECAUSE`), `@lang`, `@nlg:xx`, a section `GIVEN` (every rule that reads an input has its own `GIVEN`), `TYPICALLY`, `SET OF`, an opaque `DECLARE T`.
  I did not re-measure these; `KEEL-NOTES.md`, "What the stable binary does not accept", carries the probes.

## Before this is worth relying on

- The text is a converter's plain-text rendering of a compiled text of the Law, not the official text, and nothing here was checked against the official text; the strike-through of earlier wordings is the converter's, and I did not check whether each struck span is out of force.

- The 25 forks are readings I took, each licensed by the words I cite and none ruled by a lawyer.
  The ones that change an answer most are F-cap (5% in shares, not by value or votes), F-209 and F-209b (who must have failed, and the 30 days), F-230 (the wrong inciso), F-243a (the presumption can be defeated), F-248a and F-248b (the alíneas and the orphan paragraph), F-254A4 (which way the premium runs), F-256 (the profit figure), F-261a and F-261b (5% of the price, the number of shares) and F-264 (which company must be open).

- The counting convention of the core is assumed throughout: the day of the event is day 0, and a month ends on the last day of a shorter month.
  A different convention moves the boundary days of every rule that counts a limit from an event: Arts. 209, II, 210, III, 213, 216, § 2º, 223, §§ 3º and 4º, 230, 231, § 1º, 232, 233, parágrafo único, 236, parágrafo único, 244, §§ 4º and 5º, 248, I, 250, § 4º, 261, § 1º and 264, § 3º.

- The facts of other pieces are plain inputs (Arts. 8, 10, 30, 45, 116 and 117, 136, 137, 171, 183, 187; see "Cross-references out of my ranges").
  When br1, br3, br4 and br5 are merged those inputs should become calls, and the answers of this piece should be re-run against theirs.

- There is no deontic layer and no `#TRACE` test (see "What the stable binary does not accept"): the duties of the liquidator, the administrators, the offeror and the controlling society are predicates a case answers, not obligations a trace can follow.

- No norm of the Comissão de Valores Mobiliários or of the Banco Central do Brasil is encoded, because none is in the text; seven provisions that only give the CVM power to make norms are `inert`.
  Nothing varies with the date on which the Law is read, although the range carries amendments from 1986 to 2021; only the live wording is encoded.

- The expected values of 1,171 assertions were written by me from the text before each run and no one else has read them; a lawyer should read the findings above and the cases of the seat before relying on any answer.

- The seat has 111 questions in 14 groups, more than a short form would hold; the brief asks for a question for every provision that gives a claim, privilege, power or immunity, puts a duty or liability on the person, or decides something they would ask, and I followed it.
  Pruning or ordering them for the form is a decision for the lead; the "Jural relations" table says which question carries which provision.

- I read part of the `source-patterns/` pages of the skill (the index, and in the pages on duties, on discretion and on the unknown, the entries I needed), not all of them; a pattern in a page I did not read may have been missed.
