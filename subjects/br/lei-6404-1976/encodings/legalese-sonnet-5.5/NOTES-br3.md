## Seat

The seat is a shareholder, minority holders included, of a company, asking what the Law guarantees them, what it demands of them, and what the company, its managers and its controlling shareholder may do in relation to them.
Capítulos X and XI are where the Law speaks to the shareholder as such: what the shareholder owes (the instalments of Art. 106, the signature in the Attendance Book of Art. 127), what the shareholder is guaranteed (the essential rights of Art. 109, the vote, being called and told, the documents before the annual meeting, the ratification by the class of Art. 136 § 1º, the right to withdraw of Art. 137), and what can be done to them (the measures against a defaulter of Art. 107, the suspension of rights of Art. 120, the abusive vote and the controller's abuse of power of Arts. 115 and 117).
The minority shareholder is the person for whom most of the guarantees are written, so the seat does not split them from the majority holder.
Each question is an `@export` rule in `br3-seat.l4` that only calls a layer-1 rule; no rule is decided in the seat file.

How the questions are shaped:

- Each is phrased as the shareholder would ask it ("Devo ...?", "Posso ...?", "Tenho direito a ...?"), and its inputs are facts that person could state: a date (`AAAA-MM-DD`), a count of shares or votes, a yes or no about what the other side did.
- Where the text gives the shareholder a claim, privilege, power or immunity, the facts about the other party on which the text makes it depend are plain inputs (whether the managers made the call, whether the notice carried the matter, whether the controller contracted on equitable terms).
  Where the text also puts a duty or a liability on the shareholder, there is a paired question: the instalment (#1) and the call that must precede it (#2, #3); the default (#4) and what the company may do about it (#8); the obligation that brings suspension (#88) and the end of the suspension (#89); the abusive vote (#37) and the liability for it (#41); the right to withdraw (#181) and the loss of it (#191).
- Where a provision addresses only the company, the managers or the CVM (the call by a meeting's table, the postponement by the CVM in Art. 124 § 5º, the authorisation of Art. 136 § 2º), it is in layer 1 for what it decides and in the seat only as what the shareholder is owed or exposed to ("A CVM pode adiar a assembleia a pedido de acionista?").
  There is no seat for an official.
- A rule that returns `MAYBE BOOLEAN` is asked as it is: `JUST TRUE` and `JUST FALSE` are the Law's answers, `NOTHING` means that the provision asked about says nothing in that situation (another article may), and each such question has at least one case that asserts `NOTHING`.
- The 191 questions have 553 cases in `br3-cases-seat.l4`; every question has at least one, every case is asserted, and the expected values are written by hand in the specification from the text, not copied from the output.

Layer 1 is bigger than the seat: 202 rules are reachable from the 191 questions (the seat calls 191 directly; the other 11 are parts of a combined rule that a question calls, listed after the table).
The seat was generated from a specification kept in scratch work and the files in this folder are the deliverable; the scratch generator was deleted with the rest of `work/`, so edit the seat files by hand from here.

## Jural relations

Hohfeld's correlatives: a claim (right) is the other party's duty; a privilege (liberty) is the other party's no-right; a power is the other party's liability (being subject to it); an immunity is the other party's disability.
Each relation is encoded once, in the rule named, and neutrally; the seat layer reads it from the shareholder's side.
A provision that creates no such relation is listed once as `none`.

## What the text turns out to say

1. **Art. 107, § 2º does not list the overdue instalment among the deductions from the proceeds of the sale.** The text deducts "as despesas com a operação" and, only "se previstos no estatuto", interest, monetary correction and the fine, and leaves "o saldo" to the ex-shareholder. The debt that justified the sale (the instalment itself) is not on the list, so a literal reading leaves the ex-shareholder a balance that still includes it. Pinned by: proceeds 100,000, expenses 2,000 and charges of 1,800 provided in the estatuto give a balance of 96,200, and with no charges provided in the estatuto 98,000, whatever amounts are typed in for them (`br3-tests-cap10-sec1-capital.l4`). Asserted.
2. **Art. 110-A, § 9º makes a quorum depend on how the Law words it.** A quorum the Law states "com base em percentual de ações ou do capital social, sem menção ao número de votos" counts each share once, while a quorum stated in votes (Arts. 125, 129 and 136) counts the plural vote. A shareholder holding a plural-vote class therefore counts for 10 in a vote-based quorum and for 1 in a capital-based one. Pinned by: ten votes per share weigh 10 when the quorum is in votes and 1 when it is not (`br3-tests-cap10-sec3-voto-plural.l4`). Asserted.
3. **Art. 110-A, § 1º counts half of all the votes, not of the votes present.** Creation of the plural class needs "metade, no mínimo, do total de votos conferidos pelas ações com direito a voto", the total of all votes (the core's `o total de votos que podem ser dados`); 500 of 1,000 is enough and 499 is not. If preferred shares without vote or with restricted vote are issued, half of those shares must also approve, in a special meeting held with the Law's formalities. Pinned by the cases with 500 and 499 of 1,000 votes and 50 and 49 of 100 preferred shares (`br3-tests-cap10-sec3-voto-plural.l4`). Asserted.
4. **Art. 111, § 1º gives the preferred shares the vote only when the estatuto's period has run, and the two payment facts are not interchangeable.** The vote is acquired when the company fails to pay for the period in the estatuto (at most 3 consecutive fiscal years) and is kept until the dividend is paid or, for cumulative dividends, until the arrears are paid. Pinned by: a cumulative dividend with only the year's dividend paid still leaves the vote, and a non-cumulative dividend with only the arrears marked paid still leaves it (`br3-tests-cap10-sec3-voto.l4`). Asserted.
5. **Art. 115, § 2º lifts only one of the four grounds of impediment of § 1º.** If all subscribers are co-owners of what they contributed they may approve the valuation report, but the shareholder is still barred if the deliberation benefits him in a particular way, concerns his accounts as manager, or finds him in conflict. Pinned by the five-input cases of `o acionista pode votar a deliberação (art. 115, §§ 1º e 2º)` (`br3-tests-cap10-sec3-voto.l4`). Asserted.
6. **The live text of Art. 126 has no rule for proving one's capacity as holder of endorsable shares.** The old inciso II about them is struck; the live inciso II is about book-entry shares and inciso IV repeats it word for word. Pinned by: for endorsable shares the answer is NOTHING (`br3-tests-cap11-sec1-instalacao-deliberacao.l4`). Asserted.
7. **Art. 129 does not say whether abstentions that are not blank votes count in the base of the absolute majority.** With 100 present, 45 in favour, 30 against and 25 abstentions that are not blank, the vote passes if the base is the valid votes cast and fails if it is the votes present; the rule says NOTHING. Pinned by that case and by 51 in favour, 30 against, 19 abstentions, which passes under both readings (`br3-tests-cap11-sec1-instalacao-deliberacao.l4`). Asserted.
8. **Art. 120 and Art. 109 are not reconciled by the text.** The general meeting may suspend the exercise of a defaulting shareholder's rights, and may not deprive any shareholder of the five essential rights; whether a suspension of an essential right is a deprivation is not said. Pinned by: for an essential right the answer is NOTHING, and for another right of a defaulter it is JUST TRUE (`br3-tests-cap10-sec6-7-exterior-suspensao.l4`). Asserted.
9. **Art. 136 lets only a company whose shares are not admitted to trading raise the quorum by estatuto, on the natural reading.** The qualifier "cujas ações não estejam admitidas à negociação em bolsa ou no mercado de balcão" hangs on "companhia". With 550 of 1,000 votes in favour and an estatuto asking 60%, the vote fails in a company whose shares are not admitted and is undecided (NOTHING) in one whose shares are, because the readings part. Pinned by those two cases (`br3-tests-cap11-sec3-age-quorum.l4`). Asserted.
10. **Art. 137, inciso II: exactly half held by the controlling group is not dispersion, and liquidity and dispersion are both needed.** "Menos da metade" is strict: 49 of 100 shares is dispersion, 50 is not; a class that is in the CVM's index but 51 of 100 held by the controller still has no dispersion. Pinned by the cases at 49, 50 and 51 of 100 and by the case that lacks the index (`br3-tests-cap11-sec3-retirada.l4`). Asserted.
11. **The withdrawal runs on three clocks that are easy to run together.** The shares count if held on the earlier of the first notice and the relevant-fact notice (§ 1º); the claim for reimbursement is due within 30 days of the publication of the minutes (incisos IV and V); the managers then have ten more days to convene the meeting (§ 3º); and the payment can be demanded only after that (inciso VI). With the minutes published on 4 May 2026 the claim runs to 3 June, the managers' window to 13 June, and payment is demandable from 14 June; a shareholder who has not claimed by 4 June has lost the right (§ 4º). Pinned by those dates (`br3-tests-cap11-sec3-retirada.l4`). Asserted.
12. **Art. 137, § 2º makes the abstainer and the absentee dissenters.** Only a holder who voted in favour is not a dissenter (the rule says so by "ainda que o titular ... tenha se abstido de votar contra a deliberação ou não tenha comparecido"). Pinned by the two cases (`br3-tests-cap11-sec3-retirada.l4`). Asserted.
13. **Art. 136, § 1º counts shares of the class, not votes, and "mais da metade" is strict.** 51 of 100 shares of the prejudiced class approve; 50 do not. This is the only place in the Arts. 135 to 136 that counts shares, beside Art. 136-A, § 2º, inciso I (a listing segment's dispersion requirement, stated as a fact). Pinned by 51 and 50 of 100 (`br3-tests-cap11-sec3-age-quorum.l4`). Asserted.

Pinned by a case for each constructor in the three files (`br3-tests-cap11-sec3-age-quorum.l4`, `br3-tests-cap11-sec3-retirada.l4`). Asserted.
15. **Art. 133, § 4º cures the notices, not the documents.** A meeting of all the shareholders may deem the missing notices or time limits cured, but the documents must still be published before the meeting; and § 3º's five days do not apply to the fiscal council's opinion or to the other documents (incisos IV and V). Pinned by the two rules of § 4º and by NOTHING for incisos IV and V (`br3-tests-cap11-sec2-ago.l4`). Asserted.
16. **Art. 136-A's arbitration clause binds everyone from the day after the thirtieth day, and the dissenter's right to withdraw is lost in two cases.** With the minutes published on 4 May 2026 the clause is effective from 4 June, not 3 June. The right to withdraw does not apply if the clause is a condition of listing in a segment that demands 25% dispersion, or if the company is open and the class has liquidity and dispersion (Art. 137, inciso II). Pinned by those dates and by the cases of each exception (`br3-tests-cap11-sec3-age-quorum.l4`). Asserted.

## Before this is worth relying on

Read these before anyone relies on this encoding for a real shareholder.

1. **The text is a converter's rendering, not the official text.**
   Every rule quotes `lei-6404-1976.pt.txt`, a plain-text rendering by a converter script of Planalto's compiled text retrieved on 2026-10-21; Planalto's own footer says only the Diário Oficial da União text is official.
   Nothing in this folder was compared with the official text, and where Planalto shows superseded wording struck through the encoding follows the live wording only.
   A lawyer should check each quoted `-- src:N |` line against the official text before the answer of any question is acted on.
2. **The expected values were written by the same agent that wrote the rules, from the same rendering.**
   The 12 test files and the seat cases (1,361 assertions in all: 808 in the test files and 553 in the seat cases, as `## What check.sh prints` counts them) show that the rules do what their author read the text to say.
   They do not show that the author read it correctly; no second reader and no lawyer has looked at any of them.
3. **Thirty readings are the author's own** (`## Fork register`, F-br3-1 to F-br3-30).
   None was ruled by anyone; each is labelled in the register as the reading taken, with the text that licenses the others.
   Where the text does not settle a point and the rule would otherwise guess, the rule returns `NOTHING` (the Law answers nothing here), and the seat passes that on as "the provision says nothing"; a caller who treats `NOTHING` as "no" is making a reading of their own.
4. **A total of zero is not guarded in the quorum rules.**
   `0 AT LEAST 5% TIMES 0` is TRUE on the stable binary (`## What the stable binary does not accept`, item 4).
   The rules that compare a count or an amount with a fraction of a total (Arts. 110-A §§ 1º and 3º, 122 inciso X, 123 alínea c, 124 § 3º, 125, 126 § 3º, 129, 135, 136 and its §§ 1º, 2º and 2º-A, and 137 inciso II) take that total as an input and do not guard against a zero; only the second limb of Art. 123, parágrafo único, alínea d does, and a case pins that guard.
   With a zero total an AT LEAST test is satisfied by a zero count, while a LESS THAN or GREATER THAN test is not, so the error runs in different directions in different rules.
   No case asserts the unguarded behaviour; callers must pass the real total, which is more than zero for any company that has shares and any meeting that has a vote to count.
5. **Facts the Law leaves to others are inputs, not computed.**
   Whether the shares are "admitidas à negociação em bolsa ou no mercado de balcão" (Arts. 124 § 6º, 136, 137), whether a class "integra índice geral representativo de carteira de valores mobiliários definido pela CVM" (Art. 137), the criteria of relevance of the CVM (Art. 122, X) and the fiscal year's end (Art. 175, in another piece's range) are plain inputs; the encoding does not know them and the seat asks the shareholder for them.
   The table `## Cross-references out of my ranges` has 25 rows: 17 depend on provisions in other pieces' ranges, 5 on rules outside the Law (regulations of the CVM and of the federal Executive, the CVM's index definition, the notice of a relevant fact), 2 point at other provisions inside this range, and 1 records that three other pieces read Art. 116 of this one.
   Where a value is needed it is taken as an input of the right type, so a case here shows nothing about the rule it depends on.
6. **The Law is encoded as in the compiled text of 2026-10-21, with no history.**
   The range holds no date of enactment, publication or entry into force (they are at line 5, in br1's range, and at lines 2555 and 2592, in br7's), so no date is carried as data and the encoding cannot answer "what was the rule on this date".
   The `(Vigência)` notes under Art. 136-A are Planalto's notes on the amending Lei nº 13.129, de 2015, and are not encoded as a commencement rule.
7. **The seat's wording was machine-assisted.**
   The 191 questions and their 553 cases come from a specification that was run through a generator in scratch work, which was deleted; the questions, the input descriptions (`@desc`) and the English glosses in `## Seat` are mine and have not been read by a lay shareholder.
   Some input names are long because they repeat the layer-1 name of the fact; a front end would reword them.
8. **What was not run:** no query with real facts from a real company, no mutation or fuzz run, no `l4 export`, and no second binary.
   `check.sh` was run as the brief directs, with `L4_TARGET=unstable`, which only tells the script not to compare with a second binary; every result in this folder comes from the one stable `l4` 1.6.98, and nothing was compared with a newer or an older build.
