## Seat

The seat is the one the brief fixes: "um acionista, inclusive o acionista minoritário, de uma companhia (sociedade anônima), perguntando o que a Lei lhe garante, o que lhe exige, e o que a companhia, os administradores e o acionista controlador podem fazer em relação a ele".
Layer 2 is two files.
`br1-seat.l4` holds 165 questions, each an `@export`ed `DECIDE`, grouped under four `§` chapter headings and 15 `§§` groups in the order of the text.
`br1-cases-seat.l4` holds 477 hand-written `#ASSERT` cases over those questions, all satisfied.

Why this seat.
Chapters I to IV are about what a shareholder holds: the share, the capital it belongs to, its form and certificate, how its circulation is limited, pledged or kept in custody, and how it is redeemed or reimbursed; Chapter IV adds the partes beneficiárias, a title that is not a share.
A holder asks "how much can I be made to pay for my shares", "may the company buy my shares back", "can I convert my bearer shares", "what do I get if I dissent".
Those are the questions layer 2 asks.
The brief says to ask a question for every provision that gives the person a claim, privilege, power or immunity, puts a duty or liability on them, or decides something they would ask.
It does not give a number of questions.

"Thin" means what the brief means by it.
Each of the 165 questions is one `DECIDE` whose body is a call of exactly one layer-1 rule, and no two questions call the same rule (measured on the file: 165 questions, 165 distinct layer-1 rules named in their bodies, at most one in any body).
No rule is restated, and layer 1 is not touched by layer 2.
A question that needs a record builds it in its `WHERE` from plain inputs (18 questions do), and any field the called rule never reads gets a fixed placeholder with a comment saying so.
A `MAYBE` fact in layer 1 (the estatuto says something, or says nothing) is asked as two plain inputs and assembled in the `WHERE`.

Inputs.
Every input is a plain yes/no, number, date or one of the core's enumerations, with an `@desc` worded as the shareholder would be asked.
12 of the keel's 16 canonical facts are asked under their canonical names with their canonical `@desc` strings as they stand: `o tipo da companhia`, `a companhia tem conselho de administração`, `a companhia tem capital autorizado`, `a espécie das ações`, `a forma das ações`, `as ações do acionista estão integralizadas`, `as ações do acionista têm valor nominal`, `o total de votos que podem ser dados`, `os votos dos titulares presentes`, `os votos a favor`, `os votos contra` and `os votos em branco`.
The other four (`a companhia tem conselho fiscal permanente`, `a companhia é de economia mista`, `a quantidade de ações do acionista`, `o acionista é acionista controlador (art. 116)`) are named in neither layer.
Nothing in Articles 1 to 51 turns on the first two; Art. 1 asks the count of shares under a name of its own (`a quantidade de ações que eu subscrevi ou adquiri`); and Arts. 4 § 4º and § 6º ask whether the offeror or the acquirer is the controlling shareholder, not the asking shareholder (see "Needs from the core", item 2).
Money is in reais and a count is a whole number of shares.

How to read an answer.
A rule that states a prohibition ("é vedado", "é proibido") answers TRUE when the forbidden thing holds and FALSE when it does not.
A rule that says what the Law "pode" allow answers `JUST TRUE` or `JUST FALSE` inside the scope the text covers, and `NOTHING` outside it; `NOTHING` means that in these articles the Law does not answer the case, and it does not mean "no" (fork F15).

Policy on duties and deadlines (a decision, not an accident): constitutive rules only.
Neither layer has a `PARTY ... MUST` obligation and neither has a `#TRACE`.
Reason: the shareholder states the facts after the event, so a deadline is a question about dates ("was it done in time?"), answered through the core's `o termo final do prazo`, `o ato foi praticado dentro do prazo` and `o ato respeitou a antecedência`, with the counting convention of the keel (the day of the event is day 0 and day N is the last day in time).
A regulative encoding would model a party that has not yet acted, whereas the seat's shareholder reports what has already happened, so each duty is a question about the facts (was the notice given in time?).
The cost is that nothing here can be run forward as a trace of what a party must do next; it can only judge what has already happened.
A cap on a duration that is not counted from an event (Art. 37's 15 and 90 days) is a bare number of days (fork F38).

One question asks for today's date: Art. 45 § 6º asks `a data de hoje`, because "the capital is deemed reduced" is a state that depends on the day it is asked, and the keel has no clock (see "Needs from the core").

## Jural relations

One row per provision that creates a relation, in the order of the text, and one `none` row for each provision that creates none: 162 relation rows and 9 `none` rows, 171 in all.
The first column is the provision as the Law numbers it.
The second names the party who holds the claim, privilege, power or immunity, and the third the party who bears the correlative duty, no-right, liability or disability (Hohfeld's pairs).
The fourth is the chapter and section of the Law, and the fifth the layer-1 rules that encode the relation, each ending in its citation.
Where the text names no holder of a duty it imposes (most prohibitions), the holder given is the person the rule protects.
That is my inference and not the text's; rows where the choice was real say "the text names no claimant", and the others rest on the same inference without saying so.
A "pode" is split: it is a privilege (the other side has a no-right) where the permitted act changes no one else's legal position, and a power (the other side has a liability) where it does.
Where the bearer is a state of affairs and not a person (a classification, a counting rule, a status) the row says `none` and why.
Three rows have no layer-1 rule of their own: Art. 4 § 3º and Art. 4-A § 4º (the CVM's regulation-making powers, inert in the coverage table) and Art. 43 § 5º (a citation of another law, a `none` row).

## Needs from the core

Used from `br-core.l4`: the enumerations `Tipo de companhia`, `Espécie de ação`, `Forma da ação` and `Valor mobiliário`; the records `Participação do acionista` and `Votação em assembleia`; the choice `Parte`; the three `Prazo` rules `o termo final do prazo`, `o ato foi praticado dentro do prazo` and `o ato respeitou a antecedência`; and the `YMD` shim (layer 1 uses it for the date of the Law; the tests and cases use it for every date).
Not used: `Perfil da companhia` (layer 1 asks its two relevant facts, `a companhia tem conselho de administração` and `a companhia tem capital autorizado`, as plain inputs under their canonical names) and `Espécie de assembleia`.

What I found missing or worth changing, none of it blocking:

1. The keel's one canonical `@desc` for the four vote counts says "Quantos votos estavam presentes, a favor, contra e em branco?".
   The seat uses it verbatim under each of `os votos dos titulares presentes`, `os votos a favor`, `os votos contra` and `os votos em branco`, so the shareholder is asked the same four-part question four times.
   One `@desc` per name would read better.
2. `o acionista é acionista controlador (art. 116)` is the asking shareholder's own status, which nothing in my range asks.
   Arts. 4 § 4º and § 6º need the same fact about another person, the offeror or the acquirer, so layer 1 has `o ofertante é acionista controlador (art. 116)` and `o adquirente é acionista controlador (art. 116)` and does not reuse the canonical name.
   A canonical way to say "this person is the controlling shareholder" would let br3, br6 and br1 ask it once.
3. There is no canonical date for "today".
   Art. 45 § 6º needs one (`a data de hoje`, asked in the seat and in layer 1), because whether the capital "se considera reduzido" depends on the day it is asked.
4. The core's `Prazo` is for a time limit counted from or before an event.
   Art. 37's 15 and 90 days are caps on how long a suspension may last, with no event to count from, so they are bare day counts (fork F38), and so are the "once a year" intervals of Art. 5 and Art. 35 § 2º (forks F20 and F35).
   If another piece meets a cap on a duration it will make the same choice or a different one.

## What the text turns out to say

Each finding is a reading of the text that the layer-1 rules take, with the boundary cases that pin it down.
"Asserted." means that a case in a `br1-tests-*.l4` file asserts it, with the boundary values quoted; every finding below has at least two such cases.
What the text leaves open is in the fork register and is not repeated here.

1. Art. 1 sets a ceiling and says nothing about what has been paid: the most a shareholder can be made to pay is the number of shares times the issue price, and the rule does not net payments already made against it (fork F13, a reading and not something the text says). A demand of 1,250 reais on 100 shares at 12.50 is within it, and 1,251 is not. Asserted.
2. Art. 4-A § 3º puts the cost of a new valuation on those who asked for it and those who voted for it when the new value is "inferior ou igual" to the initial offer, so a new value equal to the offer (100 against 100) already costs them and 101 does not. Asserted.
3. Art. 4 § 5º lets the general meeting redeem the shares that remain in circulation only when fewer than 5% of the shares issued remain (49 of 1,000 yes, 50 of 1,000 no), after the offer period has ended, at the offer value and with the redemption value deposited. Asserted.
4. Art. 44 § 6º requires, for the redemption of one or more classes, approval in a special meeting by holders of at least half of the shares of the affected classes (exactly half is enough) unless the estatuto says otherwise; and the redemption of Art. 4 § 5º is expressly outside that requirement, so the squeeze-out needs no class vote. Asserted.
5. Art. 15 § 2º lets non-voting or restricted-vote preferred shares be exactly half of the shares issued (500 of 1,000) and not one more (501). Asserted.
6. Art. 16-A forbids more than one class of ordinary shares in an open company unless plural voting meets Art. 110-A; a closed company is outside the ban, and a single class is no breach. Asserted.
7. Art. 17 § 1º, inciso I is a conjunction: at least 25% of the net profit (250 of 1,000 yes, 249 no), a priority dividend of at least 3% of the share's equity value (3 of 100 yes, 2.99 no), the right to take part in the dividend, and equal sharing with the ordinary shares afterwards; each limb was dropped in turn and the rule failed (fork F27). Asserted.
8. Art. 20 says shares "devem ser nominativas", and Arts. 22 parágrafo único, 26 and 38 still regulate the bearer share; layer 1 accepts nominativa, endossável and escritural under Art. 20, refuses the bearer share, and still answers the bearer-share rules (fork F29). Asserted.
9. Art. 22 parágrafo único gives the holder of bearer ordinary shares of an open company a right to convert them at will, and the holder in a closed company that right only for a class the estatuto made convertible; in a closed company at least one of its bearer ordinary classes must be convertible, and in an open company all of them (fork F33). Asserted.
10. Art. 29 forbids trading an open company's shares below 30% of the issue price paid, and exactly 30% is enough (29.99 no, 30 yes); a closed company is not caught (fork F34). Asserted.
11. Art. 30 § 1º alínea b lets the company acquire its own shares by donation without regard to the profit balance or the reduction of capital: an acquisition of 500 against a balance of 100, with capital reduced, passes when it is a donation, and an acquisition of 101 against a balance of 100 does not pass otherwise (fork F37). Asserted.
12. Art. 36 lets only a closed company limit the circulation of nominative shares, in detail, and without preventing trading or putting the shareholder at the arbitrary will of the administration or of the majority; failing any one of those answers `JUST FALSE`, while an open company or a bearer share answers `NOTHING`; and a limitation created by amendment binds only a holder who agreed expressly and asked for the annotation, both needed (forks F15, F36). Asserted.
13. Art. 37 lets an open company suspend the certificate services for at most 15 days at a time and 90 days in the year, counting the current period (15 and 90 are allowed, 16 and 91 are not), and only after notice to the exchanges and a published announcement, both needed. Asserted.
14. Art. 43 § 2º makes the deposited shares, their yield and the redemption or amortisation value unreachable by seizure once the deposit certificate is issued, while the certificate itself can still be attached for its holder's own debts. Asserted.
15. Art. 45 § 1º sets the floor of the reimbursement at the net equity value in the balance sheet the reimbursement rests on (10 passes, 9.99 does not), unless the estatuto fixed it on the company's economic value by appraisal, in which case 9 against 10 passes (fork F43). Asserted.
16. Art. 45 § 2º lets the dissenter ask for a special balance sheet only when the meeting is more than 60 days after the last approved balance sheet (61 days yes, exactly 60 no); the company then pays 80% at once and the balance is the special-balance value less that 80% (100 and 120 give 40; 100 and 100 give 20), and day 120 from the resolution is the last day in time for it (1 March to 29 June yes, 30 June no). Asserted.
17. Art. 45 § 6º deems the capital reduced when the shareholders reimbursed out of capital have not been replaced within 120 days of the publication of the minutes: with publication on 1 January, a replacement on 1 May (day 120) prevents it and one on 2 May (day 121) does not; the general meeting is then to be called within five days, counted from the end of the 120 days (a call on 6 May is in time, on 7 May is not) (fork F42). Asserted.
18. Art. 46 § 2º caps the share of the profits for partes beneficiárias, including the redemption reserve, at a tenth: 10 of 100 passes, 10.01 does not. Asserted.
19. Art. 47 parágrafo único forbids an open company to issue partes beneficiárias, which by inference makes every right Chapter IV gives to their holders a right against a closed company. Asserted for the ban.
20. Art. 48 § 1º limits partes beneficiárias granted free to ten years, with the last day in time the tenth anniversary (issued on 1 January 2026, ending 1 January 2036 passes and 2 January 2036 does not), and exempts those destined to welfare entities of the employees (a 2040 term passes for them). Asserted.
21. Art. 51 § 1º requires a notice of one month through the press, observing the calling rules for shareholders' meetings (notice on 1 January for a meeting on 1 February passes, on 2 January does not); and after two callings that failed for want of a quorum a new calling is allowed only on the day six months later (a failure on 15 January: 15 July passes, 14 July does not) (fork F45). Asserted.
22. Art. 8 § 3º voids the whole project of constituting the company if the meeting does not approve the valuation or the subscriber does not accept the approved one: either refusal alone suffices, and only the approval and the acceptance together keep it alive. Asserted.
23. Art. 13 forbids issuing shares with nominal value below that value (9.99 against 10 is forbidden, 10 and 10.01 are not), and shares without nominal value are not caught. Asserted.
24. Art. 14 parágrafo único lets part of the issue price go to capital reserve, but for preferred shares with priority in the reimbursement of capital only the part above the reimbursement value: with a price of 10 and a reimbursement value of 6, 4 may go to the reserve and 4.01 may not. Asserted.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Measured on the stable `l4` 1.6.98 (`1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`), `JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` unset, on 2026-10-22.
Each of the three is loud: a diagnostic and no result.

1. `JUST NOT b` is a parse error (`unexpected NOT`).
   Write `JUST (NOT b)`.
2. The infix form `` x `elem` xs `` is a type error.
   Write the prefix call `elem x xs`.
3. `#ASSERT g WITH a IS 1, b IS 2 EQUALS 3` is a type error ("An ASSERT directive is expected to be of type ..."), because `EQUALS` binds into the last field.
   Parenthesise: `#ASSERT (g WITH a IS 1, b IS 2) EQUALS 3`.

Not claimed.
An earlier note of mine said that a `JUST (NOT (g b` continued over two lines is a parse error; three probes written on 2026-10-22 (the call continued after `b`, inside an `IF ... THEN`, and with the name on its own line) all parsed, so I do not claim it.
In this encoding every `JUST (NOT ...)` is on one line.

## Before this is worth relying on

1. One author wrote the rules, the tests and the seat, and no one has read them independently; the expected values were written by the same hand that wrote the code, from the text.
   An independent test author and a Brazilian company lawyer should read at least Arts. 4 to 4-A, 8, 17, 29 to 31, 36 to 45 and 51.
2. The text is a converter's rendering of Planalto's compiled page of 2026-10-21, not the Diário Oficial.
   Nothing here is checked against the official text, and the `⟦STRUCK: ...⟧` wording was never read as law.
3. The 34 forks F12 to F45 each took one reading.
   Those worth a lawyer's ruling first: F12 (what "preço justo, ao menos igual" requires), F13 (the ceiling of Art. 1), F29 (bearer shares after Art. 20), F37 (the donation in Art. 30 § 1º b), F41 (the base of the "maioria absoluta" of Art. 45 § 4º) and F44 (half of what in Art. 51).
4. The seat's conventions are mine: a prohibition answers TRUE when the forbidden thing holds, and `NOTHING` means "the Law, in these articles, does not answer", which a reader may mistake for "no".
5. Every module was run on the stable `l4` 1.6.98 only.
   `check.sh` was run with `L4_TARGET=unstable`, which the brief prescribes and which skips the comparison with a second binary, so nothing here says how the modules read on any other build.
6. `check.sh` counts errors and assertion lines and nothing else: it does not compare dialects between builds, and nothing here uses `#TRACE`.
7. Where a rule depends on an article outside Articles 1 to 51, the fact is a plain input and the other article is not read (see "Cross-references out of my ranges"); a mismatch between a piece's input and the other piece's output would show only when the pieces are put together.
8. 63 of the 228 layer-1 rules have no seat question (see "Seat").
