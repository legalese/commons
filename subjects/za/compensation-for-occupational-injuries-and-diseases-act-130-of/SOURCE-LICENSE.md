## Consequence for this subject

**Metadata only until resolved.** The register's website terms allow only non-commercial use. Section 12(8)(a) of the Copyright Act 98 of 1978 is generally read as excluding official legislative texts from copyright, but that has not been confirmed from an official source. Resolve before depositing or quoting text.

## Language

English is not the only authoritative language in principle. Constitution s 82 (checked in the justice.gov.za PDF 1996-108.pdf) says 'The signed copy of an Act of Parliament is conclusive evidence of the provisions of that Act.' Acts are gazetted bilingually, English plus one other official language that rotates (Afrikaans, Tshivenda, Sepedi and others), and only the signed text is conclusive. In practice the signed text is English: the Gazette PDFs checked for Act 9 of 2018 and Act 18 of 2017 both say '(English text signed by the President)'. Older Acts, especially before 1994, were sometimes signed in Afrikaans, so the signed language should be checked Act by Act on the Gazette copy. For the Constitution itself, s 240 makes the English text prevail. The Interpretation Act 33 of 1957 (justice.gov.za 1957-033.pdf) was searched for a signed-text rule and none was found; the s 82 rule is the operative one.

### Copyright position relied on

Section 12(8)(a) of the Copyright Act 98 of 1978, read on 2026-10-19 in the Government Gazette copy of the Act on gov.za
(https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201504/act-98-1978.pdf, OCR text layer), provides that no copyright
subsists "in official texts of a legislative, administrative or legal nature". Acts, regulations and Gazette notices are such
texts. The deposits rely on that provision. The sites' non-commercial terms are recorded above and are **flagged for a
decision before commit** (see the subject README): they are website terms, not a copyright licence, and gov.za's own terms
disclaim creating any agreement, but they are not a permission for commercial use either.
