## Language

English is not the only authoritative language in principle. Constitution s 82 (checked in the justice.gov.za PDF 1996-108.pdf) says 'The signed copy of an Act of Parliament is conclusive evidence of the provisions of that Act.' Acts are gazetted bilingually, English plus one other official language that rotates (Afrikaans, Tshivenda, Sepedi and others), and only the signed text is conclusive. In practice the signed text is English: the Gazette PDFs checked for Act 9 of 2018 and Act 18 of 2017 both say '(English text signed by the President)'. Older Acts, especially before 1994, were sometimes signed in Afrikaans, so the signed language should be checked Act by Act on the Gazette copy. For the Constitution itself, s 240 makes the English text prevail. The Interpretation Act 33 of 1957 (justice.gov.za 1957-033.pdf) was searched for a signed-text rule and none was found; the s 82 rule is the operative one.
