## Seat

**The seat.**
An individual who is 65 or older, or about to be, asking what the monthly pension (Part I) and Part IV (General) mean for them: whether they qualify, how much, when it is paid, suspended or stopped; applying, having the application waived or deemed made earlier, incapacity and death; asking for reconsideration and appealing; what happens if they are paid too much; what can be done to them if they give false information; and when the Minister may inspect their documents or enter their home.
The Guaranteed Income Supplement and the Allowances (Parts II and III) are asked about in oa2's seat layer and the Regulations in oa3's; this seat touches them only where a Part I or Part IV rule reaches them (for example s 30, the survivor's allowance, and s 26.1 for any benefit).

**Why this seat.**
The text confers a status and a payment on a person who is, for the most part, not a lawyer and not an official: the person at or past 65 who wants to know whether and how much, and what they must do or may demand.
An official's seat (the Minister, a judge, a province) would be a second product and is not written.
The Minister's duties appear only as what the person is owed (for example s 5(5), s 27.1(2), s 32), and the Minister's powers only as what the person is liable to (for example ss 5(4), 28.1, 44.1).

**How the seat is built.**
`oa1-seat.l4` has 131 questions in three `§` parts, each divided by `§§` groups that a form can show or hide: the questions about qualifying (23: "Do I qualify for a pension?" 10, "Being in prison" 4, "Applying, having my application waived, and withdrawing" 9); how much and when (25: "How much pension?" 13, "When is my pension paid, suspended, stopped or cancelled?" 12); and Part IV (83: "Waiver, incapacity, death and error" 17, "Reconsideration and appeal" 7, "Information, form and attendance" 7, "Benefits cannot be assigned or seized, and what happens if I am paid too much" 19, "What can be done to me if I give false information or do not comply" 11, "If the Minister wants to inspect documents or enter my home" 15, "Agreements with other countries and the transitional provisions" 7).
Every question has `@export` and `@desc`, takes plain inputs (yes or no, a number, a date) worded as the person would be asked, and calls the layer-1 rule; the fact records the layer-1 rules take (`s 3: Facts of age and residence`, `Sentence of imprisonment` and the like) are built inside the question, in its `WHERE`, from those plain inputs.
No rule is restated in the seat.
`oa1-cases-seat.l4` has 362 assertions, at least one per question, each worked by hand from the text and not copied from the output.
A question whose answer the Act does not give returns `NOTHING` (a `MAYBE`), for example the most a person can be fined under s 44(1), and the cases assert it.

**Where the Minister's judgement or a regulation decides.**
The seat asks for it as a fact ("Is the Minister satisfied that ...?", "Do the regulations let ...?", "Did the Minister allow a longer period?"); nothing is guessed.
The figures the Act leaves to the regulations (the earlier effective days of s 8, the time for cancelling a pension in s 9.3, the manner of the quarterly adjustment) are asked as inputs, and a different figure in the Regulations would change those answers.
The full monthly pension for a month cannot be derived from the Act, so the questions on the amount ask for the published figure for the month.

**Provisions with no question.**
Encoded in layer 1, and named by no question in `oa1-seat.l4` (the coverage table marks with `*` the rules a question calls by name; a rule that a called rule reaches is not marked, so a row without `*` is not necessarily unreachable from the seat): s 27 (how the Consumer Price Index is adjusted when its basis changes), the lead-in and three definitions of s 33(1), ss 39 and 40(1) (the Minister's agreements with a province and with another country), s 41(2) (notice of an agreement by proclamation), s 42 (laying and coming into force of the order, 42(1), (2), (7), (8) and (9)), s 43, ss 45, 46 and 47 (the fund, who administers the Act, the annual report), and the lead-in and three definitions of s 44.2(1).

## What the stable binary does not accept

The target is `main`, build `1.6.98` (the keel's `NOTES-oas-g0.md` gives `7689b0d, 2026-10-19`; sha256 `82ea44addf093ea4dd84c4e4416184920857ca9c762c5b31feed20541c20263e`).
Every module was run on that binary only: nothing in this deposit has been run on `unstable`, because the brief gave this piece only the stable binary, and the keel's `YMD` shim in `oas-core.l4` is expected to collide with `unstable`'s own prelude `YMD` (the keel's note; not measured here).

**Avoided by following the writing skill's `dialects.md` and the keel's list, not re-measured by this piece.**
`REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` and `BECAUSE`: a rule with no answer for a case returns a `MAYBE` with `NOTHING`, and a case asserts it. Twenty layer-1 rules return a `MAYBE` (`GIVETH A MAYBE`): ss 7(1), 7(2) to (4), 7.1(3), 8(2.1), 9.1(2), 9.2, 26.1, 28(3), 28.1(1), 28.1(2), 29(3), 30(2), 31(1), 31(3), 36(2), 36(3), 37(2.3), 44(1), 44.2(13) and 2021, c. 23, s. 275.
A section `GIVEN`: a `GIVEN` is written on every rule, which is why the same inputs recur across rules in a module.
`TYPICALLY`, sets, the ledger, `@lang` and the other annotations the keel lists: not used.
Brackets: every `NOT` expression and every `YMD` in a comparison is in brackets.

**Met while writing, on 1.6.98.**

- A bare identifier with an apostrophe does not lex: a rule named `max'` fails with `unexpected '''`. An apostrophe inside a backticked name is fine (`` `the person's application` ``). Measured again for these notes.
- A mixfix pattern must end on an argument, and its arguments are bracketed at the call (the keel's finding, relied on here).
- There is no record update. A record is built in full with `Type WITH field IS value ...`, or positionally with `OF`, and the field of a record is read with `record's \`field\``. The seat builds the fact records inside each question's `WHERE` for that reason.
- `#ASSERT` sits on one line; none is written across lines.
- `DATE PLUS NUMBER` and `DATE MINUS NUMBER` give a DATE (probe: `(YMD 2026 1 30) MINUS 3` equals `YMD 2026 1 27`, and the same assertion against 28 fails), and `DATE MINUS DATE` gives a NUMBER of days (probe: `(YMD 2026 1 30) MINUS (YMD 2026 1 27)` equals 3, and against 4 it fails). The keel's note says a DATE minus a NUMBER is a number of days; on this build a DATE minus a NUMBER is a DATE.
- `add months` takes a negative count, and clamps to the end of a shorter month (the keel's finding, relied on at ss 28.1 and 44(3): February 29, 2024 plus five years is February 28, 2029, asserted).
- A comment on the same line after a `GIVEN` input, and a comment on its own line inside a `GIVEN` list, both compile clean with no `follows code on its line` diagnostic; `srcrefs.py` reports no problem for either.
- The `-- src:` lines that `srcrefs.py quote` writes are checked by `srcrefs.py check --refs`, which reads the Act's file; it does not check that an encoding matches the law.
- `l4 run` evaluates the assertions and `l4 check` does not: a file whose only assertion is false (`1` against `2`) prints `Check succeeded.` under `l4 check` and `assertion failed` under `l4 run`. The counts in this file are from `l4 run`, through `check.sh`.

**Cautions this piece took after early failures that it could not reproduce on a small probe.**
It brackets arithmetic that continues on a new line, and a mixfix call that is followed by `PLUS` or `MINUS`.
A probe of each in its simplest form (`10` then `PLUS 5` on the next indented line; `` `a _ plus the second _` 1 2 PLUS 10 ``) evaluated the same bracketed or not on 1.6.98, so the original binding problem, which arose inside larger expressions, is not characterised here and the brackets are kept as a precaution.

## Before this is worth relying on

1. **Whether the encoding may rely on the Open Government Licence is undecided, and the decision is the lead's.** The Reproduction Order covers reproduction and does not expressly grant a right to adapt; the repository's Open Government Licence does, and `../../SOURCE-LICENSE.md` leaves it to a human to record that decision before an encoding quotes the text. Every rule here carries a `-- src:` quote. This piece wrote no `SOURCE-LICENSE.md`. If the lead publishes the encoding, the attribution line and the statement that the quoted text is not an official version go with it.
2. **The text is an unofficial plain-text rendering** (line 1 of the file says so) of a consolidation "current to 2026-09-30; last amended 2025-10-01". It omits the historical notes, the footnotes and the French version. `srcrefs.py check` proves that each quoted line is a line of the rendering; nothing here has been compared with the official text, and a conversion error in the rendering (a dropped line, a mis-set paragraph) would be encoded faithfully.
3. **Every expected value in the tests and the seat cases was worked by hand by the model that wrote the encoding**, from the text, and not copied from the output; a test that failed because the expected value had been worked wrongly was corrected by re-reading the text. That is one reader, not two. A lawyer has not read any of it, and no second encoder has encoded the same range for comparison.
4. **The 24 forks are readings, not rulings.** The ones that can move a person's answer are OA1-F1, F4, F6, F7, F8, F10, F13 and F20; they are worth a lawyer's attention first. The register says what the other readings would be, so a different ruling is a change to one rule, not a rewrite.
5. **The pension for a month is an input.** The Act gives one figure ($273.80, January to March 1985) and a rule for deriving each later quarter from the one before and two published indices. No index figure is in the file, so no amount of pension is computed from the Act alone; the seat asks for the published figure.
6. **The Regulations are not read here.** What they prescribe (residence, legal residence, the prescribed information, the earlier effective day, the time for cancelling, the manner of set-off, the conditions of a deduction, the longer period for reconsideration, the Consumer Price Index average) arrives as plain inputs named for the fact, each listed under "Cross-references out of my ranges". A composition has to wire them to oa3's answers; no module of this piece imports another piece's, and the wiring has not been tried.
7. **The Act as consolidated on one day.** The rules apply the text as it stands in the file. Earlier versions of a section (for example s 29 before 2007, kept alive by 2007, c. 11, s. 38) are inputs of the related provisions, not versions of the rule, so a question about a past date is not answered by the current text.
8. **The Minister's discretion is an input everywhere the text says "may", "satisfied", "considers" or "opinion".** An answer of yes means the Act allows it if the Minister is so satisfied. The seat words these inputs as questions to the person ("Is the Minister satisfied that ...?"), which a person often cannot answer; a deployment would take them from the Minister's decision, not from the person.
9. **One statement in this file is about the text and not about any rule, and no case asserts it:** finding 37 (the references in s 44.1(1)(a.1) to subsections that exist only in amendments not in force). It rests on the line numbers given there.
10. **Nothing has been run on `unstable`**, and the seat's questions have not been put to a person; their wording is the model's, and the `@desc` of each input is a first draft for a reader who has never seen the Act.
