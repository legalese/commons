## Required notice and attribution

Not an official version. Each deposited text file opens with a notice that it is an unofficial reproduction of the Department of Justice Canada consolidation, with its source and "current to" date. Any encoding or projection that quotes it must keep that notice and must not present the text as official.

Attribution under the Open Government Licence - Canada, which asks for this statement where the user does not have a specific one from the Information Provider (the repository names none): "Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence – Canada."

The Justice Laws Important Note adds: "The Department of Justice Canada assumes no responsibility for the accuracy or reliability of any reproduction derived from the legislative material on this site." Only the official consolidation (and, for interpretation, the enacted Acts and registered regulations in the Canada Gazette) has legal status.

## Consequence for this subject

The Reproduction Order covers reproduction and does not expressly grant a right to adapt. The XML repository's Open Government Licence does grant adaptation, which is why the text was taken from the repository. Whether an L4 encoding that quotes this text may rely on the Open Government Licence is a human decision to record here before an encoding quotes it.

## Language

English is not the only authoritative language. Federal Acts are enacted in both English and French, and the two versions have equal authority (Constitution Act, 1982, s. 18; Official Languages Act, s. 13). Only the English version is deposited; Justice Laws publishes both, at /eng/ and /fra/.
