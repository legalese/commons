# Northwest Territories Social Assistance Act, with the Income Assistance Regulations and the Income Assistance for Seniors and Persons with Disabilities Regulations — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited texts, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
There is no REFUSE in it. A question that can reach a case outside the encoding answers `not covered` with the reason, and tests assert it.
Checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98, with `JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` unset.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
**Not for public use as advice.**

The Act is a framework: who is a "person in need" and how much assistance is given are left to regulations made under ss 16 and 17. Following the default "the Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers", the encoding takes the Act with its two regulations, which are two separate streams of income assistance:

- the **Income Assistance Regulations**, R-038-2024 ("IA"), the general stream; and
- the **Income Assistance for Seniors and Persons with Disabilities Regulations**, R-037-2024 ("SPD"), for a person 60 or over or with a disability the Director has determined, and their spouse.

The rules are written seat-neutrally in `nt-sa.l4` (computations and conditions) and `nt-sa-duties.l4` (the dated duties, kept apart so that only it loads the date library). The applicant's questions are in `nt-sa-seat.l4`, which IMPORTs both and calls their rules; nothing is restated. The four benefit tables and the community-to-zone map were generated by script from the deposited text and checked against it, so that no figure or community name was retyped. Each question has `@export` and every input a `@desc`.

There was no earlier encoding of these instruments to keep.

## Seat

**The applicant for, or recipient of, income assistance**, and through the SPD Regulations the senior, the person with a disability and their spouse: "Which program do I apply under? Am I eligible? Am I a person in need? What counts as my income? How much can I get each month? What must I report, and by when? Can I appeal?"

Provisions addressed to officials are encoded as what they mean to that person:

- the Officer's duty to refuse with written reasons within three business days (IA s 14, SPD s 14) is a regulative rule on the Officer, showing what the applicant must be given;
- the Officer's power to terminate for an unreported change appears in the `LEST` of the recipient's reporting duty;
- the Director's determinations are inputs, answered conditionally: that a person has a disability (SPD s 2), that a surplus is inadequate for an unexpected situation, that an asset is exempt, which items are paid.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act, with the instruments that set its numbers:** the two regulations. Nothing else is brought in. The Social Assistance Appeals Regulations are not deposited, so the time limits for an appeal are not encoded.
- **The actors the text names:** the applicant or recipient, the Social Welfare Officer, the Director.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally**, as listed above.
- **The regulations' own definitions are worked out from facts:** adult (19), child and mature child (under 19), senior (60), budget deficit, monthly income (IA s 22(1)), allowable income (IA s 22(8), (9); SPD s 22(4), (5)), net annual income (SPD s 22(1)), the bank money exemption (IA s 22(7)(h)), person in need (IA s 2; SPD s 3), the zones (IA Schedule item 1). **Borrowed terms are asked as facts:** immigration status (Immigration and Refugee Protection Act), line 23600 of the notice of assessment.
- **One case:** one family's application, for one month (IA) or one eligibility period (SPD).
- **The texts' own terms**, quoted in the comment above each rule.
- **Machinery out:** the Appeal Board and committees (Act ss 6, 7, 9), agreements with Canada (ss 11, 12), confidentiality (s 12.1), regulation-making (ss 16, 17), delegation (IA s 7, SPD s 8).

## Regulative rules

Five, in `nt-sa-duties.l4`, each `LEST BREACH ... BECAUSE`:

1. `my duty to report a change` (IA s 19(1), SPD s 19(1)): the recipient MUST inform the Officer of a change by the first of the following month (IA) or within 60 days (SPD). The `LEST` cites termination or a warning (s 20(1)(c), (2)) and ineligibility (IA s 5, SPD s 6).
2. `my duty to prove a social insurance number application` (IA s 10(3), SPD s 11(5)): the applicant MUST, within two months after the application, provide proof of applying for a social insurance number.
3. `my duty to provide the SPD documents` (SPD s 11(3), (4)(b)): the applicant MUST provide the s 11(2) information within 60 days, or the longer time the Officer allows, or the application must be refused.
4. `what I must be given on a refusal` (IA s 14, SPD s 14): the Officer MUST inform the applicant and give written reasons within three business days. The calendar days to the third business day are an input.
5. `my duty not to make a false statement` (Act ss 13, 15; IA s 4; SPD s 5): the applicant SHANT make a false or misleading statement to obtain assistance, over a period given as an input.

The right of appeal (Act s 8) is a permission. Because a permission cannot be breached, it is encoded as a decision (`may appeal under Act s 8(1)`) rather than as a `MAY` rule.

## What the texts turn out to say

1. **Two streams that exclude each other.** Each regulation bars a person who has a pending application under the other, or who is or was a recipient under it (IA s 3.1, SPD s 4.1). Each has its own way back: IA s 3.1(3) readmits a former SPD recipient whose assistance ended because they were not a person with a disability, and SPD s 4.1(3) readmits a former IA recipient who becomes a senior, a person with a disability or the spouse of one. Asserted both ways.
2. **The status list grew, but the statement did not.** R-059-2025 and R-058-2025 added a fifth group: a foreign national with a pending s 25(1) humanitarian request (IA s 3(e), SPD s 4(b)(v)). The statement every applicant must sign still lists only "paragraphs 3(a) to (d)" (IA s 11(a)) or "subparagraphs 4(b)(i) to (iv)" (SPD s 12(a)(ii)). An applicant in the new group therefore cannot truthfully make the required statement. The encoding treats the group as eligible, following ss 3 and 4. Not asserted, because the statement is not encoded.
3. **Earned income of mature dependants.** IA s 22(8)(a) allows $500 of the earned income "earned by each adult in the family". But s 22(2) makes "the income ... of an applicant" the income of the applicant and the spouse only, so a mature dependant's earnings never enter A. Their $500 allowance therefore has nothing to apply to. The encoding allows $500 plus 25% each to the applicant and the spouse. Asserted.
4. **Twice a month's needs.** IA s 22(7)(h) exempts bank money "up to a maximum of twice the amount that would be required for the applicant to adequately provide for their family for one month". The encoding reads "the amount required for one month" as the family's monthly budget under the Schedule. The text does not tie the two together. Asserted ($1,000 over twice a $1,500 budget counts).
5. **A net annual income below nil.** Under SPD s 22(5), allowable income cannot reduce net annual income below $0. But where GNWT assistance and CPP disability benefits (B and C) exceed line 23600, the formula is already negative before D, and the text leaves it there. The encoding returns the negative figure and treats it as nil against the budget. Asserted.
6. **"Income received" is undefined.** SPD s 22(4) allows "up to $6,000 of income received by" the senior or person with a disability, and by the spouse. The regulation does not say which measure of income. The encoding uses each person's line 23600 amount, the measure s 22(1) uses. Asserted.
7. **An annual test against a monthly benefit.** The SPD test is annual (net annual income over an eligibility period from 1 July to 30 June), but the benefits are monthly table amounts "provided monthly during the eligibility period" (s 16(1)(b)). The regulations do not say how a budget deficit is computed across the two. The encoding sets one-twelfth of net annual income against each month's budget. Asserted.
8. **SPD s 4.1(1)(b) has no work to do.** A former IA recipient is barred from the SPD stream "subject to subsection (3)", and (3) readmits anyone who becomes a senior, a person with a disability or the spouse of one. Section 4(a) requires that of every SPD applicant, so the bar never operates against an eligible applicant. The encoding therefore does not ask about past IA receipt. Not separately asserted.
9. **Spelling in the SPD tables.** The SPD basic benefit table spells "Délîne", where its room and board table and the IA Schedule spell "Délînê". The room and board table says "Kátł’odeeche (Hay River Reserve)", where the basic table says "Kátł’odeeche First Nation (Hay River Reserve)". They are read as the same communities. Asserted for both.
10. **The SPD tables are by community, not by zone, and do not follow the IA zones.** In the one-adult column of the SPD basic benefit table, most communities of IA Zones B, C and E share one figure, but a few differ: Paulatuk ($1,291 against $1,246), Wrigley, Nahanni Butte, Sambaa K’e and Fort Resolution. In Zone D every community has its own one-adult figure, and Łutselk’e also has its own two-adult figure ($1,500 against $1,443). The room and board table varies in the same way, for example Sachs Harbour ($557 against $512). This is recorded as read, not judged. Paulatuk, Łutselk’e and Sachs Harbour are asserted.
11. **No time limit for an appeal in the Act.** Section 8 gives the right of appeal but leaves procedure to regulations (s 16(m)), and the Social Assistance Appeals Regulations are not deposited.
