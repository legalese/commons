## What this is, and what it is not

- **The text is a converter rendering, not the official text.**
  It is a plain-text rendering, made by a converter script, of the Justice Laws Website consolidation (current to 2026-09-30, `inputs/SOURCES.md`).
  Nothing in this encoding is claimed verified against the official text, and the French version (equally authoritative) was not among the sources.
- **Where PLAN.md and the brief differ, the brief governs.**
  PLAN.md section 1 says a part receives only the core.
  The brief says a part imports both the core and the definitions, so every layer-1 module of ccc7 imports `ccc-core` and, where it needs a section 2 term (seven of the ten modules), `ccc-definitions`; the three keel files sit beside them in this folder.
- **Layer 1** is the ten modules `ccc7-s*.l4` (isomorphic, tested L4 for the law); **layer 2** is the one seat module `ccc7-seat.l4` (61 questions) with its case file `ccc7-cases-seat.l4`; the tests are `ccc7-tests-*.l4`.
- **The seat** is a person who is, or may be, accused of an offence under the Code.
  Part VI mostly speaks to officials (judges, the Attorney General, the Minister, peace officers), so the seat asks, for each of those provisions, only what it means to the person: what may be done to them, what they must be given, and by when.
  There is no seat for an official.
- **Files written** (all under `deposit/encodings/legalese-sonnet-5.5/`): `ccc7-s183-base.l4`, `ccc7-s183-definitions.l4`, `ccc7-s183-offence.l4`, `ccc7-s184-interception.l4`, `ccc7-s184-applications.l4`, `ccc7-s185-applications.l4`, `ccc7-s186-authorizations.l4`, `ccc7-s187-packet.l4`, `ccc7-s191-offences.l4`, `ccc7-s195-notification.l4`, nine `ccc7-tests-*.l4`, `ccc7-seat.l4`, `ccc7-cases-seat.l4`, `NOTES.md`, `PROGRESS.md`, `NEEDS-CORE.md`.
  The module that holds ss 195 to 196.1 is named `ccc7-s195-notification.l4` because s 196 and s 196.1 are the notification duties.

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (ccc7)

These are the places where the answer the encoding gives is one a lawyer could reasonably expect to be different.
Each is stated with the unit it concerns so that it can be overturned in one place.

- **(a) An unmet saving limb or offence element answers JUST FALSE for that provision's own question.**
  A JUST FALSE on `s 184(1) ... guilty` means the subsection does not make the conduct an offence; it is not a finding of innocence and says nothing about another offence.
  A saving limb answers JUST TRUE when its facts hold and JUST FALSE when its own conditions fail (for example 184(2)(c) for a person who does not provide a communication service); NOTHING only when a borrowed term it turns on is itself not covered.
- **(b) A "may ... where" grant whose "where" fails answers JUST FALSE.**
  They are listed, one row per function, in the fork register (F11 and the name-test sweep subsection).
  A lawyer may read these as NOTHING (the text grants; it does not forbid).
- **(c) A "may ... if" grant whose gate fails answers NOTHING (ruling of 2026-11-12, F8).**
  A lawyer may say "may not".
- **(d) A cap on a permission answers NOTHING beyond the cap; a cap on what an instrument or an extension "shall" be answers JUST FALSE.**
  Permissions with a cap (NOTHING beyond it, kind "limited by its own wording"): 188(2) ("up to thirty-six hours"), 186.1 ("each not exceeding one year"), 185(2) ("such longer period not exceeding three years"), 194(1) ("an amount not exceeding five thousand dollars").
  Requirements with a cap (JUST FALSE beyond it): 184.2(4)(e) and 186(4)(e) ("be valid for the period, not exceeding sixty days"), 186(7) ("no renewal shall be for a period exceeding sixty days"), 185(3) ("he shall fix a period, not exceeding three years"), and the extensions of 196(3), 196(5), 196.1(3) and 196.1(5) ("each extension not to exceed three years", "but no extension may exceed three years").
  For 194(1), a lawyer will read "an amount not exceeding five thousand dollars" as a flat cap on the order; the encoding says the text grants up to it and is silent beyond.
- **(e) s 189(6): with the consent of the person who enjoys the privilege, NOTHING.**
  The text keeps the information privileged and inadmissible "without the consent" of the holder and says nothing about what follows with consent; a lawyer will say it becomes admissible.
- **(f) s 183.1 for a one-to-one call answers JUST FALSE.**
  The section speaks only of a communication made by, or meant for, more than one person; for a one-to-one call the answer to "is one person's consent enough?" is the section's silence made JUST FALSE by the "Where" (F6), though in fact the one party's consent is simply the consent.
- **(g) A territory gives NOTHING for "solicitor" (F5), and a terrorism ground in the closing words of "offence" is narrower than s 2's definition (F2).**
- **(h) Notification clocks: s 196(1) replaces the 90 days with "such other period"; s 196.1(1) adds an extension to them (F15).**
  A lawyer may read the two alike.
- **(i) The penalty questions answer the indictable limb only.**
  Every offence section of the Part says "an offence punishable on summary conviction" and states no penalty; the summary penalty is s 787(1), in another part, and is an input of the layer-1 functions (`s 787(1) the general penalty for an offence punishable on summary conviction`); the seat answers the maximum on indictment and the class of the offence.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ccc7)

A yes/no function of this encoding answers one of three things.

- **JUST TRUE:** yes, on the text's own words.
- **JUST FALSE:** no, and the text says so in its own words.
  The source of the no is quoted in a `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<quote>"` comment beside the branch.
  Four kinds of text give a JUST FALSE here: a definition that says "means"; a condition introduced with "if", "where", "unless", "until", "after" and the like that is not met; a list the text closes; a flat negative ("shall not", "no ... may", "does not apply").
- **NOTHING:** not covered.
  Each branch carries a `-- NOTHING-kind (<kind>)` comment with one of six kinds.
  *Silent:* the text says nothing about the case (3 branches: each of the three territories under `s 183 solicitor`).
  *List not closed:* a list that does not say it is the only one (9 branches; the items a report must set out in s 195, the sections s 184.2(5) lists).
  *Includes:* a definition worded "includes" and a case it does not name (8 branches: an act that is none of those "intercept includes").
  *Undefined term:* none in this part (borrowed terms are inputs, and are counted under "points outside the text").
  *Points outside the text:* the answer turns on a term another section or Act defines, asked as a plain fact (27 branches).
  *Limited by its own wording:* a grant or a bar worded with a limit that the case goes beyond ("up to thirty-six hours", "not exceeding five thousand dollars", "by any one of those persons"; 64 branches).
- **A caller never turns NOTHING into FALSE.**
  The three-valued helpers (`s 183 three-valued all of`, `any of`, `not`) are Kleene's strong logic: one JUST FALSE decides a conjunction, otherwise one NOTHING makes it NOTHING; a NOTHING under a `not` stays NOTHING.
- **A seat answer reads the same way.**
  JUST TRUE is "yes", JUST FALSE is "no, in the text's words", NOTHING is "the text does not answer this".
  A NOTHING is never a no, and a JUST FALSE on an offence question is never a finding of innocence.
- **The counts above** are from `grep -o 'NOTHING-kind (<kind>)'` over the ten layer-1 modules at the time of writing and add up to the 111 NOTHING branches `work/verify.py` counts (together with its 134 JUST FALSE branches, 219 quotations, 0 problems; the plumbing module `ccc7-s183-base.l4` is outside that lint by design).

## Seat layer (ccc7)

`ccc7-seat.l4` holds **61** questions, each named "Under section N, ...", each with `@export` and `@desc`, grouped under `§` headings in the text's order.
Every input is plain: a yes/no, a number, a date, a choice from a list, or the Act, section and subsection of an offence (the person can state "section 380 of the Criminal Code").
A borrowed term (criminal organization, terrorism offence, computer system as defined in s 342.1(2), a radiocommunication, reasonable grounds) is asked as a plain fact, and the seat turns it into the MAYBE BOOLEAN the layer-1 function takes.
The seat restates no rule: each body is a call of a layer-1 function.
The groups whose situation the person may not be in (an authorization about them, a sealed packet, an emergency interception, being convicted) carry the situation in the heading itself ("situation: ..."), so the form can ask "Has this happened to you?" first.

**Sections with no seat question:** s 184.3, s 184.6, s 188.1, s 195.
Section 195 (annual reports) and s 188.1 (an authorization may be executed anywhere in Canada) and s 184.6 (an application may be made ... "for greater certainty") address only officials, and what they mean to the person is covered by the questions on the sections they serve; s 184.3 is the procedure for applying by telecommunication.
Inside the sections that have questions, the provisions that address only an official (for example the content of the application in 184.2(2), 186(5) to (6), 187(1) to (3), 188(1), (4), (6)) have none.

**Direction audit, first pass (title against the layer-1 function's name).**
Every seat question was compared with the layer-1 function it calls, for the direction of the answer (JUST TRUE means "yes" to the question as titled) and for the subject ("have I" against "must I", "may the judge" against "is the judge barred").

## What the stable binary does not accept (found while writing this part)

Each item was met in this part with `l4` 1.6.110; the fix is the form the part uses.

- **`NOT a AND b` parses as `NOT (a AND b)`.** Bracket every `NOT`. It bit the helper `s 183 three-valued any of`; the helper's tests caught it.
- **`x NOT EQUALS y` is not an operator, and the error is reported at an earlier, unrelated token** (the first `(` of the enclosing call). Write `NOT (x EQUALS y)`.
- **A genitive argument must be bracketed.** `` `add months` (`add years` day period's years) period's months `` fails with "unexpected (" at the inner call; write `` `add months` (`add years` day (period's years)) (period's months) ``. `` `add months` `` and `` `add years` `` take the date first, then the number, as ordinary (prefix) calls; the infix form `` d `add years` n `` did not parse.
- **`length` is not in the prelude** of the stable build. Use `null` for emptiness.
- **A `LIST` of records built with `OF` needs each element in parentheses.**
- **A DEONTIC cannot be `#ASSERT`ed.** The regulative rules are exercised with `#TRACE` and the results read by hand (the table below).
- **One `#ASSERT` per line; a continuation line is rejected.**
- **Parenthesise arguments that are applications** (`JUST TRUE`, `Date 1 1 2025`, a record built with `OF`), or the call reads them as separate arguments.
- **A function with no `GIVEN` can be `@export`ed** (the penalty and class questions of the seat take no inputs and answer a constant).
- **A `CONSIDER` on a constructor with a field** (`WHEN \`imprisonment for a term of\` term THEN term's years`) is accepted.
- **No `REFUSE`, `BECAUSE` (except `LEST BREACH ... BECAUSE`), `EVERY`, sets, section `GIVEN`, `TYPICALLY` or `YMD`:** none is used.
- **Also true of this part's own tooling, not of L4:** `sed -i` on this machine is the BSD one and rejects `sed -i 's/..'`; edits were made with Python.
