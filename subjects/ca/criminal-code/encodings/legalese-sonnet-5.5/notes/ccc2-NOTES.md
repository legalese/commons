### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ccc2)

Every decision function answers a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (the penalty functions answer a `LIST OF Punishment`, a `MAYBE` of one, or a `Class of offence`).
- `JUST TRUE`: on the facts given, the case is within the provision, on the text's own words (for a bar or a saving, the bar or saving applies).
- `JUST FALSE`, a "no": the text says no in its own words. In this range that means: a definition that says "means" or "is" and the case is outside it (s 52(2), 56.1(3), 62(2), 63(1), 64, 78(2), 78.1(5), 82.2, 83(2)); an "unless" that is met (ss 47(3), 48(2), 50(1)(a), 55); a flat negative ("No person does a prohibited act ... by reason only that", "does not prohibit", "is not material", "is immaterial", "do not apply": ss 52(3)-(5), 52.1(3)-(5), 56.1(2), 57(4)(a), 60, 63(3), 72(1.1), 82.7); a period the text fixes in its own words that has not run or is not exceeded (ss 48(1), 68(b), 68(c)); a "when" or "where" that fails (ss 46(4), 57(6), 72(1), 72(2): forks F8, F11). Every such branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`.
- `NOTHING`, "not covered": an offence-creating clause ("Every one who ... is guilty of ...") whose element is unmet (the clause names conduct and does not say no to other conduct: fork F1); a case outside a limit the text sets in its own words ("in Canada", "a Canadian ship", "without lawful excuse", "twelve or more persons"); a list that is not closed ("Without limiting the generality", "includes", "including the following"); a term the Code leaves to another instrument or Act (the Canadian Passport Order, the Citizenship Act, the law of nations, regulations under s 52.1(6), s 342.1(2), s 467.1(1)); a section that states no penalty for the case (s 80 with no explosion; the indictable-only offences have no summary limb). Every such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, with <why> one of: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`: "not covered" is not "not guilty". In the seat layer, an answer the person gives about a borrowed term (the Passport Order, the Citizenship Act, s 467.1(1), s 342.1(2), the law of nations, regulations, "offensive weapon") is a yes/no: a "yes" is `JUST TRUE` and a "no" or "I do not know" is `NOTHING`, never `JUST FALSE`, because the person's "no" is not the text's own no.
- `REFUSE` is not used (it does not exist on the stable binary): "not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted with `EQUALS NOTHING`.
- 75 branches carry a FALSE-kind tag and 142 carry a NOTHING-kind tag in layer 1 (counted from the tags by `work/notes_gen.py`). The NOTHING-kind tags by reason: limited by its own wording 90, list not closed 22, silent 12, points outside the text 11, undefined term 5, includes 2. An audit script found no `NOTHING` or `JUST FALSE` branch in the nine layer-1 modules without its tag, and no tag whose reason is outside the six.
- The words quoted inside the tags were checked against the text by the same script: 235 fragments, of which 226 are found in lines 850-1248, 9 are found elsewhere in the Code (they quote definitions in section 2, outside this range) and 0 are not found.

## Fork register (ccc2)

Each entry: the provision, the function, the reading taken, the other reading, the text that licenses each. F1, F2 and F20 are conventions that the brief's rules decide: they are entered because the brief asks for every ambiguity, but their rows are `encoded` in the coverage table; every row marked `fork` points at one of F3 to F19. Where the brief's rules decide the case they are quoted word for word at the end of this section.

- **F1. Offence-creating clauses ("Every one who ... is guilty of ...", "Every one commits X who ..."): a convention applied, not a text ambiguity, so these rows are `encoded`.** Every offence function (48 provision keys of kind claim / duty in the jural-relations table) answers `NOTHING` when an element is unmet, not `JUST FALSE`. Reading taken: the clause names conduct and does not say no to other conduct; it is a prohibition limited by its own wording ("who", "without lawful excuse", "in Canada"), which the 2026-10-29 rule answers `NOTHING`. Other reading: "commits X who ..." defines X, so an element unmet is the text's no (`JUST FALSE`, worded "this section's offence is not made out on these facts"). Licence: "Every one commits high treason who, in Canada," (s 46(1)); "Every one who does an act of violence in order to intimidate Parliament ... is guilty of" (s 51). One decision for all offence functions; a reviewer who prefers the other reading changes the final `ELSE NOTHING` of each.
- **F2. A `JUST FALSE` carried into an offence (a convention applied; the rows are `encoded`).** Where an element of an offence is a term the text itself closes ("prohibited act means", "identity document means", "member of a force means", "ship means", "device means any of the following", "prize fight means", "civil aircraft means all aircraft other than", "A riot is an unlawful assembly", the s 2 definitions) or is denied by a saving ("No person does a prohibited act ... by reason only that"), the offence function answers `JUST FALSE`, quoting the closing words. ## What the text turns out to say (findings)

1. **s 55 names "section 47 or sections 50 to 53", not section 46.** The offences of high treason and treason are created by s 46 and punished by s 47; a charge under s 46 alone is not within the words of s 55 (asserted: `s 55 ... (Provision of the Code OF 46, "(1)")` answers NOTHING). Asserted.
2. **s 47(4) makes life a minimum only for high treason** (subsection (1)), not for the life sentences of s 47(2)(a) and (b). Asserted (`s 47(1) the punishment for high treason` has a minimum of life; `s 47(2)(a) ...` has none).
3. **s 48(1)'s three years apply only to treason under s 46(2)(a)** (force or violence to overthrow the government); no limitation period is stated for the other treasons or for high treason. **s 48(2)'s six and ten days apply only to a proceeding under s 47 for an overt act expressed by open and considered speech.** Asserted.
4. **s 78.1(4) names paragraphs (2)(a), (b) and (c) only**: a threat to commit the s 78.1(2)(d) offence is not within it. Asserted.
5. **s 63(2) adds no case that s 63(1) does not already reach.** s 63(2)'s test, "a manner that would have made the assembly unlawful if they had assembled in that manner for that purpose", is s 63(1)'s test applied to how the persons "conduct themselves when they are assembled", which s 63(1) already includes. Read from the code, not asserted: no test could tell the two arms apart.
6. **s 80 makes the failure an indictable offence whether or not an explosion results; only the penalty depends on the explosion.** Asserted.
7. **s 82(1) is hybrid (five years on indictment); s 82(2) is indictable only (fourteen years); s 82.1's consecutive-sentence rule reaches only a sentence for s 82(2).** Asserted.
8. **Four sections state only "an offence punishable on summary conviction" and no penalty: ss 54, 56, 66(1), 83(1)** (and the summary limb of the hybrid sections): the penalty is s 787(1)'s. Asserted (the punishment function returns the seam input).
9. **s 46(1) and (2) reach only acts "in Canada"; the reach beyond is by s 46(3) to a Canadian citizen or a person who owes allegiance**, and by "while in or out of Canada" in ss 57, 74(2) and 75 and "while in or outside Canada" in s 58. Asserted.
