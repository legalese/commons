## Seat

The seat is a person who is, or may be, accused of an offence under the Criminal Code (the accused), asking on facts they can state whether what they did is an offence, what its gravity and penalty are, what defences, rights and procedures apply at each stage from arrest through trial, sentence and appeal, and what follows from a conviction.
(Assumed, not ruled: Singapore's Penal Code is the model, as the maintainer instructed on 2026-11-09, but this is a plain seat layer; no charge-generator contract.)
This range holds the offences about money, about the proceeds of crime and about attempts, conspiracy and criminal organizations, and the powers of a court and the Crown over the property of a person charged or convicted.
There is never a seat for an official: a provision that addresses a judge, the Attorney General, a peace officer or a clerk is encoded for what it decides and, in the seat layer, for what it means to the person (what may be done to their property, what they must be given, by when, and what defence or relief they have).

## How the answers are read

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ccc14)
Every rule answers a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (a few answer a `MAYBE` of a core type, or a plain number or date).
- `JUST TRUE`: on the facts given, the case is within the provision, on the text's own words.
- `JUST FALSE`, a "no": the text says no in its own words. In this range that means a definition that says "means" and the case is outside it (for example `s 448 current`, `s 467.1(1) the group is a criminal organization`); an "if", "unless", "where" or "when" gate the text itself introduces that fails (for example `s 462.37(1)`: the court must order forfeiture "if" the offender is convicted and the Attorney General applies); a stated period the case falls outside (for example `s 462.42(1)`: "within thirty days after the forfeiture"); a flat negative ("does not apply", "it is not necessary", "may not apply to vary"). Every such branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`, and `work/lint.py` checks that each quoted fragment occurs in the text.
- `NOTHING`, "not covered": the text lists without closing the list, says "includes", leaves a term undefined, is silent, points to a power, regulation, rate or authority outside the sources, or limits a prohibition or a grant in its own wording ("without lawful justification or excuse", "knowingly", the conduct it names, "with leave of the court") and the case falls outside the limit. Every such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, with <why> one of: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- **In an offence section the answer is therefore `JUST TRUE` or `NOTHING`, and almost never `JUST FALSE`.** A person who states facts outside an element of the offence ("I had a lawful excuse", "I did not know") is told the section does not say they are guilty (not covered), not that they are innocent: the section describes the offence and does not say no to anyone. A `NOTHING` here does not clear the person of other offences. (A person is told `JUST FALSE` only where the text says no in its own words: a defence such as `s 457(4)`, an exception such as `s 457(2)`, an "unless", a "means" definition.)
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`; the seat layer maps a yes to an "includes" definition to `JUST TRUE` and a no to `NOTHING` (never `JUST FALSE`), and a yes to a "means" definition to `JUST TRUE` and a no to `JUST FALSE`.
- `REFUSE` does not exist on the stable binary: "not covered" is `NOTHING` or a constructor, asserted with `EQUALS NOTHING`.
