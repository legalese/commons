## Marginal readings for a lawyer (ccc24)

Each of these changes an answer if a lawyer reads the text the other way; the chosen reading is the one the layer-1 function encodes.

- **F673** (s 673 "trial court"): chosen: "means ... and includes" is read as closed: JUST TRUE for the court by which the accused was tried and for a judge or provincial court judge acting under Part XIX, JUST FALSE for any other court (the keel's reading of "means ... and includes", ccc-definitions.l4). Other: open: the "includes" limb leaves the list open, so a court outside both limbs is NOT COVERED (NOTHING).
- **F673a** (s 673 "court of appeal"; s 696.3(1) "court of appeal"): chosen: A "means" definition: a court that is not the court of appeal for the province or territory of the trial (s 673) or of the applicant's trial (s 696.3(1)) is JUST FALSE; the court that is the one gets the keel's section 2 answer for the Court of Appeal of the province. Other: The section only points at the section 2 definition, so every court gets whatever section 2 answers (NOTHING where it says so).
- **F673i** (s 673 "indictment"): chosen: "indictment includes ..." is an open list: a document outside the extension is NOT COVERED here, and the keel's section 2 "indictment" answer is carried through. Other: closed: JUST FALSE for any document that is not an information or charge on which a person was tried for an indictable offence under Part XIX.
- **F674** (s 674 (the bar on proceedings not authorized)): chosen: A flat negative with an "other than" carve-out ("No proceedings other than those authorized by this Part and Part XXVI"): a proceeding that Part XXI or Part XXVI authorizes is outside the bar, and the bar function answers JUST FALSE for it; JUST TRUE where the bar bites. Other: NOTHING for the authorized proceedings: the carve-out names what is allowed and does not itself say "the bar does not apply".
- **F675b** (s 675(1)(b)): chosen: "with leave of the court of appeal or a judge thereof unless that sentence is one fixed by law": the "unless" lifts the leave condition for a sentence fixed by law, so the appeal then lies without leave (JUST TRUE). Other: No appeal lies against a sentence fixed by law at all (the "unless" taken as taking such a sentence out of the paragraph).
- **F678** (s 678(1)): chosen: A notice given outside the period the rules of court direct answers NOTHING, because s 678(2) lets the court extend the time "at any time". Other: JUST FALSE: the period is the text's own ("within such period as may be directed by rules of court"), and a notice outside it is no notice.
- **F679i** (s 679(5.1)): chosen: "immediately" is modelled as WITHIN 0 in the regulative rule (assumed, not ruled: the text gives no number), the unit being the day; the decision function answers JUST FALSE where the appellant does not comply with the release order ("if the appellant complies"). - **F686a** (s 686(5), s 686(5.01)): chosen: Paragraph (d) governs "notwithstanding paragraph (a)" (a provincial court judge for a conviction for a section 553 offence made by a provincial court judge), and the section 553 answer is a seam input: where it is NOTHING the whole answer is NOTHING. s 686(5) answers NOTHING for the Court of Appeal of Nunavut ("Subject to subsection (5.01)") and s 686(5.01) the reverse. ### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ccc24)

A function answers `JUST TRUE` where the text's words are met on the facts given, `JUST FALSE` only where the text says no in its own words, and `NOTHING` where the text does not answer.
A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `JUST FALSE`: `NOTHING` is "not covered here", not "no".

- `JUST FALSE` comes from: a "means" definition, a condition the text introduces with "if", "only if", "unless" or "until" once the event has happened, a flat negative, a fixed period (ruling (a)), and a where/when/on-gate that fails (ruling (b), listed in G2).
- `NOTHING` comes from: a limit with no "only" (ruling (c)), a list the text does not close, "includes", silence, a term or order the text points outside itself for, and, since the ruling of 2026-11-12, a grant or a power whose "if", "with" or "who" gate fails.
- Every `JUST FALSE` branch in the modules carries a comment `FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<quote>"` and every `NOTHING` branch one `NOTHING-kind (<why>): "<quote>"`; `work/lint.py` checks that each quote is verbatim from lines 14069-14871 (curly apostrophes included): lint: 135 JUST FALSE results, 253 NOTHING results, 389 quotes, 0 problems.
- A function for a duty answers whether the duty applies on the facts: `JUST FALSE` where its own gate fails (for example s 679(5.1): "if the appellant complies"), and compliance where the text fixes a number (ss 696.5, 696.87(1), 714.2(2)).
- A bar is asked as "does the bar apply": `JUST TRUE` where it bites, `JUST FALSE` where an exception lifts it, `NOTHING` where its holder is not covered.
- In the seat layer a title that starts "may" answers a grant, a title that starts "does the bar" answers a bar, and a title that starts "what is the greatest" answers a `MAYBE Fine` or `MAYBE Imprisonment`, never a flag.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Sorted by how the failure shows: loud means an error or a failed assertion, silent means a wrong answer with exit code 0.

- **loud**: `NOT a AND b` parses as `NOT (a AND b)`; a test showed it in the first module. Every NOT is bracketed: `(NOT `x`)`.
- **loud**: A MAYBE value used as an argument must be in parentheses; the checker reports a type error otherwise.
- **loud**: `REFUSE` is not used in this family (the brief's rule for the stable line); "not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted with `EQUALS NOTHING`.
- **loud**: `TYPICALLY` is not used on fields; a field with a default is written out as a plain input.
- **loud**: A `#ASSERT` is one line: a line break inside it is a parse error on both builds.
- **silent**: A regulative rule (`PARTY ... MUST ... WITHIN n HENCE FULFILLED LEST BREACH BY ... BECAUSE "..."`) is asserted only through `#TRACE`, and `check.sh` does not count a `#TRACE`: a trace that evaluates to the wrong outcome still gives exit code 0. The six #TRACEs of this part (ss 675(4), 679(5.1), 696.5) are compared across the two builds by `work/traces.sh`, with `JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` unset and with a check that each build produced evaluations (276, 147 and 132 evaluation blocks on each of 1.6.110 and 1.6.109 in the three files; result text identical).
- **silent**: A `MAY` rule with no `LEST` ends FULFILLED when the time lapses (s 675(4)); the lapse is not a breach on either build, so the rule cannot by itself say "no" to a late filing: the decision function answers JUST FALSE for that.
- **silent**: A multi-line `GIVEN` with one `@desc` per input, an enum-typed `GIVEN` and `GIVETH A MAYBE BOOLEAN` after `@export` are accepted by both builds with no warning (a seat probe confirmed it); the generator for the seat layer relies on this.
