## Seat (ccc11)

The seat is a person who is, or may be, accused of an offence under the Criminal Code, asking on facts they can state whether what they did is an offence, what its gravity and penalty are, what defences, rights and procedures apply from arrest through trial, sentence and appeal, and what follows from a conviction (assumed, not ruled: Singapore's Penal Code is the model, per the maintainer's instruction of 2026-11-09, but this is a plain seat layer; no charge-generator contract).
`ccc11-seat.l4` has 106 exported questions, each beginning "Under section N," with the Act's own numbering, grouped under `§` headings in the text's order; groups that arise only in a situation the person may not be in (a demand, a blood sample, a prohibition order, an appeal) say so in the heading's comment. Each question takes plain inputs (yes or no, a number, a date, or a choice from a short list worded in the question's `@desc`), builds the layer-1 inputs from them (calling the keel's `s 2 motor vehicle`, `s 2 railway equipment`, `s 2 peace officer`, `s 2 bodily harm` and `s 2 justice`), and calls the layer-1 rule. It restates no rule. A string that is not one of the listed choices answers NOTHING.
Direction audit (the brief's rule that a question's name asks what its body answers, in the same direction): all 106 titles were read against the layer-1 function each calls. One mismatch was found and fixed: the first title of the 320.24(10) question was "may I not yet be registered ..." over a function that is the bar (`... the person may not yet be registered ...`, JUST TRUE meaning barred); it is now "am I still barred from being registered ...". No other question differs in direction or subject: offences ask "have I committed", bars "am I barred", exceptions "does the exception apply", duties "must" or "has ... been met", officers' powers "may a peace officer ...", rules of evidence "is ... admissible" or "am I presumed".
Provisions that address only an official have no question of their own and are encoded for what they mean to the person: 320.37 (the immunity of a practitioner or technician, F25), 320.38 (inert), 320.39 and 320.4 (approvals and designations: whether a device, container or person was approved or designated is asked through the 320.11 definitions that turn on it). There is never a seat for an official. Questions were added late for 320.32(6)(b) (a certificate of prohibition for a conveyance other than a motor vehicle, which an accused under 320.18(1) in a vessel would meet) and 320.36(4) (the offence of contravening the prohibitions of 320.36(1) or (2), which the accused can commit); the class and the punishment of 320.36(4) are layer-1 rules (`s 320.36(4) the class of the offence`, `s 320.36(4) the punishment for an offence under subsection 320.36(4)`) with no question of their own.

Four seat inputs ask the person, as a fact, for a legal conclusion that this Part itself decides: `off1` for 320.14(2) and (3) ("I committed an offence under subsection (1)"), `off151` for 320.15(2) and (3), `off161` for 320.16(2) and (3), and `lawful` for 320.15(1) ("the demand was one that section 320.27 or 320.28 allowed"). Why: the predicate offence is its own exported question and a person can ask it first; the form asks each question separately; and recomposing the predicate inside the question would need about fifteen inputs on one question. The layer-1 functions take the predicate as a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` input, so the composition exists there and the lead can wire it at the merge. A person who answers these wrongly gets a wrong answer; the seat does not check one answer against another.

The general penalty of section 787(1) is typed as two numbers. The seat reads a typed `0` as "none stated" (`no fine is stated`, `no imprisonment is stated`), never as "a fine of 0" or "a term of 0 months", and a case asserts that (`ccc11-cases-seat.l4`, the 320.13(1) summary case with zeros).

## How to read the answers

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ccc11)

Every layer-1 rule answers a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (the others answer a core value, a `MAYBE` of one, or a record, and say so in their names).
- `JUST TRUE`: on the facts given, the case is within the section on the text's own words (an offence is made out, a power is given, a presumption holds, a duty applies or is met).
- `JUST FALSE`, a "no": the text says no in its own words. In this range that means: a definition that says "means" and the case is outside it (320.11); an element of "Everyone commits an offence who ..." that is missing (F18); a condition the text introduces with "if", "only if", "unless", "in the absence of" or "except" that is unmet or met (the exceptions of 320.14(5)-(7), 320.18(2), the duties of 320.23(2), 320.24(1), 320.28(4), 320.29(5), 320.34(1), the presumptions of 320.31(6), 320.32(7), 320.35); a period the text fixes in its own words that has not run or has run (320.24(10), 320.28(10), 320.32(4), (5), (7), 320.34(3), (4)); a flat negative ("does not affect", "does not constitute", "does not operate", "incurs any liability" with its conditions). Every such branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`.
- `NOTHING`, "not covered": the text lists without closing the list ("includes", "any of the following ... is considered", "in addition to any other"), leaves a term undefined ("aircraft", "large motor vehicle"), is silent, points to something outside the sources (the regulations of 320.38, section 787(1)), or limits a grant or a prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit. In this Part that is chiefly the powers ("may ... if": the officer's demand, the justice's warrant, the court's order, the Attorney General's approval): where an "if" gate is unmet the answer is NOTHING, never "may not" (ruling of 2026-11-12), except the three powers whose gate is of the where/when/upon kind, which answer the guide's JUST FALSE and are listed (F34 to F36). Every such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, with <why> one of: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`. In particular a person's "no" to "was the officer a police officer?" is NOTHING, not "not a peace officer" (section 2 "peace officer includes"), and a vessel that is neither a hovercraft nor a vessel in the ordinary sense is NOT COVERED, not "not a vessel" (F1).
- A kind comment sits on the branch line or on the function's own line; a function composed through the three Kleene helpers (`s 320.11 helper: both hold`, `either holds`, `the opposite of`) carries the kind of each operand and one NOTHING-kind line of its own. The helpers are not provisions and carry no kind tag.
- `REFUSE` is not used: "not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted with `EQUALS NOTHING` (60 such assertions in the seat cases alone).
- What a regulative rule answers: a `DEONTIC` value that this binary cannot compare in an `#ASSERT`; each duty therefore has a decision-function twin that is asserted, and the regulative rule is traced (see "What the stable binary does not accept").

## Limits of this work

- Expected values in the tests are written by hand from the text before each run, but by the same agent that wrote the functions. The independent test pass the skill recommends (a fresh session that decides the answers before reading the encoding) was not run: no subagent was available. One composition error was found by a case and repaired (Finding 9); the others the tests could find were found the same way.
- The tests exercise each branch and each threshold of each function. They do not test that a caller composes the functions correctly across parts: that is the merge's job. The seat composes within this part (the exceptions into the offences, 320.31(4) into 320.14(1)(b), the occasion into the punishment and the prohibition period).
- The `@ref` locators and quotations are checked by `srcrefs.py check --refs` and `--strict`; the FALSE-kind and NOTHING-kind quotations by `work/lint.py` (every quoted fragment occurs in the text; every `JUST FALSE` and `NOTHING` literal has a kind tag in its function). Neither checks that an encoding means what the text says: that is for a reviewer.
- Machine-independence: no absolute path is written in any deposited file.
