## Jural relations (ccc21)

Hohfeld's pairs, read off each rule's name: the first party is the grammatical subject (or the "by the X" of a passive rule; where the text names no actor the holder is "not named in the text") and the modal ("shall" and "must" a duty, "may" a power where the subject is an official and a privilege where it is a party, "has a right" a claim); the second party is the person the action is done to or for, read from the rule's own words.
One row per rule that creates a relation, so each relation is stated once, from the side the text states it; no relation is written twice.
A duty that is encoded both as a regulative rule and as decision functions (the stable binary cannot assert the rule: see "What the stable binary does not accept") is one row, with the rule first and the decision functions after it.
Rules whose text names no actor (passive or impersonal: "shall be tried", "may be preferred", "shall be drawn up") are not in this table: they follow it, in their own list, because no holder can be read off them.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ccc21)

In this row a JUST FALSE is "the text says no on these facts, in its own words": the accused is not before a justice, no request for an inquiry has been made, the period has run, the exception applies.
Read it as "this section gives no such duty, power or right on these facts", never as "the law is the opposite": a duty with a failed "where" is JUST FALSE for that duty only, and says nothing about another section.
A NOTHING is "not covered": the section's own words limit what it says (a person to whom it does not speak, a stage it does not reach), the text is silent (an offence whose section states no maximum), the question turns on another part of the Code (section 469, section 577, section 2), or the text lists without closing the list.
A caller must never turn a NOTHING into a FALSE.
A seat question's answer reads the same way: JUST TRUE is yes on the facts as stated, JUST FALSE is the text's no, NOTHING is "not covered", and a footing or decision question returns the footing or decision wrapped in JUST.

## Rulings applied, word for word (copied by script from the brief)

The rule of 2026-10-29 for FALSE and NOTHING, as the brief states it:

- A decision function answers `JUST FALSE` (a `BOOLEAN` that is `MAYBE`-wrapped), or a constructor whose meaning is "the text says no" (for example `s 39 not entitled`, `s 39 not barred`, `s 39 not obliged`: **every constructor and type you declare begins with the section label, like every function**, so that no two parts declare the same name; the merged seat layer imports all the parts at once, and a name defined in two modules cannot be used at all), **only** where the text itself says no to the case in its own words: a definition that says "means"; a condition the text introduces with "if", "only if", "as long as" or "unless" (and their plain variants: "so long as", "while", "until", "except", "save", "provided that"); a list the text closes with "only" or "nothing but"; a flat negative ("does not", "shall not", "may not", "is not entitled").
- Everywhere else the function answers `NOTHING` (a `MAYBE` result) or a named "... not covered" constructor: where the text lists without closing the list, says "includes", leaves a term undefined, is silent, points to a power, regulation, rate or authority outside your sources, or limits a grant or prohibition in its own wording ("without", "against the will of", "when", a stated period, a named complement) and the case falls outside the limit.
- **For a lawyer, not for you to decide:** (a) a failed "if" counts as the text's no, clause by clause, within the provision that holds the "if" (an inference: "if A then B" does not itself say "if not A then not B"), and flat negatives count as the text saying no; (b) a prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not. Take these readings as the status quo, and list every branch that rests on one under a heading `## Marginal readings for a lawyer` in `NOTES.md`, with the other reading stated.

The grant rule of 2026-11-12, as the brief states it:

**A function named for a grant or a power answers `NOTHING`, not `JUST FALSE`, when an "if" gate fails (ruling of 2026-11-12, three limits).** A layer-1 function NAMED for a grant or a power ("may", "is permitted to", "has power to") that answers `JUST FALSE` because an "if" gate failed reads as "may not", a grant turned into a bar. The guide's "In short" sentence gives `NOTHING` where the text "limits a grant or prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit". (1) Answer `NOTHING` for such a function, with one fork line in NOTES.md (fork register and marginal readings) that names the other reading. (2) Keep `JUST FALSE` wherever the text closes the gate itself ("only if", "only where", "may not ... unless", a list closed with "only"), and for a bar, a duty or the provision's own question. (3) Do NOT decide two groups by yourself; list them in the fork register with a count and leave the answer as the guide's rulings give it: "may" functions whose gate is "where", "when" or "upon" (ruling (b) says `JUST FALSE`, the line on grants points the other way: a question for a lawyer), and "entitled"-named functions (a claim, not a power).
