## Seat

The seat is a person who is, or may be, accused of an offence under the Criminal Code (the accused), asking on facts they can state whether what they did is an offence, what its gravity and penalty are, what defences, rights and procedures apply at each stage from arrest through trial, sentence and appeal, and what follows from a conviction. (Assumed, not ruled: Singapore's Penal Code is the model, as the maintainer instructed on 2026-11-09, but this is a plain seat layer; no charge-generator contract.)
In Part I the questions the accused asks are: whether the act is within Canada's reach (section 7, and who must consent), whether they can be convicted at all (sections 6, 9, 13, 15), whether twice (12, 7(6)), whether they are a party or guilty of an attempt (21 to 24), what defences or justifications they have (16, 17, 25 to 35, 43, 45), what proof, procedure and notice the Code allows against them (4(7), 7(10), 7(11), 20, 25.4), what rights they have to appeal a contempt conviction (10), and what the Code says about their dealings with the police (11.1, 14, 19). The seat layer (`ccc1-seat.l4`) has one question for each, named "Under section N, ...". Provisions that address only an official are encoded in layer 1 for what they decide, and in layer 2 only for what they mean to the person: the designation machinery of section 25.1(3) to (7) and the annual report of section 25.3 have no question of their own.
Assumed, not ruled: the brief says there is never a seat for an official, and a few questions are asked as a person acting under a power or a duty, on the ground that the accused may be that person (a peace officer charged for the force he or she used, a person charged after arresting or executing a warrant). They are the officer-as-accused questions 25(5), 25.1, 31(2) and 32(1) ("a peace officer", "a public officer"), the duty-bearer questions 29(1)a, 29(1)b, 29(2) and 33(1) ("do I have the duty ..."), and, less plainly, 28(1), 28(2), 30b and 31(1) ("may I detain", "am I justified in arresting"). A reader who takes "never a seat for an official" strictly can drop these twelve; they call the same layer-1 functions as the others, and dropping them changes no layer-1 file. The 107 questions are listed, with the audit of their names and the layer-1 functions no question reaches, in the section "Seat questions (ccc1)" below.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ccc1)

Every layer-1 rule answers a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (a few answer a `MAYBE NUMBER`, a `MAYBE` of a core type, or a `DEONTIC`). The seat layer answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` too, except two questions that answer a value (section 10(3), a province or territory; section 16(3), a party): an answer is `JUST TRUE`, `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING`.
- `JUST TRUE`: on the facts given, the case is within the provision, on the text's own words. For a bar (a function named "is barred", "conviction is barred", "does not apply"), `JUST TRUE` means the bar holds.
- `JUST FALSE`, a "no": the text says no in its own words. In this range that means: a definition that says "means" and the case is outside it; a condition the text introduces with "if", "where", "when", "while", "until", "unless" or "except" that fails; a flat negative ("No civil remedy ... is suspended", "No person is entitled", "Nothing in this section justifies"); a closed gate ("may only be instituted with the consent"). Every such branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`, and `work/lint.py` checks that each quoted fragment occurs in the text. For a defence ("is justified", "is protected", "is not guilty", "is excused") a JUST FALSE reads "this section gives no defence on these facts", never "no defence at all" (ruling of 2026-11-05 (b)); for a section that says what it does ("the subsection makes the person a party") it reads "this subsection does not", never "the person is not a party".
- `NOTHING`, "not covered": the text is silent, lists without closing the list ("includes", "may appeal (a) ... or (b)"), points to a term, an answer or a provision outside the sources, or limits a grant or prohibition in its own wording ("who", "without", a lead-in that says which acts the subsection is about) and the case falls outside the limit. Every such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)` with <why> one of: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`. In particular a person who is in a capacity that section 2's "peace officer" or "public officer" does not list is NOT COVERED, not "not a peace officer": the seat layer calls the keel's own functions for those, so the keel's NOTHING reaches the person.
- `REFUSE` does not exist on the stable binary as far as the brief is concerned: "not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted with `EQUALS NOTHING`.

## Findings

### What the text turns out to say (numbered)

1. **Section 14 states no exception in its own words, and another section does.** `s 14` says "No person is entitled to consent to have death inflicted on them" and `s 227(4)` (line 4500, in another part's range) says "Section 14 does not apply with respect to a person who consents to have death inflicted on them by means of medical assistance in dying provided in accordance with section 241.2." `s 14 a person is entitled to consent ...` answers JUST FALSE on the text of section 14 alone; a caller must take section 227(4) first for that case. Asserted in `ccc1-tests-s8-20-general.l4` only for section 14's own words.
2. **Sections 8(2) and 9 are not reconciled in the text.** Section 8(2) continues "the criminal law of England that was in force in a province immediately before April 1, 1955"; section 9 ("Notwithstanding anything in this Act or any other Act") says no person shall be convicted of an offence at common law or under an Act of the Parliament of England. The text does not say which gives way; each is encoded as it reads.
3. **Section 7(4.3) names (4.1) and (4.2), not (4.11).** The consent of the Attorney General is required for acts deemed committed in Canada under (4.1) (sexual offences against children) and (4.2) (section 240.1), and the text names no consent for (4.11) (trafficking in persons). Asserted: `s 7(4.3)` with "deemed under (4.1) or (4.2)" false answers NOTHING.
4. **Two different Attorneys General.** Section 7(4.3) asks for the consent of "the Attorney General" (as section 2 defines it, which varies with the proceeding); sections 7(2.33), (2.37) and (7) ask for "the Attorney General of Canada".
5. **Section 7(1) and (4) say "punishable by indictment", the space subsections say "an indictable offence".** The text does not say whether an offence that is both indictable and punishable on summary conviction is inside; fork F5.
6. **Section 7(11) is wider than 7(10).** Subsection (10) makes the Minister's certificate admissible "in any proceedings under this Act"; subsection (11) says "in any proceedings". Subsection (10) adds "in the absence of evidence to the contrary"; subsection (11) does not.
7. **Section 7(8) and (9) bind other sections.** Subsection (8) applies "for the purposes of this section, of the definition peace officer in section 2 and of sections 27.1, 76 and 77"; subsection (9) "of this section and section 77". The keel's `s 2 peace officer` has a pilot-in-command limb that turns on "while the aircraft is in flight" and sections 76 and 77 are in another part, so `s 7(8) the aircraft is in flight` and `s 7(9) the aircraft is deemed to be in service` are the answers those parts take (Seams).
8. **The two "Partner State" definitions differ.** Section 7(2.34): "a State, other than Canada, who contracted to enter into the Agreement and for which the Agreement has entered into force in accordance with article 25"; section 7(2.38): "a State, other than Canada, that is a Gateway Partner as defined in Article 3.1 of the Agreement". Each "Agreement" is defined by reference to another Act (`s 7(2.34) Agreement`, `s 7(2.38) Agreement` pass that Act's answer through).
9. **Section 10(1) and (2) differ in who convicts.** Subsection (1) names "a court, judge, justice or provincial court judge" and contempt "in the face of the court"; subsection (2) names only "a court or judge" and contempt "not committed in the face of the court". The right to appeal is the same.
10. **Section 12 has two limbs with different exceptions.** "unless a contrary intention appears" qualifies the liability to proceedings under any of the Acts, not the bar on being punished more than once.
11. **Section 17 excludes "treason" and "high treason" separately, and ends with sections 280 to 283.** The list is of 16 items; two of them, "sexual assault with a weapon, threats to a third party or causing bodily harm" and "assault with a weapon or causing bodily harm", are the headings of the sections that create them (fork F18).
12. **Sections 25.2 and 25.4 refer to "paragraph 25.1(9)(a) or (b)", not to subsection (8).** The report and the notification attach to the two routes by which a public officer may act where property loss or serious damage is likely.
13. **Section 25.1(2) is a statement of purpose.** It creates no rule and is the one `inert` row of the range apart from headings.
14. **Section 25.1(6) has a duty in its last sentence.** "The senior official shall without delay notify the competent authority of the designation." The text gives no number of days; none is invented.
15. **Section 34(2) lists a paragraph (f.1).** The coverage table keeps the Code's own lettering.
16. **Repealed rows.** Section 7(2.32), 7(3.2) to (3.6), 7(3.76) and (3.77), 23(2), sections 36 to 42 and 44 are shown repealed in the text (12 rows); each is a string constant that quotes what the text shows.
17. **"Holiday", "police officer" and "province" are not defined in section 2.** `s 20`, `s 11.1` and `s 10(3)` take them as facts or leave territories uncovered (forks F34, F26, F20).
18. **No section of this range gives a penalty.** Sections 3.1 to 45 decide whether an act is an offence, who is a party, which defences apply and where an act is deemed committed; the punishment of any offence is in another part, so `ccc1` computes no `Punishment`.

### What the stable binary does not accept

Measured on 2026-11-13 on 1.6.110 (main, e51181d) and, for the trace comparison and the counts, also on 1.6.109 (main, 4c887e0). Each probe was a small file run with `l4 run`.
- **A regulative outcome cannot be asserted.** `#ASSERT <rule> EQUALS FULFILLED` is an error, `assertion could not be evaluated: Trying to check equality on types that do not support it` (the values compared are the `PARTY ... MUST ...` rule itself). `#ASSERT (#TRACE <rule> AT 0 WITH ...)` and `#ASSERT #TRACE ...` are `parse/unexpected-directive` (`unexpected #TRACE`). `#ASSERT <rule> AT 0 WITH ...` is `unexpected AT`. `#ASSERT REFUSED <rule>` is `assertion failed: expected a refusal, but the expression produced a value`. So every regulative rule here is run with `#TRACE` and its outcome is read from the printed result (`FULFILLED`, `DEONTIC BREACHED` with its `BECAUSE`, or the residual obligation), and the same duty is also a decision function whose answer is asserted. The 12 traces were run on both builds and the `Result:` blocks are identical (`ccc1-tests-s25-reports.l4`: 6, `ccc1-tests-s26-33-force.l4`: 4, `ccc1-tests-s33-45-defences.l4`: 2); `check.sh` does not compare traces (its own footer says so), so this was a separate diff.
- **A `MUST` with no `WITHIN` stays open.** `#TRACE` of the section 25.2 rule with only `(WAITUNTIL 30)` printed the residual obligation, not a breach: with no deadline in the rule there is nothing to lapse. The one rule with a deadline (`s 25.4(1)`, `WITHIN 1`, a clock counting years) printed `DEONTIC BREACHED` after `(WAITUNTIL 1.5)` and `FULFILLED` for an act at 0.5.
- **`NOT` takes everything to its right, even after a bracketed operand.** `NOT (x) AND NOT (y)` is read as `NOT ((x) AND NOT (y))`, and `(NOT x OR y)` as `NOT (x OR y)`. Found by a test of `s 7(8)` and `s 7(9)`, whose end-of-period tests failed until each `NOT` was bracketed with its own operand, `(NOT (x)) AND (NOT (y))`. Every `NOT` in the modules is now of the form `NOT (...)` standing alone, `(NOT x)` in brackets, or the last operand before `THEN`.
- **`x NOT EQUALS y` is a parse error** (`unexpected NOT`): write `NOT (x EQUALS y)`.
- **Accepted, and used:** `DATE PLUS 8` (a count of days), `` `add years` `` and `Date day month year` from `daydate`; `WAITUNTIL` in a `#TRACE`; `LEST BREACH BECAUSE`; a `GIVEN` that the body does not read (no warning); `@ref` above a bare `GIVETH A DEONTIC ...` rule (no "follows code on its line" or "is at the end of the file" notice from `l4 check` over all `ccc1-` modules); `CONSIDER ...
