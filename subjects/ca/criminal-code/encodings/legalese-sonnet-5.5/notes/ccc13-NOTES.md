## Marginal readings for a lawyer (ccc13)

Two readings the brief leaves to a lawyer; this part takes both as the status quo and lists every branch that rests on one.
**Reading (a):** a failed "if", "where", "when", "who" or "unless" condition counts as the text's no, clause by clause, within the provision that holds the condition (an inference: "if A then B" does not itself say "if not A then not B"). **The other reading:** the text is silent when the condition fails, so the answer is NOTHING.
**Reading (b):** a prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not give a no outside the limit; it answers NOTHING. **The other reading:** the limit is itself the text's no, so the case outside it is JUST FALSE.
Flat negatives ("does not", "shall not", "may not", "no order may be made") count as the text's no (ruling N2, option A) and are not marginal; they are listed once at the end for completeness.
Forks F1, F5, F6, F7, F12, F14 and F15 name the choice at the sites where it matters most; the tables below are the complete list from the code (`work/notes.py` scans every `FALSE-kind` and `NOTHING-kind` comment of the ten modules and `ccc13-index.l4`).

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ccc13)

In this part the answers are about whether what a person did is an offence, what the offence's class and punishment are, and what saves, presumes, bars or orders.
**JUST TRUE** is the text's own yes: every element the text lists is met, the saving applies, the presumption arises, the court is empowered or bound.
**JUST FALSE** is the text's own no, and it is given in six situations only, each with a `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the words>"` comment: an element of an offence the text states as a condition is unmet (F1); a "means" definition does not cover the case; a flat negative applies ("does not", "shall not", "may not", "no order may be made"); a condition the text introduces with "if", "where", "when", "unless" or "except" is not met or is met (F6, F14); a closed list does not name the case; a defence, saving or exception is not shown, worded "this subsection gives no saving on these facts" and its variants (F6, F12).
**NOTHING** is "not covered": the text does not answer the case.
It is given where a prohibition of the form "No person shall" is not shown to be broken (marginal reading (b)); where a "may" function's "if" gate fails (F5, F7); where a deeming rule's elements are unmet (F15); where the text says "includes" and the case is outside the list; where a term is undefined or points outside the text (a section 2 term, section 787(1), section 342.1(2)); and where a scope limit has no "only" (periods ruling (c)).
Each NOTHING carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)` with one of: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
**A caller must never turn NOTHING into FALSE.** The helpers `ccc13 all of`, `ccc13 any of` and `ccc13 not` (in `ccc13-logic.l4`) follow Kleene's rules for that reason.
At the seat the same three values reach the person unchanged; the record answering an offence question holds the three-valued answer, the class if it is one, and the punishments if it is one.

## Findings: what the text turns out to say (ccc13)

1. **A will worth $5,000 or less is in two paragraphs of s 380(1).** Paragraph (a) puts a testamentary instrument of any value in the fourteen-year limb; paragraph (b) puts anything of value not over $5,000 in the two-year and summary limbs (lines 7153-7158). The text gives two classes for a will worth $3,000. Encoded as NOTHING for the class and the punishments (F3).
2. **The minimum punishment of s 380(1.1) is its own Punishment.** "When a person is prosecuted on indictment and convicted ... the court ... shall impose a minimum punishment of imprisonment for a term of two years if the total value of the subject-matter of the offences exceeds one million dollars" (line 7160). It is a minimum with no maximum stated there, so `s 380(1.1) the minimum punishment` is a `Punishment` with a minimum and no maximum.
3. **Sections 407 to 411 state an offence and no penalty.** Section 412(1) states the penalty for all five (line 7446). The punishment function is `s 412(1) the punishments`, which takes the offence by its section number, and `ccc13-index.l4` calls it for 407 to 411.
4. **A deeming rule is not a definition of wilfulness.** Section 429(1) says a person who causes an event knowing the act will probably cause it and reckless whether it occurs "shall be deemed ... wilfully to have caused" it. It adds a case; it does not say that everyone else is not wilful, so an unmet deeming rule answers NOTHING (F15).
5. **Section 445.2(2.1) depends on a date the text does not give.** "If a cetacean is gestating on the day on which this subsection comes into force" (line 7835). The day is a `Date` input (F16).
6. **The forty-eight hours of s 415(b) run from the receipt.** "does not within forty-eight hours thereafter inform the receiver": "thereafter" is the receipt of the wreck. The input is the hours since receipt, and (F10) a person still inside the period has not yet met the element.
7. **Section 394(6) and section 394(7) are two limbs of one grant.** Subsection (6) lets the court order forfeiture of anything by means of or in relation to which the offence was committed; subsection (7) says (6) "does not apply to real property other than real property built or significantly modified for the purpose of facilitating the commission of an offence". The unqualified grant and the grant "subject to subsection (7)" are two functions, and two seat questions (F8). The same shape is in section 394.1(4) and (5).
8. **Section 424.1's two threats.** The single sentence joins a threat to commit an offence against United Nations or associated personnel and a threat to commit an offence under section 431.1; "with intent to compel" governs both (F11).
9. **The keel's "includes" terms cannot say no.** A keel function for a term the Code says "includes" answers NOTHING outside its list; a caller that needs a no from it gets none (S4, F13).
10. **Ten units of the range are shown repealed.** `s 380.3`, `s 380.4`, `s 400(1)(d)`, `s 402`, `s 404`, `s 413`, `s 423.1(2)`, `s 427`, `s 444` and `s 447(3)`; each is a constant `... as the text shows it` that returns the text's own words, and a seat user who asks about one gets no question.
11. **Section 416 is machinery.** "The Governor in Council may, by notice to be published in the Canada Gazette, prescribe distinguishing marks": a power to prescribe. The rule of the Code that matters to a person is that a mark so appropriated is a distinguishing mark under `s 417(3)`; that fact is an input (S7).
12. **Section 787(1) is another part's.** Wherever the text says "punishable on summary conviction" and states no penalty the limb is an input (S2). Where the text states a fine or a term for the summary limb (`s 445(2)(b)`, `s 445.01(2)(b)`, `s 445.1(2)(b)`, `s 447(2)(b)`, `s 445.2(5)`), it is encoded.
13. **Some seat questions have no JUST FALSE case, by the text's design.** A mechanical scan of `ccc13-cases-seat.l4` against the layer-1 code (`work/scan_cases.py`, run at the last change) found that 34 of the 205 questions have no JUST FALSE case, and that for every one of them the layer-1 function it calls, and every function that one calls, contains no `JUST FALSE` in its code: a prohibition of the form "No person shall" answers JUST TRUE or NOTHING (reading (b)); a bar or limit answers NOTHING outside its wording; a definition that says "includes" answers NOTHING outside its list; a "may" function answers NOTHING when its gate fails (F5, F7). One question, `Under section 395(2), what order shall the justice make`, has no JUST TRUE case because its answer is an order, not a yes/no. Every other question has at least one JUST TRUE case and at least one JUST FALSE case.

## What the stable binary does not accept (ccc13)

- `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` and `BECAUSE` do not exist on the stable binary (`BECAUSE` only after `LEST BREACH`); "not covered" is `NOTHING` or a constructor, and each is asserted.
- `TYPICALLY`, a section-level `GIVEN` shared over several rules, and `YMD` are not available; dates are `Date day month year` and no module defines a `YMD`.
- `X NOT EQUALS Y` is a parse error: write `NOT (X EQUALS Y)`. Every `NOT` is parenthesised: `NOT a AND b` parses as `NOT (a AND b)`.
- A keel constructor that several keel enums share (for example `a thing that is none of those`) is ambiguous unless the constant has `GIVETH A <type>`; the tests give it.
- A regulative rule (`PARTY ... MUST / MAY / SHANT`) cannot be asserted with `#ASSERT` on this binary; each is run with `#TRACE` (36 directives in six test files), and the same duty is encoded as a decision function that is asserted. `check.sh` does not see a different `#TRACE` outcome, so the outputs of the two builds were compared by hand: byte-identical on all six files.
- A court's power is a `MAY` with no `LEST`: `PROGRESS.md` (decision D5) records that a `MAY` with a `LEST` degrades silently on the main build; this was recorded at the time of writing and was not re-measured in this pass.
- A continuation line of an `#ASSERT` is rejected, so every assertion is one line; a bullet `•` in a list is a parse error, so lists are vertical `LIST` blocks.
- Constructors and types begin with their section label (`s 380.2(1) activity`), so that no two parts declare the same name.
