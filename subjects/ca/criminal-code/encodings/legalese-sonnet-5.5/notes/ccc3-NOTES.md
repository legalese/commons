## Jural relations (ccc3)

One row per provision or group of provisions that creates a relation, from s 83.01 to s 83.33.
Hohfeld's pairs: claim and duty, liberty and no-right, power and liability, immunity and disability.
A provision that creates no relation (a definition, a counting rule, a rule of construction) is listed once as `none`.
Where the text hands a decision to an official, the official is the holder of the power and the person affected is the bearer of the liability, and the rule is stated once, neutrally.
The offence sections are one relation each: the Crown holds the claim and every person bears the duty; the liability to the stated punishment is the correlative of the court's power to impose it, and is encoded as the punishment function of the same section.

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (ccc3)

Two readings are left open for a lawyer and taken here as the status quo.
**(a)** A failed "if" counts as the text's no, clause by clause, within the provision that holds the "if" (an inference: "if A then B" does not itself say "if not A then not B"), and flat negatives count as the text saying no (ruling N2).
**(b)** A prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not count as the text's no: outside the limit the answer is NOTHING.
For each branch below the other reading is the other one: a branch resting on (a) would answer NOTHING under the other reading, and a branch resting on (b) would answer JUST FALSE.
The tables are generated from the `-- FALSE-kind` and `-- NOTHING-kind` comments of the layer-1 modules (`work/marginal` in `work/notes.py`); a "means" definition and a gate the text closes with "only" are not listed, because the text itself says no there.

(c) and (d), the two groups of the sweep of 2026-11-12, are listed after the tables.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ccc3)

Every decision function in this part answers a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (a few answer a date, a count or a `Punishment`, and say so in their names).
The rule is the one of 2026-10-29, as the brief states it; it is assumed, not ruled by a lawyer.

- `JUST TRUE`, "yes": on the facts given, the case is within the term or the rule, on the text's own words.
  For an offence function, every element of the section is met; for a power, the conditions the text sets for its exercise are met; for a bar, the person is barred.
- `JUST FALSE`, "no": the text itself says no to the case, in its own words.
  In this part that means: a definition that says "means" and the case is outside it ("terrorist activity means", "terrorist group means"); an element of an offence that is unmet, because the section defines the offence by those elements (fork F-ccc3-1); a condition the text introduces with "only if", "unless", "except if", "must" or "shall" and the case fails it; a flat negative ("No person in Canada ... shall"; "is not permitted"; "does not apply"); a stated cap or period the case is outside ("not more than 12 months"; "within 60 days after"); a "where" or "when" gate that fails on a "may" function, which is LISTED for a lawyer (the group (i) below).
  It never means "the law is silent" and never means "the provision does not apply to the person".
- `NOTHING`, "not covered": the text lists without closing the list ("among other factors", "includes"); leaves a term undefined or hands it to another section, to a regulation, to a Minister or to a judge; is silent; points outside the sources (the regulations of ss 83.0391, 83.11(4), 83.14(5.2); the Canada Evidence Act ss 38.2 to 38.45; Part XII.2); or limits a grant or a prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit.
  In this part the commonest case is a power ("the Minister may ...", "the judge may ...") gated by "if": when the gate fails the answer is NOTHING, not JUST FALSE, because a grant turned into a bar is not what the text says (ruling of 2026-11-12, three limits; fork F-ccc3-20).
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.
  In particular "the judge may order ..." answering NOTHING means "this section does not give the judge that power on these facts", and it does not mean "the judge may not".
- `REFUSE` does not exist on the stable binary: "not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted with `EQUALS NOTHING`.
- Every `JUST FALSE` branch of layer 1 carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`, and every `NOTHING` branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)` with <why> one of: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- In the seat layer a question answers the same three ways: JUST TRUE "yes", JUST FALSE "no" (the text says no in its own words), NOTHING "the text does not answer this on the facts you gave (or a term you did not settle)".
  A plain input that is a term another section defines ("terrorist group", "terrorism offence") is asked as yes / no / I do not know, and "I do not know" reaches the rule as NOTHING, never as FALSE.

### A seam inside this part: s 83.3(8)(a) takes the fact that gates (8.1), not (8.1)'s answer

## Findings: what the text turns out to say

Each finding is checked against the text lines cited and against a test in the `ccc3-tests-*` files.

1. **Section 83.3(8)(a) and (8.1) cross a seam inside this part** (see Seams): (8.1) is a power with an "if" gate and answers NOTHING when its gate fails, so (8)(a) must read the gate's fact, not (8.1)'s answer, or every period over 12 months would read NOTHING instead of JUST FALSE.
   Found when five seat cases failed after the first build; two of them exposed this and are fixed in layer 1 (`ccc3-tests-recognizance.l4`, the 13-month case expects JUST FALSE).
2. **Section 83.27 is a two-step rule.**
   Subsection (1) makes a person convicted of an indictable offence whose act or omission "also constitutes a terrorist activity" liable to imprisonment for life, "other than an offence for which a sentence of imprisonment for life is imposed as a minimum punishment"; subsection (2) says (1) "does not apply unless the prosecutor satisfies the court that the offender, before making a plea, was notified that the application of that subsection would be sought" (lines 1789-1791).
   `s 83.27 the person is liable to imprisonment for life, subject to subsection (2)` reads both, and answers NOTHING once the notice is proved (the text says nothing more).
3. **Section 83.26 does not reach the hoax offences.**
   "any of sections 83.02 to 83.04 and 83.18 to 83.23" is a range of decimal section numbers; s 83.231 comes after s 83.23 in the Code's order, so a sentence for s 83.231 is outside the list, and `s 83.26` answers NOTHING for it (tested for ss 83.231(1)).
4. **The summary-conviction branch of the hoax offences states no penalty** (lines 1767-1773: "an offence punishable on summary conviction"), so `s 83.231(2)` and `(3)` take the general penalty of s 787(1) as an input (seam).
5. **Section 83.3 may have lapsed.**
   Section 83.32(1) ends s 83.3 "at the end of the fifth anniversary of the day on which the National Security Act, 2017 receives royal assent unless ... extended by resolution" (line 1886); the day of royal assent and any extension are not in the text, so the encoding takes them as inputs, and `s 83.33(2)` keeps ss 83.3(7) to (14) alive for a person already taken before a judge (line 1902).
   The seat question `Under section 83.32(1), has section 83.3 ceased to have effect on this day` therefore asks the person for the day of royal assent, whether an extension was passed and the last day of the most recent one; the other s 83.3 questions do not ask whether s 83.3 is in force, which is a fact outside this text.
6. **The definition of "terrorist activity" has an exclusion for armed conflict that paragraph (b) alone would not give.**
   "for greater certainty, does not include an act or omission that is committed during an armed conflict and, at the time and in the place of its commission, is in accordance with customary international law or conventional international law applicable to the conflict, or the activities undertaken by military forces of a state in the exercise of their official duties, to the extent that those activities are governed by other rules of international law" (line 1279).
   `s 83.01(1) the act or omission is a terrorist activity` tests both limbs of the exclusion first (tested in `ccc3-tests-interpretation.l4`).
7. **Section 83.14(11) shuts the door that s 83.14(10) opens:** a person who claims an interest in forfeited property and had no notice may move "not later than 60 days after the day on which the forfeiture order was made", and "The Court may not extend the period" (lines 1625, 1627).
   `s 83.14(10)` answers JUST FALSE after day 60, and `s 83.14(11) the Court is barred from extending the period` answers JUST TRUE for that period and NOTHING for any other.
8. **Every authorization function addresses the Minister, not the person.**
   Sections 83.031 to 83.0392 are written about the Public Safety Minister; the encoding reads them for the person who applies for, holds or is covered by an authorization: what the Minister may do on their application (and what NOTHING means there), what the person must give, and the periods that bind them (the 30-day bar of s 83.033(2), the five-year cap of s 83.032(14)).

## What the stable binary does not accept

What follows was met on `l4` 1.6.110 and 1.6.109 while writing this part; the binary governs the skills where they differ.
Each item is sorted loud (an error you see) or silent (a wrong answer with exit code 0), as the Guide asks.

- **Loud: `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` and `BECAUSE`** outside `LEST BREACH ... BECAUSE`.
  "Not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted.
- **Silent: an unbracketed `NOT a AND b`.**

- **Loud: `ELSE IF` at the same indent** is rejected; nest the `IF` under the `ELSE`.
- **Loud: an `#ASSERT` on more than one line.**
