# NOTES: Canada Pension Plan Act, piece cp5 (Part II, Divisions D to G, the Related Provisions and the Amendments Not in Force)

This folder holds the cp5 piece of the L4 encoding of the Canada Pension Plan Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-8).
Its range is lines 2252-2627 and 3138-3328 of `R.S.C.-1985-c.-C-8.txt`: Part II, Division D (payment of benefits: special rules), Division E (the amount payable under the Canada Pension Plan), Division F (reconsiderations and appeals), Division G (general: census information, presumed death, regulations, offences, penalties, inspection), the Related Provisions and the Amendments Not in Force.
Every section from 67 to 90.2 has a row in the coverage table: most are `encoded`, section 89 (regulation-making powers) is `inert` as machinery, and sections 68.1, 74(4), 84 to 86.1 are `repealed or blank in this text`; the Related Provisions are `encoded` where they still decide something for a person and `inert` where they are spent or are commencement machinery; the three Amendments Not in Force are `inert`.

**The text.**
`R.S.C.-1985-c.-C-8.txt` and `C.R.C.-c.-385.txt` are plain-text renderings made by a converter script from the Justice Laws consolidations (the Act's is current to 2026-09-30, last amended 2024-12-17; the Regulations' is current to 2026-09-30, last amended 2026-01-01).
They are not the official text and are not marked up, and nothing in this folder has been checked against the official text.
Every `src:` locator in the `.l4` files is a line of the Act file, or `src:C.R.C.-c.-385:N` for a line of the Regulations file; `python3 -I srcrefs.py check --refs` on the Act file and the `cp5-*.l4` modules checks both kinds.

**Which instrument each rule comes from.**
Every rule named `s N ...` is the Act's.
Where a section of the range leaves a figure or a condition to the regulations, the Regulations (a deposited input) were read and the figure is encoded in the rule for that section, named for the section and citing the regulation: regulation 51 (census conditions, in `s 87`), regulation 64(1) to (3) (the death benefit's time interval, amount and payees, in `s 71(2)`), regulation 65.1 (maintaining the child, in `s 76(1)(d)`), regulation 71 (reinstatement request, in `s 70.1(2)`), regulations 74.1 and 74.2 (reconsideration request, notice and longer period, in `s 81(1)`).
Piece cr2 encodes those regulations themselves; these rules only read their figures so that the seat can answer, and a change to one of those regulations would change the answers.

**Dates as data.**
No date of enactment, assent or commencement of the Act is in this range, and the range holds no commencement rule of its own.
The dates the text fixes inside rules are carried as named values with an `@ref`, except where noted: 1 January 1987 (s 67(1) and (2)), January 1987 (s 67(2)(g)), January 1995 (s 67(3)(a)), 1 January 2012 (s 67(3.1)), 1 January 2019 (s 70.01, the first month in which a disability can begin), 31 December 2011 (s 76.1(c)(i), the day after which the period of earnings must begin); and 1 January 1998 and 1 January 2019, which s 71(2)(c) with regulation 64(1) uses as literals inside the rule `s 71(2)(c) —` and in `s 73(2) —` (1 January 1998).
Where the text refers to the day an amending provision came into force and does not give it (s 73(2); 2004, c. 22, s. 23; 2007, c. 11, s. 36), the day is a stated input and the seat asks for it.

**Method.**
Layer 1 is the modules `cp5-d-*.l4` (Division D), `cp5-e-amount.l4`, `cp5-f-appeals.l4`, `cp5-g-general.l4` and `cp5-related-provisions.l4`, with the month helpers in `cp5-months.l4`; their tests are `cp5-tests-*.l4`.
Layer 2 is `cp5-seat.l4` (91 questions) and `cp5-cases-seat.l4` (the worked cases).
A month is the date of its first day; a benefit "payable for each month commencing with" a month is a rule that returns that month, or `NOTHING` where the section does not decide.
Every expected value in the tests was worked out by hand from the text before the code was run; two errors were found by running the tests, and both were in the test code and not in a rule: in `cp5-tests-d-retirement.l4` paragraph (d) of subsection 67(2) was tested with its branches reversed (corrected to "no cessation, or cessation on or after the day 65 is reached"), and in `cp5-tests-d-death-survivor.l4` a scenario for section 72(a) used the wrong year of death (corrected to the 1990 death).

## Seat

The seat is **a person who may claim from the Canada Pension Plan or be subject to the Minister's powers under it**: a retiree, a person with a disability, a survivor, a child or orphan (through whoever applies for them), or a person the Minister may penalise or inspect.
For this range (Part II Divisions D to G) the questions are: from which month a benefit is paid and until which month; what the person is owed (written notice, reasons, back payment, the minimum on reinstatement); what can be done to them (a stay of payment, an inspection, a demand for documents, a penalty, a prosecution); and by when they must act (90 days to ask for reconsideration, 12 months to ask for reinstatement, 15 days to ask for review of an authorization).
Why this seat: the individual to whom the law is applied is the beneficiary or the person subject to the Minister's powers; the officials (the Minister, the judge, the Social Security Tribunal, Statistics Canada) have no seat, and their duties appear as what the person is owed and their powers as what can be done to the person.
An estate, which section 81(1)(a) and section 71 mention, is not an individual: `cp5 Application for a retirement pension` carries `the applicant is an estate`, which the seat fixes at FALSE.
Every top-level name the piece defines is specific to what it decides (`s 67(1) — the month a retirement pension that commences before 1987 is first payable`, never `the month`), and the seat questions carry the section in their names; this run could not read other pieces' modules, so their names were not compared with these.
Plain inputs: a yes/no and a day stand for an optional day (the helper `cp5 seat helper -- a day that may not apply`); a date of birth is asked and never an age; a month is asked as any day in it; where the text leaves a judgment to the Minister or a judge, the question asks whether it has been made.
The seat never restates a rule: every question builds the long modules' inputs in its `WHERE` and calls their rule.

## What the text turns out to say

Numbered; "Asserted." means a test or case in this folder pins it, so a change of mind shows up as a failing assertion.

1. **The rendering of subsection 67(2) has no end date, so it overlaps subsection 67(3.1).** Subsection (2) as rendered applies "on or after January 1, 1987" with nothing after it, and (3.1) applies to a pension that commences on or after 1 January 2012. A reader of the text alone gets two answers for a post-2011 pension. The combined rule resolves it with an order of trial (F1); an application made before 2012 whose (3.1) month is before 2012 but whose (2) month is after 2011 gets (2)'s month (F2); a case that neither (1) nor (2) fits gives NOTHING. Asserted.
2. **Section 2(2) can defeat "had reached 35".** A survivor born 15 March 1980 whose contributor died on 20 March 2015 turned 35 five days before the death, but section 2(2) treats a person as reaching an age in the month after the birthday's month, so the survivor is treated as reaching 35 on 1 April 2015 and has not "reached 35" at the death. The pension then starts from the later month (a death on 1 April 2015 gives May 2015). Asserted.
3. **Two different months for the same 65th birthday.** Section 70 is excepted from section 2(2), so under s 70(1)(c) and (2) a person born 31 January 1960 reaches 65 in January 2025; under s 70.02(b) (not excepted) the same person reaches 65 in February 2025. Asserted.
4. **Section 90.2(11) cites paragraphs that do not exist.** It refers to "paragraphs (8)(a) to (d)" but subsection (8) has only paragraphs (a) and (b). The rule applies (a) and (b) (F26). Asserted.
5. **The knowledge element in section 90(1)(a) attaches grammatically to the first limb only.** "Knowingly makes a false or misleading statement ... or makes any application or declaration that by reason of any non-disclosure of facts is false or misleading, or obtains any benefit payment by false pretences": the second and third limbs have no "knowingly" (F16), while section 90.1(1) requires that the person knew. Asserted.
6. **Sections 69(b) and 70.01(b) rewrite another paragraph's number.** They say paragraph 42(2)(b) is to be read as if "fifteen months" were "twelve months" for a person who applies for a disability pension (or a post-retirement disability benefit). The rules return 12 (or NOTHING if the rewriting does not apply) for cp3's rule to take. Asserted.
7. **Regulation 64 sets the figures that the Act leaves open in section 71(2).** The time interval is 60 days after the death; the prescribed amount is $2,387 for a death from 1 January 1998 through 31 December 2018, and for an earlier death a fraction (two thirds of 10%) of the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings; no more than the actual funeral expenses may be paid to the individual or institution that paid them (regulation 64(2)), and the remainder goes down the order as if there were no such person (regulation 64(3)). These are in the Regulations and in no section of the range; a change to the regulation changes the answers. (the boundaries, 31 December 1997 and 1 January 1998, and 31 December 2018, are tested). Asserted.
8. **Subsection 73(2) cannot be answered from the text alone.** It speaks of a death before "the coming into force of this subsection", a date the Act does not give; it is a stated input and the seat asks for it. (a death on 5 May 2000 with the subsection in force on 31 July 2000). Asserted.
9. **The Act's text carries no time for an appeal.** Section 82 gives the right to appeal to the Social Security Tribunal and says nothing about when; the time is in the Department of Employment and Social Development Act, which is not among the texts read. Section 81(2) says the Minister must reconsider "without delay", a number of days is nowhere given, and none is encoded.
10. **Section 90(1) states no fine or term, and subsection 90.2(13) states no penalty.** The penalty is whatever the general summary-conviction penalty is, outside the texts read. Section 90(2) runs five years from the Minister's awareness; section 103(1) (Part III, cp6) runs five years from the time the subject-matter arose; the two clocks are different and only the first is encoded here.
11. **The five-year bar and the last day for proceedings count differently.** Proceedings may be started "within, but not later than, five years after" (s 90(2)), so the fifth anniversary is still in time; a penalty may not be imposed once "five years have passed" (s 90.1(3)(b)), which the rule reads as the fifth anniversary (F17) (the cases of 10 March 2025 and 9 March 2025). Asserted.
12. **The reconsideration clock starts only on notice in the prescribed manner.** A decision that was never notified in writing by the Minister never starts the ninety days (F15). Asserted.
13. **Subsections (3) and (3.1) of section 67 agree for an applicant already 65.** For a person who has reached 65 when applying and whose pension begins from 2012 on, both give the eleventh month before the application month, not earlier than the month 65 was reached. (born 15 June 1950, applied 10 March 2016: July 2015 by both). Asserted.
14. **No enactment or commencement date for the Act appears in this piece's range.** The range holds no commencement provision; the only dates of coming into force are those of amending Acts in the Related Provisions (and are inputs where a rule depends on them).
15. **The Related Provisions 2012 c. 19 ss 251 to 262 are spent by their own dates** (1 April 2013, 31 March 2014, 1 April 2014); the 2016, 2018, 2019, 2022 and 2024 Related Provisions that only disapply subsection 114(2) are commencement machinery. Only 2004 c. 22 s 23 and 2007 c. 11 s 36 still decide something for a person.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Everything here was met on the stable binary 1.6.98 while writing this piece.
Each lesson says what it cost, and "loud" or "silent" says whether the binary told me.

**Loud (an error, so the cost is time).**
- A definition head must repeat the GIVEN names exactly: `The names in a type signature must match those in the definition`. I use backticked names in both.
- `WITH` builds a record from a type name only; there is no record update on a value. A record with one field changed must be rebuilt in full.
- A nested `CONSIDER` inside a `LIST` item, a `CONSIDER` whose scrutinee is a multi-line `(LIST`, and a parenthesised argument that starts on a continuation line each fail with `unexpected (`. I use `WHERE` locals.
- `length` is not defined.
- `minimum` and `maximum` have two prelude instances (NUMBER and MAYBE NUMBER) and do not take DATEs. I wrote my own folds (`cp5 helper -- the latest of the months that apply`).

**Worked.**
- A function-typed GIVEN works, and an `@export` that supplies a lambda to a rule with a function-typed input checks ("Check succeeded"); the recomputation of the total pensionable earnings in sections 78 to 79.2 is a function the caller supplies.

**Easy to get wrong (no error).**
- `add months d (0 MINUS n)` goes back n months; there is no negative literal. A date is a serial NUMBER (`Day d`), so subtracting two dates gives days.
- `@export` on a rule returning `MAYBE DATE` works; a date prints as `DATE OF d, m, y`.
- `mapMaybe`, `any`, `map` and `sum` work.

## Before this is worth relying on

**This encoding has not been reviewed by a lawyer, and has not been checked against the official text.**
The `.txt` files are an unofficial converter rendering of the Justice Laws consolidations; the consolidation is current to 2026-09-30 and later amendments may exist; the only amendments the rendering shows as pending are 2026, c. 22, ss. 41 to 43.

1. **The Regulations' figures can change without the Act changing.** The 60 days, $2,387 and funeral-expense cap of regulation 64, the 12 months of regulation 71, the forms of regulations 74.1 and 74.2 and the conditions of regulation 51 are read from the Regulations as deposited (last amended 2026-01-01). Piece cr2 encodes those regulations themselves; the two should be reconciled.
2. **Three dates are not in the text.** The day subsection 73(2) came into force, the day section 20 of 2004, c. 22 came into force, and the day section 2 of 2007, c. 11 came into force are stated inputs. A person relying on s 73(2), s 70.1 or the 25-year test must supply them from the amending Acts.
3. **Judgments are left as inputs.** Whether the Minister is satisfied (s 70.1(3)(a), s 70.1(4), s 71(2)(a), s 88(1), s 90.1(1), s 90.1(4)), whether payment is approved (s 60), whether a disability is severe and prolonged, and what a judge is satisfied of (s 90.2) are asked, never decided.
4. **The appeal time is missing.** The Department of Employment and Social Development Act and the Federal Courts Act were not read, so the periods for an appeal to the Tribunal, for leave and for judicial review are not encoded and are asked for in s 83.
5. **The forks are assumptions.** F1 to F27 above are mine; none has been confirmed by the drafters, the Minister or a court. F1, F2, F3 and F19 matter most for the retirement and survivor's pensions.
6. **Quebec.** The Quebec Pension Plan is outside this Act; where a rule mentions a provincial plan the person's facts about it are asked and not derived.
7. **Hand-written expected values.** Every expected value was worked out by hand from the text, but by one person (a model) with no second reader; an independent test pass by someone who has not read these rules is still owed.
8. **Amendments not in force** (2026, c. 22, ss. 41 to 43) are not merged. When they come into force, Schedule 1 and section 113.1(11.07) to (11.09) change; neither is in this piece's rules.
