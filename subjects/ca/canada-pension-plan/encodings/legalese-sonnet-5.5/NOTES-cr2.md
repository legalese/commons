**NOTES: the Canada Pension Plan Regulations, Part V (piece cr2): regulations 37 to 78.4**

This folder holds the L4 encoding of Part V (pensions and supplementary benefits) of the Canada Pension Plan Regulations, C.R.C., c. 385, lines 417 to 821 of `C.R.C.-c.-385.txt`, together with one thin seat layer over it.
It is piece **cr2** of the nine pieces that together encode the Canada Pension Plan Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-8) and the Regulations; the lead merges the pieces afterwards.
The text read is an unofficial plain-text rendering made by a converter script from the Justice Laws consolidation (current to 2026-09-30, last amended 2026-01-01).
It is not the official text, and nothing here has been checked against the official text or claims to be.
Every rule here comes from the Regulations (the instrument), not from the Act; where a regulation is made "for the purposes of" a provision of the Act, that provision is outside this piece and is taken as a plain input (see the cross-reference table).

**Target and tooling.** The target dialect is `main`, written and run with the stable build `l4` 1.6.98 (`1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`).
Rule-level `GIVEN` throughout; no `REFUSE`, no section-level `GIVEN`, no `TYPICALLY`; `NOT` is always parenthesised; "not covered" is `NOTHING` or an explicit constructor and is asserted.
The keel's modules `cpp-core*.l4` are imported unchanged; nothing in them is redefined here.

**Dates.** Part V contains no date of enactment or making and no commencement rule, so no commencement date is carried as a value.
The dates that do appear (28 May 1975 in regulation 45(1); 4 June 1986 in regulation 54; the bands of years in regulations 41, 64, 78.3 and 78.4) are conditions or figures of the text, encoded as such.

**Files.** Layer 1: `cr2-nouns.l4` (declarations only) and one module per group of regulations; tests `cr2-tests-*.l4` (hand-written expected values); layer 2: `cr2-seat.l4` and `cr2-cases-seat.l4`.
The `.l4` files were generated from templates, so that every quotation is copied from the text by `srcrefs.py quote` and none is typed by hand.

**Conventions.** A rule is named `reg N(x) — what it decides`, with an em dash.
A fact the piece does not decide (another section's outcome) is a plain input, named for the fact, with a comment `-- out of range: section N (piece X)`.
A duty that the text puts on a person is a BOOLEAN rule saying that the duty arises, and a second BOOLEAN rule saying whether a thing meets the form the duty requires; none is a `DEONTIC` value, because a deontic cannot be asserted and nothing in Part V turns on a trace (each such choice is noted in the fork register).
A judgment the text hands to the Minister ("if the Minister is satisfied that ...") is a BOOLEAN input named for it, never guessed.

## Seat

**The seat is the individual who contributes to, or may claim from, the Canada Pension Plan:** the applicant for or recipient of a retirement pension, a disability pension or a post-retirement benefit; the survivor, the child and the orphan of a contributor; the former spouse or common-law partner whose pensionable earnings are divided or whose pension is assigned; and the personal representative of a deceased contributor or beneficiary.
Part V of the Regulations is almost wholly about what that person must do or show to be paid (apply, withdraw, prove age and identity, furnish information and evidence, file an attestation, report a return to work), what may be done to their benefit (deducted, withheld, cancelled, reinstated, adjusted), and the arithmetic of the pension (indexation, rounding, early and late adjustment).
Whether and how much to contribute is the business of Parts I to IV of the Regulations and the Act (other pieces); the contribution rules in Part V appear only where regulation 41 turns a provincial plan's contributions into salary and wages.

**Questions.** `cr2-seat.l4` holds 130 questions, each marked `@export`, in the text's order under `§§` headings; every input has an `@desc` worded as the person would be asked ("Did you submit the request to the Minister in writing?").
Each question calls the layer-1 rule that already encodes the provision; no rule is restated, and no number or date of the law appears in the seat.
Where a layer-1 rule takes an optional date (the date of a confirmation on appeal, of a previous application), the seat asks a yes/no question and a date, and builds the `JUST` or `NOTHING` inside the question; the date is ignored when the answer is no.
Where a layer-1 rule takes a list of items (regulations 46, 52, 53, 54, 54.1, 68, 72, 74.1), the seat asks which of them apply and which the person has already given, and the question returns what is still lacking.
Rates are fractions except in regulation 41, which is asked in per cent as the text computes with it (fork F-41).
A power of the Minister is asked as "Can the Minister ...?" or "May ...?" and a judgment the text hands to the Minister is asked as a yes/no fact about what the Minister has found, never guessed.
A duty on the person is asked as "Must I ...?" and paired, where the text also puts a liability on them, with "Can the Minister ...?" (regulations 42, 59, 68(2), 69, 70 and 76).

**Cases.** `cr2-cases-seat.l4` has one or more cases for every one of the 130 questions, with expected values worked out by hand before the rules were run, boundaries on both sides, and 6 asserted `NOTHING` cases, where the text gives no answer: regulation 41 for a second additional contribution before 2024; 78.3 for a pension payable before 2011; 78.4 for a pension payable in the month of reaching 65; regulation 75 given eleven figures; regulation 76(2) for an excess payment of exactly $50; and regulation 76(2) where a deduction has already been authorized.

**Provisions with no seat question** (each is a layer-1 rule that the seat does not ask directly, with the reason):
- Regulation 58(2), 68(3)(b) and (c), 69(3)(b) and (c): who bears the cost in the Consolidated Revenue Fund or the Canada Pension Plan Account is accounting between the Crown and the Account; the individual is paid the reasonable cost under 68(3)(a) and 69(3)(a), which are asked.
- The helper rules that a question composes rather than asks: the pieces of 62(2) and (3) and of 75(2) and 68.1(2) (rounding), 76(1), (3), (4), (6) and 76.1(2) (called through the 76(2) and 76.1(1) questions), 78(1)(a) and (b) (called through 78(1)), 78.1(1)(a) to (c) and (2) (called through 78.1(1) and the continuous-year question), and the reach-65 helpers of 78.3 and 78.4.
- The part of the text that is only a title, a heading or a repealed provision (see the coverage table).

Hohfeld's correlatives: a claim (right) is the other party's duty; a privilege (liberty) is the other party's no-right; a power is the other party's liability (being subject to the power); an immunity is the other party's disability.

## What the text turns out to say

1. Regulation 64(1) tests the size of the death benefit only for a death before 1 January 2019: the text gives two thirds of 10% of the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings for a death before 1 January 1998 and $2,387 for a death between 1 January 1998 and 31 December 2018, and no figure after that. For a later death only the two other grounds (paragraph 71(2)(a) of the Act applies; the estate has not applied within 60 days) can open a direction. Asserted.
2. In regulation 64(2) and (3) the funeral payer is paid no more than the actual funeral expenses, and the text does not say that the payment cannot exceed the death benefit itself; the rule takes the smaller of the two, because paragraph (3) speaks of "the remainder of the death benefit" and so presupposes a payment within it. Asserted.
3. Regulation 43(1.1)(b) gives two different clocks for the same request to treat a denied disability application as a retirement application: 90 days "following the month in which the applicant is notified of the denial" (so a denial notified on any day in March 2026 leaves until 29 June 2026), but 90 days "after the day on which the applicant is notified of the confirmation" when the denial is confirmed on appeal (notified on 10 January 2027, until 10 April 2027). Asserted.
4. Regulation 45(1) lets an applicant withdraw only an application "made after May 28, 1975": an application made on 28 May 1975 itself is outside it, and the text says nothing about it. Asserted.
5. Regulation 41 gives no figure for a first additional or base contribution for a year before 2019, nor for a second additional contribution for a year before 2024; for those years the rule returns not covered. Asserted.
6. Regulation 62 builds the new basic monthly amount from the ratio "expressed as a decimal fraction" to three places (62(3)), so a Pension Index ratio of 1.0235 is used as 1.024 and 725.37 becomes 742.78; used unrounded it would be 742.42, 36 cents different (fork F-62). Asserted.
7. Regulations 78.3 and 78.4 give the monthly rate B only for a month after 31 December 2010; for a pension that becomes payable earlier the text gives no rate and the rule returns not covered. Asserted.
8. A retirement pension that becomes payable in the very month in which the contributor reaches 65 is in neither regulation 78.3 ("before the month in which the contributor reaches 65") nor 78.4 ("after"); this piece returns not covered, and whatever factor applies in that month would have to come from section 46(3.1) of the Act (piece cp3). Asserted.
9. Regulation 75(1) divides the aggregate of twelve monthly Consumer Price Index figures by 12; given eleven, thirteen or no figures the rule returns not covered, not an average of what was given. Asserted.
10. Regulation 76(4)(d) permits a deduction and payment to a provincial or municipal authority only if the excess payment "is greater than $50": an excess payment of exactly $50 does not qualify, and the rule returns not covered. Asserted.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Loud (the build rejects them or the check shows an error):
- `GIVEN` names must be exactly the names used in the rule head; a name that differs by a word is an unknown identifier at the call, not a silent new parameter.
- A backslash-apostrophe inside backticks is not accepted; an ordinary apostrophe inside backticks (`the Minister's attention`) is.
- An operator must sit on the same line as its left operand, or the pieces must be named in a `WHERE` helper (the brief's rule, kept to throughout; I did not build a case to show how it fails).
- `@export` and `@desc` sit above `GIVEN`; the `@desc` of an input sits on that input's line.
- `YMD` from the daydate library is not available in the stable build; the keel (`cpp-core`) shims it and this piece uses the shim.

Silent (exit code 0, a different answer): **`NOT a AND b` reads as `NOT (a AND b)`.** I wrote `NOT x EQUALS y AND z` in `cr2-maintenance-and-school.l4` and the tests caught it (the hand-written expectation differed); every `NOT` in the `cr2-*.l4` files is now written `(NOT ...)` or `NOT (...)` (a grep for a `NOT` with no parenthesis beside it, outside comments, finds none). This is the one trap here that returns a wrong answer without saying so, which is why every rule has cases on both sides of its boundary.

## Before this is worth relying on

1. **Compare against the official text.** The text read is an unofficial converter rendering of the Justice Laws consolidation; every figure (the rates in regulations 78.3 and 78.4, the $2, $50 and $2,387 thresholds, the 90-day, 60-day and 12-month periods) should be read against the official consolidation before anyone relies on it.
2. **Rule on the forks.** Every fork in the register is assumed, not ruled; the ones with a money consequence are F-62 (36 cents on a benefit), F-41 (the unit of the rate), F-78 (the month of reaching 65), F-59 (the first day of a withholding) and F-46.2 (the last day of a request).
3. **Wire the cross-references.** Every input marked `-- out of range: section N (piece X)` has to be supplied by the piece that owns the section when the lead merges the pieces (see the cross-reference table): in particular the 36-month minimum and the joint contributory period of regulations 78 and 78.2 (pieces cp3 and cp4), the benefit amounts of regulations 56, 64, 65.1 and 76.1, and the definitions of regulation 37 that the Act also uses.
4. **The Minister's judgments are inputs.** Whether the Minister is satisfied, deems an examination necessary, or approves a payee is a yes/no the person states; nothing here decides it, and an answer of "yes" that the Minister has not in fact given makes the answer wrong.
5. **No part of this was tested against real cases.** The cases are hand-worked from the text. They show that the rules say what the author read the text to say; they do not show that the Minister or the Tribunal would agree.
6. **The Act is not here.** The Regulations are made "for the purposes of" provisions of the Act that are encoded by other pieces; a rule here that looks incomplete (the division, the assignment, the disability test, the amount of a pension) is incomplete on purpose, and only whole when merged with them.
