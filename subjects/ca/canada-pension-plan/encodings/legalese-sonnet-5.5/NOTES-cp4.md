# NOTES: piece cp4 of the Canada Pension Plan encoding (Part II, the rest of Division B, and Division C)

This piece encodes lines 1570-2251 of `R.S.C.-1985-c.-C-8.txt` (the Canada Pension Plan Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-8): the tail of section 55.2 (from its subsection (5.2)), sections 55.3, 56, 57, 58, 59, 59.1, 59.2 (the rest of Part II, Division B, "Calculation of Benefits"), and sections 60 to 66.1 (Division C, "Payment of Benefits: General Provisions").
Section 55.2 straddles two pieces: its head and subsections (2) to (5.1) are in cp3's lines (921-1569), and subsections (5.2) to (11) are here.
Every rule comes from the Act alone, with four exceptions where the Act leaves a figure to the Regulations (C.R.C., c. 385): the rule uses the regulation's figure, cites it by the locator `src:C.R.C.-c.-385:N`, and says "reg N" in a comment.
Those are regulation 42 (section 66(2.1)), regulation 46.2 (section 66.1), regulation 76.1 (section 65(3)) and regulation 78.2 (section 65.1(8)); every other thing the Act leaves to regulations is a plain input and belongs to piece cr2.

The text is a plain-text rendering of the Justice Laws consolidation made by a converter script (the Act file says it is current to 2026-09-30 and last amended 2024-12-17).
It is an unofficial converter rendering, not the official text, and nothing in this folder has been checked against the official text.
Every `src:N` locator is a line of `R.S.C.-1985-c.-C-8.txt`; a locator of the form `src:C.R.C.-c.-385:N` is a line of the Regulations file.

Layer 1 is the fifteen modules `cp4-*.l4` (nouns, Pension Index, calendar and incapacity helpers, and one module per group of sections) with eleven test files `cp4-tests-*.l4`.
Layer 2 is `cp4-seat.l4` (102 questions) and `cp4-cases-seat.l4` (176 worked cases).
The keel's `cpp-core*.l4` files sit in the same folder, unchanged.

## Seat

The seat is the individual to whom the Act is applied: the contributor, the person with a disability, the survivor, the child or orphan (or the person who looks after one), the spouse or common-law partner in a division or an assignment of a pension, and the beneficiary who has been overpaid or whose benefit someone is trying to take.
Every question is phrased as that person asks it ("How much is my monthly disability pension?", "Must I be at the place and time the Minister requires?", "How much of a benefit payment must I return at once?"), and every fact it asks is one that person could state.
The seat is recorded here as the ruling of 2026-10-18 requires; there is no other seat in this piece.

The Minister, the Governor in Council and the courts get no seat.
A provision that addresses only an official is encoded in layer 1 for what it decides, and in layer 2 for what it means to the person: the Minister's duty to consider an application at once and to notify the decision (section 60(7)) is the question "Must the Minister consider my application at once and tell me the decision in writing?", and the Minister's power to certify a debt (section 66(2.2)) is "On what day may the Minister first certify what I owe?".
A judgment the Act hands to the Minister ("if the Minister is satisfied", "in the Minister's opinion", "the Minister may") is a yes-or-no input ("Is the Minister satisfied that ...?") and is never guessed.
The answer to a question that depends on such a judgment is a `MAYBE` value where the Act gives no answer without it: `NOTHING` then means "the Act, as read here, does not decide", and never zero or "no".

No employer seat, and no other party's seat, is written.
No provision in this range puts a duty or a liability on an employer, a landlord or a business: the employer's contributions are in Part I of the Act (pieces cp1 and cp2), outside this range.
A third party who owes money to a debtor of the Crown (section 66(2.7)) is a person in the same seat, and has a question of their own ("Can I be required to pay money I owe to a debtor of the Crown to the Receiver General?").

The other design choices take the defaults of `COMMONS-CLAUDE.md` (ruled 2026-10-18): the Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers brought in; the actors the text names; a judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally; the Act's own definitions worked out from facts and borrowed terms asked as facts; one case as the natural unit (one contributor's benefit, one application, one overpayment); the Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss; the operative sections in scope and the machinery out.
Here "the instruments that set its numbers" are four regulations of the Canada Pension Plan Regulations, C.R.C., c. 385, cited by the locator form `src:C.R.C.-c.-385:N`: regulation 42 (the limit of a deduction), regulation 46.2 (the interval for cancelling a benefit and for repaying it), regulation 76.1 (the terms of a deduction for a disability income program) and regulation 78.2 (the period of cohabitation).
Everything else the Act leaves to regulations (the prescribed manner of an application, the prescribed persons, the months of cohabitation) is a plain input, because those regulations are piece cr2's.

The layer is `cp4-seat.l4`: 102 questions under 15 `§§` groups in the order of the Act, each with `@export` and `@desc`.
Each question takes plain inputs (yes or no, a number, a date, a list of numbers, or a choice from a short list such as which benefit it is), builds the records the modules expect inside the question, and calls the module's rule; no rule is restated.
A group that only arises in a situation the person may not be in says so in its heading ("ask only if you are the survivor of a contributor who has died"), so that a form can first ask whether that has happened to them.
Rights and duties come in pairs (see "Jural relations"): the other party's side is asked as inputs (has the Minister received the documents, did the administrator send a record and the irrevocable written consent), and the paired question is asked where the Act puts a duty or a liability on the person ("How much of a benefit payment must I return at once?" beside "May the Minister remit what I owe?", "How much may be deducted from other amounts payable to me to recover what I owe?" beside "On what day may the Minister first certify what I owe?").
`cp4-cases-seat.l4` holds 176 worked cases: at least one for every question, and an asserted `NOTHING` for every question that returns a `MAYBE` value, except "How much is my monthly post-retirement benefit in all?", which is handed three known portions and so never returns `NOTHING`.

## Jural relations

One row per provision that creates a relation between two parties, or a group of subsections that together create one.
A provision that creates none (a definition, a counting rule, a deeming rule, a condition on a power) is listed once as `none`.
Hohfeld's correlatives: a right (claim) is matched by a duty; a privilege (liberty) by a no-right; a power by a liability (being subject to the power, which is not an obligation); an immunity by a disability.
"Minister" stands for the Minister and the officials who act for the Minister; it is never a seat.

What reading the text closely turned up.

1. The flat rate benefit of a disability pension and the flat rate benefit of a survivor's pension are not the same amount from 1987 on.
   Section 56(2)(b) fixes 1987 at $233.38 times the 1987/1986 Pension Index ratio, so with a ratio of 103/100 it is 240.3814; section 58(1.1)(b) runs 1987 from the 1986 figure of $91.06, so with the same ratio it is 93.7918.
   Each later year multiplies the previous year's amount by that year's ratio, so the two series stay apart (fork F-b).
   The case is in `cp4-tests-survivors-pension.l4`, section 58(1.1) and the finding beside it. Asserted.

2. The portion of a retirement pension assigned under section 65.1(9) can exceed the portion itself.
   Each of the three terms is 50% of the ratio of the months of cohabitation to the months of the joint contributory period of its own kind, but the months of cohabitation are the same for every term, while the first additional and second additional joint contributory periods start in 2019 and 2024.
   With 262 months of cohabitation, 66 months in the first additional period and 6 in the second, the ratios are 262/66 and 262/6, and the first additional portion assigned is about 198 where the portion itself is 100 (fork F-x).
   The case is in `cp4-tests-assignment.l4`, section 65.1(9). Asserted.

3. The day section 63.1 came into force is not in this text.
   Sections 63(1), (2), (4), (8) to (10) and 63.1(1) to (3) turn on "the coming into force of this section" and the file carries no date for it, so every rule that needs it takes it as an input, and the tests use 1 January 1987 as an invented stand-in (fork F-q).
   Not asserted: a case cannot assert a date the text lacks; it asserts only that the rule gives the right answer either side of whatever date is supplied.

4. For a contributor deemed disabled before 1997 the text gives no average monthly pensionable earnings.
   Section 56(3) reads "calculated as provided in subsections (4) and (5)", and (4) covers a contributor deemed disabled after 1997 and (4.1) one deemed disabled in 1997 (fork F-c).
   The base portion is `NOTHING` for an earlier year, not zero and not the section 48 average.
   The case is in `cp4-tests-disability-pension.l4`, section 56(3), and `cp4-cases-seat.l4`. Asserted.

5. A young survivor's reduction under section 58(1)(a) can be more than the whole pension, and the text gives no floor.
   The pension is reduced "by 1/120 for each month by which the age of the survivor ... is less than forty-five years": exactly 120 months of reduction leave 0, and 121 months would leave a negative amount (fork F-s).
   The rule returns `NOTHING` at 121 months and the amount 0 at 120.
   The cases are in `cp4-tests-survivors-pension.l4`, section 58(1)(a); `cp4-cases-seat.l4`. Asserted.

6. The top-up in section 58(2) can be negative.
   "An amount that, when added to the amount of the portion of the survivor's retirement pension ..., is equal to the amount of a benefit of 25% of 1/12 of ..." is less than zero when the survivor's own portion exceeds that benefit, and the lesser of that and C - D is then negative; nothing in the text raises it to zero (fork F-t).

    The cases are in `cp4-tests-overpayment.l4`, section 66(2.2); `cp4-cases-seat.l4`. Asserted.

15. The twelve months to ask for a benefit to be cancelled are counted from the day payment commenced, inclusive, and a commencement on 29 February clamps.
    A request on the same day a year later is in time and one a day later is not; a commencement on 29 February 2020 gives 28 February 2021, because `add months` clamps (fork F-v).
    The repayment time in regulation 46.2(4) is "twelve months following the month in which the request is granted": the end of that twelfth month.
    The cases are in `cp4-tests-overpayment.l4`, section 66.1; `cp4-cases-seat.l4`. Asserted.

16. Within a year of a marriage, the survivor of a contributor who died is not left without a pension once cohabitation adds up to a year.
    Section 63(7.1) adds the cohabitation during the marriage to the conjugal cohabitation just before it; read as 365 days (fork F-p), 200 days in the marriage and 165 days before it make 365 and the bar of section 63(7) falls away.
    The cases are in `cp4-tests-survivor-remarriage.l4`, section 63(7.1); `cp4-cases-seat.l4`. Asserted.

17. Three differing rules sit under the one heading "death benefit".
    Before 2019 the lump sum is the lesser of six times the retirement pension and 10% of the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings, and for a death after 1997 not above $2,500; after 2018 it is $2,500; and after 2024, where no pension was received and no survivor's pension is payable, it is $5,000 in place of subsection (1) (section 57(1.2)).
    The boundary day for the $5,000 is 1 January 2025: a death on 31 December 2024 gets $2,500.
    The cases are in `cp4-tests-death-benefit.l4`, section 57(1) and (1.2); `cp4-cases-seat.l4`. Asserted.

18. The assignment of a pension ends on different clocks for different events.
    Section 65.1(11) names "the month" a spouse dies, the "twelfth month following" the month they separate, the month a divorce is issued, and the "month following" the month the Minister approves a joint request to cancel; the earliest governs.
    A joint request approved in March 2024 ends the assignment in April 2024 while a separation in November 2023 ends it in November 2024.
    The cases are in `cp4-tests-assignment.l4`, section 65.1(10) to (12); `cp4-cases-seat.l4`. Asserted.

19. The earnings threshold for a division differs by tier.
    A year of low earnings bars the division of base earnings (section 55.2(8)(a)) and of first additional earnings (8.1)(a) when the persons' total does not exceed twice the Year's Basic Exemption; for second additional earnings (8.2) there is no earnings threshold at all, only the age and retirement pension limbs.
    The case is in `cp4-tests-division-tail.l4`, section 55.2(8) to (8.2). Asserted.

20. A division cannot create a benefit for the month of the division or before.
    Section 55.2(9) pays the adjusted benefit "effective the month following the month in which the division takes place" and never pays "a benefit that was not payable in the absence of the division ... in respect of the month in which the division takes place or any prior month".
    The cases are in `cp4-tests-division-tail.l4`, section 55.2(9); `cp4-cases-seat.l4`. Asserted.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Sorted by how the failure shows itself.

Loud (an error, nothing runs):

- `f r's foo` parses as `(f r)'s foo`, so a field of a record passed as an argument needs parentheses: `f (r's foo)`.
  The error ("unexpected PLUS", "unexpected (") appears far from the cause.
- A `min` or `max` call whose arguments are themselves long mixfix calls across several lines is rejected; hoist each argument into a `WHERE` helper with a name.
- Positional record construction (`Record OF a, b, c`) broken across lines with leading commas fails with "incorrect indentation ... greater than 33"; use `WITH` and named fields, or keep the `OF` on one line.

Neither loud nor silent:

- `l4 check` or `l4 run` on a file that does not exist hangs instead of reporting an error; every run in this piece is wrapped in a timeout and only names files that exist.

Silent (exit code 0, no diagnostic, a wrong or surprising answer):

- `l4 run` exits 0 when an `#ASSERT` fails; a failure is a `DiagnosticSeverity_Error` line.
  `check.sh` reads the diagnostics and counts them, and the numbers below are its.
- Positional `OF` is order-dependent: swapping two same-typed fields compiles and changes the answer.
  This piece uses positional `OF` only in the fixtures of five test files (for `cp4 Retirement pension portions`, `cp4 Deceased contributor` and `cp4 Earnings record for a disability pension`); the modules and every record in the seat are built with `WITH` and named fields.
- `add months` clamps to the last day of the month: 29 February 2020 plus 12 months is 28 February 2021.
  It is recorded as fork F-v and asserted.

Slow, not wrong: `l4 run cp4-cases-seat.l4` takes about two minutes and the whole `check.sh` about seven.

## Before this is worth relying on

- The text is a plain-text rendering made by a converter script, not the official text, and nothing here has been checked against the official text.
  Every `src:N` locator is a line of that rendering; the file says it is current to 2026-09-30 and last amended 2024-12-17.
- The expected values in the tests and in the seat cases were worked out by hand from the text by the same author as the rules.
  No independent test pass has been made of this piece; a second reader should work a case from the text without looking at the rules.
- The Pension Index, the earnings and the amounts of sections outside this range are inputs.
  A wrong Pension Index series, a wrong Maximum Pensionable Earnings Average or a wrong adjustment ratio gives a wrong amount without any warning.
  A form that uses `cp4-seat.l4` has to fill them from the person's statement or from the other pieces' rules.
- `NOTHING` means "the Act, as read here, does not decide", and never zero or "no".
  A caller that reads `NOTHING` as zero will overstate or understate what a person is owed; the cases where it appears are the findings above and the forks marked NOTHING in the register.
- The forks are readings, and a reviewer should look at each before relying on the rule it governs: above all F-q (the day section 63.1 came into force is a stated input, and the tests use an invented one), F-s (more than 120 months of reduction under section 58(1)(a) gives `NOTHING`, though the likeliest intention is a nil pension), F-t (the top-up of section 58(2) is not floored), F-x (the portion assigned under section 65.1(9) is not capped) and F-b (the two flat rate benefits differ from 1987).
- Only four regulations are used with their figures (42, 46.2, 76.1 and 78.2); every other thing the Act leaves "prescribed" is a plain input and belongs to piece cr2.
  A change to those four regulations is not reflected until the cited lines are re-read.
- Amendments not in force and related provisions are piece cp5's; nothing in this range's rules merges their wording.
- The seat questions have not been shown to a person: the `@desc` wording is the encoder's, not user-tested.
- The 154 rows of the coverage table cover every non-blank line of the range once (674 lines).
  One row is inert machinery (section 55.2(11), a regulation-making power), seven are group headings, and two are repealed (section 64, section 65.1(2)).
