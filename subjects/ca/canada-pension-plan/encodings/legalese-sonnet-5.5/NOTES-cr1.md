# NOTES: the Canada Pension Plan Regulations, piece cr1 (Parts I to IV, Part VIII, Schedules I to IX, Related Provisions)

Encoder: one agent (Claude Sonnet 5.5), working alone from the brief, on the stable `l4` 1.6.98 (target dialect `main`).
Status: draft. No lawyer or domain expert has read these modules against the text.

**What this folder is.** Piece cr1 of a nine-piece encoding of the Canada Pension Plan Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-8) and the Canada Pension Plan Regulations (C.R.C., c. 385).
This piece encodes lines 1-416 and 822-1162 of `C.R.C.-c.-385.txt`: the Regulations' title and interpretation (reg 1 to 3), Part I (regs 3 to 8.3, collection and payment of contributions), Part II (regs 9 to 14, information returns), Part III (regs 15 to 34.1, employment included in or excepted from pensionable employment), Part IV (regs 35 and 36, prescribed interest and refunds), Part VIII (regs 79 to 88), Schedules I to IX and the two Related Provisions.
It reads only the Regulations file; every rule here comes from the Regulations, and the Act is met only as a cross-reference (see "Cross-references out of my ranges").

**The text is not the official text.**
`C.R.C.-c.-385.txt` is a plain-text rendering made by a converter script from the Justice Laws consolidation (current to 2026-09-30, last amended 2026-01-01).
It is not the official text and nothing here has been verified against the official text.
The converter drops historical notes, footnotes and the French version; it joins table cells with ` | `.
Two facts about the file that the brief did not mention:
(1) it has no `AMENDMENTS NOT IN FORCE` section (the file ends at line 1162 with `RELATED PROVISIONS`), so no row below is an amendment not in force;
(2) Part V (regs 37 to 78.4) is followed directly by Part VIII (line 822): the file shows no Part VI or Part VII.

**Two layers.**
Layer 1 is the law as the text states it: modules `cr1-interpretation`, `cr1-part1-*`, `cr1-part2-returns`, `cr1-part3-*`, `cr1-part4-interest`, `cr1-part8-*`, `cr1-schedules`, `cr1-related-provisions`, each rule carrying an `@ref` with `src:` locators into the Regulations file and the quoted lines above it.
Layer 2 is `cr1-seat.l4` (the individual's questions) and `cr1-cases-seat.l4` (worked cases); it calls layer-1 rules and restates none.
Tests of layer 1 are the `cr1-tests-*.l4` files.

**Which instrument each rule comes from.**
Every rule in this folder comes from the Regulations (`C.R.C., c. 385`).
Where a Regulation leaves a figure to the Act (the contribution rates, the basic exemption, the maximum pensionable earnings) the figure is a plain input named as the Act's defined term, with a comment `-- out of range: section N (piece X)`.
Where a Regulation leaves a figure to another instrument that is not deposited (the Income Tax Regulations, the provincial plans' rates, the Interpretation Act's list of holidays, the Bank of Canada's weekly rate, Treasury Bill yields) the figure is a plain input and says so.

**Names.**
Every top-level name is specific.
Layer-1 rules are `reg N(x) — ...`, `Schedule III` to `Schedule IX` (and their `... — ...` rules and `Schedule N, item M` values), `Related provision ...`; types and helpers are `cr1 ...`.
The Schedules keep the Regulations' Roman numerals; the Act's own Schedules are numbered 1 and 2 (lines 3101 and 3123 of the Act file), so no name clashes with a piece of the Act.
Seat questions are plain-English questions (for example `Is this craft a ship`), and the one seat helper is `cr1 seat: a day if it has happened`.

## Seat

The seat is the individual to whom the Regulations are applied, in the roles the text gives them: a worker (an employee), a self-employed person, a person who runs a business and has employees (where the Regulations speak of "the employer", the seat's questions are put in that person's voice as well), a legal representative of a person who has died, a debtor, and a person who is owed interest.
Decisions are phrased as that person would ask them ("How much will my employer deduct from this pay?", "Is my employment outside Canada pensionable employment?", "By what day must I file the form?"), and every input is a fact that person could state.
Where a provision addresses only an official, no question is asked of the individual: regulation 79 (the Minister's power to make agreements) and regulation 85 (when and how the Minister calculates the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings) stay in layer 1 only; no seat question is asked of them.
The brief's seat sentence also names the retirement, disability, survivor's and children's benefits; none of them is in this piece's ranges (they belong to the Act's Part II, pieces cp3 to cp5, and the Regulations' Part V, piece cr2).
This piece's questions are the contribution, pensionable-employment, return, interest, election and Social Insurance Number questions.

**What the seat layer is.**
`cr1-seat.l4` has 113 questions (every one `@export`ed with a one-sentence description and an `@desc` on every input), grouped under `§§` headings in the text's order: A regs 3 to 7, B regs 8 to 8.3, C regs 9 to 14, D regs 15 to 34.1, E regs 35 and 36, F the elections and agreements (regs 82 to 86), G interest on a debt (reg 87), H the Social Insurance Number (reg 88), I the Related Provisions.
`cr1-cases-seat.l4` has 215 assertions: at least one case for every question, in a real person's facts, and cases the Regulations do not decide, asserted `EQUALS NOTHING`.
Each question takes plain inputs (yes/no, a number, a date, a string, or one of the layer-1 enumerations), builds the layer-1 records in its `WHERE` from those answers, and calls the same layer-1 rule; no rule is restated.
A record field the called rule never reads is given a fixed placeholder, with a comment saying so.
A fact that may be absent (a day an event happened) is asked as a yes/no and a date, and passed on through `cr1 seat: a day if it has happened`.

**Hohfeld pairs.**
Where the text gives the individual a claim and gives someone else the correlative duty, both sides are asked: what my employer must remit and whether it did (reg 8), what copies I must be given and what my employer owes if it does not give them (regs 13 and 14(2)), the interest I am paid on a refund and the interest I owe on a debt (regs 35, 36(2)(b), 36(2)(a), 87), the election I may make and its deadline (regs 82 to 84).
The other party's side is an input, never decided: the Minister's satisfaction, approval and signifying (regs 16(1)(f), 16(1)(g), 20, 22(2) and (3)) are asked as facts about the Minister's decision.

**Assumed, not ruled (each is a choice that can be changed alone).**
(1) Two pay-deduction questions are offered: the full one (regs 4 to 6, 20 inputs) and a short one for the first pay of a year with no provincial plan (reg 5 alone, 9 inputs).
(2) The Act's figures (the three contribution rates, the basic exemption, the maximum and additional maximum pensionable earnings) are outside this piece (sections 11.1, 11.2, 17 to 20 of the Act, piece cp1): the questions ask for them as published for the year, worded as the fact, with no figure stated; a merged seat should take them from cp1's rules instead.
(3) Regulation 8(1.1)(b) counts days that are not Saturdays or holidays, and regulations 8(1.12), 8(1.13) and 8.2 turn on an employer's history of remitting; they are the employer's duties to the Receiver General, not a claim the worker holds, so the seat asks only regulation 8(1) (monthly), 8(2) (ceasing) and the questions about what the worker is owed or can check; the rest stay in layer 1, with their `LIST OF DATE` holiday input.
(4) Regulation 36(2) is asked as the one published percentage the rule takes the mean of (the average equivalent yield of three-month Treasury Bills sold at auction in the first month of the preceding quarter); the rule rounds it up itself.
(5) Regulation 87(1)'s "due date" takes a list of scheduled payment days; the seat asks for one scheduled day at a time.
(6) The Schedule questions take the heading and paragraph as the Schedule writes them (for example "Province of Ontario"); a heading the Schedule does not contain answers no.
(7) Regulation 16(1) is one question with 30 inputs (3 about the job, 17 about the person, whether they have a spouse or partner, and 9 about the partner); the partner's fields that paragraphs (c), (d) and 250(1)(b) never read are placeholders.

## Needs from the core

1. **A date-pair helper module.** `cr1-helpers.l4` defines four small date helpers (`cr1 helper: the later of two dates`, `cr1 helper: the earlier of two optional dates`, `cr1 helper: the later of two optional dates`, and the earlier/later of two dates) that many pieces will want; in a merged folder they should be one core rule, not one per piece.
2. **A reusable "includes" convention.** The Regulations define a term with "includes" in several places (employer operating in Canada, placement or employment agency, and others). This piece answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` with `NOTHING` outside the listed cases (fork F1). If the core adopts a convention (a type, or a helper), these rules should follow it.
3. **The `Province` record.** The core's `Province` has a `name of the province` string and a `a province providing a comprehensive pension plan` flag, so Schedule III (a list of provinces by heading) is read by the heading string, not by a `Province` value. A merged core could give `Province` a stable code.
4. **A shared "Part of the Regulations" type.** `cr1 Part of the Regulations` (regs 2, 9, 15) names Parts I to V and VIII; cr2 will need Part V and perhaps the same type. The core should own it.
5. **A prescribed-form rule.** `s 2(1) — prescribed` is used for every "prescribed form" in the Regulations (fork F8); a core rule taking just the Minister's authorization as a BOOLEAN would remove the two `FALSE FALSE` arguments.
6. **Calendar.** `the date a time limit ends`, `a date is within a time limit after a start date` and the month helpers are used throughout and work as they are.

## What the text turns out to say

Each item is what the text says (line numbers are those of `C.R.C.-c.-385.txt`), not a claim about the official text or about the external Acts it names.
"Asserted." ends an item where a case in a tests file or in `cr1-cases-seat.l4` asserts it.

1. **Part VIII never says who "the Minister" is.** Regulation 2(1) defines `Minister` for Parts I to IV (the Minister of National Revenue) and for Part V (the Minister of Employment and Social Development); Part VIII uses "the Minister" (regs 82 to 84, 87) with no definition, so `reg 2(1) — the Minister in a Part of the Regulations` answers NOTHING for Part VIII (fork F2). Asserted.
2. **Regulation 17(2) omits section 21.** Section 17(2) excepts international-transportation employment unless "included by section 18, 19, 20 or 22"; section 21 (employment on a motor vehicle) also includes such employment, and 17(1) names sections 18 to 21. The rule treats 21 as an inclusion too (fork F12). Asserted.
3. **A ship under an agreement with the crew entered into outside Canada is in neither regulation 18(1) nor 18(2).** Section 18(1) needs an agreement entered into in Canada; section 18(2) is for ships not under an agreement; the case in between is not decided. Asserted.
4. **Schedule VIII, Australia: "(a) is not a citizen of Canada; or (b) is not permanently resident in Canada" is met by anyone who lacks either status.** Only a person who is both a citizen and permanently resident stays included under regulation 26. Asserted.
5. **Regulations 28(2)(d) and 28(5) overlap between 34 and 35 hours.** Paragraph (2)(d)(ii) says "less than 35 hours" and (5) says "exceeds 34 hours"; (1) is "subject to" (5), which prevails (fork F14). Asserted.
6. **Regulation 33 follows from regulation 7.** The employer's contribution equals the employee's contribution required to be deducted, which is nil for an elected employment, so "for greater certainty" restates what regulation 7 already gives. Asserted.
7. **Regulation 83.2(a) and (b) end on the same day.** Both say "within one year from June 15 in the year following the year for which the return is filed"; they differ only in whether the form comes with the return or after it. Asserted.
8. **Regulation 13(3) and (4) permit the electronic format if the person has consent OR is not precluded.** A person who has no consent may still supply it electronically unless subsection 209(5) of the Income Tax Regulations precludes it. Asserted.
9. **Regulation 19 asks for an international and a domestic licence; regulation 15(1) asks only for an international licence.** Section 19 says "licensed to operate an international service and a domestic service under the Canada Transportation Act", while the definition of employment in international transportation (aircraft) says only "an international service". Asserted.
10. **The text cites different Acts in two places.** Regulation 8(1.12)(b) names the "Employment Insurance Act" and regulation 8.2(1)(b)(iii) names the "Unemployment Insurance Act" (line 183); regulation 18(3) names the "Immigration and Refugee Protection Act" and Schedule VI names the "Immigration Act" (lines 1017, 1033, 1040). The encoding follows each, and says nothing about the external Acts.
11. **Regulation 87(2) refers to "the day on which this section comes into force", and the text gives no such day.** Whether a debt is "recoverable on or after" it is a plain input (`reg 87(2)`). The Regulations carry no date of making or of coming into force anywhere in these ranges.
12. **Regulation 87(4) says interest is "calculated daily and compounded monthly" and does not say on what day count or when it joins the debt.** The rate (average bank rate plus 3 per cent) is encoded exactly; the amount takes the days in the year as an input (fork F19). Asserted for the rate and for one worked month.
13. **Regulation 5(2) can give a negative result below the exemption and still be added to 5(3) and (6).** The formula (A minus B) times (C plus D) is negative when the payment is below the exemption; the total is kept as the text gives it and floored at nil only at the end (fork F3): a bi-weekly payment of 130 in a year with 27 periods gives 0.02. Asserted.
14. **Regulations 8(1.1) say "shall" while 8(1.12) and (1.13) say "may".** An employer in the 8(1.1) band shall remit twice a month; an employer who meets the 8(1.12) or (1.13) conditions may remit quarterly, so the latest day on which remittance is permitted is the later of the two (fork F6). Asserted.
15. **Regulation 85(3) and (4) round by looking at one more digit than they keep.** The text drops the third digit if under five, otherwise adds one to the second: 1.2345 gives 1.23, not 1.24 (fork F17). Asserted.
16. **Regulation 36(2)(b) adds zero per cent for a corporation and two per cent for anyone else to the whole percentage; regulation 36(2)(a) adds four.** The whole percentage is the mean of the Treasury Bill yields rounded up to the next whole percentage unless it already is one. Asserted.
17. **Regulations 29(f) and 29.1(2)(d) give the end of the window but not its start.** A payment before 30 April of the year after the contributions' year is in time (fork F15). Asserted.
18. **Regulation 16(2)'s undertaking.** The clause "and the employer has undertaken in prescribed form to make payment ..." is read as a condition of every case (fork F11). Asserted.
19. **The file has no `AMENDMENTS NOT IN FORCE` section and no Part VI or VII.** Part V (regulations 37 to 78.4) is followed directly by Part VIII at line 822.
20. **Several definitions use "includes"** (agriculture, forestry, lumbering, ordinary remuneration, pay period, employer operating in Canada, placement or employment agency, prescribed security interest, among others). Where the rule is a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (employer operating in Canada, placement or employment agency) it answers NOTHING outside the listed cases (the reading taken in fork F1). Asserted.

## What the stable binary does not accept

## Before this is worth relying on

1. A lawyer or an official who knows the Regulations should read the modules against the official text. Nothing here has been verified against the Justice Laws text; the file read is a converter's rendering (see the preamble).
2. The Act's figures (the three contribution rates, the basic exemption, the maximum and additional maximum pensionable earnings, the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings) are inputs here and invented round numbers in the tests; they must come from the Act's rules (piece cp1) when the pieces are merged.
3. The twenty forks in the register need a decision, most of all F1 (what an "includes" definition answers outside its list), F2 (who "the Minister" is in Part VIII), F3 (the negative formula in 5(2)), F12 (section 21 in 17(2)), F17 (the rounding of 85) and F19 (the day count of 87(4)).
4. The tests were worked out by hand from the text, by the encoder who wrote the rules. They were not independently re-derived.
5. The Treasury Bill yields, the Bank of Canada's weekly bank rate, the Wage Measure and the Industrial Aggregate are external series that no deposited text contains; a caller must supply them.
6. The Schedules are matched by the headings the text prints. A different spelling of a province, organization or country answers "not named".
7. The external Acts the Regulations name (Canada Transportation Act, Canada Shipping Act, 2001, Indian Act, Income Tax Act and Regulations, Employment Insurance Act, Unemployment Insurance Act, Immigration Act, Immigration and Refugee Protection Act, Federal Courts Act) are not deposited; whatever they say is an input here.
8. The seat layer asks 113 questions; whether a person can answer them from their own knowledge (the 30-input regulation 16(1) question especially) has not been tried with a user.
