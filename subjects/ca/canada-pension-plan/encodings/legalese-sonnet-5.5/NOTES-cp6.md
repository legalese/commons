This folder holds piece **cp6** of the encoding of the Canada Pension Plan Act (R.S.C. 1985, c. C-8) and the Canada Pension Plan Regulations (C.R.C., c. 385).
The piece is lines 2628 to 3137 of `R.S.C.-1985-c.-C-8.txt`: the heading of Part III, sections 91 to 118 (Part III, "Administration") and the two Schedules of contribution rates that follow section 118 (Schedule 1 at lines 3101 to 3121, Schedule 2 at lines 3123 to 3136).
The section index lists sections 91 to 118 only; the Schedules sit inside the piece's line range and are encoded here (they are cited by section 11.1(2) and section 11.2, piece cp1, and by section 113.1).

**The source.** `R.S.C.-1985-c.-C-8.txt` is a plain-text rendering of the Justice Laws consolidation made by a converter script (consolidation current to 2026-09-30, last amended 2024-12-17).
It is not the official text and it is not marked up; nothing in this folder has been checked against the official text, and nothing here claims to be "verified against the official text".

## Seat

**The seat is the individual to whom Part III is applied:** a worker, self-employed person, survivor, child or person with a disability who contributes to or may claim from the Canada Pension Plan, asking what Part III means for them.
Part III is the Act's administration, so most of it is addressed to Ministers, the Chief Actuary, the Auditor General and departments, and the individual meets it as what is done with their information and their record, what they must give, tell or apply for, and what they can be prosecuted for.
The questions are phrased as that person asks them ("Must I apply ...?", "May I require ...?", "Can I still question ...?", "By what date must I ...?") and take facts that person can state; where the text turns on an official's judgment ("it appears to the Minister that ...") the question takes the judgment as a stated fact and says so in its `@desc`.
There is no seat for an official. Where a provision addresses only an official, layer 1 encodes what it decides and layer 2 asks only what it means to the person.

**What the seat asks (57 questions in `cp6-seat.l4`, grouped under `§§` headings in the text's order):**
who administers the Act; the Record of Earnings (the statement, reconsideration, the four-year rule, rectification upwards, removal of an entry, reduction to correct another's account, notice of a reduction); the Social Insurance Number (must I apply under 98(1), (2) or (3), by when, the prescribed form, the signature, the employer's request and my reply, a new name, a number assigned through my province); offences (false information, applying again, the greatest fine, the five-year limit, an officer's liability, section 41(6)); information (passing between departments, onward passing, my province's authority, statement and reconsideration from the province, oaths, agreements with other countries); gifts to the Minister (a situational group); my contribution (which account, the rate in the Schedules, the limits on a change of rates, when a regulation changing them comes into force, when rates are found insufficient and what rate then applies, whether the Act has stopped owing benefits from a province that has taken over the plan, the earliest day a change can come into force, whether provincial consent is needed).

**What the seat does not ask, and why (each also has a coverage row):**
- the accounts of Canada and the Investment Board (sections 108(2) to (4), 108.1 to 111.1, 112, 115, 117, 118): a contributor cannot state the balance of an account of Canada or the Chief Actuary's estimates. The one fact about the accounts a contributor can state, which account their contribution is paid into, is asked;
- the Governor in Council's regulation-making powers and the agreement-making powers of the Minister over another government (sections 100(1), 101, 107(3), 107(4), 113(3), 113.1(6), (11.144), (14)): they decide nothing for the individual until an agreement or regulation exists and none is among the texts read;
- the employer's side of section 98(5) and the employer's offence (section 102(3)): the employer is another party; the employee's side (must my employer ask me, by when, must I answer) is asked. No employer seat is written in this piece.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Hit while writing this piece (the stable `l4` 1.6.98; the target dialect is `main`):
- `JUST (CONSIDER ...)` with `CONSIDER` on a new line is a parse error; the value is bound with `WHERE` first, or the `CONSIDER` is put on the line of the `JUST` rules.
- A percent literal used as an argument to a rule has to be in parentheses (`(4.95%)`); unparenthesised it is not read as one argument.
- Arithmetic that runs across lines has to be bracketed.
Followed from the brief and not tested for failure here: no `REFUSE`, `REFUSED` or `BECAUSE`; every `NOT` parenthesised; one-line `#ASSERT`; `YMD` is not in the stable prelude, so the core's shim (`YMD y m d`) is used; `@export` and `@desc` on every seat question.

One note about the language's behaviour that is not a rejection: `ROUND` rounds a tie to the even integer, so the rounding of 113.1(11.14) is written out (fork C6-F19).
