# NOTES: Canada Pension Plan Act, piece cp2 (Part I, Division C, Division D, Division E)

This is the encoding of lines 587 to 920 of `R.S.C.-1985-c.-C-8.txt`: Part I (contributions), Division C (collection of contributions: sections 21 to 29), Division D (collection in respect of self-employed earnings: sections 30 to 37) and Division E (general: sections 38 to 41).
The range has 27 sections by label: 21, 21.01, 21.1, 22 to 26, 26.1, 27, 27.1, 27.2, 27.3, 28 to 41.
It is written for the stable `l4` 1.6.98 (the build `main` reads), and every file runs clean on it.
The source is an unofficial plain-text rendering made by a converter script from the Justice Laws consolidation (current to 2026-09-30, last amended 2024-12-17).
It is not the official text, and nothing here has been checked against the official text.
All the rules are of the Act; none is of the Regulations (the Regulations are cr1's and cr2's).
Where the Act leaves a figure or a condition to the Regulations, the figure is a plain input with a comment `-- out of range: ...` and a row under "Cross-references out of my ranges".
Every `src:N` locator is a line number of the Act file.
The shared vocabulary is the keel's `cpp-core*.l4`: this piece calls its rules (`s 2(1) — an employer`, `an employee`, `employment`, `the balance-due day of a person for a year`, `the last day of a year`, `a representative of a person`, `the date a time limit ends`, `a date is within a time limit after a start date`) and defines none of its names again.
The probe that imports all eight layer-1 modules and the five core modules into one file reported no name clash (nine assertions, one from each of several modules, all satisfied).

**Dates.** The range contains no date of enactment, assent or commencement, and no commencement rule.
It contains two rules about the effective date of other instruments, which are encoded because they decide something for a person: subsection 40(3) (a regulation prescribing the deduction rules has effect from the day it is published in the Canada Gazette, or an earlier or later day it specifies) and subsection 40(4) (an agreement may reach back to a period before it was made).
The Act's Related Provisions (line 3138) and Amendments Not in Force (line 3298) are piece cp5's.

**Layers.** Layer 1 is eight modules `cp2-<topic>.l4`, one per natural group of the text, each with a test file `cp2-tests-<topic>.l4` written from the text.
Layer 2 is `cp2-seat.l4` (177 questions, each with `@export` and `@desc`, grouped under `§` headings in the text's order) and `cp2-cases-seat.l4` (at least one case per question).
Layer 1 states each relation once, from neither side, as a BOOLEAN ("the employer must deduct", "the Minister may refund", "the person is guilty of an offence"), a NUMBER (the amount, the penalty, the interest) or a DATE, and a "must" or "may" is the BOOLEAN that the duty or power exists, never a deontic that nobody can assert.
Where the text does not answer, the rule returns NOTHING or a constructor that says so, and a case asserts it.

## Seat

The seat is the brief's: a worker, self-employed person, survivor, child or person with a disability who contributes to or may claim from the Canada Pension Plan, asking whether and how much they must contribute, whether they qualify, how much, and what they must do or tell the Minister.
This range is the collection and refund side of Part I, so the questions it supports are about money taken from pay, a self-employed person's return and instalments, rulings and appeals, refunds, what an officer may do to or demand of the person, and the penalties.
It has four faces, each a `§§` group of `cp2-seat.l4`, and the situational groups carry a comment telling the website what to ask first ("Has this happened to you?"):

1. **An employee**: CPP deductions from pay (ss 21, 21.01, 26); rulings and appeals about their job (ss 26.1 to 29); what an official owes them (ss 26.1(3), 27.2, 28(2), 29(3)).
2. **A self-employed person**: the return (ss 30 to 32, 35); paying, interest and which debt a payment goes to (ss 33, 34, 36, 37); refunds (s 38(4), (4.1)).
3. **Any person asked for documents, visited by an authorized person (s 25), or accused (ss 40, 41)**.
4. **An individual who is also an employer, a director, or an executor, administrator or other person who winds up another's affairs.**
   The Act puts those duties and liabilities on "every employer", "the directors" and "every person ... administering", and they are individuals.
   The brief fixes layer 2 at the two files `cp2-seat.l4` and `cp2-cases-seat.l4`, so the employer's questions are a group of their own in the one seat file (asked in the first person, "Must I, as an employer, ..."), and there is no `cp2-seat-employer.l4`.
   The group also serves the survivor: an executor of a deceased contributor is a "responsible representative" (s 23(5), (5.1)) and files the deceased's returns (s 30(1), (3)).

There is no seat for an official.
The Minister's, the Canada Revenue Agency's and the court's duties appear as what the person is owed (the right to a ruling made in a reasonable time, to notice of an appeal, to a decision on it, to a refund, to written reasons) and their powers as what may be done to the person (inspection, requirements for information, assessment, the set-off of a refund).

Every question takes plain inputs: yes/no, a number, a date, a per cent, a string for a province, or a choice from a layer-1 enumeration whose members the `@desc` lists.
A fact that may not have happened is asked as a yes/no plus a date and joined into a MAYBE DATE inside the question.
The seat builds the layer-1 records from the answers and calls the layer-1 rule; it never restates a rule, and no layer-1 module changes for it.
A figure the person cannot state (the CPP deduction the prescribed rules give, the prescribed rate of interest, the day an interest period begins under Regulations section 35, the allowances the Treasury Board approves) is an input whose `@desc` says where to find it; a judgment the Act hands to someone else (the Minister "is satisfied", a time is "reasonable", the case is "appropriate", a judge is satisfied) is an input worded as the person would know it.
Rates are asked in per cent a year ("5 for 5%") and divided by 100 inside the question.

## What the text turns out to say

Numbered findings; each ends "Asserted." where a test or a seat case asserts it (the tests are `cp2-tests-*.l4`, the cases `cp2-cases-seat.l4`).

1. A request for a ruling made on 30 June of the year after the year in question is too late: section 26.1(2) says "before June 30". The Minister of Employment and Social Development may ask at any time. Asserted.
2. An employee notified of a disability pension decision on 1 September 2010 itself has four years, not ten, to apply for a refund: subsections 38(1), (3) and (4)(b) give ten years only to one notified "after September 1, 2010". Asserted.
3. The employer has four years and no more to apply for a refund of the excess over its section 9 contributions (subsections 38(3.1), (3.3)), and the agreement refund of section 39(1) also four years; only employees and self-employed contributors can have ten. Asserted.
4. A self-employed person who files a return exactly on the four-year day (four years after the filing day) is not saved from subsection 30(5), because the return was not filed "before" that day; an assessment made on that day is still "before the end of those four years". Asserted.
5. Subsection 41(3) asks for a fine of "not less than twenty-five dollars a day ... but not exceeding in all one thousand dollars". At 40 days of default the floor equals the cap; beyond that the two limits cannot both hold, and the cap is taken to govern both (fork F26). Asserted.
6. Subsection 38(3.2) bars any refund of an employer's contribution required by section 9; subsection 38(3.1) lets the Minister refund only the excess over it. Asserted.
7. Section 37 applies to the self-employed contributions the part of a mixed payment that section 228 of the Income Tax Act would apply to tax, "despite any direction", and puts no cap at the contributions then owing (fork F22). Asserted.
8. The penalty for not remitting (subsection 21(7)) is 3 per cent when the Receiver General has the amount by the due day but not in the manner required, or within three days after it; 5 per cent for four or five days; 7 per cent for six or seven; 10 per cent after that or never. Twenty per cent needs both an earlier penalty under the subsection in the year and a knowing or grossly negligent failure, and it needs an actual failure. Asserted.
9. The deemed notification in subsection 21(3.1) arises only for an employer relieved by subsection (3): a ruling given in writing, not based on incorrect information from the employer, and later overruled. Asserted.
10. An employer may recoup a missed deduction from a later payment only within 12 months and only one earlier missed amount at a time (subsection 21(4)). Asserted.
11. A case in neither (entry necessary but not refused) is not decided by the text. Asserted.
12. Under subsection 25(3) a mobile unit used as a residence is a "dwelling-house", so an authorized person needs the occupant's consent or a warrant to enter it. Asserted.
13. Subsection 41(2) makes a failure to keep records or to comply with section 25 an offence "punishable on summary conviction" and names no maximum fine or term. Asserted (NOTHING).

## Before this is worth relying on

- No domain expert has read this against the official text. The source is a converter's rendering of the Justice Laws consolidation current to 2026-09-30, and an exact wording (subsection 35(1) is garbled in it) may differ in the official text.
- Five Acts are not among the texts read: the Income Tax Act (sections 155, 156, 160, 161, 162, 167, 220 to 224.3, 227, 227.1, 228, 229, 236, 244, 248; Divisions I and J and Part XV of Part I; and the day an income tax return is due), the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act (sections 69, 69.1, 81.1, 81.2), the Companies' Creditors Arrangement Act (section 11.09), the Tax Court of Canada Act (paragraph 20(1.1)(h.1) and the rules under it), and the Department of Employment and Social Development Act (sections 54 and 59). What they add is a plain input wherever a rule needs it. A person who relies on an answer about directors' defences (section 21.1(2)), the trust and its priority (section 23), the extension of time to appeal (section 28(1.2)), the instalment rules (section 33) or which debt a payment goes to (section 37) must supply those inputs from the other Acts.
- The Regulations are cr1's and cr2's. Several answers here need their figures and are inputs until the merge wires them in: the CPP deduction the prescribed rules give for a payment (Regulations Part I), the employer's prescribed amount (section 7), the prescribed persons (section 8.2), the prescribed security interests (section 8.3), the day an interest period begins (section 35) and the prescribed rate of interest (section 36). The rate is a quarterly figure built from Treasury Bill auctions, which no person can state: **a website that deploys `cp2-seat.l4` must supply the rate itself**, and the interest answers change with it.
- Section 6 (pensionable employment), sections 8 to 10 (the contributions required of an employee, an employer and a self-employed person) and subsection 8(2) (the excess) are piece cp1's. They are inputs here, named for the fact; at the merge the seat should read them from cp1's rules instead of asking the person.
- Thirty-two forks are recorded in the fork register; none is ruled by Meng or by a court, and each can be reversed alone. The ones most likely to change an answer are F2 (simple interest over a 365-day year), F10 (the Acts that are not deposited), F19 (the four-year edge), F20 (the choice of base under subsection 33(2)), F21 (annual amounts in four quarters under subsection 34(4)), F22 (no cap on the part of a payment applied to contributions) and F26 (the cap meets the floor in subsection 41(3)).
- The seat is one module with 177 questions, and its employer-and-director group is large. The brief fixes layer 2 at one module; if the website prefers a short form for an ordinary employee or self-employed person, the groups can be deployed separately under different names once the lead splits the file.
- The seat takes enumeration inputs (the base of the instalments, the capacity of a person who winds up an estate, the place where records are kept, the kind of appeal and order). Whether the website's Interact form shows a layer-1 enumeration as a choice list was not tested here; if it does not, each of those inputs should become a string or a small number that the question maps to the constructor.
- The seat takes no MAYBE and no LIST input, but its outputs include MAYBE values (NOTHING means the Act does not answer). How the website shows a null was not tested.
- The merged folder was probed for name clashes between the eight layer-1 modules and the core (none), not with the other pieces' modules, which could not be seen. A name in this piece that another piece defines will make both unusable together; every name here carries its section number or a `cp2` prefix to make that unlikely.
- One related provision of piece cp5's range touches this one: 2008, c. 28, s. 38(3) and (4) (Act lines 3159 to 3161) on remittances by a prescribed person first due after 25 February 2008. It reads like a transitional rule for subsections 21(1) and (1.1) here; whoever merges the pieces should check that cp5's encoding of it and `s 21(1.1) — the prescribed person is deemed to have remitted ...` agree.
