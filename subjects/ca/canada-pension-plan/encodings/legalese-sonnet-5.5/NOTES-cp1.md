## Seat

The seat is the individual to whom the law is applied: a worker or self-employed person who contributes to the Canada Pension Plan, asking whether and how much they must contribute, what their employer must contribute in respect of them, what counts as their earnings, and what they may elect or must tell the Minister.
That is the seat the Commons guide rules for every encoding (ruled 2026-10-18), and the brief for this text states it for Part I.
The benefit questions (retirement, disability, survivor's and children's benefits) are in the seat layers of the pieces that encode Parts II and III.
No official has a seat: the Minister, the Governor in Council, the Receiver General and the Chief Statistician appear only as the other party of a relation (table "Jural relations") or as an input.

Two layers, in this folder:

- Layer 1 is the law, objectively: `cp1-nouns.l4`, `cp1-amount-helpers.l4`, `cp1-months-helpers.l4`, `cp1-div-a-pensionable-employment.l4`, `cp1-div-a-employee-employer-contributions.l4`, `cp1-div-a-self-employed-contributions.l4`, `cp1-div-b-contribution-rates.l4`, `cp1-div-b-contributory-salary-and-wages.l4`, `cp1-div-b-self-employed-earnings.l4`, `cp1-div-b-salary-and-wages-contributed-on.l4` and `cp1-div-b-maximum-earnings-and-exemption.l4`, with one test file `cp1-tests-*.l4` per module that states rules.
- Layer 2 is `cp1-seat.l4` (the individual's questions, each with `@export`, and `@desc` on every input) and `cp1-cases-seat.l4` (worked cases, hand-written expectations).

The seat layer imports the layer-1 modules and calls their rules; it restates none, and no layer-1 module was changed to suit it.
Each question takes plain inputs (yes/no, numbers, dates and the modules' own enumerations); the records the modules expect are built inside the question, in its `WHERE`.
An optional fact (an event that may not have happened) is a yes/no answer plus a date or an amount.
A record field that no rule called ever reads gets a fixed placeholder with a comment saying so (the date of death, in the questions that do not count death).

What the seat takes as numbers because the text does not contain them: the contribution rates (Schedules 1 and 2), the Year's Maximum Pensionable Earnings and the other figures of sections 16 to 20 where a question needs them from another question, the amount prescribed as or on account of the basic exemption, and the provincial plans' own rates and amounts.
A change to any of them changes the answers; the cases use illustrative 2024-style figures (employee base rate 4.95%, first additional 1%, second additional 4%, maximum pensionable earnings 68,500, additional maximum 73,200, exemption 3,500) and say so.

Simplifications of the seat layer (assumed, not ruled; each is only in the seat, the layer-1 rules take any number):

- the excess-amount questions take up to two employments under the Plan and one under a provincial pension plan;
- the self-employed earnings question takes up to two businesses;
- the pay of a year is asked month by month (twelve amounts), dated the first of the month, because every boundary in subsection 12(1) falls on the first of a month;
- where the Minister must be satisfied of something (subsections 11(3) and 11(6)), the question asks whether the Minister is satisfied and answers conditionally.

## What the text turns out to say

1. Contributions have no floor. Wages below the exemption give a negative base contribution under 8(1) (and 8(1.1), 9(1)); self-employed earnings below the exemption give a negative contribution under 10(1). Nothing in sections 8 to 10 says "not less than nil".
   Asserted (`cp1-tests-employee-employer-contributions.l4`, wages of 2,000 against an exemption of 3,500, result minus 74.25; `cp1-tests-self-employed-contributions.l4`, earnings of 1,000, result minus 247.5).
2. Read as written, subsection 15(1) cannot be about what was deducted. Paragraph (b) is the aggregate deducted under the Act or a provincial plan, minus (the amount of 8(2)(b) plus (that aggregate minus the amounts of 8(2)(a) and (b))), which is the amount of 8(2)(a). The greater of (a) and (b) is therefore the amount of 8(2)(a) whenever (a) is not larger, whatever was deducted; the salary and wages "on which a contribution has been made" are then the 8(2)(a) amount over the rate. Subsection 15.1(1) has the same shape.
   Asserted (`cp1-tests-salary-and-wages-contributed-on.l4`, "the figure does not depend on what was deducted": under-deducted, exactly deducted and over-deducted all give 65,000).
3. The overpayment of subsection 8(3) can be negative: when more was deducted under a provincial plan than the plan required and less under this Act than this Act required, paragraph (a) is negative and the lesser of it and the excess is negative.
   Asserted (`cp1-tests-employee-employer-contributions.l4`, "facts 4c: provincial over-deduction", result minus 80.875).
4. Paragraph 13(1)(a) does not mention death, while 17(a), 17.1(a) and 19(a) do. A person who dies in a year has their section 13 earnings prorated only by the other events; the maximums and the exemption are prorated to the month of death.
   Asserted (`cp1-tests-self-employed-earnings.l4`, months after the 70th birthday or death for `paragraph 17(c)` against `paragraph 13(1)(c)`).
5. A person who turns eighteen on 20 December 2024 is, by section 2(2), deemed to reach eighteen on 1 January 2025. For 2024 they are under eighteen for the whole year: 13(2)(a) leaves their 2024 self-employed earnings out, and by the same rule 12(1)(a) would leave out income received in December 2024 after the birthday (the case asserted is the section 13 one). The same person has all twelve months of 2025.
   Asserted (`cp1-tests-self-employed-earnings.l4`, "person born 20 December 2006, in 2024" gives 0 and "in 2025" gives 60,000; and "a paragraph (a) event occurs in the year").
6. Where a year has an election event, a count of months can come out negative (9 months after a revocation less 10 months after the 70th birthday); the paragraph speaks of "the number of months", so it is read as zero (F-08).
   Asserted (`cp1-tests-self-employed-earnings.l4` and `cp1-tests-maximum-earnings-and-exemption.l4`, "person aged 70 who revokes a 12(1)(c)(ii) election on 20 March 2024").
7. Subsection 13(1.2)(c) is one of the ten places where the keel makes the actual birthday govern, while 13(1.2)(a) uses the deemed date. A person born on 31 December 1958 may name December 2028 (the month of the actual seventieth birthday) but not January 2029, although section 2(2) would deem seventy reached on 1 January 2029.
   Asserted (`cp1-tests-self-employed-earnings.l4`, `s 13(1.2)(c)` with a birth date of 31 December 1958: 1 December 2028 is allowed, 1 January 2029 is not).
8. Two election events in one year (an election of subparagraph 12(1)(c)(ii) made and an election of paragraph 13(1)(b) revoked, say) are not addressed: the bars in 12(1.1) and 13(1.1) are each within one kind. The rules answer NOTHING.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Found while writing against the stable `l4` 1.6.98; each cost an error message that reads like a mistake in the rule.

- A `%` literal as a function argument must be parenthesised: `(4.95%)`.
- `JUST x` as an argument needs parentheses: `(JUST x)`.
- A `LIST` whose elements are `OF`-record constructions fails; bind each record to a name and list the names.
- `NOT EQUALS` is not an operator; write `NOT (a EQUALS b)`.
- An `ELSE IF` chain, or a `THEN` that continues on the next line at the wrong indent, fails with "incorrect indentation"; `BRANCH` with `IF ... THEN ...` lines and `OTHERWISE` is used instead.
- `min` and `max` work on NUMBER only; for dates write an `IF`.
- `foldl` with a two-parameter lambda fails (`unexpected (`); `maximum`, `filter`, `map`, `count` and `at` are used instead.
- `x IS JUST` followed by a line starting `(` inside a `WITH` record field fails; bind the value in a `WHERE` first.
- `l4 run` of the larger files is slow (see "What check.sh prints"): every module that imports others is re-checked with its imports.
- Accepted, though one might doubt it: a multi-line rule head with its arguments on the following lines; a `WHERE` inside a `CONSIDER` branch; `min` and `max` on `MAYBE` values when typed; calling a rule with `WITH name IS value` on several lines.
