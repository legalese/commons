# Source license

The terms the quoted source text carries are recorded in the subject-level note `STUB-SOURCE-LICENSE.md` (in the run's `inputs/` folder), written when the text was deposited.
This file points at it and does not repeat it.

What this encoding adds to the question that note leaves open:

- The layer-1 modules quote the Act (S.C. 2023, c. 17) and the Regulations (SOR/2025-35) verbatim in `-- src:` comment lines, as `srcrefs.py` requires.
  That is reproduction of the text, and the note says anyone may reproduce federal enactments free of charge on two conditions: due diligence over accuracy, and not presenting the copy as an official version.
- The note says that whether an L4 encoding that quotes the text may rely on the Open Government Licence - Canada (which does grant adaptation) is "a human decision to record here before an encoding quotes it".
  That decision has not been taken by this encoding: it is **undetermined**, and a human must record it before this encoding is published.
- Not an official version.
  The deposited texts are plain-text renderings made by a converter script from the Department of Justice Canada consolidation (current to 2026-09-30), and nothing in this encoding has been verified against the official text.
  Only the official consolidation, and the enacted Acts and registered regulations in the Canada Gazette, have legal status.
- Only the English version is deposited; the French version has equal authority and was not read.
- Attribution under the Open Government Licence - Canada: "Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence - Canada."

The encoding itself (the L4 modules, tests, `check.sh` and these notes) is Apache-2.0 like the rest of canon.
