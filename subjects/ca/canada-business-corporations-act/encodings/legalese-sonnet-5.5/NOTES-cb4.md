## Seat

The seat is the one the brief gives: a person who incorporates, directs or holds shares in a corporation under the CBCA: an incorporator, a director or officer, or a shareholder (including a minority shareholder).
Such a person asks four things of Parts XV to XVIII: what the Act lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.
Parts XV to XVIII are the Act's chapters on the big, rare events of a corporation's life: changing its articles, merging it, moving it, selling it, buying out dissenters, taking it private, bidding for it, and winding it up.
The person in the seat meets them as a shareholder who is being outvoted, bought out or squeezed out, as a director who must run a meeting or a wind-up properly, or as a founder who wants to end the company.

There is no seat for an official (the Director, a court, a liquidator or the Receiver General).
An official's duty is asked from the side of the person who relies on it: "Must the Director issue me a certificate of revival?", "Must the liquidator give notice of their appointment?".
Where a director or shareholder may themselves become an official (a director appointed liquidator, a person given custody of the records), the question is put in the first person ("Am I, as liquidator, free of liability for a decision?", "Am I guilty of an offence if I fail to produce records I have custody of?").

## Fork register

Every ambiguity I met in the range: the readings I saw, the one I took, the text that licenses each, and how to change it.
None is resolved by a court or the regulator that I know of; none is settled by anything in the sources.
The numbering F1 to F8 is mine; the keel's own register (its F1 is how a time limit is counted) is separate, and every time limit of mine uses the keel's counting unchanged.
Where I looked, for all of them: every subsection of the range, read in full, and the sections they cross-refer to inside the range.

### F1: a class vote on top of, or in place of, the general special resolution (cb4-amendment, cb4-amalgamation, cb4-borrowing-sale)

- The text: s 173(1), src:2097, "Subject to sections 176 and 177, the articles of a corporation may by special resolution be amended"; s 176(6), src:2175, a proposed amendment "is adopted when the holders of the shares of each class or series entitled to vote separately thereon ... have approved such amendment by a special resolution"; s 183(5), src:2218, an amalgamation agreement "is adopted when the shareholders of each amalgamating corporation have approved of the amalgamation by special resolutions", "Subject to subsection (4)"; s 189(8), src:2355, a sale "is adopted when the holders of each class or series entitled to vote thereon have approved ... by a special resolution".
- Readings: (i) the general special resolution of all the voting shareholders AND a special resolution of each separately voting class or series [taken]; (ii) the class resolutions replace the general one for the classes that vote separately.
- Why (i): s 173(1) and s 183(5) state the general approval and make it "subject to" the class provisions; they do not say the general approval falls away. Reading (ii) would let a class vote substitute for the vote of the shareholders who hold no separately voting class.
- Not a fork here: ss 210(2), 210(3) and 211(3) say in terms "by special resolution of the shareholders or, where it has issued more than one class of shares, by special resolutions of the holders of each class whether or not they are otherwise entitled to vote" (src:2611, 2613, 2627). That is an alternative on the text's own wording, and the modules encode it so: one class, the shareholders' tally; more than one, every class.
- To change it: the `AND` of the separate-class condition in `the amendment is adopted within s 173(1) and s 176(6)`, `the shareholders of an amalgamating corporation have approved the agreement within s 183(5)` and `the sale, lease or exchange is adopted within s 189(8)`.

### F2: "ceases to apply" / "ceases to exist" on the date shown (cb4-amalgamation, cb4-continuance, cb4-dissolution, cb4-liquidator)

- The text: ss 186.1(5) src:2271 and 188(9) src:2326, "This Act ceases to apply to the corporation on the date shown in the certificate of discontinuance"; ss 210(6) src:2621, 211(16) src:2657, 212(4) src:2678, 213(5) src:2693, 223(8) src:2815, "The corporation ceases to exist on the date shown in the certificate of dissolution".
- Readings: (i) the Act applies (the corporation exists) strictly before the date shown, and not on it [taken]; (ii) it applies on the date shown too and ends at the end of that day.
- Why (i): "ceases ... on the date" puts the cessation on that day. By contrast "effective on the date shown" (ss 209(3.1), 211(12)) is read as on or after it.
- To change it: `LESS THAN` to `AT MOST` in the seven `exists on a day within ...` and `still applies` rules.
- A tension the readings do not remove: s 211(6), src:2633, says the corporation's "corporate existence continues until the Director issues a certificate of dissolution", and s 211(16) says it ceases on the date shown in the certificate. If the date shown is later than the day of issue the two say different things for the days between. Taken: the date shown governs (s 211(16)).

### F3: s 188(7) points at a repealed subsection (cb4-continuance)

- The text: src:2322, "one of the Acts referred to in subsection (2.1)"; src:2312, "(2.1) [Repealed, 2001, c. 14, s. 92]".
- Readings: (i) the Acts listed in subsection (2) [taken]; (ii) no Act at all, so only a continuance "under the laws of another jurisdiction" counts.
- Why (i): the repealed subsection must have carried the list, and the live list of Acts is subsection (2).
- To change it: the second alternative of `the Director must file the notice and issue a certificate of discontinuance within s 188(7)`.

### F4: "within a further period of twenty days" (cb4-dissent, cb4-takeover)

- The text: s 190(16), src:2406, a dissenting shareholder may apply "within a further period of twenty days or within such further period as a court may allow"; s 206(10), src:2546, a dissenting offeree may apply "within a further period of twenty days".
- Readings: (i) the further twenty days start the day after the first applicant's twenty days end, so the dissenting person's window is days 21 to 40 [taken]; (ii) they start from the day the first applicant's period began (a window overlapping the first); (iii) they start when the dissenting person learns that nobody has applied.
- Why (i): "further" follows the period just given; (ii) would make the dissenter's time end before the first applicant's did; the text names no event of knowledge.
- To change it: the `counted from` argument in `the last day for the dissenting shareholder to apply to a court within s 190(16)` and `the last day for the dissenting offeree to apply to a court within s 206(10)`.
- A difference between the two, not a fork: s 190(16) lets a court allow a longer period, and s 206(10) does not; the dissent module has the court's extension and the take-over module has none.

### F5: when a one-year default becomes a ground (cb4-dissolution, `the corporation is in default for one year in sending a fee, notice, document or information within s 212(1)(a)(iii)`)

- The text: src:2663, "the corporation is in default for a period of one year in sending to the Director any fee or any notice, document or other information required by this Act".
- Readings: (i) the ground exists from the first anniversary of the day the default began, for as long as the default continues [taken]; (ii) a default that has run for more than a year, so from the day after the anniversary.
- Why (i): "for a period of one year" is satisfied when the year is complete, and the keel counts the last day of a year from a start day as the same date a year on; the test puts the anniversary on the TRUE side.
- To change it: `AT LEAST` to `GREATER THAN` in that rule.

### F6: the form of the liquidator's financial statements (cb4-liquidator, s 221(h))

- The text: src:2775, "financial statements of the corporation in the form required by section 155 or in such other form as the liquidator may think proper or as the court may require".
- Readings: (i) the three forms are alternatives, so a form the liquidator thinks proper is enough, whatever s 155 or the court would say [taken]; (ii) the court's requirement overrides the other two once it is made.
- Why (i): the words are a plain "or". Under (ii) the phrase "as the liquidator may think proper" would give the liquidator a freedom the court can take away, which the sentence does not say.
- To change it: make `the statements are in the form the court requires` an overriding condition in `the form of the financial statements is acceptable within s 221(h)`.

### F7: "including reliance in good faith" (cb4-liquidator, s 222(2))

- The text: src:2788-2790, a liquidator "is not liable if the liquidator exercised the care, diligence and skill that a reasonably prudent person would have exercised in comparable circumstances, including reliance in good faith on" (a) financial statements represented by an officer or an auditor's written report, or (b) a report of a professional.
- Readings: (i) good-faith reliance on (a) or (b) is itself enough to satisfy the standard [taken]; (ii) reliance is only evidence of care, so the care must still be shown.
- Why (i): "including" makes the reliance part of the standard of care, and the sentence is a defence, so what it lists is what suffices.
- To change it: the two reliance disjuncts of `the liquidator is not liable within s 222(2)`.

### F8: the gate at the head of s 217 (cb4-court-liquidation)

- The text: src:2728, "the court may, if it is satisfied that the corporation is able to pay or adequately provide for the discharge of all its obligations, make any order it thinks fit including ... (a) ... (o)".
- Readings: (i) the satisfaction is a condition of every order in the section [taken]; (ii) it conditions only the orders that deal with paying claims or distributing property (paragraphs (h), (l), (m)).
- Why (i): the condition stands before the list and applies to "any order it thinks fit". The consequence is awkward: s 221(g), src:2774, tells a liquidator who finds that the corporation CANNOT pay to "apply to the court for directions", and s 217(j) is the paragraph that gives directions; read as (i), the court has no power under s 217 in exactly that case (s 213(3) and s 214 give other powers).
- To change it: take the gate out of `the court may make this kind of order within s 217` for the kinds that (ii) frees.

## Needs from the core

Nothing in the keel was edited and nothing was copied out of it into a different name.
These are the gaps I met and what I did about each; the lead decides what to promote.

1. **`ordinary resolution within s 2(1)` does not model s 142(1)(a).**
   Section 142(1)(a), src:1765-1766 (outside every range of mine), makes a resolution in writing signed by all the shareholders entitled to vote as valid as one passed at a meeting.
   The keel's definition of "special resolution" carries its own written-consent alternative (s 2(1)); its "ordinary resolution" has none, which is faithful to s 2(1), so a tally of no votes signed by all does not pass as an ordinary resolution.
   My only use is the squeeze-out approval of s 194; the seat question about it says in its `@desc` that a written resolution is not modelled.
   Wanted: either a rule in the core for "resolution passed as the kind, counting s 142(1)(a)", or a statement that every caller must add the written route.
2. **A shared "strictly before the date shown" rule.**
   Seven sections end the Act's application or the corporation's existence "on the date shown in the certificate" (ss 186.1(5), 188(9), 210(6), 211(16), 212(4), 213(5), 223(8)); I wrote seven small rules, one per section, so that each can carry its own `@ref`.
   Two more say "effective on the date shown" (ss 209(3.1), 211(12)), which I read as on or after.
   If the core offered `the day is before the date shown` and `the day is on or after the date shown`, the nine rules would call them (fork F2 would then live in one place).
3. **Date helpers in `cb4-common`.**
   `the later of two dates`, `the earlier of two dates` and `the day immediately before` are in `cb4-common.l4` and are generic.
   The name `the day before` could not be used: the prelude's `daydate` already defines it (see "What the stable binary does not accept").
4. **Class votes.**
   `every tally in the list passes as a special resolution` and `... as an ordinary resolution` (in `cb4-common`) run the keel's resolution rules over a list of tallies, one per class.
   Five sections need "the holders of each class pass a special resolution" (ss 176, 183, 189, 210, 211) and I take the answer as one BOOLEAN input from the seat; if the core carried a class-vote record with a tally per class, the seat could take the tallies.
5. **The Director's duty "on receipt, shall issue a certificate in accordance with section 262".**
   Occurs at ss 178, 180(3), 185(4), 186.1(3), 187(4), 188(7), 191(5), 192(7), 209(3), 210(5), 211(5), 211(11), 211(15) and 223(7) (src:2181, 2191, 2252, 2267, 2281, 2322, 2447, 2481, 2591, 2619, 2631, 2647, 2655, 2813); ss 186.1(4) and 188(8) deem a notice to be articles "for the purposes of section 262".
   I encode each as a BOOLEAN that is TRUE on receipt; the core could name the pattern once.
   Section 262 is cb5's.
6. **The word "court".**
   Section 207 defines "court" for Part XVIII as a court with jurisdiction where the corporation has its registered office; other Parts say it differently (s 190(17), s 206(12)).
   My rule `a court is the court of this Part within s 207` is local, as the keel's table of terms defined more than once suggests.

## What the text turns out to say

Findings, numbered.
Each ends "Asserted." where a test or a seat case asserts it, and otherwise says why it is not asserted.

1. **"Ordinary resolution" has no signed-by-all route in s 2(1), and my first draft of one test expected the opposite.**
   Section 2(1), src:55, defines an ordinary resolution as passed "by a majority of the votes cast by the shareholders who voted"; the definition of "special resolution" (src:72) adds "or signed by all the shareholders entitled to vote".
   I wrote the squeeze-out test (s 194, the one place my range uses an ordinary resolution) by copying the special-resolution shape, expecting a tally of 0 for, 0 against, signed by all, to pass.
   The assertion failed; I read the definition, found my expectation wrong for the s 2(1) rule, and changed the assertion to say it does not pass, with a comment in the test recording the first draft.
   The change is a correction of my own expectation, made after reading the text; the encoding was not changed to fit.
   But s 142(1)(a), src:1765-1766, which I read afterwards, makes a resolution in writing signed by all the shareholders entitled to vote "as valid as if it had been passed at a meeting", so in law a signed-by-all written resolution may be a valid ordinary resolution. The assertion records the keel's s 2(1) rule only (see "Needs from the core", item 1).
   Asserted (the s 2(1) rule).
2. **Two solvency tests, not one.**
   Section 190(26)(b), src:2432, stops a payment to a dissenting shareholder if the realizable value of the assets would be "less than the aggregate of its liabilities".
   Section 192(2)(b), src:2466, calls a corporation insolvent if the realizable value is less than "the aggregate of its liabilities and stated capital of all classes".
   A corporation with assets of 1,000, liabilities of 600 and stated capital of 400 is solvent under s 192(2) by exactly one dollar of margin and could be insolvent at 999; the same figures bear no weight in s 190(26)(b), which counts liabilities only.
   Section 190(26) is encoded as two BOOLEAN inputs ("reasonable grounds for believing"), so only the s 192(2) arithmetic is a number test.
   Asserted (s 192(2), at 1,000, 999, 1,001).
3. **"Includes" lists leave the rest undecided.**
   Section 192(1) says an arrangement "includes" eight kinds of transaction, s 206(1) says an offer "includes an invitation", and ss 209(6) and 226(1) say "interested person" and "shareholder" include named persons.
   A transaction, offer or person not on the list is not thereby outside the word; the Act is silent.
   The encoding returns `JUST TRUE` for a listed one and `NOTHING` for the rest, never `FALSE`.
   Asserted.
4. **A Quebec contract by gratuitous title is carved out of s 209(6)(b) only.**
   Section 209(6)(b), src:2605, includes a person with a contract "other than, in Quebec, a contract by gratuitous title". Such a person is not within paragraph (b) but may still be an interested person under another paragraph or by the general word, so the encoding gives `NOTHING`, not `FALSE`.
   Asserted.
5. **Section 188(7) points at a repealed subsection (fork F3).**
   Asserted (the rule for the Acts listed in s 188(2)).
6. **Section 190(25) says nothing about a notice delivered late, or where s 190(26) does not apply.**
   Src:2426-2428 gives a shareholder 30 days "by written notice" to withdraw the dissent or keep the status of a claimant, but only "if subsection (26) applies". The encoding returns "the Act does not say what the effect is" in the other two situations.
   Asserted.
7. **"A further period of twenty days" has no stated start (fork F4), and s 206(10) allows no court extension where s 190(16) does.**
   Asserted (the last day, days 21 to 40).
8. **The deemed election in a take-over is stated twice and is a third thing in subsection (11).**
   Section 206(3)(d), src:2517, requires the offeror's notice to say that a dissenting offeree who does not notify "in accordance with subparagraph (5)(b)(ii)" is deemed to have elected to transfer; s 206(5.1), src:2528, states the deemed election itself; s 206(11), src:2548, deems a transfer election again where no court application is made under subsection (10), which reaches an offeree who DID elect to demand fair value.
   An offeree who demands fair value therefore loses that election by default if neither the offeror nor the offeree applies to the court in time. That consequence is not stated anywhere as a rule; it follows from (11) alone.
   Asserted (the seat case for s 206(11): `JUST` the transfer election when no application was made, `NOTHING` when one was).
9. **A shareholder who never got the offeror's notice has ninety days from termination, or from learning of the bid if no offer ever reached them (s 206.1(1)).**
   Src:2570-2575. The ninety days run from the LATER of termination and the day the shareholder learned of the bid, only when no offer was received; otherwise from termination.
   Asserted (2026-09-18 against 2026-08-30 on the same facts).
10. **The date a one-year default becomes a ground for dissolution is not stated (fork F5).**
    Asserted.
11. **Section 212(3): the 120 days run before the Director can issue the certificate, not before it can decide.**
    Src:2674, "the certificate is not to be issued until after the end of the period": notice given on 1 March 2026 ends the period on 29 June, and the first day the certificate may issue is 30 June. Subsection (2.1) removes the notice for the listed-entity ground, and the certificate then has no waiting period.
    Asserted.
12. **The opening of s 217 is a gate on every order (fork F8), and s 221(g) sends the liquidator to the court in exactly the case the gate shuts.**
    Asserted (a gate that fails refuses even a paragraph with every other condition met).
13. **Voluntary and court-ordered liquidation use different tests for what business may go on.**
    After a certificate of intent to dissolve (s 211(6), src:2633) the corporation may carry on business "to the extent necessary for the liquidation" (an objective test); after a court order (s 219(1)(a), src:2755) it may carry on the business "that is, in the opinion of the liquidator, required for an orderly liquidation" (the liquidator's opinion).
    Asserted (each in its own module).
14. **Section 223(5)(c), "subject to subsection (6)", cannot be a precondition of the discharge.**
    Subsection (6) (src:2811) makes the liquidator send the Director a certified copy of the order made under (5); the copy cannot be sent before the order, so the order cannot wait for it.
    Read as: the discharge does not release the liquidator from (6). The discharge rule has one input, the court's approval of the final accounts.
    Asserted (the rule has no (6) input).
15. **The liquidator's year (s 223(2)) does not move for an extension unless an extension is applied for within it.**
    Src:2798-2800: "within one year after appointment ... apply to the court ... (b) for an extension of time". An extension application made on day 366 is as late as a final-accounts application made then. The Act does not say what happens if the liquidator has not provided for all claims by then; the extension is the only exit it offers.
    Asserted (both applications, 2027-07-01 in time, 2027-07-02 late).
16. **An order made under s 223(5) can shorten, but not lengthen, the time a custodian of the records stays liable (s 225(1)).**
    Src:2826, "the end of the prescribed period or of any shorter period fixed by an order". A longer period fixed by an order does not extend the prescribed period.
    Asserted.
17. **Service on a dissolved corporation "may be effected" on the named person, and that is all it says (s 226(3), (3.1)).**
    A person not so shown is not thereby wrongly served; the encoding gives `NOTHING`.
    Asserted.
18. **A former shareholder's liability is capped by what they received, and by what is claimed (s 226(4), (5)(b)).**
    Src:2841, "to the extent of the amount received by that shareholder on such distribution": 400 received against a claim of 1,000 gives 400; against a claim of 300 gives 300; and a referee's contribution under (5)(b) cannot exceed what that person received.
    Asserted.
19. **Section 228(1) is subject to s 226(2) and s 227: property that remains available for judgments, or is the portion of an unfindable creditor or shareholder, does not vest in the Crown.**
    Asserted.
20. **Dates the keel counts: two months from 31 December is 28 February.**
    Section 221(b)(iii), src:2769, gives creditors "not later than two months after the first publication"; the keel's `add months` clamps an end-of-month date to the last day of a shorter month (keel F1). This is the keel's behaviour and not something the text says.
    Asserted (31 December 2026 gives 28 February 2027), as a statement about the keel.
21. **"Forthwith", "immediately" and "without delay" are not converted to days.**
    Ss 187(6), 190(10), 206(8)(c)(iii), 211(7)(a) ("immediately"), 211(7)(b), 221(a), 221(b) and 223(6) give no day count; no rule invents one. The duty arises on the event.
    Not asserted: nothing to assert.
22. **Several rules hand a judgment to an official and cannot be computed.**
    "Satisfactory to the Director", "reasonable conditions precedent" (s 209(3)(a)), "valid reason for refusing" (s 209(3)(b)), "reasonable grounds for believing" (s 190(26)) are plain BOOLEAN inputs.
    Not asserted: the encoding passes the judgment through.
23. **Section 192(4)(a) does not let the court dispense with notice to the Director.**
    Src:2471: paragraph (a) of subsection (4) lets the court determine the notice to be given to "any interested person" or dispense with notice "to any person other than the Director"; subsection (5), src:2477, separately requires an applicant to give the Director notice.
    Asserted (`an order dispensing with notice to the Director` is refused).

## What the stable binary does not accept

Everything below was met running `l4` 1.6.98 (stable `main`), and each cost a failed run or a wrong-looking error before it was understood.

1. **A name the prelude already defines.** `the day before` is defined by the prelude's `daydate.l4`, so my helper of that name collided (the keel's instruction: a name defined in two imported modules cannot be used). Renamed `the day immediately before`. Prelude and keel names must be grepped before a helper is named.
2. **`EQUALS JUST TRUE` after a bare `WITH` call.** `` #ASSERT `rule` WITH `x` IS 1 EQUALS JUST TRUE `` failed with a type error ("ASSERT expected BOOLEAN but MAYBE OF BOOLEAN"). Bracketing the whole call fixed it: `` #ASSERT (`rule` WITH `x` IS 1) EQUALS JUST TRUE ``. I applied the same bracketing to every comparison of a call with `NOTHING` or an enumeration constructor.
3. **`MAYBE` of a named type needs backticks.** `` A MAYBE `Election of a dissenting offeree under s 206(5)` `` parses; the same without backticks fails with "unexpected" the number in the name.
4. **A record type with a backticked name needs backticks in `MEANS ... WITH`.** `` `Vote tally` WITH ... `` and not `Vote tally WITH ...`.
5. **`length` is not in the prelude.** Use `all`, a count by folding, or a numeric input.
6. **A module that uses a keel type must import the module that defines it.** `cb4-common` needed `IMPORT cbca-core-definitions` for `special resolution within s 2(1)`; the error names the missing definition, not the missing import.
7. **A failing assertion shows as an error, a refused one does not.** `l4 run` prints a failed assertion as an Error diagnostic and a refused one as a Warning; `check.sh` reads both (its header explains why the exit code is not enough).
8. **Two imported modules each defining an enumeration constructor of the same name.** The binary raised no diagnostic and the cases that used such a name still passed, but the keel's rule 3 (every top-level name unique across the whole Act) forbids it. Found by a script after the fact (`work/namecheck.py`, not a deliverable) and renamed: six constructors, listed in `PROGRESS.md`.
9. **`@export` with a `MAYBE DATE` input.** Accepted by `l4 run` and `l4 check`; whether a generated web wizard offers an optional date input is not something I could run here.

## Before this is worth relying on

1. **A person who knows the CBCA should read the fork register and findings 1, 2, 8, 12 and 14.** The encoding is a reading of a converter-made plain-text copy of the Act, written and tested by one agent; every expected value was written by the same agent that wrote the rules, with no second reader and no counsel. The tests show that the rules do what the agent understood the sections to say, not that the agent understood them.
2. **Nothing was checked against the official text, and the French version was not read.** The source is a converter's rendering; a dropped line, a mis-merged paragraph or a wrong marginal heading would be encoded faithfully.
3. **Judgments are inputs.** "Satisfactory to the Director", "reasonable grounds for believing", "the court is satisfied", "just and equitable", "oppressive or unfairly prejudicial" are plain BOOLEANs. The seat can ask a person whether these hold; nothing here can tell them.
4. **The regulations, the other Acts and the Quebec civil law are out.** The "prescribed period" of s 225(1), "prescribed law" of s 174, the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act, the Criminal Code and the securities laws are inputs. Where the Act says "in Quebec" (s 209(6)(b), s 222(1)(e)) the Quebec position is carried only as far as the text carries it.
5. **Time is counted in calendar days, weeks, months and years by the keel's convention (its F1), and nothing else.** No holiday roll-forward, no time of day, no business-day count. Two months from an end-of-month date clamps to the last day of the month (finding 20).
6. **Deadlines are rules about dates, not clocks.** "Forthwith", "immediately" and "without delay" have no day count (finding 21).
