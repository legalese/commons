**Conventions the whole folder follows**

- A rule is named `<what it decides> within s N(x)`; the coverage renderer maps rules to provisions by that suffix.
- A rule that says what follows in a stated case is TRUE when the case holds.
  FALSE then means only that this provision does not apply, never that the opposite is true.
- A rule whose result is a MAYBE returns NOTHING where the text is silent for the facts given.
- A rule whose text states only a necessary condition ("only if", "unless") is named for the condition met, not for the outcome.
- A defined term used to classify a person or thing is a plain BOOLEAN input named for it.
  A rule for the term exists where this piece defines it, so a caller who wants the answer worked out can call it.
- A rule calls its dependency inside a module when the dependency takes eight facts or fewer.
  Across modules the fact is a plain input whose comment names the rule that works it out.
- Anything that points outside ss 48-125 is a plain input with the comment `-- out of range: section N (piece X)`, and is listed under "Cross-references out of my ranges".
- Time limits use the keel's `Time limit` and `done within the time limit` (the keel's fork F1: the start day is day 0, the last day is the start plus N units, and both are in time).
- Fractions are NUMBERs written like `25%`, and money is a NUMBER of dollars.
- Dates are whole days (fork F-P10-8 and F-P10-12).
- Every provision's text is quoted verbatim in `-- src:N | ...` comment lines above the rule, and `python3 -I srcrefs.py check --refs` reports 1254 `src:` lines, 402 runs, 420 locators and 0 problems.
- I checked every type, constructor and rule name against the keel's code (176 type and constructor names and all 383 rule names).
  Two constructor names clashed with the keel's (`the Director` and `a managing director`) and were changed.
  I cannot check my names against the other pieces' names.

## Seat

The seat is a person who incorporates, directs or holds shares in a corporation: an incorporator, a director, an officer, or a shareholder, including a minority shareholder.
The questions ask what the Act lets that person do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.
There is no seat for the Director, a court, a registrar or any other official; where a question turns on what an official or the other party did, that act is a plain input the person states.

`cb2-seat.l4` has 330 questions, each with `@export` and a `@desc` on every input, grouped under `§` headings in the order of the text.
Every question calls exactly one layer-1 rule and restates none.
The file is generated from the compiled signatures of the layer-1 modules by a script that is not part of this folder, so that the inputs, their types and the order of the call cannot drift from the rules.
Inputs are plain: yes or no, numbers, dates, and the modules' own choices (an enumeration).
A fact that may be absent in the rule is asked as two inputs, "yes or no" and "the value", and the question builds the MAYBE itself.
A list of dates or numbers is asked as three inputs, with a date repeated or a number set to 0 if fewer apply.
A question that answers NOTHING is one the Act does not answer for the facts given.

`cb2-cases-seat.l4` has at least one case for every question.
For each question it ports up to eight rows of the layer-1 tests, chosen so that every answer value, every yes or no and every choice value in each position, and every pair of same-kind inputs that take different values, appear in some case where the tests hold such a row.
Seventy-nine cases assert NOTHING, the "not covered" value.
The last section, "Worked scenarios in the seat's words", has 45 cases written afresh in a questioner's terms, for example a founder asking whether a board of three, two of them outside, is enough (s 102(2)).
The expected values in the ported cases come from the layer-1 test rows, which I wrote from the text, so those cases check the wiring of the seat to the rules and not the law a second time; the 45 scenarios are the independent check, and were also written by me.

## Fork register

### F-P7-1: "issuer includes a corporation ..." is read as an open list (s 48(2), `issuer within s 48(2)`)

- The text, src:711-714: "issuer includes a corporation (a) that is required by this Act to maintain a securities register, (b) that, in Quebec, issues securities that confer directly or indirectly rights in its property, or (c) that, elsewhere in Canada, directly or indirectly creates fractional interests ...".
- Readings: (i) "includes" widens the ordinary meaning, so an issuer is anyone who issues securities in the ordinary sense, and also each of the three corporations [taken]; (ii) the list is closed and only the three corporations are issuers.
- Taken because the text says "includes", not "means", as it does for "bearer", "broker" and "holder"; the ordinary meaning is a plain input, which the caller states.
- To change it: drop the first disjunct of `issuer within s 48(2)`.

### F-P7-2: the three forms of a security are not said to exclude each other (s 48(4) to (6), `the forms of a security within s 48(4) to (6)`)

- The text, src:728-735: a security "is in registered form" if (a) or (b); a debt obligation "is in order form" where ...; a security "is in bearer form" if payable to bearer according to its terms and not by reason of any endorsement.
- Readings: (i) the definitions are independent tests and a security may meet more than one, so the rule lists every form the facts make out [taken]; (ii) the three are mutually exclusive.
- Taken because nothing in ss 48(3) to (6) says "only one", and a security that "specifies a person entitled" and also "is payable to bearer" is at least arguable.
- A security that meets none is answered with the named constructor `a security in none of the three forms in section 48`, never an empty list.
- To change it: make the rule return the first form that matches.

### F-P7-3: "scrip certificates equalling a full share" (s 49(15)) and the date a scrip certificate becomes void (s 49(16)(a))

- The text, src:785-786: scrip certificates "that entitle the holder to receive a certificate for a full share in exchange for scrip certificates equalling a full share"; src:789-792: scrip certificates "become void ... if not exchanged for a share certificate representing a full share before a specified date".
- Readings of s 49(15): (i) each whole share's worth of scrip is exchangeable, and a remainder of less than a whole share is not [taken]; (ii) only an exact whole-share total is exchangeable.
- Readings of s 49(16)(a): (i) the scrip certificate is void once the specified date is reached without an exchange made before that date, so it is void on the specified date itself [taken]; (ii) it is void only after the specified date.
- To change it: `FLOOR` in the rule for s 49(15); `AT LEAST` in the rule for s 49(16)(a).

### F-P7-4: "six years after the date of its cancellation" (s 50(7)(a), `the corporation, its agent or a trustee need not produce the instrument within s 50(7)`)

- The text, src:816: a cancelled security certificate in registered form need not be produced "six years after the date of its cancellation"; (b) and (c) say "after the date of its cancellation" and "after the date of its expiration".
- Readings: (i) from the day that is six years on from the cancellation date, counted by the core's convention (fork F1) [taken]; (ii) only after that day.
- Taken because "six years after the date" names a date, which is the sixth anniversary; (b) and (c) say "after the date", which is read as strictly later than that date.
- A cancellation on 29 February reaches 28 February six years on (the core's clamp).
- To change it: `AT LEAST` to `GREATER THAN` in the registered-form arm.

### F-P7-5: "shall, and any other corporation may" (s 51(2), `whether the corporation must or may treat the person as a registered security holder within s 51(2)`)

- The text, src:821-822: "a corporation whose articles restrict the right to transfer its securities shall, and any other corporation may, treat a person as a registered security holder ...".
- Readings: (i) one provision with two modalities, answered by a two-valued result (`must`, `may`), with NOTHING where the person has no listed capacity or furnishes no evidence [taken]; (ii) two provisions, a duty and a permission.
- Taken because the facts and the evidence are the same for both; only the corporation's articles decide which modality applies.
- To change it: split the result into two rules.

### F-P7-6: the carve-out in s 49(8) is expressed as "ineffective", not "effective" (s 49(8), `the item is ineffective against the transferee within s 49(8)`)

- The text, src:759: "No restriction, charge, lien, hypothec, agreement or endorsement ... is effective against a transferee ... who has no actual knowledge ... unless it or a reference to it is noted conspicuously".
- The rule is TRUE exactly when the subsection makes the item ineffective.
- FALSE does not mean the item is effective: where the transferee knew, or the item was noted, or it is not of the four kinds, the subsection says nothing and whether the item binds is for other law.
- The "constraint under section 174" is carved out of paragraph (a) by a separate constructor; the 174 constraints are cb4's.

### F-P7-7: "only to the extent of the interest or right purchased" is read as a quantity limit (s 60(3), `the purchaser acquires the right claimed within s 60(3)`)

- The text, src:906-907: "A purchaser acquires rights only to the extent of the interest or right purchased."
- Readings: (i) a scope rule, measured as a quantity: the extent of what the purchaser claims must not exceed the extent of what was purchased, both as NUMBERs in the same unit (a number of securities, or a fraction of the right) [taken]; (ii) a rule about kinds of interest (a purchaser of a lien does not acquire ownership), which would take an enumeration of interests the text does not give.
- Taken because the text names only the extent, and a caller who needs the second reading can state each interest as a unit of the same kind.

## What the text turns out to say

Each finding ends with "Asserted." where a test or a case asserts it, and says where.

1. The printed text of s 123(3) repeats a fragment: "unless within seven days after becoming aware of the resolution, the director aware or the resolution, the director (a) ... or (b) ...".
   I read it as "unless within seven days after becoming aware of the resolution, the director (a) causes a dissent to be placed with the minutes of the meeting; or (b) sends a dissent by registered mail or delivers it to the registered office of the corporation", and counted the seven days by the keel's convention.
   The repeated words are most likely an artefact of the plain-text rendering.
   Asserted: `cb2-tests-part10-duty.l4`, a dissent on 8 March 2024 for a resolution the director learned of on 1 March 2024 is in time, and one on 9 March 2024 is late.
2. The text of s 49(15.1) says "before the coming into force of this subsection" and gives no date, so the day it came into force is a stated input.
   Asserted: `cb2-tests-part7-certificates.l4`, with an invented commencement day of 1 January 2001 a bearer certificate issued on 31 December 2000 is caught and one issued on 1 January 2001 is not.
3. In s 106(3.4) a tie is not a majority, and no votes at all is not a majority either; abstentions do not count, since the test is of "the votes cast for and against".
   Asserted: `cb2-tests-part10-election.l4`, 51 for and 49 against elects, 50 for and 50 against does not, 0 for and 0 against does not.
4. In cumulative voting, s 107(g) lets a director be removed only if the votes in favour are greater than the product of the number of directors required by the articles and the votes cast against, so a bare majority never suffices.
   Asserted: `cb2-tests-part10-election.l4`, with five directors and 100 votes against, 500 votes in favour fail and 501 succeed.
5. The 25 per cent of s 105(3) is a floor on a count, so it rounds up: seven directors need two resident Canadians, three need one.
   Asserted: `cb2-cases-seat.l4`, "How many of our directors must be resident Canadians", 7 directors gives 2 and 3 directors gives 1; with the s 105(3.1) exception 3 directors give 2.
6. The two-year limit in s 119(3) runs from ceasing to be a director, counted in calendar years, so a director who ceased on 29 February 2024 is sued in time through 28 February 2026.
   Asserted: `cb2-tests-part10-liability.l4`, sued on 28 February 2026 is in time and on 1 March 2026 is late.
7. Under s 106(8) the one-third ceiling on appointed directors is a total of everything appointed since the previous annual meeting, not a limit on each appointment.
   Asserted: `cb2-cases-seat.l4`, nine directors elected at the last annual meeting, one appointed and two more proposed is within the ceiling (three), and one appointed and three more proposed is not (four).
8. The offence in s 85(6) is committed by using a list obtained under s 85 for none of the three purposes s 85(5) permits, without reasonable cause; the fine and the term of imprisonment are stated in a comment and not computed.
   Asserted: `cb2-tests-part8-trust-indentures.l4`, the rule is TRUE only when the list was obtained under s 85, the use falls under none of the three purposes, and the person acted without reasonable cause.
9. Section 124 gives the corporation a permission to indemnify (s 124(1)) and the individual a right to be indemnified in one case (s 124(5)): the costs of defending, where the individual was not judged at fault and meets the s 124(3) conditions.
   Asserted: `cb2-tests-part10-indemnity.l4`, the right exists for a director or officer who meets the conditions and not for someone who is neither.
10. S 122(1.1) lists factors the directors and officers "may consider, but are not limited to"; it creates no duty to weigh any of them, so the rule returns the listed factors as a permission.
11. Sections 49(5) and 115(2) are shown as repealed, and no rule is written for them.
12. The source file is an unofficial plain-text rendering of the consolidation current to 2026-09-30, so any difference from the official text, including the s 123(3) fragment, is not something I can check.

## Before this is worth relying on

- A lawyer needs to read it against the official text.
  The source is an unofficial plain-text rendering, and the one place where it is visibly damaged (s 123(3)) is a guess.
- The 38 fork rows are readings I chose.
  The ones that change an answer a person could act on are F-P10-2 (the resident Canadian count for a holding corporation), F-P10-4 (what "a greater number of votes" means), F-P10-5 (how a class-elected director is removed), F-P10-11 (the cap on a director's liability for wages after execution) and F-P10-15 (indemnity in a derivative action).
  Each is stated with the reading not taken and the one-line change that would switch it.
- Several answers depend on regulations that are not in the sources: the prescribed fee (s 49(2)), the prescribed business sectors, classes and circumstances (ss 105(3.1), 106(3.2), (3.3), (3.4), (8.1)).
  They are plain inputs, so a caller who supplies the wrong answer gets a confident wrong result.
- The tests and the seat cases were written by the same encoder as the rules, from the same reading of the text.
  They check that the code does what I meant and that the seat is wired to the rules; they do not check that I read the text correctly.
- A mutation sample was run on the layer-1 modules: one operator flipped per run in a copy, the module's own tests re-run.
  In Part X, 79 mutants gave 71 killed, 5 invalid (they did not compile) and 3 survivors; one survivor was a real gap (`the number of directors present PLUS 1` in s 114(4)), which I closed with two test rows and then killed, and two are equivalent mutants (`GREATER THAN` against `AT LEAST` where the equal case returns the same value).
  A second Part X sample of 30 mutants over the election, liability and office modules gave 29 killed and 1 survivor, again an equivalent `GREATER THAN` against `AT LEAST`.
  In Parts VII to IX, 80 mutants gave 76 killed, 4 invalid and no survivors.
  The sample is small (about eight per module) and not exhaustive.
- A second sample swapped two adjacent inputs in a seat question's call: 8 mutants gave 4 killed and 4 survivors, and all four survivors are swaps of operands that are symmetric in the rule (a conjunction or a product), where a swap cannot change the result.
- Nothing was run through a verifier: no property such as "no director can be both required and forbidden to act" has been checked, and the layer-1 rules are not a model of any process.
- `l4 check` and `check.sh` are clean, but `check.sh` was run with `L4_TARGET=unstable`, so how a main-build compiler reads these files was not tested here.
- Names were checked against the keel and not against the other four pieces.
  If two pieces declare the same top-level name the combined Act will not load.
