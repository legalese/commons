## Seat

**The seat** is the one the brief pins for every piece: a person who has lost work, or cannot work because of sickness, pregnancy, caring for a child or a family member, or who is self-employed or a fisher, asking whether they qualify for Employment Insurance benefits, how many hours or weeks they need, how much they would receive and for how long, and what they must do or tell the Commission.
The keel's seat is the same person.

**Why it fits Parts VI to VIII.3.**
These Parts say little about who qualifies; they say what happens around a claim.
A decision about the claim can be changed, reconsidered, appealed and paid meanwhile (ss 111 to 115).
The Commission can investigate, prosecute, certify and garnish a debt, inspect, and demand documents (ss 124 to 126).
Offences, fines, the Social Insurance Number and the sworn-evidence rules decide what can be done to the person and what they must do (ss 125 to 139).
Part VII makes a high earner repay part of the benefits (ss 144 to 151).
The rest (Parts VIII to VIII.3) is mostly powers to make regulations and orders, of which only what decides something for the person is encoded (s 153.1(1) and (3), s 153.3).
Officials have no seat: the Commission, the Minister of National Revenue, judges, authorized persons and the Board of Appeal appear as the other party in each relation, as what the person is owed or exposed to.

**Which instrument each rule comes from.**
Every rule, type and quotation in the `ei4-` files is from the Act (`src:N` is a line of `S.C.-1996-c.-23.txt`).
The Regulations are named in comments only where the Act leaves a fact to them: reg 78 (the form of a request for reconsideration), reg 56 (write-off), regs 80 to 82 (cases where benefits are suspended pending appeal), reg 89 (Social Insurance Numbers), reg 25 and s 2(5) (how many weeks were paid).
Those regulations belong to other pieces, so their outcomes are inputs here (see "Cross-references out of my ranges").

**Dates.**
This range holds no enactment date, assent date or commencement rule; the Act's coming into force is s 190 (piece ei6).
It states two dates, both carried as defined values with an `@ref`: 30 June 1996 (`s 145(3) June 30, 1996`) and 30 September 2020 (`s 153.3(9) September 30, 2020`).
Nothing is limited to a date range, so no rule takes a rule-effective date.

**Defaults followed (CLAUDE.md, ruled 2026-10-18).**
A judgment the text hands to someone else is asked as a conditional input and never guessed: the Commission's satisfaction (s 111), the further time it allows (s 112(1)(b)), its opinion that a person is avoiding payment (s 126(1)), a judge's satisfaction (ss 126(12), (13), (16)), the court's view that a case is appropriate (s 135(3)(a)(ii)), whether a person appears to the Commission to have an interest (s 124(3)), the Minister's notice (s 146(c)).
The Act's own definitions are worked out from facts through the keel (affidavit, benefits, special benefits, dwelling-house, confirmed delivery service, the Minister for Part VII); borrowed terms (income, taxation year, filing-due date) are inputs.
One case is the text's natural unit: one decision, one year's repayment, one offence, one interim order.
The text's own words name every rule and every input, with a plain gloss in a comment.
Departures: none from the defaults; the "Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers" default is met by naming the regulations and taking their outcomes as inputs, because they are another piece's work.

**What a seat answer means.**
A question that asks whether a power or an effect applies returns TRUE when the section gives it and FALSE when it does not; FALSE never means "forbidden" or "the opposite is the case".
A question that can fall outside the text returns a MAYBE and says so in its comment: NOTHING means the section does not speak (a Part IV offence in s 125(4); a repayment that is not under s 145 in s 145(5); an amount outside s 150; a duty that does not arise in s 139; no receipt in s 126(2) and (6)).
No MAYBE input is asked directly: it is asked as a yes/no and a date, and the date is ignored on "no".

## Jural relations

## What the text turns out to say

Each finding is a reading of the text in the folder; a finding that ends "Asserted." has a test or a seat case that asserts it, with the expected value written from the text before the run.

1. **Payment pending appeal needs both limbs of the exception, and the 21 days are counted from the day the decision was given.** Under s 114(1), once the General Division or the Board of Appeal allows a claim, benefits are payable in line with the decision even while an appeal is pending, and benefits paid after the decision are "treated as having been duly paid and are not recoverable from the claimant, even if the final determination of the question is adverse". Subsection (2)(a) takes this away only for an appeal to the Appeal Division brought within 21 days after the decision **and** on the ground that the claimant ought to be disentitled under section 36; (2)(b) adds any other case the Regulations prescribe (regs 80 to 82, piece er5). A decision given on 1 May: an appeal on that ground on 22 May stops payment; on 23 May it does not. Asserted.
2. **Reconsideration: 30 days, and some decisions are shielded.** A request made on the thirtieth day after the decision is communicated is in time; the thirty-first is not unless the Commission allowed more time (an input: the Commission's judgment). A decision under the Regulations about writing off a penalty, an amount payable or interest "is not subject to review under section 112" (s 112.1), however prompt the request; the Act does not say what remedy the person then has. Asserted.
3. **The text of s 126(5) reads "Part VII.I"** (capital letter I, src:1912) where Part VII.1 is meant; the rule reads it as Part VII.1. The same subsection and s 126(1) and (4) name "Part I, II or VII.1" correctly. Not asserted (a reading of a typographical oddity).
4. **"Other than Part IV" is said in most of s 125, not in all of it.** It appears in subsections (1), (2), (3), (4), (6), (7), (8), (11), (12), (13), (14) and (17), but not in (5) (the certificate), (9) (proof of documents), (10) (presumption) or (16) (proof of records in "a prosecution for an offence under this Act"). Subsection (4), the five-year limit, says nothing for an offence under Part IV, so the rule answers NOTHING there, not a date and not FALSE. Asserted (the NOTHING case; the other omissions are visible in the signatures of `s 125(5)`, `s 125(9)`, `s 125(10)` and `s 125(16)`, which have no Part IV input).
5. **Part VII does not round.** The only rounding rule in the Act's early Parts, s 6(2), is "in this Part" (Part I); s 145 has none. An income exceeding 1.25 times the maximum by one cent gives a repayment of 0.003 dollars, not zero and not a whole dollar. Asserted.
6. **Two subsections say the same death window in different words.** Section 145(4)(a): "dies after October in the year and before May in the next year"; s 146(a): "dies after October 31 in the year and before May 1 in the next year". Both are read as from 1 November to 30 April; the two rules agree at both edges. Asserted.
7. **"Exceeds" is strict.** An income exactly equal to 1.25 times the maximum yearly insurable earnings gives no repayment; one dollar over gives 30% of the lesser of the benefits and the excess (worked example: income 100,000, maximum 60,000, regular benefits 20,000: 25,000 excess, 30% of 20,000 is 6,000). Asserted.
8. **The ten-year exception, and 30 June 1996.** Subsection (2) excuses a claimant "paid regular benefits for less than one week in the ten years before the taxation year": zero weeks excuses, one week does not. Subsection (3) leaves out weeks beginning before 30 June 1996; 30 June 1996 was a Sunday, and a week begins on a Sunday (s 2(1) "week"), so the week beginning on that day is the first one counted. Asserted (including that the Sunday that begins the week of 30 June 1996 is 30 June 1996 itself).
9. **Which benefits count toward the repayment.** Section 145(1)(a) counts regular benefits under Part I and Part VIII and leaves out special benefits and benefits under Part VII.1; a payment under Part VIII.4 is not "benefits" at all, because s 2(1) limits the word to unemployment benefits under Part I, VII.1 or VIII and s 144 reads it "without reference to this Part". Asserted.
10. **"The Saturday after the first anniversary" is strictly after.** An interim order that takes effect on 12 March 2021 has its first anniversary on Saturday 12 March 2022, and ends on Saturday 19 March 2022, not on the anniversary. An order that takes effect on 29 February 2020 has its first anniversary on 28 February 2021 (the keel's `add years` clamps), a Sunday, and ends on 6 March 2021. The earliest of the order's own day, its repeal and that Saturday governs. Asserted.
11. **An interim order cannot touch the benefit repayment.** Section 153.3(2) bars an order "in respect of Part IV or VII" or of a regulation for those Parts, and s 153.3(9) ends the power on 30 September 2020: an order made on 1 October 2020, or one in respect of Part VII, is not validly made. Asserted.
12. **Contravention is punished less than false statements.** An offence under s 135(1) carries a fine of $200 to $5,000, plus up to double an amount tied to the benefits, and imprisonment of up to six months with the fine; contravening the Act or the Regulations (s 136(1)) or obstructing an authorized person (s 136(2)) falls to the general penalty of s 137, $100 to $2,000 or imprisonment of up to six months or both. Sections 138 and 139 state no penalty; a person who does not have a Social Insurance Number or does not report a change of name contravenes the Act, which s 136(1) makes an offence, and as no penalty is stated for it elsewhere s 137 applies. Asserted (the constants, the limits at each edge, and the s 137 liability rule).
13. **A benefit repayment cannot be certified under s 126(1).** The subsection lists amounts under "Part I, II or VII.1"; Part VII (the benefit repayment) is not in the list. A Part VII amount is a debt due to Her Majesty (s 150) recoverable in court or as the Income Tax Act provides (s 149). Under (a) the Commission may certify "without delay" if in its opinion the person is attempting to avoid payment; under (b) only after the 30 days (fork F-ei4-6). Asserted.
14. **Section 149 imports the Income Tax Act and cannot be applied from this text.** It applies a list of Income Tax Act provisions to Part VII "with such modifications as the circumstances require", and reads six words differently (paragraphs (a) to (d)) and one paragraph of that Act (paragraph (e)). Without the Income Tax Act the rules carry the list and the readings as data and answer NOTHING for a word the section does not list; what the listed provisions do (assessment, interest, penalties, objections, appeals) is not answerable. Asserted (the list, the readings and the NOTHING case).
15. **The claimant's own failure does not trigger s 130.** The section lets the Commission pay when "the failure or neglect of any person" makes "another person" lose the right to claim; read literally, the person who loses the right is a different person from the one who failed (fork F-ei4-10). The Commission's "may" gives the claimant no claim. Asserted (the true case and the false case).
16. **Where the text defers to something that is not among the inputs, the rules take its outcome as an input or return NOTHING:** the time limit for an appeal to the Board of Appeal (Department of Employment and Social Development Act); the Income Tax Act's income, taxation year and filing-due date; the published maximum yearly insurable earnings; what "qualified privilege" covers (s 129); what an "appropriate case" is (s 135(3)(a)(ii)); the circumstances in which the Commission may allow a longer period to request reconsideration (s 112(3)); the fishing regulations (s 153).
