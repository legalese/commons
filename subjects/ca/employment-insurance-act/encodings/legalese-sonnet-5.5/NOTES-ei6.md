## Seat

The seat is the individual to whom the Employment Insurance Act is applied: someone who has lost work, or cannot work because of sickness, pregnancy, caring for a child or a family member, or who is self-employed or a fisher.
They ask whether they qualify, how many hours or weeks they need, how much they will get and for how long, and what they must do or tell the Commission.
The seat is that person, never the Commission, the Minister, the Board or the employer; the Commons ruling of 2026-10-18 fixes it.
A provision that addresses only an official is encoded for what it means for the person, and is asked from the person's side ("May the Commission write off the amount I owe?", "Must the Commission decide my claim and tell me its decision?").
I wrote no second seat layer: the employer's duties in this range are s 153.8(4), s 153.195 and Schedule II paragraphs 13 to 15, encoded in layer 1 only, and an employer seat layer could be added later.
The seat layer is `ei6-seat.l4`.
Its questions are grouped under `§` headings in text order: Choices the person makes; Part VIII.4; Part VIII.5; Part VIII.6; Part IX; the tables of Schedules I, IV, V and VI; and the interim provisions of Schedule II.
Each question takes plain inputs (yes or no, numbers, dates, a few choices), builds the records and flags the layer-1 rules expect inside its own `WHERE`, and calls those rules by name; it never restates a rule and never edits layer 1.
Rates of unemployment are asked in per cent and divided by 100 in the seat; layer 1 takes fractions.
Because a person cannot say whether an Interim Order was repealed early, the questions about ss 153.14 and 153.196 pass `NOTHING` for the repeal date, so they assume the dates of the text (June 2, 2027; September 25, 2021; October 31, 2020; December 18, 2021).
The Part VIII.4 and VIII.5 groups are only worth asking of a person whose claim falls in 2020 or 2021, the Part IX group of one whose claim goes back to before 1997, and the Schedule II group of one whose claim falls from June 30, 1996 to January 5, 1997.

## What the text turns out to say

1. Subsection 159(1.1) lets the Commission deduct for undeclared earnings in a period "beginning after June 30, 1996", while subsection 190(3.2) says "beginning on or after June 30, 1996"; the two differ for a period that begins on June 30, 1996 itself, and the text does not say which governs. Both are encoded as written. Asserted.
2. The last two-week period a claim can cover starts on Sunday, September 20, 2020 (fork F-ei6-2), and its second week begins on September 27, 2020, which subsection 153.8(1.1) bars unless the benefit was paid or payable for an earlier week. Asserted.
3. Section 153.11 gives 28 weeks less the weeks of CERB or CESB with no floor; someone with more than 28 such weeks has a result the text does not state, so the encoding returns NOTHING. Asserted.
4. Subsection 153.5(3)(a) takes in sickness benefits (s 21) and self-employed sickness benefits (s 152.03) but not maternity, parental, caring or work-sharing benefits (ss 22 to 24); paragraph (3)(b) leaves out ss 21 to 24 as well. 5. Paragraph 153.6(1)(d) applies Part VI except subsections 112(3) and 114(2) and sections 115 and 140, so a request for reconsideration under s 112(1) and (2) is available while s 112(3) is not. Asserted.
6. Subsection 153.9(4) deems the stop-work and no-income requirements met when the income over four weeks is $1,000 or less; its text says the four weeks "succeed each other in chronological order but not necessarily consecutively", so the four weeks are those for which the benefit is paid, which is the input the encoding takes. Asserted.
7. The rate of the benefit is a flat $500 a week (s 153.10(1)), so a family supplement is the only variable, and it is an input out of range. Asserted.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Loud (an error tells you):
- `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` and `BECAUSE` are not used; a case outside the encoding is `NOTHING`.
- Every percent argument is bracketed, as `f (6.5%)`, as the Commons guide requires for the stable binary.
- A call with arguments on a continuation line must indent them further than the call's own first column; `incorrect indentation (got 13, should be greater than 31)` is the message when it does not.
- A call under `100 TIMES` whose arguments continue on the next lines, the first beginning with `(`, gave `unexpected (`; I put the call in a `WHERE` and multiplied the name instead.
- The prelude has `count`, not `length`; `elem`, `filter`, `map`, `mapMaybe`, `maximum`, `null` and `at` exist.
- `OTHERWISE` takes no `THEN`.
- `YMD` is used only from `eia-core`; I did not define it again.
Silent (the binary returns a wrong answer and says nothing):
- Positional record construction with `OF` takes the fields in declared order, so two fields of the same type could be swapped with no diagnostic: `ei6 Place for Schedule VI` has two NUMBER fields (the census division and the census subdivision). The seat builds that record in one place, and its cases assert a region for Quebec, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, Yukon and the other provinces and territories.
- An argument list one too short is an error, but a boolean in the wrong position among several booleans is not; the seat's longer questions (the eligibility question has 25 arguments) are therefore asserted case by case, one flag at a time.

## Before this is worth relying on

- Every figure and date is read from an unofficial plain-text rendering of the Act (current to 2026-09-30); nothing is verified against the official text, and the French version is not read.
- The expected values in `ei6-tests-*.l4` and `ei6-cases-seat.l4` were written by the same agent that wrote the rules, by hand from the text and before each run; they have not had an independent test pass.
- The Employment Insurance (Fishing) Regulations are not among the inputs, so every rule that depends on them (fisher earnings, the 8.1 benefits) takes the regulation's result as a plain input; a user of these questions must supply it.
- The sixteen forks are assumed, not ruled; the ones most likely to matter are F-ei6-2 (the last claimable period), F-ei6-4 (the $1,000 deeming), F-ei6-9 (which rules the cessation days gate) and F-ei6-14 (the instant of repeal).
- The repeal dates of the Interim Orders are not in the text; the seat assumes none was repealed early, so a repeal before June 2, 2027 or September 25, 2021 would change those answers.
- Part VIII.4 closed to new claims on December 2, 2020 and Part VIII.5 ceased in 2021; these questions are only useful for historical claims, and the Schedule II questions only for claims between June 30, 1996 and January 5, 1997.
- Judgments the text gives to the Commission or the Minister (undue hardship, "uncollectable", the dependants of the prescribed circumstances) are asked as facts about what they decided, never guessed.
- The regional rate of unemployment, the family supplement, the premium percentages and the former Act's 1996 rate are inputs from outside this range; a merged encoding should connect them to the rules of pieces ei1, ei3, ei5 and er1.
- The tables (Schedules I, II paragraph 18, IV, V) were copied by script and cross-checked in the script for 13 cells to a row and contiguous row bounds; they have not been compared cell by cell to the official Schedules.
