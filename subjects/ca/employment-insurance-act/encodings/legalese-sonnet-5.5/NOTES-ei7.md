Written by the piece agent `ei7` (Sonnet 5.5) on 2026-10-24 for the lead, who merges the fourteen pieces.
Target compiler: the stable `main` build `l4 1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`, sha256 `82ea44addf093ea4dd84c4e4416184920857ca9c762c5b31feed20541c20263e`.

**The text.**
Lines 3599 to 4201 of `S.C.-1996-c.-23.txt`, a plain-text rendering made by a converter script (`jl_xml2txt.py`) of the Justice Laws consolidation current to 2026-09-30, last amended 2026-06-18.
It is not the official text and is not marked up.
Nothing here is claimed to be verified against the official text; the French version, which is equally authoritative, is not read; historical notes and footnotes are not in the conversion.

**What the range is.**
`RELATED PROVISIONS` (lines 3599 to 4000): 70 transitional and savings provisions of amending Acts and of ten Interim Orders, shown by the consolidation for information.
`AMENDMENTS NOT IN FORCE` (lines 4001 to 4201): 20 amendments that the source shows as enacted but not yet in force.
The range has no section of the Act itself, so none of the Act's own rules is encoded here.
What a related provision still decides for a person is *which version of the Act governs* a benefit period, a claim or a week (the older text that continues to apply, or the newer text that applies only from a day), *which election* the person may still make and by when, and *what became of an appeal or request that was pending* when the appeal bodies changed.
The amended sections are other pieces' (ei1 to ei6); the older text that 'continues to apply' is not in the deposited texts, so the rules say THAT it governs and WHICH provisions it governs, never what they say.

**Which instrument each rule comes from.**
Every rule comes from the Act's file.
Two amending provisions are also shown in the Regulations' file, which piece er6 encodes: 2009, c. 2, s. 226 (the Regulations' file shows subsections (1) and (2); the Act's file shows subsection (2) only) and 2009, c. 30, s. 7 (the same words, except that the Regulations' file hyphenates 'claim- ant' at one line break).
Rule names in this piece begin `Act RP` (related provision of the Act), so the two pieces cannot define the same name.

**Dates.**
The range contains no date of enactment or commencement of the Act itself (the Act's commencement is s 190, piece ei6).
A day that the text states is a named constant with an `@ref` (for example `Act RP 2003 c. 15 s. 25(1) January 4, 2004`).
A day that an amending Act leaves to 'the day on which ... comes into force', to royal assent or to an order is not in the deposited texts: it is a DATE input of the rule that needs it, named for the day, with a comment.
There are eighteen such days (seventeen input names: 'the day on which the 2009 Act receives royal assent' serves 2009, c. 2 and 2009, c. 33) and they are listed under `## Cross-references out of my ranges`.
Nothing supplies a real figure for any of them; every test that uses one says so in a comment and picks a day to test the boundary.

**Method.**
A related provision whose dates are all past is still encoded when it decides which text governed a person's past benefit period (the consolidation is current to 2026-09-30, and a benefit period lasts up to a year and a claim can be reconsidered or appealed long after); it is `inert` as spent only when nothing a person could ask depends on it (the Crown's accounts, the tenure of former office-holders).
Each source line is quoted by script (`srcrefs.py quote`) and every rule carries an `@ref` with `src:` locators; `srcrefs.py check --refs` reports 0 problems.
Rules are named for what they decide, and each takes its own `GIVEN` (the stable binary rejects a `GIVEN` on a section).
A rule that cannot answer for a case the text does not cover returns `MAYBE` and `NOTHING`, and a test asserts it.
A judgment the text leaves to someone else (whether good cause for delay is shown, s 293(4)) is a BOOLEAN input named for the showing.

## Seat

The seat is a person who has lost work, or cannot work because of sickness, pregnancy, caring for a child or a family member, or who is self-employed or a fisher, asking whether they qualify for Employment Insurance benefits, how many hours or weeks they need, how much they would receive and for how long, and what they must do or tell the Commission.
It is the seat the brief fixes for the whole text, and it is the individual to whom the law is applied.
Officials have no seat: the Commission, the Minister, the board of referees, an umpire, the Social Security Tribunal and its Divisions appear only as what the person is owed or exposed to (a decision by a day, an appeal to the Appeal Division), as constructors of `ei7 Body named in a transitional provision about appeals`, or as the holders of a judgment the text leaves to them.
The employer's seat is not written in this piece.
For this range the person's questions are about the version of the Act that governs their benefit period, claim or week, about the elections of 2018, about appeals pending on April 1, 2013, and about the other special situations; the seat layer has 69 questions in 8 groups, each question with `@export` and `@desc`, plain inputs only, the layer-1 records and the core's types built inside it, and the same layer-1 rule called.

## What the text turns out to say

1. Most of the provisions of this range that pick a version of the Act test one day, and the day is not in the text: eighteen days are left to 'the day on which ... come into force', to royal assent or to an order (listed above). The rules therefore answer 'which version governs' only when the person supplies the day. Not asserted as one fact; each rule's tests use an invented day and say so.
2. The deemed premium rates are 2.2% for 2002, 2.1% for 2003 and 1.95% for 2005; for any other year the deeming sections say nothing and the answer is NOTHING (s 66 governs). The text prints 'Premium rates for 2002 and 200366.1Notwithstanding section 66' run together at src:3625 (the marginal note and the section number fuse in the conversion). Asserted.
3. In the deemed reg 55(10), the total weeks a claimant may combine cannot exceed 50, unless the section 225 maximum is 50 or more, when it is that maximum; at exactly 50 the two agree. Asserted.
4. 2010, c. 9, s. 4(1) to (3) are three nested cases that differ at the day itself: (1) needs the s 23(2) period not ended before the day (or ended and a deferral not ended); (2) needs the deferral ended before the day and the benefit period not ended; (3) needs the deferral ended before the day, both periods begun less than 104 weeks before it and both ended before it. A deferral that ends on the day itself is outside (2). Asserted.
5. The board of referees must decide by October 31, 2013 and, if that day passes without a decision, the General Division is seized and the appeal is deemed filed on November 1, 2013: the last day and the deemed filing day are consecutive. With an order under s 263(4) fixing a day D, the last day is D minus 1 and the deemed filing day is D itself. A decision made on November 1 is late. Asserted.
6. Sections 267 and 268 split the appeals before an umpire without a gap or an overlap: one filed and heard before April 1, 2013 stays with the umpire (267(1)); one filed and not heard before it gets leave deemed granted on April 1, 2013 (268). An umpire who has not decided by March 31, 2014 loses the appeal to the Appeal Division, with leave deemed granted on April 1, 2014. Asserted.
7. Section 293 has no end date for informing the Commission: the 30th day runs from the day the last notification is issued, and a late election needs only good cause for the whole delay (subsection (4)), so an election told eight years late can still be treated as in time. Asserted.
