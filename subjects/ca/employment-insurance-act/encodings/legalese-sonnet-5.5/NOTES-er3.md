# NOTES: Employment Insurance Regulations (SOR/96-332), regulations 35 to 57, piece `er3`

Written by the piece agent `er3` (Sonnet 5.5) on 2026-10-24 for the lead's merge of the Employment Insurance Act and Employment Insurance Regulations encoding.
It covers lines 540 to 992 of `SOR-96-332.txt`: Part I (benefits), third part, regulations 35 to 57 (determination and allocation of earnings, supplemental unemployment benefit plans, leave plans, the waiting period, sickness, pregnancy, medical certificates, the division of benefits, work-sharing benefits, prescribed employment measures, work-force reduction, part-time employment lost to a work stoppage, the end of a work stoppage, inmates, claimants outside Canada, employers' information, ratification of amounts paid, write-off, interest and deductions).

**The text.**
`SOR-96-332.txt` is an unofficial plain-text rendering, made by a converter script (`jl_xml2txt.py`), of the Justice Laws Website consolidation of the Employment Insurance Regulations current to 2026-09-30, last amended 2026-04-01 (`../../registers/source-bundle/*.meta.json`).
It is not the official text and nothing here is claimed to be verified against the official text.
The French version is equally authoritative and is not read; historical notes and footnotes are omitted from the conversion.
`src:N` in a comment or an `@ref` is line N of that file.

**Which instrument each rule comes from.**
Every rule in the `er3-*.l4` modules comes from the Regulations (SOR/96-332).
The Act (S.C. 1996, c. 23) is not quoted by this piece; where a regulation reads with a section of the Act (for example s 19, s 25 or s 36 of the Act), that section's outcome is a plain input named for the fact, with a comment `out of range: section N (piece X)`.
The keel's `eia-core` modules are imported (the date shim, `Period of days` and the rest of the shared vocabulary); no `eia-core*` file is edited or copied.

**Two layers.**
Layer 1 is the `er3-*.l4` modules other than the seat: one module per group of regulations, in the text's order, each rule quoted and cited.
Layer 2 is `er3-seat.l4` with its cases `er3-cases-seat.l4`: the questions a person who has lost work or cannot work asks, which call layer 1 and never restate it.
The tests of layer 1 are `er3-tests-*.l4`.

**Dates.**
Nothing in lines 540 to 992 states the date the Regulations were made or came into force (that is reg 95 and the Related Provisions, piece er6).
Two provisions of this range speak of their own coming into force without giving the date: reg 39.1(c) ("after the coming into force of this section") and reg 56.1(3) ("on or after the day this section comes into force").
Each takes the commencement date as a stated DATE input, named for the fact; the lead may replace it with a defined value if the commencement provisions of the whole text are merged.
Reg 36(10.1)(a) and reg 55.2 carry fixed dates in 2009 and 2010 (January 25, 2009 to May 29, 2010; January 4, 2009 to September 11, 2010 and October 25, 2009); they are encoded with `YMD` as the text gives them.

**How the files were made.**
The `.l4` files in this folder were produced from templates by a script that copied the quotation lines mechanically from `SOR-96-332.txt` (the same text `srcrefs.py quote` prints).
The templates and the script were scratch files kept outside this folder and were deleted before delivery; the `.l4` files here are the only copies, so edit them directly.
`python3 -I srcrefs.py check --refs SOR-96-332.txt er3-*.l4` is the gate: it checks every quotation line and locator against the text, and it must keep printing 0 problems.

## Seat

**The individual: a person who has lost work, or cannot work because of sickness, pregnancy, caring for a child or a family member, or who is self-employed or a fisher, asking whether they qualify for Employment Insurance benefits, how many hours or weeks they need, how much they would receive and for how long, and what they must do or tell the Commission** (the brief's seat, unchanged).
For this range that person asks: does a payment I received count as earnings and what is its value (reg 35); to which weeks is it allocated and how much falls on each (reg 36); is my employer's plan a supplemental unemployment benefit plan, and what part of my leave top-up is excluded (regs 37, 38); what is deducted for earnings in my waiting period, and is my waiting period waived (regs 39, 39.1, 40(6)); is my medical certificate enough, can the Commission make me be examined and who pays, which illness or pregnancy counts, what proof of pregnancy must I give (regs 40, 41, 41.2); how are the remaining weeks divided when the claimants cannot agree (reg 41.6); my work-sharing benefits (regs 42 to 49); which measures are prescribed and may I be paid after leaving under a work-force reduction process (regs 50, 51, 51.1); how many days of disentitlement for a part-time job lost to a stoppage, and has the stoppage ended (regs 52, 53); am I disentitled for being in an institution or outside Canada, and for how many weeks may I be paid there (regs 54, 55, 55.01); what will my employer tell the Commission, and can amounts I was paid be ratified (regs 55.1, 55.2); can what I owe be written off, does interest accrue and when does it stop, and can a deduction be made from my benefits for welfare or advances (regs 56, 56.1, 57).
Officials have no seat: the Commission appears only as the holder of judgments the text leaves to it (its being satisfied, its determination of a value, its direction to be examined), each taken as an input and never guessed.
The employer appears in reg 55.1 as the party that owes the information; the person asks what must be told about them, not what the employer must do.

The seat takes as plain inputs the figures the Regulations leave to someone else: the maximum yearly insurable earnings (regulation 36(6.1)), the value B (regulation 36(17); a person cannot state survival probabilities), the weeks the Act gives for a reason (regulation 55(7)), the days of the year for interest (regulation 56.1(3)) and the commencement days of regulations 39.1 and 56.1.
The Regulations' own figures (95 per cent in 37(2)(d), 85 per cent in 53, $100 in 56(1)(a), and so on) are the modules' constants, so an amendment would change the rules and not the questions, and a substitution by the Commission or the Minister of any of the inputs above would change the answers without changing the files.
Seat questions are grouped under `§` headings in the text's order, and a comment under each heading says in which situation the group arises, so that the form can first ask whether it has happened to the person.
The employer's, the Commission's and the debtor's side of the relations are in the Jural relations table; no second seat is written (the brief asks for the individual's seat only).
Eight questions take a list of numbers, which is not a plain input in the sense of the guide, because the text itself works week by week: the other earnings from the same employment on each week of a vacation (two questions), from the week of a lay-off (three) and from the first week awarded (one) of regulation 36(8) to (11), the weeks of each kind of benefit to be combined (regulation 55(10)), and the bank rates established in the month before (regulation 56.1(3)).
Each is a `LIST OF NUMBER` with one number per week or per rate, and the form may have to ask it as a repeated field; a week not listed counts as having no other earnings.

## What the text turns out to say


1. Regulation 37(1) takes payments under a supplemental unemployment benefit plan out of earnings only "for the purposes of section 19, subsection 21(3), section 45 or 46, subsection 152.03(3) or section 152.18 of the Act". It does not name section 14 of these Regulations (the interruption of earnings), which regulation 35(4) (subsection 14(2)) and regulations 35(5) and (6) (section 14) do name for their own exclusions. So regulation 37(1) does not take a payment from a plan that satisfies 37(2) out of the earnings that regulation 35(2) counts for deciding whether an interruption of earnings has occurred, and the rule returns false for that purpose. Asserted.
2. Regulation 36 words the "top up to normal weekly earnings" rules differently. Subsections (8)(a)(ii) and (9) say the claimant's total earnings from that employment are, in each consecutive week (except the last, in (9)), equal to the normal weekly earnings, so other earnings from the same employment on a week reduce what is put on it. Subsection (8)(b)(ii) says only that the amount "allocated under this subsection" equals the normal weekly earnings for each week except the last, so other earnings do not reduce it. The vacation-pay rules therefore behave differently for a specific vacation period and for any other vacation pay. Asserted.
3. Regulation 36(8)(a) allocates vacation pay for a specific vacation period only to weeks that end "not later than the last week of the vacation period or periods", and says nothing of vacation pay that is more than those weeks can take. The encoding reports the surplus (fork F5) and does not put it on any week. Asserted.
4. Regulation 48 gives the rate of weekly benefits under a work-sharing agreement as a ratio of the rate under section 14 of the Act and prescribes no rounding. The rule returns the exact ratio; the seat case with 7 of 40 hours not worked on a rate of 500 gives 87.5. Asserted.
5. Regulation 39(1) deducts the earnings of the waiting period "from the benefits payable for the first three weeks" and regulation 39(2) caps the total at one week's rate, but the text does not say how the amount is shared among the three weeks. The encoding returns the total, and a separate rule returns the number of weeks it is taken from (three). The split is not encoded. Asserted for the total and the three weeks.
6. Regulation 41.6 has three cases, (a), (b) and (c), whose rules are the same round-robin: weeks go to the claimants in turn, starting with the first claimant to make a claim, until the weeks run out (case (a) is just the instance with one week each). Clauses (b) and (c) differ only in their conditions (more weeks than claimants, fewer weeks than claimants); the words of what is to be done are the same. With 7 weeks and 3 claimants the shares are 3, 2 and 2, and with 2 weeks and 3 claimants the third claimant gets none. Asserted.
7. Regulation 55(1)(b) lets a claimant attend the funeral of seven kinds of relative beyond the immediate family (grandparents, grandchildren, and so on), while 55(1)(c) and (d) (accompanying, visiting) are for members of the immediate family only, and 55(1.1) cumulates a funeral and a visit only for the same person. A grandparent's funeral is covered; a visit to a grandparent is not. Asserted.
8. Regulation 55.01, for self-employed persons, repeats regulation 55(1)(a) to (d) but has no counterpart to 55(1)(e) and (f), the bona fide job interview and job search. A self-employed person abroad for either is not protected by 55.01. Asserted.
9. Regulation 55(7)(b) gives the maximum weeks by a table of whole hours (for example "420 - 454" and "455 - 489"). A number of hours between two rows (454.5) falls in no row, and so does any number below 420. The rule returns no answer for those and does not round. Asserted.
10. Regulation 51(1) says that the claimant who left in accordance with an employer work-force reduction process "may be paid benefits" where they accepted an offer to leave voluntarily and the employer has confirmed that the leaving preserved a co-worker's employment. The text does not say that the benefits are payable as of right; the rule answers only whether the conditions are met (fork F20). Asserted.
11. Regulation 56(2) lets the Commission write off the portion of an amount owing in respect of benefits received more than 12 months before the debtor was notified, "including the interest accrued on it". The text does not say how the interest charged under regulation 56.1 on the whole debt is divided between the older and newer portions, so the interest accrued on the older portion is a stated input. Asserted.
12. Regulation 56.1(6) says that a reconsideration under section 41, 52 or 111 of the Act is not an "appeal or other review" that stops interest from accruing under 56.1(5). A debtor whose only pending matter is such a reconsideration therefore still owes interest for that period. Asserted.
13. Regulation 40(6)(b) is rewritten by regulation 76.421 where provincial benefits were paid (piece er4); this piece encodes regulation 40(6) as written and does not read 76.421 into it. Not asserted.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Each item below was checked with the stable `l4` (1.6.98) on a file of a few lines while writing this piece, and the diagnostic quoted is what it printed.
Sorted by how the failure shows: the first four are loud (a parse or indentation error), the last three are silent (the file runs and the answer is not the one meant), so the silent ones are the ones to watch for.

Loud:

1. A hyphenated name that is not in backticks does not parse: `GIVEN work-sharing IS A BOOLEAN` gives `unexpected -`. Write `` `work-sharing` ``.
2. A parameter that is a `LIST OF` something cannot be followed by a comma and a second parameter on the same `GIVEN` line: `GIVEN xs IS A LIST OF NUMBER, n IS A NUMBER` gives `unexpected IS`. Put each such parameter on its own line without commas.
3. The arms of a `BRANCH` must start strictly to the right of the column of the `BRANCH` keyword: `incorrect indentation (got 3, should be greater than 3)`. `ELSE BRANCH IF ...` on one line puts the keyword in a column the next arm cannot clear, so put `BRANCH` on a line of its own.
4. A mixfix rule names its parameters in the order they appear in its head: `the seat's amount on week number n of amounts` with `GIVEN amounts ... n ...` gives `The names in a type signature must match those in the definition.` Declare `n` before `amounts`.

Silent:

5. `NOT (x) OR y` on one line is read as `NOT ((x) OR y)`, not `(NOT (x)) OR y`. A test `f TRUE TRUE` of `DECIDE f IF NOT (a) OR b`, which should be true on the second reading, is false, and nothing is reported. Write `(NOT (x)) OR y`, and parenthesise the whole operand of every `NOT`.
6. `EXPONENT` is not exact: `1.05 EXPONENT 2 EQUALS 1.1025` is an assertion failure, while `2 EXPONENT 3 EQUALS 8` is satisfied. The power in regulation 36(17), (1 + i) to the t, is therefore built by recursive multiplication (`reg 36(17) one plus the yield to the power of the number of years`), not by `EXPONENT`; regulation 56.1(3) is applied one month at a time and needs no power.
7. In the other direction `PLUS`, `TIMES` and `DIVIDED BY` are exact (`0.1 PLUS 0.2 EQUALS 0.3` is satisfied), so amounts such as 87.5 are exact and a test can compare them with `EQUALS`.
