## Seat

The seat is **a person who has lost work, or cannot work because of sickness, pregnancy, caring for a child or a family member, or who is self-employed or a fisher, asking whether they qualify for Employment Insurance benefits, how many hours or weeks they need, how much they would receive and for how long, and what they must do or tell the Commission**.
It is the seat the brief fixes for the whole text; it is the individual to whom the law is applied, as Commons requires.
There is no seat for the Commission, the Minister, the employer or the trustee: the Commission and the employer appear only as the other party in a right-and-duty pair, and what they may do to the person (reg 93(4), Schedule III ss 19(5) and 20(4)) is asked as what can be done to the person.

How this range answers it.
Most of this range is not about the person at all: it is a region list, four tables, the interim provisions of 1996 and the Related Provisions.
So `er6-seat.l4` has five groups, in the text's order:

1. Part VI, reg 93: whether a person with 600 or more hours but too few for section 7 qualifies for special benefits, gets a benefit period, is paid for a week, is refused for want of proof, and can move on to regular benefits (questions A1 to A8).
2. Schedules I and II: which region the person lives in, whether it is in a pilot project, and what a pilot project's table gives (B1 to B10).
3. Part VII: the transition from weeks to hours and the days things came into force (C1 to C15).
4. The Related Provisions: old claims (D1 to D14).
5. Schedule III: the interim provisions of 1996 (E1 to E19).

Groups 3, 4 and 5 arise only in a situation the person may not be in (a claim that relies on employment before 1997; a benefit period established years ago; employment or a record of employment in the second half of 1996); each heading's comment says so, so the form can ask "Does this describe you?" first.
The id of each question (A1 to E19) is the first line of its comment in `er6-seat.l4` and the heading of its cases in `er6-cases-seat.l4`.
Every question takes plain inputs (yes/no, numbers, dates, the province, the region, the reason for the claim, the kind of pay period), each with an `@desc` worded as the person would be asked.
The same fact has the same name and wording in every question that asks it.

Defaults ruled for Commons (2026-10-18) and where this piece departs from them.

- *Actors the text names:* followed.
- *A judgment the text hands to someone else* is answered conditionally: for example, whether section 7 is met, whether a week is a week of unemployment, whether a claimant is one "referred to in subsection 55(7)" are inputs, never guessed.
- *The Act's own definitions are worked out from facts:* followed where the core has them (`s 2(1) insured person`, `s 2(1) special benefits`, `s 2(1) claimant`).
- *The text's own words, each with a plain gloss:* followed in the rule names; the glosses are in comments.
- *Machinery out:* the Amendments Not in Force and the lead-in sentences of Schedule III are `inert`; reg 95 is not machinery here, because it fixes the days on which the interim provisions apply, and the brief says dates are data.
- *Departure, recorded not fixed:* the region of Schedule I is found from census facts (province, census division, census subdivision, metropolitan area, agglomeration, "east of London"), which a person can state but may have to look up; the seat also lets a person who already knows their region name it (B2 to B6).
- *The figures the person cannot state from the text* are asked as facts and say so in the `@desc`: the day reg 93(4.1) came into force, the hours that the table to section 7(2) of the Act requires, the day of royal assent of the Budget Implementation Act, 2009, the day the Regulations SOR/2002-280 came into force.
  A different figure would change the answers; none is guessed.

For 420 to 454 hours in the band "more than 13% but not more than 14%", II.2, II.7 and II.93 give 31 weeks and II.94 gives 35; at 1820 hours or more in the lowest band they give 41 and 45. (Where: tests of the cells; cases B7 to B10.) Asserted.
2. **Schedule II.6 lists "Price Edward Island"**, which is no region of Schedule I; the other four lists name Prince Edward Island. The rule answers NOTHING for the region of Prince Edward Island and a yes or no for every other region. (Where: fork F13; case B3.) Asserted.
3. **Reg 93(1)(b) fixes a threshold of 600 hours**, where section 7 asks for a number of hours that depends on the regional rate of unemployment. A person with 599 hours does not qualify under reg 93(1); one with 600 does. (Where: tests and case A1.) Asserted.
4. **Reg 93(1) cannot take anything from a person who qualifies under section 7:** it speaks only of one who "does not qualify to receive benefits under section 7". Such a person gets FALSE from the rule, not a different route to the same benefits. (Where: case A1.) Asserted.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Binary: `l4 1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`.

**Measured by this piece** (a probe or a run, each reproduced in this session):

- A chain of 62 `PLUS` terms, `(IF a THEN 1 ELSE 0) + (IF b THEN 1 ELSE 0) + ...`, did not finish type-checking in 60 seconds (the first test file I wrote ran for over five minutes with no output); the same count written as `sum (map (GIVEN b YIELD IF b THEN 1 ELSE 0) (LIST a, b, ...))` checks and runs the whole file of 422 assertions in under 3 seconds. Avoid long `PLUS` chains; use a list.
- `MIN` and `MAX` do not exist as functions: `I could not find a definition for the identifier MIN`. `minimum` is ambiguous (the prelude has two definitions). Write `IF a AT MOST b THEN a ELSE b`.
- The list-length function is `count`, not `length`: `length` gives `I could not find a definition for the identifier length`.
- A constructor with a field is built by applying it (`` `er6 a multiple of a week` 2 ``) and matched as `WHEN ctor n THEN`.
- A `LIST` may be written over several lines inside brackets, with the commas leading each continuation line.
- A DATE takes `PLUS` and `MINUS` with a NUMBER of days, and `AT LEAST`, `AT MOST`, `LESS THAN` and `GREATER THAN` compare two DATEs, including a `YMD` literal on the right (the core's shim).
- An accented or dashed backtick name (`` `er6 Chicoutimi — Jonquière CMA` ``) and a typographic apostrophe (`St. John’s`) are accepted in identifiers.

**Inherited from the keel and from the skill's `dialects.md`, and respected:** `YMD` only through the core; `f (6.5%)` not `f 6.5%`; a record type with spaces backticked before `OF`; no `(f x)'s field`; no section `GIVEN`; no `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED`, `BECAUSE`, `TYPICALLY`, sets, the ledger or `EVERY`; one-line `#ASSERT`.
