**Which instrument each rule comes from.**
Every rule in this folder comes from the Act (Parts III, IV and V, `src:1127` to `src:1803`).
The Regulations (SOR/96-332) are named only in comments: regs 62 to 76.45 (Parts III and III.1 of the Regulations, piece er4) exercise s 69; reg 21 and others use "insurable earnings determined pursuant to Parts III and IV of the Act".
The Regulations that actually prescribe the amounts, times, rates and persons the Act's Part IV leaves to "the prescribed" are the Insurable Earnings and Collection of Premiums Regulations (SOR/97-33), which are not among the inputs: every such figure is a plain input here.

## Seat

The seat is the one the brief fixes: **a person who has lost work, or cannot work because of sickness, pregnancy, caring for a child or a family member, or who is self-employed or a fisher, asking whether they qualify for Employment Insurance benefits, how many hours or weeks they need, how much they would receive and for how long, and what they must do or tell the Commission.**
For Parts III, IV and V that person asks about the premium taken from their pay, the refund of premium, rulings about their employment, appeals, being asked for information, and what the law lets others do to them or in their name.
No official has a seat: the Commission, the Minister of National Revenue, an authorized person, a judge, the Tax Court of Canada and the Governor in Council appear in layer 1 as the holders of powers and duties and in layer 2 only as what the person is owed or exposed to.

Seat questions are asked only for the person in that seat.
Provisions that bind other individuals (a director, s 83; a responsible representative, s 86; an officer of a corporation, s 107; an employer, ss 82, 82.01, 82.1, 85, 87, 92, 96(3.1) and 96(6) to (9)) are encoded in layer 1 and listed under `## Jural relations`; no question about them is asked in `ei3-seat.l4`, which is for the insured person.
They are candidates for an employer seat layer and a director/representative seat layer, which the lead may add over these same modules.
Provisions that bind "any person" (s 88(1)(b), (2), (5), (13); s 100; s 106(2), (4), (5)) are asked in the seat, because the person in the seat is one of "any person".


**The seat layer** is `ei3-seat.l4` (88 questions, each with `@export` and `@desc`, grouped under `§` headings in the text's order) with its worked cases in `ei3-cases-seat.l4` (218 assertions; every question is the subject of at least one case, checked by script).
Every question name carries its section in the name, so the names cannot collide with another piece's.
A rate is asked as a percentage and divided by 100 in the seat; the layer-1 rules take a fraction.
A question whose rule can answer "not covered" returns a MAYBE value (NOTHING); a refund question returns `ei3 Refund` (`ei3 no refund is payable under the provision` or `ei3 a refund is payable` with the amount), never 0.

What the seat does not ask: provisions of other individuals (an employer under ss 68, 82, 82.01, 82.1, 85 except 85(5), 87, 92, 96(3.1) and 96(6) to (9); a director, s 83; a responsible representative, s 86; an officer or signing officer of a corporation, ss 101 and 107), and provisions that address only officials, the Crown's accounts or its collection machinery (ss 65.21 to 66.6, 69(1), 70.2 to 76, 77(1), 77.1 to 81, 97 to 99, 108 other than (1)(c), 109).
Those are in layer 1 and the coverage table.

## Needs from the core

Nothing blocks. Every out-of-range fact above is a plain input, and no module of this piece changes if the core changes.
Three things would help the merge, in this order of usefulness:

1. **A shared "amount or nothing payable" type.** `ei3 Refund` (`ei3 no refund is payable under the provision` | `ei3 a refund is payable` HAS an amount) is defined in `ei3-part4-refunds.l4` and used only there. If other pieces return amounts that may be "none payable under the text's own gate" (benefit rates, repayments), one shared union in the core would let them agree on the shape; as it is, each piece will invent its own. This piece uses MAYBE NUMBER where the text says nothing, and `ei3 Refund` where the text itself says no refund is payable; the two must not be confused.
2. **More `Party` constructors.** `Party` has the claimant, the insured person, the self-employed person, the employer, the Commission, the Minister and the Minister of National Revenue. Parts III to V also name the Minister of Finance, the Governor in Council, the Treasury Board, the Tax Court of Canada, the Commissioner of Revenue, the Receiver General, an authorized person and a judge. They are BOOLEAN inputs here (for example `is a person authorized by the Minister for the purposes of this Part`); if the core adds them, `ei3-part4-deduction.l4` and `ei3-part4-rulings-appeals.l4` could take a `Party` instead.
3. **A pass-through for s 2(1) "insurable earnings".** The keel names the term but the figure is an input everywhere; a single keel rule would let ei1, ei3 and ei5 feed the same value.

## What the text turns out to say

1. **Section 67 states no cap.** The premium is the insurable earnings multiplied by the rate, and the section says nothing of the maximum yearly insurable earnings. The maximum bites in two other places: s 82(2) (the employer stops deducting once the earnings it has paid in the year have reached the maximum) and s 95 (premiums deducted above the maximum are an overpayment). A person with 100,000 of insurable earnings at 1.63% has a s 67 premium of 1,630, not the premium on the maximum. Asserted (`ei3-tests-part3-premiums.l4`, the 100,000 case; `ei3-tests-part4-deduction.l4`, s 82(2) at 38,999.99, 39,000 and 45,000 against a maximum of 39,000).
2. **Section 95 does not say how to find the premiums "deducted from the insurable earnings in excess of the maximum".** Read as the premiums actually deducted minus the premium on the earnings up to the maximum, never below zero (fork F-ei3-20), the answer for two employers each paying 30,000 against a maximum of 39,000 at 1.63% is 342.30 (978 deducted, 635.70 owed on 39,000). The reading "rate x earnings above the maximum" gives the same 342.30 there but differs when deductions were not at the full rate: 900 deducted on 60,000 gives 264.30 under the encoded reading and 342.30 under the other; with only 300 deducted the other reading would give 342.30, more than was deducted in all, and the encoded reading gives 0. Asserted (`ei3-tests-part4-refunds.l4`, s 95 cases).
3. **Section 96(2) and s 96(10) conflict after a late appeal decision.** Section 96(2) gives its own window (30 days after an appeal decision is communicated); s 96(10) says a refund "under this section is payable only if" the application is made within three years after the end of the year the premium was deducted. An appeal decision can arrive after those three years. The encoding applies s 96(10) to the subsections that state no window of their own (ss 96(4), (5), (5.1), (6) to (8.98)) and lets s 96(2) stand on its 30 days (fork F-ei3-21). Asserted for the 30-day window only: `s 96(2)` takes no year argument (`ei3-tests-part4-refunds.l4`, decision 1 March 2026, application 31 March and 1 April 2026).
4. **The temporary refunds of s 96(6) to (8.98) are dead in practice.** They are for the years 1997, 1998, 1999, 2000, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2015 and 2016 (the text has none for 2014), and s 96(10) allows an application only within three years after the end of the year. The latest, 2016, closed on 31 December 2019. They are still in the text, so they are encoded in force with the year as a literal. Asserted (`ei3-tests-part4-refunds.l4`: 2016 in time on 31 December 2019, not on 1 January 2020, not on 30 September 2026).
5. **The cap in s 96(8) is continuous, and s 96(8) is silent above $60,000.** For a 1996 employer's premium under $50,000 the cap is $10,000; from $50,000 to under $60,000 it is $60,000 minus the premium (exactly $10,000 at $50,000, $5,000 at $55,000, $1 at $59,999). Sections 96(6) and (7) apply only if the 1996 premium is under $60,000, so at $60,000 or more there is nothing for (8) to cap and the encoding answers NOTHING. Asserted (`ei3-tests-part4-refunds.l4`, s 96(8) at 49,999, 50,000, 55,000, 59,999, 60,000).
6. **The s 82(9) penalty ladder has no gap and (b) prevails.** On the Receiver General receiving the amount: on the due day but not in the manner required, 3%; up to three days late, 3%; four or five days late, 5%; six or seven days late, 7%; not paid by the seventh day after it was due (including never), 10%; and 20% under (b) when a penalty under the subsection was already payable in the year and the failure was knowing or grossly negligent. "Subject to paragraph (b)" gives (b) the priority (fork F-ei3-11). Asserted at 0, 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 days late, and for (b) (`ei3-tests-part4-deduction.l4`).
7. **The interest in s 82(8) cannot be computed from the Act.** The rate is "prescribed" and the Regulations are not deposited. The duty to pay it and the period it runs over are encoded; the dollars are not (fork F-ei3-12). No assertion: there is no value to assert.
8. **Section 99(a) reads "152. 24" in the converted text, with a stray space.** It is read as section 152.24 (the Minister's duty to examine and assess a return of self-employed earnings under Part VII.1, the counterpart of s 85; `src:2594`). The string value in the rule has no space. Asserted (`ei3-tests-part4-administration.l4`, line 44, `EQUALS "section 85 or 152.24, as the case may be, of the Employment Insurance Act"`).
9. **Section 106(3)'s minimum can exceed its maximum.** A fine "of not less than $25 a day for each day of default, but not more than $1,000 in all": for more than 40 days the daily minimum (25 x days) is above the ceiling. The ceiling is read as prevailing (fork F-ei3-24). Asserted at 0, 10, 39, 40 and 41 days (`ei3-tests-part4-offences.l4`: 0, 250, 975, 1,000, 1,000) and in the seat (`ei3-cases-seat.l4`, case BQ).
10. **Section 90(2) is strictly "before the June 30".** A request made on 30 June is too late; on 29 June it is in time (fork F-ei3-18). Asserted (`ei3-tests-part4-rulings-appeals.l4`; seat case AC).
11. **The two-stage time limit of s 103(1) runs to day 180.** The appeal is in time within 90 days after the decision is communicated; or, if the Court has allowed a longer time on an application made within 90 days after those 90 days, within that longer time. For a decision communicated on 1 March 2026 the first stage ends on 30 May and the application for more time on 28 August 2026. Asserted (`ei3-tests-part4-review.l4`, lines 29 to 34).
12. **A hand-arithmetic error in a test, caught by the rule.** In `ei3-tests-part4-rulings-appeals.l4` I first wrote the expected value for s 91 with a decision notified on 3 March 2026 as "31 May". The rule gave 1 June. I recomputed by hand (28 days to 31 March, 58 to 30 April, 89 to 31 May, so the 90th day is 1 June) and by Python's `datetime`; the expected value was wrong, the rule right. The expectation was corrected and a comment records the correction; the rule was not changed. Asserted (the corrected case).
13. **Section 66(3) reads "[No subsection (3)]"** in the converted text, a placeholder for a number the Act skips. Nothing is encoded for it (coverage table).
14. **Section 80.1 excludes Parts IV and VII from its own interest regime** (subsections (1) and (4) say "other than Parts IV and VII"). Interest on a Part IV amount is therefore not under s 80.1: ss 82(8) and 96(13) each provide for interest at a prescribed rate. The 72-month limit in s 80.1(3), which speaks of interest "due under this section", does not by its terms reach it. Asserted for the 72 months themselves (`ei3-tests-part3-account.l4`, 31 March 2020 to 31 March 2026 in time, 1 April 2026 not, and the 29 February 2020 start clamped to 28 February 2026) and for s 80.1(4) (NOTHING for a Part IV or Part VII amount).
15. **Section 104(2) is final "except as otherwise provided in this Act"**, and s 105 makes the Tax Court's decision final "except for an appeal under the Federal Courts Act". The first is a BOOLEAN, true unless the Act otherwise provides; the second returns JUST FALSE for any other proceeding and NOTHING for the Federal Courts Act appeal (the Act leaves that to the other Act). Asserted (seat cases BM and BO).

## What the stable binary does not accept

Binary: `l4` 1.6.98, the stable `main` build named in the brief (sha256 `82ea44addf093ea4dd84c4e4416184920857ca9c762c5b31feed20541c20263e`, checked with `shasum -a 256`). The keel's own notes record the same version as `(main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`; that commit and date are the keel's, not read from the binary here.
Measured by this piece (each reproduced in this session):

- **A percentage after `JUST`.** `JUST 20%` reads as `(JUST 20)%`, and the `%` then expects a NUMBER but gets a MAYBE NUMBER. Write `JUST (20%)`. This was hit in `ei3-part4-deduction.l4` (s 82(9)).
- **`NOT EQUALS`.** `x NOT EQUALS y` does not parse. Write `NOT (x EQUALS y)`. Every NOT is parenthesised throughout.
- **A constructor pattern with a field.** In `CONSIDER`, a constructor that carries a value is written `WHEN ctor x THEN ...`, not `WHEN ctor WITH x`. This was hit on `ei3 Refund` in `ei3-part4-refunds.l4`.
- **`add years` and `add months`.** They are backticked names applied prefix: `` `add years` d n `` (not `d add years n`). Used in `ei3-part3-account.l4` (s 80.1(3), 72 months), `ei3-part4-administration.l4` (s 102(4)) and `ei3-part5-pilot-projects.l4` (s 110).
- **A multi-line mixfix argument in a THEN branch** does not parse; the error reads "expects 2 arguments". Bind the argument with WHERE or move it to a helper rule. This was hit in s 96(5.1) (`ei3-part4-refunds.l4`).
- **A mixfix call after `AT MOST`** can be fragile; it is parenthesised wherever it occurs.
- **Top-level rules may be used before they are defined.** That helps (a rule can sit where the text puts it) but a typo in a name reads as an "I could not find a definition" error far from the cause.
- **`@export` with a tagged-union `GIVETH`** passed `l4 run` in a probe (the seat's refund questions return `ei3 Refund`). Whether Legalese Studio's Interact form can render a tagged union was not tested here.
- **Diagnostics print twice** in `l4` output; `check.sh` counts accordingly.
- **No `REFUSE`, no `#ASSERT REFUSED` and no `BECAUSE`** are used (the stable binary does not take them), so no rule gives a reason trace. A refund that is not payable says so by a constructor (`ei3 no refund is payable under the provision`), not by a refusal.
- **A failing assertion is counted.** A negative control (a probe file with one wrong and one right expected value, since deleted) printed `errors 1, satisfied 1, failed 1`, so `check.sh` does see a failure.

## Before this is worth relying on

1. **Compare the quotations with the official text.** Every `-- src:N` line is copied from the plain-text conversion, and the conversion is not the official Justice Laws text (the stray space in s 99(a) shows it can be wrong). A reader with the official Act should check the quotations of the sections that move money: ss 66, 82(9), 95, 96, 106.
2. **Rule on the forks that change an answer.** F-ei3-20 (s 95), F-ei3-21 (s 96(2) against s 96(10)), F-ei3-9 (s 82(2) "have reached"), F-ei3-11 (s 82(9)), F-ei3-18 (s 90), F-ei3-24 (s 106(3)). The rest change a boundary day or a label.
3. **Deposit the Insurable Earnings and Collection of Premiums Regulations (SOR/97-33).** Every "prescribed" figure, time, rate and person in Part IV is a plain input here, so no answer in Part IV is a number until they are. The interest in s 82(8) cannot be computed without them.
4. **Write the employer seat and the director/representative seat.** Ss 68, 82, 82.01, 82.1, 85, 87, 92, 96(3.1), 96(6) to (9), 101 and 83, 86, 107 are in layer 1 and tested, but no seat asks them. Each would be a module `ei3-seat-<party>.l4` over the same layer-1 modules.
5. **Decide whether the spent provisions stay in force.** Ss 96(6) to (8.98), 98(2) and (3) and 73.1 are in the consolidation, so they are encoded; they are practically dead (finding 4).
6. **An independent test pass has not been run.** The rules, the tests and the cases were written by one agent, and each expected value was written by hand from the text, not copied from the output (a failing assertion would have been a finding), but a second reader writing cases blind to the rules has not done so.
7. **The seat has not been run through Legalese Studio's Interact form.** `l4 check` and `l4 run` accept it. Whether the form renders the `ei3 Refund` tagged union, and whether each `@desc` reads well to a person, was not tested.
8. **No deontic or temporal verification was run.** The stable binary takes neither `REFUSE` nor `BECAUSE`, so there is no reason trace and no check for the double-binds the language is meant to find. In this range the likeliest candidate is s 96(2) with s 96(10), which is a conflict of windows, not of obligations.
