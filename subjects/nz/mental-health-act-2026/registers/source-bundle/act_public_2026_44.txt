Mental Health Act 2026
1 Title
This Act is the Mental Health Act 2026.
2 Commencement
This Act comes into force on 1 July 2028.
However, section 10, in so far as it relates to clauses 5 and 6 of Schedule 1, comes into force on 10 July 2026.
 Part 1 — Preliminary provisions
 Purpose
3 Purpose
The purpose of this Act is to provide for compulsory mental health assessment and care in a manner that—
promotes the decision-making capacity of tāngata whaiora, including while they are subject to compulsory care; and
improves equity in mental health outcomes among New Zealand’s population groups by striving to eliminate mental health care disparities, in particular for Māori; and
safeguards the rights of tāngata whaiora; and
protects the safety and well-being of tāngata whaiora and their communities.

 Te Tiriti o Waitangi
5 Te Tiriti o Waitangi (Treaty of Waitangi)
The following provisions of this Act provide for the Crown’s intention to give effect to the principles of te Tiriti o Waitangi (the Treaty of Waitangi):
section 3(b), which states that it is a purpose of this Act to provide for compulsory mental health assessment and care in a manner that improves equity in mental health outcomes among New Zealand’s population groups by striving to eliminate mental health care disparities, in particular for Māori:
section 6, which establishes a compulsory care principle that supports whanaungatanga and recognises the importance of family and cultural ties:
section 17, which provides for hui whaiora (well-being meetings) that may be convened to assist, among other things, tāngata whaiora to make decisions about their care:
section 27, which provides that a person under compulsory care is entitled to proper respect for their cultural, ethnic, and individual identity and their religious or cultural beliefs:
section 41, which provides for the approval of advisers with expertise in matters involving tāngata whaiora Māori to advise on complaints and inquiries under this Act:
section 43, which requires provision of a rōpū whaiora (collaborative care team) for each person under compulsory care that includes the expertise necessary to meet the needs of the person, including cultural expertise:
section 44(4)﻿(a) and (b), which requires a care plan for each person under compulsory care that includes a holistic assessment of the person, including cultural considerations in relation to the person, and non-pharmaceutical options for care:
sections 164(4)﻿(b) and 174(3)﻿(b), which require the membership of a Mental Health Review Tribunal and the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal to include knowledge of tikanga and mātauranga Māori.
 Compulsory care matters
6 Compulsory care principles
For the purpose of this Act, the compulsory care principles are as follows:
compulsory care should be used only—
to protect, promote, and improve a person’s mental health; and
if suitable care is available to protect, promote, and improve a person’s mental health:
compulsory care should be applied in the least restrictive manner, for example,—
voluntary care options should be actively offered and preferred unless they are unlikely to be sufficient to prevent the need for compulsory care; and
compulsory care should be applied for no longer than is necessary; and
support available to the person, including whānau and cultural support, should be used to reduce the need for compulsion and to support whanaungatanga:
compulsory care should—
seek at all times to encourage the person to develop and exercise capacity and choice; and
reflect the needs of the person, including their cultural needs, and be responsive to any trauma experienced by them; and
be delivered in a manner that is responsive to, and guided by, the person’s will and preferences (including any advance mental health directive made by the person); and
recognise the importance and significance of the person’s ties to their family, whānau, hapū, iwi, and family group, and the contribution those ties make to well-being:
compulsory care should recognise the person’s right to take reasonable risks to achieve personal growth, self-esteem, and quality of life.
The following must be guided by the compulsory care principles, to the extent that is reasonably practicable and having regard to all the circumstances:
a court, tribunal, or person performing a function or duty or exercising a power under this Act in respect of a person:
a court or tribunal conducting proceedings under this Act in respect of a person.
7 Compulsory care criteria
The compulsory care criteria under this Act are met if—
the person has seriously impaired mental health; and
the seriously impaired mental health causes, or is likely to cause in the near future, in the absence of care, serious adverse effects; and
the seriously impaired mental health causes the person to lack capacity to make decisions about their mental health care.
Serious adverse effects means—
serious physical harm to self or others; or
serious psychological harm to others; or
serious deterioration in the person’s mental or physical health.
8 Meaning of seriously impaired mental health
Seriously impaired mental health means a serious impairment of mental functioning (which may be continuous or intermittent) that is characterised by delusions or by disorders of mood, perception, volition, or cognition.
However, a person cannot be assessed to have seriously impaired mental health—
on the basis of—
sexual orientation; or
gender identity; or
solely on the basis of—
political, religious, philosophical, or cultural beliefs, values, or opinions; or
lack of social engagement; or
criminal or antisocial behaviour; or
substance use; or
intellectual disability; or
previous mental health care; or
refusal to access mental health care; or
neurological or other brain diseases resulting in impaired mental state; or
seriously impaired mental health arising from primary physical illness.
9 Meaning of capacity to make decisions about mental health care
A person is presumed to have capacity to make decisions about mental health care unless they cannot, on a sustained basis,—
understand the mental health care options available to them; or
understand the consequences of making a particular decision to accept or not accept mental health care options; or
retain, use, and weigh relevant information to make decisions about mental health care options; or
communicate decisions about mental health care in any way.
A person who determines whether a person lacks capacity to make decisions about mental health care—
must have regard to any relevant guidelines issued under section 214; and
may disregard brief or intermittent indications of capacity, for example, the states described in subsection (1)﻿(a) to (d).
Subsection (2)﻿(a) does not apply to a Mental Health Review Tribunal, the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal, a court, or a judicial officer.
However, a person cannot be considered to lack capacity to make decisions about mental health care solely on the basis—
that they have seriously impaired mental health; or
that they are unable to understand matters of a technical or trivial nature; or
that they can retain information relevant to the decision for a limited time; or
that they need support to make or communicate decisions; or
that their decision making results in, or may result in, poor outcomes for the person; or
of their age.

 Part 2 — Tāngata whaiora rights and support
 Subpart 1 — Advance mental health directives
12 Advance mental health directives
An advance mental health directive may specify the matters listed in the first column of the table below, to which a person performing a function or duty or exercising a power under this Act must give effect in accordance with the second column.
To avoid doubt, an advance mental health directive that makes no statement regarding a method of care is not evidence of the maker’s consent to that method of care.
13 Making advance mental health directives
A person may make or revoke an advance mental health directive about themselves (the maker) if—
they consult a mental health practitioner; and
the mental health practitioner considers that the maker—
has the capacity to make decisions about mental health care; and
is able to give informed consent in accordance with section 14(2).
If the maker meets the criteria in subsection (1)﻿(b), the mental health practitioner must—
inform the maker of the purpose and effect of an advance mental health directive, including its legal effect in relation to compulsory care; and
give the maker reasonable assistance to make or revoke an advance mental health directive.
An advance mental health directive or the revocation of an advance mental health directive must be—
witnessed in accordance with section 14; and
recorded in a form approved by the Director.
14 Witnessing advance mental health directive
An advance mental health directive or the revocation of an advance mental health directive must be witnessed by a person who is—
acceptable to the maker; and
18 years of age or older; and
not subject to compulsory care.
The maker, the witness, and the supporting mental health practitioner must, if satisfied of the following, confirm in writing that the maker—
is making or revoking the directive of their own free will; and
understands the nature and effect of the directive or revocation.
15 Replacing or revoking advance mental health directive
The maker may replace or revoke their advance mental health directive in accordance with section 13.
The mental health practitioner consulted under section 13 in relation to an advance mental health directive that replaces or revokes another directive must, before the directive is replaced or revoked, take reasonable steps—
to obtain the existing advance mental health directive; and
to confirm the effect of the proposed replacement or revocation with the maker.
An advance mental health directive is revoked by a subsequent advance mental health directive made by the maker.
 Subpart 2 — Participation in decision making
16 Participation by person under compulsory care
This section applies to the following proceedings and processes under this Act:
proceedings in a court, a Mental Health Review Tribunal, or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal:
processes for assessing a person against the compulsory care criteria:
processes for convening, and the proceedings of, a hui whaiora (well-being meeting):
the preparation or review of a care plan:
inquiries undertaken by a district inspector:
making a complaint to a district inspector and investigating that complaint.
In proceedings or processes to which this section applies,—
persons under compulsory care must be encouraged and assisted, to the maximum degree appropriate to their capacity, to participate; and
persons under compulsory care must be given reasonable assistance to understand the reasons for the proceedings or processes, the possible decisions, and how decisions may affect them; and
persons under compulsory care must be given a reasonable opportunity and support to express their views and be understood, including communication support if required; and
any views expressed by persons under compulsory care must be taken into account in any decision relating to them.
In proceedings or processes to which this section applies, the court, tribunal, or person responsible for the proceedings or process must ensure that the services of an interpreter are provided for a person under compulsory care whose first or preferred language is a language other than English, or who is unable to understand English, unless—
it is not practicable to provide the services of an interpreter; and
delaying proceedings to enable the services of an interpreter to be provided would have a serious detrimental effect on the person under compulsory care.
A person who makes a decision in relation to a proceeding or process to which this section applies must take reasonable steps to explain to the affected person the decision and the reasons for the decision in a manner that the affected person understands.
The following persons must either perform the duties imposed by subsection (2)﻿(a) to (d) or be satisfied, before undertaking any proceeding or process involving persons under compulsory care, that those duties have been performed by another person:
for proceedings before a court, a Mental Health Review Tribunal, or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal, the Judge or other person presiding, and the lawyer representing the person:
for processes for assessing a person against the compulsory care criteria, the mental health practitioner or responsible practitioner:
for processes for convening, and the proceedings of, a hui whaiora (well-being meeting), the responsible practitioner:
for preparation or review of a care plan, the responsible practitioner:
for the matters described in subsection (1)﻿(e) and (f), the district inspector.
In this section,—
communication support includes support from an independent support person; and
person under compulsory care includes a proposed person under compulsory care, unless the context otherwise requires.
17 Hui whaiora (well-being meetings)
A hui whaiora (well-being meeting) may be convened for the following purposes:
to assist tāngata whaiora to make decisions about their care:
to consider matters relating to the care of tāngata whaiora:
to resolve issues, disputes, or complaints and to prevent the need for escalation to more formal processes:
to support restorative practice to uphold the mana of all parties following the use of coercive practices.
A responsible practitioner must require a hui whaiora (well-being meeting) to be convened if the practitioner is satisfied that—
the person under compulsory care—
does not have an advance mental health directive or nominated person; or
has requested a hui whaiora (well-being meeting); and
a hui whaiora (well-being meeting) would assist with 1 or more of the purposes listed in subsection (1); and
it is reasonably practicable for all participants to take part in the hui whaiora (well-being meeting).
The responsible practitioner must convene a hui whaiora (well-being meeting) by notifying a suitable hui whaiora co-ordinator who arranges and facilitates the hui whaiora (well-being meeting).
The following persons may attend a hui whaiora (well-being meeting), unless the hui whaiora co-ordinator considers the person’s attendance would not be in the best interests of the person under compulsory care or would limit the ability of the hui whaiora (well-being meeting) to achieve its purposes:
the person under compulsory care:
the rōpū whaiora (collaborative care team):
the person under compulsory care’s support network:
any other person requested by the person under compulsory care:
a district inspector:
the person’s advocate (if any).
The hui whaiora co-ordinator may regulate proceedings at a hui whaiora (well-being meeting), having regard to any guidance issued by the Director-General on hui whaiora (well-being meeting) procedure under section 214.
The hui whaiora co-ordinator must make a record of the outcome of the hui whaiora (well-being meeting).
 Subpart 3 — Support network
18 Support network
A person’s support network is 1 or more of the following:
any nominated person, if appointed:
their welfare guardian, if the scope of their appointment includes matters related to mental health care:
their attorney, if the scope of their appointment includes matters related to mental health care:
their primary support person:
an independent support person, if the person has requested an independent support person or if no support network can be identified.
A person’s support network includes whānau or family members for the purposes of sections 17(4), 26(2)﻿(a)﻿(i), 46(1)﻿(b) and (2)﻿(b), 47, 58(1)﻿(e), and 59(2)﻿(a)﻿(ii).
Nothing in this Act affects the rights and duties under other legislation of persons listed in subsections (1) and (2).
 Nominated person
19 Nominated person
A person may appoint 1 or more other persons to be a nominated person.
A nominated person—
receives information about the appointing person related to mental health; and
represents the appointing person’s views in decision-making processes.
A nominated person appointment must be—
signed by the appointing person and the nominated person; and
recorded in a form approved by the Director; and
witnessed in accordance with section 20.
20 Witnessing nominated person appointment
A nominated person appointment must be witnessed by a person who is—
acceptable to the appointing person; and
18 years of age or older; and
not subject to compulsory care.
The witness must, if satisfied of the following, confirm in writing that—
the appointing person is making the appointment of their own free will; and
the nominated person accepts the appointment of their own free will; and
the appointing person and the nominated person understand the nature and effect of the appointment.
21 Revocation of nominated person appointment by parties
A person who has appointed a nominated person may revoke the appointment if, at that time, they have the capacity to make decisions about mental health care.
A nominated person may resign their appointment for any reason.
A revocation or resignation of appointment must be by notice in writing—
to the other party; or
if the appointing person is subject to compulsory care, to the responsible practitioner.
A responsible practitioner who receives a notice of revocation or resignation must inform the person under compulsory care or the nominated person, as the case may be.
22 Revocation of nominated person appointment by Mental Health Review Tribunal
A person under compulsory care’s responsible practitioner may apply to a Mental Health Review Tribunal to revoke the appointment of the person’s nominated person under section 140 if—
the responsible practitioner considers that the appointment is not in the best interests of the person under compulsory care; or
the nominated person is unable or unwilling to participate in processes required by the Act; or
there are reasonable grounds to believe that the appointment was obtained by undue influence or fraud.
 Independent support person
23 Independent support person
A person under compulsory care is entitled to the services of an independent support person who is acceptable to them to—
advise them on their rights; and
support them in understanding their rights, and the processes and proceedings provided for under this Act; and
help them to make decisions; and
help them to participate in processes and proceedings provided for under this Act, including the complaints processes under section 40; and
provide reasonable assistance to use other complaints processes.
Health New Zealand must ensure that independent support persons—
are available to all persons under compulsory care; and
operate independently from—
decision makers under this Act; and
Health New Zealand; and
the Ministry of Health.
The manager of a unit providing compulsory care to persons subject to a hospital care order must take reasonable steps to ensure that independent support persons providing services under this section have access to—
persons under compulsory care and facilities; and
information about a person under compulsory care, if that information is reasonably necessary for the independent support person to perform their role.
The Director-General may issue guidelines under section 214(1)﻿(a) relating to independent support persons.
 Advocate
24 Advocate
A person under compulsory care is entitled to the services of an advocate who is acceptable to them to perform the functions listed in subsection (2).
An advocate provided under this section may perform, in addition to the functions of an independent support person, the following functions in a lay capacity:
helping persons under compulsory care to exercise their rights:
making complaints on behalf of persons under compulsory care:
representing the views, will, and preferences of persons under compulsory care in processes and proceedings provided for under this Act:
receiving and facilitating resolution of complaints in relation to a service, including representing a person under compulsory care in processes for resolving complaints under section 40:
reporting to the Director on matters relating to the operation and effectiveness of processes and systems for care under this Act.
Health New Zealand must ensure that a reasonable number of advocates are available to persons under compulsory care.
Health New Zealand must ensure that—
advocates operate independently from—
decision makers under this Act; and
Health New Zealand; and
the Ministry of Health; and
an independent support person does not act as an advocate in the same case, unless it is not reasonably practicable for those roles to be performed separately.
The manager of a unit providing compulsory care to persons subject to a hospital care order must take reasonable steps to ensure that advocates providing services under this section have access to—
persons under compulsory care and facilities; and
information about a person under compulsory care, if that information is reasonably necessary for the advocate to perform their role.
The Director-General may issue guidelines under section 214(1)﻿(a) relating to advocates.
 Subpart 4 — Rights of persons under compulsory care
 Rights
25 Rights of proposed persons under compulsory care and voluntary mental health consumers
In this subpart, other than section 26(2) and (3)﻿(a) to (f), person under compulsory care includes a proposed person under compulsory care.
In this subpart, other than sections 28 and 39, person under compulsory care includes a voluntary mental health consumer.
A voluntary mental health consumer has the right to stop receiving care and to leave the place at which they are receiving care at any time.
26 General rights to information
A responsible practitioner must take all reasonable steps to ensure that a person, on becoming a person under compulsory care for whom the responsible practitioner is responsible, receives a written statement of their rights as a person under compulsory care.
A responsible practitioner must take all reasonable steps to ensure that each person under compulsory care for whom they are responsible—
is advised of their rights as a person under compulsory care—
in the presence of a member of their support network, an independent support person, or an advocate; and
in a form, language, and manner that enables the person to understand their rights; and
continues to understand their rights.
A responsible practitioner must take all reasonable steps to ensure that each person under compulsory care for whom they are responsible is kept informed of their legal status as a person under compulsory care and the following rights:
their right to hui whaiora (well-being meetings) under section 17:
their right to have their condition reviewed by a Judge under section 65 or a Mental Health Review Tribunal under section 136 in the second assessment period:
their right to have their condition reviewed by a Mental Health Review Tribunal under section 136 while subject to a mental health care order:
their right to request a Mental Health Review Tribunal or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal to co-opt a suitable person under section 166(2):
their right to appeal against Mental Health Review Tribunal decisions or Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal decisions to the court under section 142 or 176:
their right to seek judicial inquiry under section 143 into their condition while subject to a mental health care order:
their right to seek independent advice from a mental health practitioner under section 30:
their right to seek independent legal advice under section 31:
their right to complain—
to a district inspector or official visitor under section 40; and
using the internal complaints process of a mental health service; and
to the Health and Disability Commissioner under Part 4 of the Health and Disability Commissioner Act 1994:
their rights under section 16(2).
A person under compulsory care must also be informed of—
all orders made by a court or a Mental Health Review Tribunal or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal in respect of their case; and
the functions and duties of the following:
district inspectors:
official visitors:
independent support persons:
advocates.
27 Right to respect for culture and identity
A person under compulsory care is entitled to proper respect for—
their cultural, ethnic, and individual identity; and
their religious or cultural beliefs.
A court, tribunal, or person performing a function or duty or exercising a power under this Act in respect of a person under compulsory care must act in a manner consistent with subsection (1).
28 Right to medical and other health care
A person under compulsory care is entitled to medical and other health care suitable for their mental and physical health needs.
A responsible practitioner must take reasonable steps to ensure that suitable medical and other health care is available to the person under compulsory care.
29 Right to be informed about medical and other health care
A person under compulsory care is entitled to receive an explanation, before care is commenced, of—
the care options available to them, including the expected benefits and the likely effects of those care options; and
the expected effects of any care offered to them, including the expected benefits and risks and the likely side effects.
A responsible practitioner must take reasonable steps to ensure—
that the explanation is given in a form, language, and manner that enables the person under compulsory care to understand it; and
that the person under compulsory care understands the proposed care.
30 Right to independent health advice
A person under compulsory care is entitled to seek a consultation with a mental health practitioner of their choice to get a second opinion, and, if the practitioner agrees to the consultation, they must be permitted access to the person upon request.
31 Right to legal advice
A person under compulsory care is entitled to request a lawyer to advise them, and, if the lawyer agrees to act for the person, they must be permitted access to the person upon request.
To avoid doubt, in this section lawyer does not include a district inspector.
32 Right to company
A person under compulsory care is entitled to the company of others, except as provided in section 50.
The person in charge of a hospital must ensure that a person under compulsory care has access to the company of others.
33 Further rights in case of visual or audio recording
A person under compulsory care is entitled to be informed if it is intended to make or use a visual or audio recording of any interview with, or any other part of the care of, the person.
A recording referred to in subsection (1) must not be made or used without the prior consent of the person under compulsory care or, if the person is not capable of giving consent, the prior consent of their nominated person or whānau.
34 Right to receive visitors and to communicate with others
A person under compulsory care is entitled to receive visitors and to communicate with others (for example, by telephone or other communication device) at reasonable times and at reasonable intervals.
Subsection (1) does not apply if the responsible practitioner considers a visit or communication would be detrimental to the care of a person under compulsory care or would otherwise have a detrimental effect on the person.
The Director of Area Mental Health Services must ensure that the person under compulsory care has access to the entitlements under this section.
Nothing in this section limits or affects anything in section 30 or 31.
35 Right to send and receive communications
A person under compulsory care is entitled to send and receive letters, electronic communications, and postal articles (a communication) without interference, except that—
the responsible practitioner may, if the practitioner reasonably believes that sending or receiving the communication may have a detrimental effect on the person, direct that a communication—
be opened or read; and
be withheld from the person; or
the responsible practitioner may withhold sending a communication if it is addressed to a person who has notified the mental health service that they do not want to receive communication from the person.
However, a communication may not be withheld from a person under compulsory care if it is addressed to, or sent by or on behalf of, any of the following:
a member of Parliament:
a Judge or an officer of any court or other judicial body:
the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security:
an Ombudsman:
the Privacy Commissioner:
a Human Rights Commissioner:
the Health and Disability Commissioner:
the Approved Agency appointed under the Harmful Digital Communications Act 2015:
the Director-General or the Director:
a district inspector or official visitor:
the person in charge of the service in which the person under compulsory care is being treated:
the person under compulsory care’s nominated person:
an independent support person:
an advocate:
a lawyer:
a mental health practitioner from whom the person under compulsory care is seeking advice:
any person prescribed in regulations.
If a communication sent by the person under compulsory care is withheld under this section, it must be given or sent to a district inspector or an official visitor as soon as practicable after it is withheld.
If a communication sent to the person under compulsory care is withheld under this section, it must be returned as soon as practicable after it is withheld to—
the sender; or
if the sender is unknown, to a district inspector or an official visitor.
A person under compulsory care must be informed when a communication is withheld, unless the responsible practitioner reasonably believes that informing the person may have a detrimental effect on them.
The person in charge of a hospital must ensure that the person under compulsory care has access to the entitlements under this section.
36 Right to communication aid
A person under compulsory care is entitled to use a communication aid, unless withholding access to the aid is reasonably necessary to—
save the person’s life; or
prevent serious damage to the health of the person; or
prevent the person from causing serious injury to themselves or others.
A responsible practitioner must—
take reasonable steps to ensure that the person under compulsory care has access to their communication aid; and
notify the district inspector or an official visitor if access to a communication aid is withheld, as soon as practicable after it is withheld.
37 Right to mobility aid
A person under compulsory care is entitled to use a mobility aid, unless withholding access to the aid is reasonably necessary to—
save the person’s life; or
prevent serious damage to the health of the person; or
prevent the person from causing serious injury to themselves or others.
A responsible practitioner must—
take reasonable steps to ensure that the person under compulsory care has access to their mobility aid; and
notify the district inspector or an official visitor if access to a mobility aid is withheld, as soon as practicable after it is withheld.
38 Rights of children and young persons
A person under compulsory care who is under the age of 18 must wherever practicable be cared for by child and adolescent mental health services.
A person under compulsory care who is under the age of 18 must not be—
given electroconvulsive therapy, except as permitted under section 51(2); or
placed in seclusion; or
given a restricted treatment as defined in section 52(2)﻿(a).
Despite section 36 of the Care of Children Act 2004 or any other enactment or rule of law to the contrary, in respect of a person under compulsory care who is 16 years of age or older, the consent of a parent or guardian to any assessment or care is not sufficient consent for the purposes of this Act.
If a Mental Health Review Tribunal or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal considers a matter concerning a person under compulsory care who is under the age of 18, the membership of the tribunal must include at least 1 person with appropriate expertise in child and adolescent development.
39 Rights of birth parent
Subject to any order of the Family Court under the Oranga Tamariki Act 1989 authorising custody (as defined in section 2(1) of that Act), a person under compulsory care who is in a hospital, or is being transported to a hospital, and is a birth parent is entitled to have their baby remain with them unless the responsible practitioner responsible for the person considers that their separation is in the best interests of the person or their baby.
A responsible practitioner who is considering what is in the best interests of a person under compulsory care or their baby—
must give priority to the safety and well-being of the baby; and
must make reasonable endeavours to consider—
any preferences as to childcare set out in the birth parent’s advance mental health directive; and
the views of any guardian of the baby, any person who has day-to-day care of the baby, and any nominated person.
A responsible practitioner must notify the district inspector or an official visitor if a birth parent is separated from their baby, as soon as practicable after the separation.
In this section,—
a person is a birth parent if they have given birth to a baby and the baby is less than 1 year of age:
guardian has the meaning set out in section 15 of the Care of Children Act 2004.
 Complaints
40 Complaint of breach of rights
If a complaint is made by or on behalf of a person under compulsory care that a right under this Act has been breached, the matter must be referred to a district inspector or an official visitor for investigation.
A district inspector or an official visitor may investigate, on receipt of a complaint or on their own initiative,—
a breach of a person under compulsory care’s rights under this Act; or
a case where detention or compulsory care may not have been provided in accordance with this Act; or
a case where—
a voluntary mental health consumer may have been detained; or
a voluntary mental health consumer may have been given care without their consent.
A district inspector or official visitor investigating a complaint—
must discuss the matter with—
the person under compulsory care; and
the complainant; and
the responsible practitioner; and
the rōpū whaiora (collaborative care team); and
any other relevant person; and
may rely on advice from advisers approved by the Director under section 41 in matters involving tāngata whaiora Māori.
A district inspector or an official visitor must be guided by the principles in section 171 and must comply with any requirements prescribed in regulations relating to timeliness and process when investigating a complaint.
If the district inspector or official visitor is satisfied following an investigation that a complaint has substance, they must report the matter to the Director of Area Mental Health Services, together with any recommendations they think fit, and the Director of Area Mental Health Services must take all reasonable steps to rectify the matter.
On concluding any investigation under this section, the district inspector or official visitor must inform the person under compulsory care or other complainant, and any other relevant person, of their findings.
If the district inspector or official visitor has reported a matter, including recommendations to the Director of Area Mental Health Services, and they consider that the recommendations have not been satisfactorily addressed, the district inspector or official visitor must report the matter to the Director.
If the district inspector or official visitor has reported a matter to the Director and the Director considers that the recommendations have not been satisfactorily addressed, the Director may require a service to notify the public on a publicly accessible internet site—
how the recommendations will be addressed; and
when they have been addressed.
If the person under compulsory care or other complainant is not satisfied with the outcome of the complaint, they may refer the case to a Mental Health Review Tribunal or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal for further investigation, in which case subsections (3) to (5) apply with any necessary modifications.
41 Approval of advisers
The Director may approve persons with expertise in matters involving tāngata whaiora Māori for the purposes of advising on complaints and inquiries under this Act.
 Part 3 — Compulsory care
 Subpart 1 — Compulsory care requirements
 General care requirements
42 Duty to assign responsible practitioner
A Director of Area Mental Health Services must ensure that each person under compulsory care has a responsible practitioner.
The responsible practitioner must be a mental health practitioner who the Director of Area Mental Health Services considers has undergone training in, and is competent in, the assessment and care of persons who require compulsory care.
43 Rōpū whaiora (collaborative care team) required during compulsory care
The responsible practitioner must ensure that an appropriately qualified and experienced rōpū whaiora (collaborative care team) is provided to a person under compulsory care.
In this section, appropriately qualified and experienced means having the qualifications and experience necessary to meet the care needs of the person under compulsory care, including clinical and cultural expertise, and expertise in lived experience of mental distress.
44 Care planning
The responsible practitioner must ensure that a person under compulsory care has a recorded care plan.
The responsible practitioner must ensure that the person’s care plan—
is kept under review; and
is updated as circumstances require.
The responsible practitioner must engage with the person’s rōpū whaiora (collaborative care team) to—
develop the person’s care plan; and
review and update the care plan.
The care plan must include—
a holistic assessment of the person, including—
their mental health, physical health, and social needs; and
their socio-economic circumstances; and
cultural considerations in relation to the person; and
their strengths and protective factors in their environment; and
the care, including non-pharmaceutical options, that will be provided to meet the person’s assessed needs, including any need for compulsory care; and
planning for a transition from—
hospital care (if the person is a person subject to a hospital care order) to community care; and
compulsory care to voluntary engagement with mental health services.
The care plan must—
give effect to the will of the person under compulsory care, including the content of any advance mental health directive that they have made; and
take into account the care preferences of the person under compulsory care.
45 Provision of care
The responsible practitioner may only direct the provision of care to a person under compulsory care in accordance with their care plan.
The responsible practitioner must, in addition to satisfying subsection (1), and to the maximum extent that the person under compulsory care’s capacity allows, seek the person’s consent before providing care.
46 Care plan and status reviews
When required by this Act to conduct a care plan review, the responsible practitioner must—
assess the person under compulsory care’s progress under the care plan; and
have regard to the views of the person under compulsory care and their support network on the person’s mental condition and personal circumstances.
When required by this Act to conduct a status review, the responsible practitioner must—
assess the person under compulsory care against the compulsory care criteria; and
have regard to the views of the person under compulsory care and their support network on the person’s mental condition and personal circumstances.
47 Support network access and support
A person under compulsory care’s support network must be given reasonable opportunity to—
visit and communicate with the person under compulsory care; and
express their views on the person’s condition and care; and
contribute to care planning in respect of the person, provided for under section 44.
The responsible practitioner may decline a member of the person under compulsory care’s support network the opportunity under subsection (1)﻿(c) if the practitioner is satisfied on reasonable grounds that—
it reflects the person’s will and preferences; or
it is necessary to avoid a detrimental effect on the person.
48 Emergency care
Despite section 45, the responsible practitioner or a person authorised by the responsible practitioner may provide care in any case if it is reasonably necessary to—
save the life of a person under compulsory care; or
prevent serious damage to the health of a person under compulsory care; or
prevent a person under compulsory care from causing serious injury to themselves or others.
If it is not practicable to obtain authorisation from the responsible practitioner, a nurse or other health professional who has immediate responsibility for a person under compulsory care may provide care under this section, but must immediately bring the case to the attention of the responsible practitioner.
49 Duty to report emergency care
If action is taken under section 48, the responsible practitioner must report it to the Director of Area Mental Health Services as soon as practicable after the action is taken.
The report must include—
the action taken that was inconsistent with the advance mental health directive or the refusal of consent by the person under compulsory care’s attorney; and
the statement in the advance mental health directive that was overridden (if any); and
the grounds on which the responsible practitioner relied; and
the reasons for taking the action.
The report must also be provided to the person under compulsory care’s support network, to the extent that doing so is consistent with the advance mental health directive.
50 Reduction and elimination of seclusion
Every person in charge of a service must use their best endeavours to eliminate the placement of persons under compulsory care in seclusion.
A person in charge of a service must report annually to the Director-General on the steps taken to eliminate the placement of persons under compulsory care in seclusion.
Despite section 48, a person must not be placed in seclusion unless—
it is necessary to prevent an imminent risk to life when all other less restrictive strategies and approaches have been tried without positive effect; and
it is used in accordance with subsection (4).
A person under compulsory care may only be placed in seclusion in accordance with—
any relevant guidelines and standards for compulsory care issued by the Director-General under section 214; and
the following provisions:
a person under compulsory care must be placed in seclusion in a room or other area that is designated for the purpose by, or with the approval of, a Director of Area Mental Health Services:
except as provided in subparagraph (iii), seclusion may be used only with the authority of the responsible practitioner:
if it is not practicable to obtain authorisation from the responsible practitioner, a nurse or other health professional who has immediate responsibility for a person under compulsory care may place the person in seclusion, but must immediately bring the case to the attention of the responsible practitioner:
seclusion may be used only for as long as necessary to ensure the safety of people accessing the service, staff, or others:
the duration, the grounds, and the reasons for each episode of seclusion must be recorded in accordance with section 216(2):
the record of each episode of seclusion must be provided to the person under compulsory care’s support network as soon as practicable after the episode, to the extent that doing so is consistent with any advance mental health directive made by the person.
Despite subsections (1) to (4), regulations may prohibit, or restrict the use of, the placement of persons under compulsory care in seclusion.
See section 38(2)﻿(b), which prohibits the placement of a person under compulsory care who is under the age of 18 in seclusion.
51 Electroconvulsive therapy
The responsible practitioner must not give electroconvulsive therapy to a person under compulsory care (other than a person who is under the age of 18) unless—
the therapy is in accordance with the person’s care plan and the person consents to the therapy by informed consent given—
in writing; or
in an advance mental health directive; or
if the person does not have capacity to make decisions about mental health care,—
a psychiatrist with expertise in electroconvulsive therapy who works independently of the rōpū whaiora (collaborative care team) and is appointed by a Mental Health Review Tribunal for the purposes of this section considers that electroconvulsive therapy is reasonably necessary to—
save the person’s life; or
prevent serious damage to the health of the person; or
prevent the person from causing serious injury to themselves or others; and
they take into account the person’s care plan (including any advance mental health directive the person has made) to the greatest extent possible in providing the therapy.
The responsible practitioner may only give electroconvulsive therapy to a person under compulsory care who is under the age of 18 if—
a psychiatrist with expertise in electroconvulsive therapy and a child and adolescent psychiatrist who both work independently of the rōpū whaiora (collaborative care team) and are appointed by a Mental Health Review Tribunal for the purposes of this section consider that electroconvulsive therapy is reasonably necessary to—
save the person’s life; or
prevent serious damage to the health of the person; or
prevent the person from causing serious injury to themselves or others; and
they take into account the person’s care plan (including any advance mental health directive the person has made) to the greatest extent possible in providing the therapy.
The responsible practitioner must record the use of electroconvulsive therapy in accordance with section 49, which applies with any necessary modifications as if the use of electroconvulsive therapy were action taken under section 48.
See section 38(2)﻿(a), which prohibits the provision of electroconvulsive therapy to a person under compulsory care who is under the age of 18, except where permitted under subsection (2).
52 Restricted treatments
Despite section 48, a person under compulsory care must not be given a restricted treatment unless—
the person consents to the treatment in writing or in an advance mental health directive; and
a Mental Health Review Tribunal has considered the case and is satisfied that the person gave that consent freely and, in giving that consent, understood the nature, purpose, and likely effect of the treatment; and
the treatment is considered to be in the interests of the person by—
the responsible practitioner; and
a psychiatrist who has been appointed for the purposes of this section by the Mental Health Review Tribunal and who has consulted at least 2 health professionals (neither of whom is a medical practitioner) currently concerned in the person’s care.
In this section, restricted treatment means—
any treatment intended to destroy any part of the brain or brain function; or
any other emerging or higher-risk treatment specified in regulations (which may also include conditions on the provision of the treatment, including whether the treatment may be provided to a person under the age of 18).
See section 38(2)﻿(c), which prohibits the provision of a restricted treatment as defined in subsection (2)﻿(a) to a person under compulsory care who is under the age of 18.
 Right to appeal or review
53 Right to appeal or review
The following general appeals and reviews are available in relation to a person under compulsory care:
a person under compulsory care or a member of their support network may apply to the Family Court to have the person’s condition reviewed (see section 65):
a Mental Health Review Tribunal or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal may, of its own motion, review the condition of any person under compulsory care (see section 136(3)):
a Judge of the High Court may, on the Judge's initiative or on the application of any person,—
make an order that directs a district inspector or any 1 or more persons whom the Judge selects—
to visit and examine any person who the Judge has reason to believe is being detained in a hospital as a person under compulsory care; and
to inquire into and report on any matters relating to that person; or
make an order directing the responsible practitioner to bring any person who is being detained as a person under compulsory care in the hospital before the Judge in open court or in chambers, for examination at a time to be specified in the order.
(See section 143.)
The following appeals to a Mental Health Review Tribunal are available in relation to a status review of a person under compulsory care:
any person to whom a copy of a record of status review is sent under section 82 may apply to the Mental Health Review Tribunal to appeal against the outcome of a status review (see section 136):
a person under compulsory care or other complainant under section 40 who is not satisfied with the outcome of a complaint to a district inspector or official visitor may apply to the Mental Health Review Tribunal for further investigation (see section 40(9)):
if it appears that for any reason a status review of a person under compulsory care has not taken place, the Mental Health Review Tribunal may review the person’s condition, either of its own motion or on application by any person to whom a copy of a record of status review would have been required to have been sent if the review had been held (see section 136(4)).
If a responsible practitioner makes a direction in relation to a community care order, the person under compulsory care or a member of their support network may apply to the court to have the person’s condition reviewed under section 65 (see section 83(2)﻿(c)).
Any person to whom a copy of a record of tribunal review is sent under section 137(2)﻿(d) to (f) may apply to the court to appeal against the finding of the Mental Health Review Tribunal (see section 142).
The following appeals and reviews are available in relation to a person under forensic compulsory care or a person on a restricted order:
any person to whom a copy of a record of status review is sent under section 107 may apply to the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal to appeal against the outcome of a status review of a person under forensic compulsory care (see section 138):
any person to whom a copy of a record of status review is sent under section 135 may apply to the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal to appeal against the outcome of a status review of a person on a restricted order (see section 139):
if it appears that for any reason a status review of a person under forensic compulsory care or a person on a restricted order has not taken place, the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal may review the person’s condition, either of its own motion or on application by any person to whom a copy of a record of status review would have been required to have been sent if the review had been held (see sections 138(6) and 139(3)):
a person under forensic compulsory care or the person’s nominated person may appeal to the court against a decision by the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal (see section 176).
This section is intended as a guide only.
 Release from compulsory care
54 Release from compulsory care
If, at any time, the responsible practitioner considers that a person under compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria, that practitioner must direct that the person be immediately released.
If the responsible practitioner considers that the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria but a district inspector or an official visitor disagrees, the district inspector or official visitor must refer the case to a Mental Health Review Tribunal for consideration under section 136.
If the release of a person from compulsory care is directed under this section, the following applies:
if the person is undergoing second assessment, the second assessment period expires on the date specified in the direction:
if a mental health care order is in force in respect of the person, the mental health care order expires on the date specified in the direction.
To avoid doubt, this section does not apply to a person under forensic compulsory care.
 Subpart 2 — Compulsory care assessment
55 Overview
Compulsory care assessment consists of—
application for first assessment:
first assessment:
second assessment:
the decision whether to apply for a mental health care order.
See Schedule 2 for a diagram summarising the process steps from application for first assessment to the issue of a mental health care order.
 Examination and application for first assessment
56 Assistance arranging examination for application
This section applies if a person believes that another person may meet the compulsory care criteria.
The person may request an authorised person to assist in arranging for an examination for an application for first assessment.
The authorised person must investigate the matter to the extent necessary to satisfy themselves whether there are reasonable grounds to believe that the person meets the compulsory care criteria.
If the authorised person considers that there are reasonable grounds to believe that the person meets the compulsory care criteria, they must immediately arrange or assist in arranging the examination required under section 57(1)﻿(a).
57 Examination and application
A mental health practitioner or a clinical psychologist may apply to the Director of Area Mental Health Services for first assessment of a person if they—
have examined the person within the previous 72 hours; and
consider that there are reasonable grounds to believe that the person meets the compulsory care criteria.
An application under subsection (1)—
may be made in the prescribed form; and
must include the reasonable grounds relied on under subsection (1)﻿(b).
When the Director of Area Mental Health Services receives an application under subsection (1), they must immediately arrange for first assessment of the person.
 First assessment
58 Arrangements for first assessment
A Director of Area Mental Health Services must—
determine the mental health practitioner who will conduct the first assessment (who must not be the practitioner who conducted the examination under section 57); and
determine, in consultation with the assessor, the time and place at which the first assessment is to be conducted; and
take reasonable steps to determine whether the proposed person under compulsory care has a welfare guardian, an attorney, or a nominated person; and
give the proposed person under compulsory care a written notice—
requiring the person to attend at the specified place and time for the purposes of the first assessment; and
explaining the purpose of the first assessment; and
stating the name of the person who is to conduct the assessment examination; and
explain to the proposed person under compulsory care, in the presence of a member of the person’s support network,—
the nature of the first assessment and the possible consequences; and
the time and place of the first assessment, including details of who will conduct the assessment; and
make appropriate arrangements for the person’s transport to, and from, the place of assessment.
If no member of the proposed person under compulsory care’s support network is available, the Director of Area Mental Health Services or the authorised person must ensure that an independent support person is present for the purpose of subsection (1)﻿(e).
59 First assessment
The mental health practitioner conducting the first assessment must assess the proposed person under compulsory care to determine whether there are reasonable grounds to believe that they meet the compulsory care criteria.
For the purpose of subsection (1), the mental health practitioner must—
take reasonable steps to—
determine whether the proposed person under compulsory care has an advance mental health directive, nominated person, welfare guardian, or attorney; and
ascertain the views of the proposed person under compulsory care and their support network on the person’s mental condition and personal circumstances; and
have regard to—
any advance mental health directive that the proposed person under compulsory care has made; and
the views of the proposed person under compulsory care and their support network on the person’s mental condition and personal circumstances.
In assessing the steps that are reasonable under subsection (2)﻿(a), the mental health practitioner must balance the accurate assessment of the proposed person under compulsory care’s mental condition, personal circumstances, and preferences relating to mental health care (for example, an advance mental health directive) with—
the person’s will and preferences in relation to their support network; and
the need to avoid unduly prolonging the assessment.
60 Record of first assessment
After completing the first assessment, the mental health practitioner must record—
that they have carefully assessed the proposed person under compulsory care against the compulsory care criteria; and
that they consider that—
the proposed person under compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria; or
there are reasonable grounds to believe that the proposed person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria; and
for an assessment described in paragraph (b)﻿(ii), the reasonable grounds.
The mental health practitioner must provide the record to the following persons:
the proposed person under compulsory care:
the mental health practitioner who conducted the examination under section 57:
the primary health care provider who usually attends the proposed person under compulsory care:
the Director of Area Mental Health Services:
the proposed person under compulsory care’s support network:
a district inspector:
an official visitor:
the proposed person under compulsory care’s advocate (if any).
61 Compulsory care criteria met
If the mental health practitioner records that there are reasonable grounds to believe that the proposed person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria, the mental health practitioner must require the proposed person under compulsory care, by notice in writing, to—
receive second assessment as a person under compulsory care at their residence, or another suitable place; or
if the mental health practitioner considers that the person under compulsory care cannot be assessed and given care adequately in the community, be detained in a hospital for second assessment.
62 Compulsory care criteria not met
If the mental health practitioner records that they do not consider that the proposed person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria, the person is not required to receive compulsory care and must be immediately released.
 Second assessment
63 Second assessment
This section applies to a person who is a person under compulsory care following notification under section 61.
A responsible practitioner may require a person under compulsory care to undergo second assessment, comprising assessment and care in accordance with a care plan, for a maximum period of 19 days starting on the day on which they are notified.
The responsible practitioner must undertake a care plan review and a status review of the person under compulsory care no later than day 5, no later than day 12, and no later than day 19 of the second assessment period.
The care plan review and status review must be conducted in accordance with section 46.
The responsible practitioner must provide the records of each care plan review and status review to the following persons:
the person under compulsory care:
the primary health care provider who usually attended the person under compulsory care immediately before the person was required to undergo second assessment:
the Director of Area Mental Health Services:
the person under compulsory care’s support network:
a district inspector:
an official visitor:
the person under compulsory care’s advocate (if any).
64 Location of compulsory care
If, at any time during the second assessment period, the responsible practitioner considers that a person under compulsory care in a hospital can continue to be assessed and given care adequately in the community, the practitioner may give a written notice—
directing the person in charge of the hospital to discharge the person; and
directing the person to attend at their place of residence, or at some other place nominated in the notice, for second assessment during the remainder of the maximum period.
If, at any time during the second assessment period, the responsible practitioner considers that a person under compulsory care cannot continue to be assessed and given care adequately in the community, the practitioner may give a written notice—
directing the person to present themselves at a specified hospital to be admitted and detained for the purposes of second assessment during the remainder of the maximum period; and
directing the person in charge of the hospital to admit and detain the person for the purposes of second assessment during the remainder of the maximum period.
65 Examination of person under compulsory care undergoing second assessment by Judge
At any time during the second assessment period, the person under compulsory care, a member of their support network, a district inspector, or an official visitor may apply to the court to have a Judge examine the person.
When an application is made under subsection (1),—
if the application is the only application that has been made during the second assessment period,—
the court must grant the application; and
a Judge must examine the person under compulsory care as soon as practicable; and
if the application is the second or subsequent application that has been made during the second assessment period, a Judge must decide whether or not to grant the application, having regard to any evidence before the Judge that indicates that the person under compulsory care’s condition has not changed since the last review.
An examination under this section must be conducted at the place where the person under compulsory care is undergoing assessment or another appropriate place.
The Judge must do the following things before and during the examination, as appropriate and practicable:
identify themselves to the person under compulsory care; and
explain to the person under compulsory care the purpose of the examination; and
discuss with the person under compulsory care the person’s situation, the proposed care plan, and the person’s views on those matters.
As well as examining the person under compulsory care, the Judge—
must consult, regarding the person’s condition,—
the responsible practitioner; and
at least 1 other health professional involved in the case; and
must make reasonable efforts to consult the person’s nominated person (if any), primary support person, and family group or whānau.
If the Judge is satisfied that the person under compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria, the Judge must order that the person be released from compulsory care immediately.
 Decision whether to apply for mental health care order
66 Record of second assessment
Before the expiry of the second assessment period, the responsible practitioner must record—
that they consider that—
the person under compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria; or
the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria; and
for an assessment described in paragraph (a)﻿(ii), the reasons for that assessment.
The responsible practitioner must provide the record to the following persons:
the person under compulsory care:
the primary health care provider who usually attended the person under compulsory care immediately before the person was required to undergo second assessment:
the Director of Area Mental Health Services:
the person under compulsory care’s support network:
a district inspector:
an official visitor:
the person under compulsory care’s advocate (if any).
The district inspector who receives a copy of the record of second assessment described in subsection (1)﻿(a)﻿(ii) must—
communicate with the person under compulsory care and find out, if possible, whether the person wants the district inspector to appear before the court to be heard on the application for a mental health care order; and
decide, having regard to any view expressed by the person under compulsory care, whether the district inspector should appear before the court to be heard on the application for a mental health care order.
67 Compulsory care criteria not met following second assessment
If the responsible practitioner records that they consider that the person under compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria, the person is not required to receive compulsory care and must be released immediately from compulsory care.
68 Compulsory care criteria met following second assessment
If the responsible practitioner records that the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria, the responsible practitioner must, before the expiry of the second assessment period, apply to the court for the making of a mental health care order under subpart 3.
69 Status of person under compulsory care pending determination of application
If the responsible practitioner applies to the court for a mental health care order, the person under compulsory care remains subject to second assessment in accordance with the terms of the notice given under section 61 until the expiry of 14 days after the second assessment period ends.
If, after examining the person under compulsory care under section 74, the Judge considers that it is not practicable to determine the application within the 14-day period, the Judge may, by interim order, extend that period for a further period not exceeding 1 month.
If the application is not finally determined before the expiry of the period under subsection (1) or, if that period is extended under subsection (2), the expiry of the further period under subsection (2),—
the application must be dismissed; and
the person under compulsory care must be released from compulsory care.
 Subpart 3 — Mental health care orders
 Mental health care order applications
70 Mental health care order applications
An application under section 68, an application under section 86(2), and an application under section 87(2) (a mental health care order application) must be accompanied by—
the person under compulsory care’s care plan, including any assessments relating to the person’s cultural considerations; and
the record of the most recent care plan review and status review under section 63(4) or 81 of the person under compulsory care; and
the person under compulsory care’s advance mental health directive (if any).
71 Jurisdiction of Family Court
Every application under this Act must be heard and determined by the Family Court unless otherwise provided.
If an application under this Act needs to be determined within a particular period and it is not practicable to have the application determined in that period by a Family Court Judge, any District Court Judge may exercise the jurisdiction of the Family Court—
by hearing the application, if that is necessary; and
by determining the application.
An examination required under this Act to be conducted by a Judge must be conducted by a Family Court Judge.
Despite subsection (3), if it is not practicable for an examination of a person under compulsory care to be conducted by a Family Court Judge, that examination may be conducted by any District Court Judge.
72 Notice of mental health care order application
The responsible practitioner must provide copies of a mental health care order application, together with the documents described in section 70, to the following persons:
the person under compulsory care:
the person under compulsory care’s support network:
the Director of Area Mental Health Services:
a district inspector:
an official visitor:
the person under compulsory care’s advocate (if any).
73 Notice of requirement to attend examination and hearing
The responsible practitioner must ensure that the person under compulsory care is served with the following as soon as practicable after a mental health care order application is filed:
a notice requiring the person under compulsory care to attend at a place specified in the notice for an examination under section 74; and
a notice requiring the person under compulsory care to attend at the court hearing of the mental health care order application under section 93.
74 Judge to examine person under compulsory care
If a mental health care order application is made, a Judge must examine the person under compulsory care as soon as practicable and no later than 14 days after the application is filed in the court.
The examination must be conducted at the place where the person under compulsory care is undergoing assessment or care or another appropriate place.
The Judge must do the following things before and during the examination, as appropriate and practicable:
identify themselves to the person under compulsory care; and
explain to the person under compulsory care the purpose of the examination; and
discuss with the person under compulsory care the person’s situation, the care plan, and the person’s views on those matters.
As well as examining the person under compulsory care, the Judge—
must consult, regarding the person’s condition,—
the responsible practitioner; and
at least 1 other health professional involved in the case; and
must make reasonable efforts to consult the person’s nominated person (if any), primary support person, and family group or whānau.
If the Judge is satisfied that the person under compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria, the Judge must order that the person be released from compulsory care immediately.
75 Hearing of mental health care order application
A hearing of a mental health care order application must be held in accordance with subpart 4.
The Judge who examines the person under compulsory care under section 74 must, wherever possible, conduct the hearing.
The hearing must not be held until the person under compulsory care has been examined under section 74.
76 Court may dispense with hearing in certain circumstances
The court may determine an application for a mental health care order without a formal hearing if it is satisfied that no person wishes to be heard in respect of the application.
 Mental health care orders
77 Court to determine if compulsory care criteria met
On a mental health care order application, the court must determine whether the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria.
If the court considers that the person under compulsory care does not meet the criteria, it must order that the person be immediately released from compulsory care.
If the court considers that the person under compulsory care does meet the criteria, it must determine, having regard to all the circumstances of the case, if it is reasonably necessary to make a mental health care order.
78 Mental health care orders
A mental health care order may be, as specified by the court in the order,—
a community care order; or
a hospital care order.
The court must make a community care order unless the court considers that the circumstances of the case require the person under compulsory care to be treated as a person subject to a hospital care order.
However, before the court makes a community care order, it must be satisfied that the person under compulsory care—
will receive care that is appropriate to their needs; and
can be adequately cared for in the community.
If the person under compulsory care has a welfare guardian, the court may suspend or modify the appointment of the welfare guardian for the period of compulsory care if it considers it reasonable to do so in the circumstances of the case.
When the court makes an order under this section, it must provide a copy of the order to the person under compulsory care.
79 Community care orders
A community care order must require the person under compulsory care to attend at their place of residence, or at some other place specified in the order, for care by employees of the service, and to receive that care.
An employee of the service specified in the order who is authorised to provide care to the person under compulsory care may enter the person’s place of residence or other specified place when reasonably necessary to provide care to the person.
80 Hospital care orders
A hospital care order must require,—
for the purposes of care,—
the continued detention of the person under compulsory care in the hospital specified in the order; or
if the person under compulsory care is being detained at some other hospital, the admission of the person and their detention in the hospital specified in the order; and
the person under compulsory care to receive care at the hospital specified in the order.
 Care plan and status reviews
81 Care plan and status reviews of persons subject to mental health care orders
This section applies in respect of every person under compulsory care, other than a person on a restricted order, who is subject to—
a mental health care order; or
an order under section 34(1)﻿(a)﻿(i) of the Criminal Procedure (Mentally Impaired Persons) Act 2003.
The responsible practitioner must conduct a care plan review of the condition of the person under compulsory care at monthly intervals starting no later than 1 month after the date of the applicable order.
The responsible practitioner must conduct a status review of the condition of the person under compulsory care at 3-monthly intervals starting no later than 3 months after the date of the applicable order.
Despite subsection (3), the responsible practitioner must conduct a status review of the condition of the person under compulsory care when the responsible practitioner has reason to believe that the condition of the person has changed.
The responsible practitioner must ensure that a notice is given to the person under compulsory care before each review that requires them to be examined at a place specified in the notice.
A care plan review must be conducted in accordance with section 46(1).
A status review must be conducted in accordance with section 46(2).
At the conclusion of a status review, the responsible practitioner must record—
that in their opinion the person under compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria; or
that in their opinion the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria.
The responsible practitioner must send to the Director of Area Mental Health Services—
the record of status review; and
the reasons for their opinion of the person under compulsory care’s condition, and any relevant reports from other health professionals involved in the case.
82 Outcome of status review
If the responsible practitioner considers that the person under compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria, the person must be immediately released from compulsory care and the mental health care order is revoked.
However,—
if the responsible practitioner considers that a person under forensic compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria and paragraph (b) does not apply, the person must be dealt with in accordance with section 108(2), and section 108(4) and (6) applies:
if a person under compulsory care or a person under forensic compulsory care is subject to a mental health care order that was made following an application under section 136(2) of the Intellectual Disability (Compulsory Care and Rehabilitation) Act 2003, they must be dealt with in accordance with section 109.
If the responsible practitioner considers that the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria, the practitioner must send a copy of the record of status review to each of the following persons:
the person under compulsory care:
the person under compulsory care’s support network:
the primary health care provider who usually attended the person under compulsory care immediately before the person was required to undergo second assessment under subpart 2:
a district inspector:
an official visitor:
the Director of Area Mental Health Services.
The responsible practitioner must send a statement to the persons listed in subsection (3)﻿(a) to (c) setting out—
the legal consequences of the finding set out in the record of status review; and
the recipient's right to apply to a Mental Health Review Tribunal for a review of the person under compulsory care’s condition.
The district inspector who receives a copy of the record of status review must—
ask the person under compulsory care whether they want an application to be made to a Mental Health Review Tribunal for a review of their condition; and
decide, having regard to any view expressed by the person under compulsory care, whether an application should be made to a Mental Health Review Tribunal for a review of the person’s condition.
If the district inspector considers that an application for review should be made, the district inspector must take whatever reasonable steps they think necessary to encourage or assist the person under compulsory care, or any person specified in subsection (3)﻿(b) and (c), to make an application.
If, in any case to which subsection (5) applies, the district inspector considers that an application should be made to have the person under compulsory care’s condition reviewed by a Mental Health Review Tribunal but neither the person nor any person specified in subsection (3)﻿(b) and (c) intends to make such an application, the district inspector may report the matter to a Mental Health Review Tribunal and the Mental Health Review Tribunal may, of its own motion, review the person’s condition under section 136.
The district inspector may arrange for an official visitor to perform the functions specified in subsections (5) to (7).
 Changes to mental health care orders
83 Changes to community care orders
If, at any time while a community care order is in force, the responsible practitioner considers that the person under compulsory care cannot continue to be given care adequately in the community, the responsible practitioner may direct that the person—
be given care as a person subject to a hospital care order for a period of up to 14 days; or
be further assessed in accordance with section 63.
The following provisions apply when a responsible practitioner gives a direction:
if the direction is given under subsection (1)﻿(a),—
the community care order does not cease to have effect, but is treated as a hospital care order for the period specified in the direction; and
the responsible practitioner must make appropriate arrangements to transport the person under compulsory care to and from the hospital:
if the direction is given under subsection (1)﻿(b),—
the community care order ceases to have effect; and
sections 63 to 76 apply, with any necessary modifications:
if the direction is given under subsection (1)﻿(a) or (b), a person specified in section 72 may apply to the court to have the person under compulsory care’s condition reviewed under section 65.
The responsible practitioner may not give a direction under subsection (1)﻿(a) in respect of a person under compulsory care more than twice in any 6-month period.
As soon as practicable after giving a direction under subsection (1), the responsible practitioner must give written notification, including the reasons for giving the direction, to the following:

the person under compulsory care’s usual primary care provider (if any):

the Director of Area Mental Health Services.
84 Changes to hospital care orders
If, at any time while the hospital care order is in force, the responsible practitioner considers that the person under compulsory care can continue to be given care adequately in the community, that practitioner must, by notice in writing,—
direct that the person be discharged from the hospital; and
direct the person to attend at their place of residence, or at some other place nominated in the notice, for the purposes of care.
A hospital care order in respect of a person under compulsory care who receives a notice of direction under subsection (1) must be treated as a community care order, as if the terms of the notice were the terms of the order.
 Expiry and extension of mental health care orders
85 Mental health care order to expire after 6 months
Subject to sections 86 and 87, every mental health care order continues in force for a period of 6 months commencing on the day on which it is made, and then expires.
86 First extension
Within 14 days before the date on which a mental health care order is to expire under section 85, the responsible practitioner must arrange for a care plan review and a status review of the person under compulsory care under section 81.
If, following the status review, the responsible practitioner is satisfied that the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria, the practitioner may apply to the court to extend the order for a further period of 6 months, commencing on the day after the date on which the order would otherwise have expired.
87 Second and subsequent extensions
If a mental health care order has been extended by application under section 86(2) or under subsection (2), the responsible practitioner must, within 14 days immediately before the date on which the extended mental health care order expires, arrange for a care plan review and a status review of the person under compulsory care under section 81.
If, following the status review, the responsible practitioner is satisfied that the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria, they may apply to the court to extend the order for a period of up to 12 months commencing on the day after the date on which the order would otherwise have expired.
88 Determination of applications under sections 86 and 87
Unless the court determines an application in accordance with section 76 or 100, a Judge must, within 2 months after the date on which the application under section 86 or 87 is filed in the court,—
examine the person under compulsory care; and
hear and determine the application.
However, a Judge may extend the period in which they must comply with subsection (1) by up to 1 month if—
the Judge receives an application requesting the extension; and
the purpose of the requested extension is to obtain a second opinion in relation to the status review referred to in section 86 or 87.
If, at the time immediately before the expiry of the relevant extended mental health care order, the court has not determined an application under section 86 or 87, the order does not expire and continues in force until the court determines the application.
89 Mental health care order ceases to have effect or is changed in certain cases
If, at any time while a mental health care order is in force in respect of any person, that person becomes subject to an order made by a court under section 38(2)﻿(c) or 44(1) of the Criminal Procedure (Mentally Impaired Persons) Act 2003, the mental health care order is suspended while that other order is in force.
A mental health care order in respect of any person ceases to have effect if that person—
becomes subject to an order under section 24 or 34 of that Act; or
is sentenced to imprisonment.
If, at any time while a community care order made under section 78 is in force in respect of any person, that person is remanded into the custody of the chief executive of the Department of Corrections, the following applies to the community care order:
the order is suspended for the period that the person is remanded, up to a maximum of 3 months:
if the person is remanded for a period exceeding 3 months, the order ceases to have effect.
If a community care order is suspended, it remains in force for the purposes of section 83 for the period of the suspension, up to a maximum of 3 months.
At any time while a community care order is suspended, the superintendent of the institution in which the person is remanded may, if the superintendent considers that the person meets the compulsory care criteria, apply to the responsible practitioner to consider whether the person should receive care as a person subject to a hospital care order in accordance with section 83.
The superintendent must include in an application under subsection (5) their reasons for considering that the person meets the compulsory care criteria.
If the superintendent applies to the responsible practitioner under subsection (5) and the responsible practitioner, after considering the superintendent’s description, has informed the superintendent that they agree that the person meets the compulsory care criteria, the superintendent may direct that the person—
be removed to a hospital for the purposes of an assessment by the responsible practitioner as to whether the person under compulsory care should receive care subject to a hospital care order; and
following removal to a hospital, be taken back to the institution.
A person who is removed to a hospital under subsection (7)—
must not be detained in the hospital overnight, except in accordance with a direction given under section 83(1); and
must be treated as continuing to be under the legal custody of the chief executive of the Department of Corrections, despite the person being absent from the institution.
If the responsible practitioner gives a direction under section 83(1),—
that direction is sufficient authority—
to detain the person in a hospital; or
to remove the person from the institution to a hospital; and
the person is treated as having ceased to be remanded into the custody of the chief executive of the Department of Corrections; and
the responsible practitioner must conduct a care plan review.
 Leave and absence without leave
90 Leave for person subject to a hospital care order
This section applies to a person under compulsory care, other than a person under forensic compulsory care, who is in a hospital in accordance with a hospital care order.
The responsible practitioner may grant the person under compulsory care leave of absence from the hospital for a period specified by the responsible practitioner on any terms and conditions that the responsible practitioner thinks fit if the responsible practitioner—
is satisfied that the leave of absence is appropriate, taking into account the person’s clinical circumstances and their care plan; and
has considered whether a hospital care order remains appropriate for the person.
The responsible practitioner may extend the period of leave.
The responsible practitioner may cancel the leave at any time during the period of leave by notice in writing to—
the person who has undertaken to take care of the person under compulsory care during the period of leave; or
the person under compulsory care.
If leave is cancelled, the person under compulsory care may be taken to a hospital by—
an authorised person; or
any person to whom the charge of the person under compulsory care has been entrusted during the period of leave.
91 Absence without leave
A person under compulsory care to whom section 90 applies who is absent without leave from the hospital in which they are detained may be returned to hospital by any of the following persons during the period of 3 months starting on the day on which the absence commences:
an authorised person:
a constable:
a member of the person’s rōpū whaiora (collaborative care team).
A person under compulsory care may be taken back to the hospital in which they were detained immediately before the absence or, if that is not reasonably practicable, to the emergency department of any other hospital.
However, a person under compulsory care who is absent without leave from a hospital may be released from compulsory care in accordance with this Act at any time while they are absent.
The Director of Area Mental Health Services must ensure that a record is made within 24 hours after the commencement of an absence, and after the return or release, of a person under compulsory care.
A person under compulsory care who leaves their escort while being lawfully transferred to any other hospital—
must be treated as being absent without leave under subsection (1); and
if they are returned in accordance with this Act, may be taken to the hospital to which they were being transferred, despite the time limit under section 227(2) having elapsed.
 Subpart 4 — Procedure
92 Application of this subpart
This subpart applies to mental health care order applications.
93 Attendance of person under compulsory care and other persons
The person under compulsory care must be present throughout the hearing by the court of an application for a mental health care order unless—
the Judge who examines the person in accordance with section 74(1) certifies that attendance would have a detrimental effect on their health; or
the person under compulsory care is excluded by the court under subsection (2).
The court may exclude the person under compulsory care from any stage of the hearing if it is satisfied that—
attendance would have a detrimental effect on their health; or
the person lacks the capacity to understand the nature and purpose of the proceedings; or
the person is causing such a disturbance that it is not practicable to continue with the hearing in the presence of the person.
The person under compulsory care must be present while the court makes any order on the application unless—
they have been excluded under subsection (2); or
there are exceptional circumstances that justify the court making an order in the absence of the person.
A person notified under section 72 is entitled to be present throughout the hearing, unless the Judge orders otherwise.
94 Court may call for report on person under compulsory care
The court may, if it is satisfied that it is necessary to properly dispose of an application for a mental health care order, request any person whom it considers qualified to do so to prepare a report on any relevant aspect of the person under compulsory care’s clinical, personal, family, whānau, community, or cultural background.
In deciding whether to request a report under subsection (1), the court may ascertain and have regard to the wishes of the person under compulsory care and any other party to the proceedings.
A copy of the report must be given by the Registrar of the court to—
the lawyer for the person under compulsory care and to each of the other parties to the proceedings; and
if any party is not represented by a lawyer, to that party.
The court must order that a copy of a report given to a lawyer not be given or shown to the person for whom the lawyer is acting if the court has reason to believe that disclosure of the contents of the report may pose a serious threat to the health or safety of the person under compulsory care or of any other person.
Any party to the proceedings may tender evidence on any matter referred to in the report.
The court may call the person making the report as a witness—
of its own motion; or
on the application of any party to the proceedings.
A court that requests a person to prepare a report must make either of the orders described in subsection (9).
A court considering whether to make an order under subsection (9)﻿(a) must hear the party or parties affected.
The orders are—
an order for the fees and expenses of the person to be paid by any party or parties to the proceedings, as the court orders:
an order for the fees and expenses of the person to be met from any appropriation by Parliament for the purpose.
95 Evidence
The court may receive any evidence that it thinks fit in proceedings on an application for a mental health care order,—
whether by way of hearing in the first instance, by way of appeal, or otherwise; and
whether or not it is otherwise admissible in a court of law.
96 Right of notified persons to be heard and call evidence
A person notified under section 72 is entitled to—
be heard by the court in person or through a lawyer or through some other person that they nominate; and
call witnesses; and
cross-examine a witness called by another party to the proceedings.
97 Power of court to call witnesses
Without limiting anything in section 96, the court may, of its own motion on any application for a mental health care order, call any person as a witness whose evidence it considers may be of assistance to the court.
A witness called by the court under this section has the same privilege to refuse to answer any question as the witness would have if the witness had been called by a party to the proceedings.
A witness called by the court under this section may be examined and re-examined by the court, and may be cross-examined by or on behalf of any party to the proceedings.
Sections 159 and 161 to 165 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2011, to the extent that they are applicable and with the necessary modifications, apply with respect to every person called as a witness by the court under this section as if that person had been called by a party to the proceedings.
The expenses of any witness called by the court under this section must be met, in accordance with the prescribed scale of witnesses' expenses, in the first instance from any appropriation by Parliament for the purpose.
98 Proceedings not open to public
Only the following persons may be present during the hearing of any proceedings on an application for a mental health care order:
the Judge:
officers of the court:
parties to the proceedings and their lawyers, and any other person nominated by the person under compulsory care:
witnesses:
a person notified under section 72.
A witness must leave the courtroom if asked to do so by the Judge.
This section does not limit the power of the court to hear proceedings in private or to exclude any person from the court.
99 Publication of reports of proceedings
Sections 11B to 11D of the Family Court Act 1980 apply to the publication of a report of any proceedings under this Act—
in the Family Court:
in any other court, in which case references in those sections to the Family Court or court must be read as references to that other court.
100 Court may dispense with examination and hearing
The court may determine an application under section 86 or 87 without examination of the person under compulsory care and without a formal hearing if the court is satisfied that—
the person has given consent in accordance with subsection (2) for the application to be determined in that way; and
no person wishes to be heard in respect of the application; and
it is appropriate to do so in the circumstances.
The consent of the person under compulsory care must be—
given on the advice of a lawyer; and
provided in writing to the court and the Director of Area Mental Health Services.
In this section, lawyer has the meaning given to it by section 6 of the Lawyers and Conveyancers Act 2006.

superintendent,—

106 Detained persons in need of care
This section applies to a person who is detained in a prison or in a residence established under section 114 of the Public Safety (Public Protection Orders) Act 2014, whether or not that person meets the compulsory care criteria, if—
the chief executive of the Department of Corrections considers that the person would benefit from mental health care available in a hospital but not available in the institution in which the person is detained; and

At the conclusion of a review, the Mental Health Review Tribunal must set out its findings in a record of tribunal review, stating whether, in its opinion, the person meets the compulsory care criteria.

If a Mental Health Review Tribunal considers that the person under compulsory care does not meet the compulsory care criteria, the person must be immediately released.
If a Mental Health Review Tribunal considers that the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria, the convener must send a copy of the record of tribunal review to the following persons:
the Director:
the Director of Area Mental Health Services:
the responsible practitioner:
the person under compulsory care:
the person under compulsory care’s support network:
the primary health care provider who usually attended the person under compulsory care immediately before the person was required to undergo second assessment under subpart 2 of Part 3:
a district inspector:
an official visitor.
The convener must also send to the persons specified in subsection (2)﻿(d) to (f) a statement of the legal consequences of the decision and of the recipient's right to appeal to the court against the decision.
The district inspector who receives a copy of the record of tribunal review must, after talking to the person under compulsory care and ascertaining their wishes in the matter, consider whether an appeal should be made to the court against the Mental Health Review Tribunal's decision.
If the district inspector considers that such an appeal should be made, the district inspector must take whatever steps they think necessary to encourage or assist the person under compulsory care, or any person specified in subsection (2)﻿(e) or (f), to make an appeal.
If, in any case to which subsection (4) applies, the district inspector considers that an appeal against the Mental Health Review Tribunal's decision should be made, but neither the person under compulsory care nor any person specified in subsection (2)﻿(e) or (f) intends to make such an appeal,—
the district inspector may report the matter to the court; and
a Judge may, of their own motion, review the condition of the person under compulsory care as if an appropriate appeal had been made to the court.
The district inspector may arrange for an official visitor to perform the functions specified in subsections (4) to (6).
138 Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal reviews
Any person to whom a copy of a record of status review is sent under section 107 may apply to the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal for a review of the condition of the person under forensic compulsory care.
Without limiting anything in subsection (1), the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must review the person under forensic compulsory care’s condition on the application of the Attorney-General under section 107(8)﻿(d).
The convener of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must notify the Director of an application under subsection (1) for a review of the condition of the person under forensic compulsory care.
On receiving notice, the Director must take all reasonable steps to—
give notice to each victim of the person under forensic compulsory care that the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal has received an application under subsection (1) for a review of the person under forensic compulsory care’s condition; and
give the victim an explanation of—
the process under this section for reviewing a person under forensic compulsory care’s condition; and
how the victim may participate in that process.
Any person notified under subsection (4) may write to the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal, by the date specified by the tribunal, making submissions on the tribunal’s review of the person under forensic compulsory care’s condition.
If it appears that for any reason a status review of a person under forensic compulsory care has not taken place, the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal may review the person’s condition, either of its own motion or on application by any person to whom a copy of a record of status review would have been required to be sent if the review had been held.
Section 136(3) to (6) applies in respect of every review under this section as if it were a review under that section.
In the case of a person under forensic compulsory care who was ordered to be detained following a finding of unfitness to stand trial, the following provisions apply to any review of that person’s condition under this section:
the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must record its findings in a record of tribunal review, stating whether, in its opinion,—
the person is no longer unfit to stand trial; or
the person is still unfit to stand trial but it is no longer necessary to safeguard the interests specified in section 31(3A) of the Criminal Procedure (Mentally Impaired Persons) Act 2003 (the CPMIP Act); or
the person is still unfit to stand trial and should continue to be subject to the order of detention as a person under forensic compulsory care:
the convener of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must send a copy of the record of tribunal review to each of the persons specified in section 137(2):
the convener of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must also send a copy of the record of tribunal review to the Attorney-General for the purposes of section 31 of the CPMIP Act if the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal considers that—
the person is no longer unfit to stand trial; or
the person is still unfit to stand trial but it is no longer necessary that the person should be subject to the order of detention as a person under forensic compulsory care.
In the case of a person under forensic compulsory care who was ordered to be detained following acquittal on account of insanity, the following provisions apply to any review of that person’s condition under this section:
the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must, under section 33(3A) of the CPMIP Act, record its findings in a record of tribunal review and make a direction:
the convener of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must send a copy of the record of tribunal review and the direction to each of the persons specified in section 137(2).
139 Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal reviews of persons on a restricted order
Any person to whom a copy of a record of status review is sent under section 135 may apply to the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal for a review of the person on a restricted order’s condition.
Without limiting anything in subsection (1), the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must review the person on a restricted order’s condition on the application of the Director under section 135(9)﻿(b) or (10)﻿(b)﻿(ii).
If it appears that for any reason a status review of a person on a restricted order has not taken place, the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal may review the person’s condition, either of its own motion or on application by any person to whom a copy of a record of status review would have been required to be sent if the review had been held.
Section 136(3) to (6) applies in respect of every review under this section as if it were a review under that section.
At the conclusion of the review, the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must record its findings in a record of tribunal review, stating whether it considers that—
the person on a restricted order does not meet the compulsory care criteria; or
the person on a restricted order meets the compulsory care criteria but it is no longer necessary that the person should be declared to be a person on a restricted order; or
the person on a restricted order meets the compulsory care criteria and must continue to be declared to be a person on a restricted order.
The convener of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal must send a copy of the record of tribunal review to each of the persons specified in section 137(2).
In any case where the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal considers that the person on a restricted order does not meet the compulsory care criteria, the Director must direct that the person be released from compulsory status accordingly.
In any case where the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal considers that the person on a restricted order meets the compulsory care criteria but it is no longer necessary that the person should be declared to be a person on a restricted order, the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal may revoke the declaration that the person is a person on a restricted order.
140 Application to revoke appointment of nominated person
A Mental Health Review Tribunal may revoke the appointment of a person under compulsory care’s nominated person if it receives an application from the person under compulsory care’s responsible practitioner under section 22, and it considers that—
the appointment is not in the best interests of the person under compulsory care; or
the nominated person is unable or unwilling to participate in processes required by the Act; or
there are reasonable grounds to believe that the appointment was obtained by undue influence or fraud.
141 Procedural provisions
A Mental Health Review Tribunal and the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal may regulate their procedures as they see fit, subject to this Act and any regulations made under it.
142 Appeal against Mental Health Review Tribunal's decision in certain cases
If, on a review under section 136, a Mental Health Review Tribunal considers that the person under compulsory care meets the compulsory care criteria, any person specified in section 137(2)﻿(d) to (f) may, within 1 month after the date of the Mental Health Review Tribunal's decision, appeal to the Family Court against that decision.
On appeal,—
the Family Court must review the person under compulsory care’s condition to determine whether they meet the compulsory care criteria; and
section 65 applies with any necessary modifications.
143 Judicial inquiry
A Judge of the High Court may, on the Judge's own initiative or on the application of any person, make an order that directs a district inspector or any 1 or more persons whom the Judge selects—
to visit and examine any person who the Judge has reason to believe is being detained in a hospital as a person under compulsory care; and
to inquire into and report on any matters relating to that person.
Despite subsection (1), a Judge of the High Court may, on the Judge's initiative or on the application of any person, make an order directing the responsible practitioner to bring any person who is being detained as a person under compulsory care in the hospital before the Judge, in open court or in chambers, for examination at a time to be specified in the order.
Unless the person is a person under forensic compulsory care or is lawfully detained for some other reason, the Judge must order that the person be discharged from the hospital immediately if the Judge is satisfied on the evidence provided under this section—
that the person is detained unlawfully in the hospital as a person under compulsory care; or
that the person is fit to be discharged from the hospital.
Without limiting subsection (5), the Judge has the same powers as the Attorney-General under section 31 of the Criminal Procedure (Mentally Impaired Persons) Act 2003 if—
the person has been found to be unfit to stand trial and is detained as a person under forensic compulsory care under section 24 of that Act; and
the Judge is satisfied that the person is capable of being tried on the charge against them.
If the person has been found unfit to stand trial and is detained as a person under forensic compulsory care under section 24 of that Act, the Judge may order that the charge be dismissed if the Judge considers—
it is proper in the circumstances of the case; and
it is in the interests of justice to do so.
An order under subsection (5) may—
require that the person be released from compulsory care if the Judge is satisfied that the person does not meet the compulsory care criteria; or
require that the person continue in compulsory care as a person subject to a hospital care order if the Judge is satisfied that the person meets the compulsory care criteria.
For the purposes of any examination under this section, the Judge may—
summon any medical or other witnesses to testify on oath in respect of any matter involved in the examination, and to produce any relevant documents; and
call for any report on the person's condition by a Mental Health Review Tribunal or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal.
The Judge may report their opinion to the Minister, with any comments and recommendations that the Judge thinks fit.
Nothing in this section prevents the exercise of any other remedy or proceeding available to any person who is or is alleged to be unlawfully detained, confined, or imprisoned.

153 Visits by district inspectors and official visitors

 Mental Health Review Tribunals
163 Review Tribunal continued
The Review Tribunal appointed under section 101 of the Mental Health (Compulsory Assessment and Treatment) Act 1992 that is in existence immediately before the commencement of this Act is continued as a Mental Health Review Tribunal.
164 Appointment of Mental Health Review Tribunals
The Minister may appoint as many Mental Health Review Tribunals as the Minister determines necessary for the purposes of this Act.
The appointment of a Mental Health Review Tribunal must specify the area covered by that tribunal.
A Mental Health Review Tribunal comprises up to 4 persons appointed by the Minister, and must include—
a lawyer:
a suitably qualified mental health practitioner.
When appointing members, the Minister must be satisfied that the membership of the tribunal has—
lived experience of being subject to compulsory mental health care:
knowledge of tikanga and mātauranga Māori:
if considering a matter concerning a person under compulsory care who is under the age of 18, expertise in child and adolescent development.
A person must not act as a member of a Mental Health Review Tribunal in any case where, given the identity of the person under compulsory care, a conflict of interest may arise.
Subject to section 167(3), a Mental Health Review Tribunal is not affected by any vacancy in its membership.
Subject to subsection (5), nothing in this section prevents the appointment of a member of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal to the Mental Health Review Tribunal.
165 Functions and powers of Mental Health Review Tribunals
The principal function of a Mental Health Review Tribunal is to consider the condition of a person under compulsory care who has applied for a review, or in respect of whom an application for a review has been made, under section 136 or 138.
A Mental Health Review Tribunal may at any time, and must whenever required by the Director to do so, report to the Director on any matter relating to the exercise or performance of its powers and functions under this Act.
A Mental Health Review Tribunal has the functions and powers conferred on it by this Act or any other enactment.
166 Co-opting suitable persons
A Mental Health Review Tribunal may, for the purposes of any particular case, co-opt—
a person whose specialised knowledge or expertise would be of assistance to the Mental Health Review Tribunal in dealing with the case; or
a person of the same ethnic identity as the person under compulsory care, if no member of the Mental Health Review Tribunal has that ethnic identity; or
a person of the same gender as the person under compulsory care, if no member of the Mental Health Review Tribunal is of that gender; or
a person with a disability, if no member of the Mental Health Review Tribunal has a disability.
However, if in any case no member of the Mental Health Review Tribunal has the same ethnic identity as the person under compulsory care, is of the same gender as the person under compulsory care, or has a disability, the Mental Health Review Tribunal must co-opt a suitable person under subsection (1)﻿(b), (c), or (d) if the person under compulsory care or the applicant requests it to do so.
A person who is co-opted under this section must be treated as a member of the tribunal in respect of the case for which they are co-opted.
167 Meetings and powers
A Mental Health Review Tribunal must meet at the times and places that the Mental Health Review Tribunal or the convener appoints.
Meetings may be held by audiovisual link.
No business may be transacted at any meeting of a Mental Health Review Tribunal unless each member, or their deputy, is present.
A Mental Health Review Tribunal has the same powers and authority to summon witnesses and receive evidence as an inquiry has under the Inquiries Act 2013, other than the powers under section 28 of that Act (which relates to costs).
 Further provisions relating to Mental Health Review Tribunals
168 Deputies of members
The Minister may appoint persons to be deputies of members of each Mental Health Review Tribunal.
The deputy of each member who is a lawyer must be a lawyer, and the deputy of each member who is a mental health practitioner must be a mental health practitioner.
A deputy may act for the member for whom they are appointed during any period when that member is incapacitated by illness, or prevented by any other sufficient cause from performing the duties of office.
No deputy may act for more than 1 member at the same time.
A deputy is a member of a Mental Health Review Tribunal.
169 Terms of office
Every member of a Mental Health Review Tribunal, and every deputy of a member, holds office for a term not exceeding 3 years and may be reappointed.
Any member of a Mental Health Review Tribunal, and any deputy of a member, may at any time resign by notice in writing addressed to the Minister.
Any member of a Mental Health Review Tribunal, and any deputy of a member, may at any time be removed from office by the Minister for neglect of duty, misconduct, bankruptcy, or inability to perform the functions of the office proved to the satisfaction of the Minister.
However, every member of a Mental Health Review Tribunal, and every deputy of a member, must, unless the member sooner dies, resigns, or is removed from office, continue in office until a successor is appointed despite the expiry of their term of office.
170 Convener
The members of a Mental Health Review Tribunal must elect one of the members to be the convener of the Mental Health Review Tribunal.
The convener presides at every meeting of the Mental Health Review Tribunal at which they are present.
If the convener is not present at any meeting, the members present must elect one of the members to preside at that meeting as convener.
The convener may vote on any matter and must cast a deciding vote if the Mental Health Review Tribunal cannot make a decision.
171 Review principles
A Mental Health Review Tribunal must be guided by the following principles when reviewing applications and complaints:
fairness:
accessibility of processes, having particular regard to tikanga Māori:
timeliness and efficiency:
resolution of complaints at an appropriate level:
the need to—
uphold the mana of parties involved and promote restorative practices:
ensure that all tāngata whaiora, including those with physical, sensory, intellectual, and other disabilities, can access and participate in the process.
172 Fees and travelling allowances
A Mental Health Review Tribunal is a statutory Board within the meaning of the Fees and Travelling Allowances Act 1951.
The members of Mental Health Review Tribunals are paid remuneration by way of fees, salary, or allowances, and travelling allowances and expenses, under that Act.
The remuneration referred to in subsection (2) is met from any appropriation by Parliament for the purpose.
173 No proceedings against members of Mental Health Review Tribunals unless bad faith shown
No civil proceedings may be brought against any member of a Mental Health Review Tribunal for anything they may do, report, or say in the course of the exercise or performance or intended exercise or performance of their powers, duties, or functions under this Act, unless it is shown that they acted in bad faith.
Nothing in this section affects the right of any person or organisation to apply, in accordance with law, for judicial review of a Mental Health Review Tribunal member's exercise or performance of powers, duties, or functions under this Act.
 Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal
174 Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal
The Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal is established.
The Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal comprises up to 4 persons appointed by the Minister, and must include—
a lawyer; and
a suitably qualified mental health practitioner with expertise in forensic mental health care.
When appointing members, the Minister must be satisfied that the membership of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal has—
lived experience of being subject to compulsory mental health care; and
knowledge of tikanga and mātaurangi Māori.
A person must not act as a member of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal in any case where, given the identity of the person under forensic compulsory care or person on a restricted order, a conflict of interest may arise.
Subject to subsection (4), nothing in this section prevents the appointment of a member of the Mental Health Review Tribunal to the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal.
175 Functions of Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal
The principal functions of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal are to—
determine applications for leave of absence for persons under forensic compulsory care and persons on a restricted order under section 114; and
review the condition of persons under forensic compulsory care under section 138; and
review the condition of persons on a restricted order under section 139; and
determine applications for change of status under section 33 of the Criminal Procedure (Mentally Impaired Persons) Act 2003.
The Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal may at any time, and must whenever required by the Director to do so, report to the Director on any matter relating to the exercise or performance of its powers and functions under this Act.
The Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal has the functions and powers conferred on it by this Act or any other enactment.
176 Appeal against decisions of Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal
The following may, within 1 month after the date of a decision by the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal, appeal to the Family Court against that decision:
the Director:
the person under forensic compulsory care or the person on a restricted order in respect of whom an application is made to the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal:
the person’s nominated person.
177 Further provisions relating to Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal
Sections 140 and 166 to 173 apply in respect of the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal with any necessary modifications.
 Public assistance
178 Advice and assistance of general nature
An authorised person must provide the following services to the public:
act as a ready point of contact for anyone in the community requiring assistance in relation to tāngata whaiora:

 Part 7 — Powers and offences
 Powers
182 Police powers in relation to person appearing to meet compulsory care criteria in public place
This section applies if a person is found in a public place acting in a manner that gives a constable reasonable grounds to suspect that the person may meet the compulsory care criteria.
In forming their suspicion that a person may meet the compulsory care criteria, the constable—
is not required to make a medical assessment or clinical judgment; but
may seek the advice of an authorised person (see section 178).
The constable may, if they consider it desirable in the interests of the person or of the public,—
detain and take the person to a hospital or any other place specified by a Director of Area Mental Health Services; and
arrange for a mental health practitioner to examine the person at that place as soon as practicable.
If the mental health practitioner examines the person and does not consider that the person meets the compulsory care criteria, the person must be released immediately.
Subsection (6) applies if the mental health practitioner examines the person and considers that—
there are reasonable grounds to believe that the person meets the compulsory care criteria; and
it is desirable in the person's own interests or the interests of any other person for the person to urgently undergo first assessment.
The mental health practitioner must, as soon as possible, make an application under section 57(1).
A constable may detain the person for the purposes of subsections (3) to (6) for 6 hours or the time it takes to carry out the actions described in those subsections, whichever is shorter.
However, the constable must immediately release the person when the constable no longer has reasonable grounds to suspect that the person may meet the compulsory care criteria.
The constable must record the reasons for their suspicion that the person may meet the compulsory care criteria and, if the person is released under subsection (8), the reasons for the decision to release them. The constable must give a copy of the record to the Director of Area Mental Health Services as soon as practicable after making it.
183 Powers of mental health practitioner when urgent examination required
Subsection (2) applies to a mental health practitioner who—
conducts an examination of a person who is acting in a manner that gives reasonable grounds to believe that they meet the compulsory care criteria; and
concludes that—
there are reasonable grounds to believe that the person meets the compulsory care criteria; and
it is desirable for the person to urgently undergo first assessment.
The mental health practitioner must, as soon as possible, make an application under section 57(1).
A mental health practitioner acting under this section must, before calling for Police assistance under subsection (4), make every reasonable effort to—
meet in person with the person; and
get the advice and assistance of an authorised person under section 180(2).
A mental health practitioner who has complied with subsection (3) and needs assistance to conduct an examination may call for Police assistance to be provided under section 186.
184 Power to sedate person when sedation urgently required
This section applies to a mental health practitioner who—
makes an application required by section 183(2); and
believes on reasonable grounds that it is necessary to sedate a person to prevent serious harm to that person or any other person.
The mental health practitioner or a paramedic acting on the request of the mental health practitioner may immediately administer an appropriate sedative drug to the person, by injection if necessary, in accordance with any relevant guidelines and standards for compulsory care issued by the Director-General under section 214.
The mental health practitioner who administers or requests the administration of a sedative drug under subsection (2) must, as soon as practicable,—
record the circumstances in which the drug was administered, including the reasons for administering it, in accordance with section 216(2); and
give a copy of the record to the Director of Area Mental Health Services.
A mental health practitioner acting under this section must, before calling for Police assistance under subsection (5), make every reasonable effort to—
meet in person with the person; and
get the advice and assistance of an authorised person under section 180(2).
A mental health practitioner who has complied with subsection (4) and needs assistance to administer a sedative drug may call for Police assistance to be provided under section 186.
185 Powers of mental health practitioner when urgent assessment required
This section applies to the mental health practitioner who conducts the first assessment of a proposed person under compulsory care following an application required by section 183(2).
The mental health practitioner must conduct the first assessment under section 59 as soon as possible.
A mental health practitioner acting under this section must, before calling for Police assistance under subsection (4), make every reasonable effort to—
meet in person with the proposed person under compulsory care; and
get the advice and assistance of an authorised person under section 180(2).
A mental health practitioner who has complied with subsection (3) and needs assistance to conduct the first assessment may call for Police assistance to be provided under section 186.
186 Powers of Police when urgent assistance required
A constable called to the assistance of a mental health practitioner—
may enter the premises where the person or proposed person under compulsory care is; and
must, if that constable is not in uniform, produce to any person occupying the premises their badge or other evidence that they are a constable.
A constable who enters premises under subsection (1) may, at the request of the mental health practitioner,—
detain the person or proposed person under compulsory care at the premises; or
take the person or proposed person under compulsory care to a place nominated by the mental health practitioner and detain the person or proposed person under compulsory care at that place.
The mental health practitioner must be present during the entire detention of the person or proposed person under compulsory care, unless it is not reasonably practicable to do so, in which case the constable may exercise the powers under subsection (2) as directed by the authorised person by telephone or by other means of audio communication.
Detention under subsection (2) may last for no longer than 6 hours, or the time until the constable is satisfied on reasonable grounds that the person may undergo 1 of the following procedures without further police detention, or the following time, whichever is shortest:
for the purposes of section 183(1)﻿(a), the time it takes to conduct the examination:
for the purposes of section 184(2), the time it takes to administer the sedative drug:
for the purposes of section 185(2), the time it takes to conduct the examination.
187 Powers of nurse if urgent assessment required
This section applies if—
a person has been brought to or admitted to hospital (not being a person under compulsory care or a proposed person under compulsory care); and
the nurse who has responsibility for the person considers that—
there are reasonable grounds to suspect that the person may meet the compulsory care criteria; and
it is desirable for the person to urgently undergo an assessment examination in the person's own interests or the interests of any other person.
In forming their suspicion that a person may meet the compulsory care criteria, the nurse may seek the advice of an authorised person (see section 178).
The nurse must arrange as soon as practicable for a mental health practitioner to examine the person for the purposes of section 57.
The nurse may—
detain the person where they are until a mental health practitioner has examined the person; or
take the person to some other place to enable a mental health practitioner to examine the person, and detain the person at that other place until a mental health practitioner has examined the person.
However, the nurse must immediately release the person when the nurse no longer has reasonable grounds to suspect that the person may meet the compulsory care criteria.
The nurse must record the reasons for their suspicion that the person may meet the compulsory care criteria and, if the person is released under subsection (5), the reasons for the decision to release them. The nurse must give a copy of the record to the Director of Area Mental Health Services as soon as practicable after making it.
A person must not be detained under this section for more than 6 hours from the time when the nurse first calls for a mental health practitioner to examine the person.

 Offences
190 Neglect or ill-treatment
This section applies to—
the person in charge of a service at which a proposed person under compulsory care attends for first assessment; and
the person in charge of a hospital in which a person is subject to a hospital care order or is a voluntary mental health consumer; and
a person employed in any such service and engaged—
in the conduct of first assessment of a proposed person under compulsory care; or
in the care or assessment of a person under compulsory care or voluntary mental health consumer; and
the person in charge of a home, house, or other place where a person under compulsory care, proposed person under compulsory care, or voluntary mental health consumer resides.
A person who intentionally ill-treats or intentionally neglects a person under compulsory care, proposed person under compulsory care, or voluntary mental health consumer commits an offence and is liable on conviction to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years.
191 Failure to ensure access to communication aid
A responsible practitioner commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $15,000 if they fail to take reasonable steps to ensure that a person under compulsory care has access to their communication aid.
192 Failure to ensure access to mobility aid
A responsible practitioner commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $15,000 if they fail to take reasonable steps to ensure that a person under compulsory care has access to their mobility aid.
193 Failure to comply with electroconvulsive therapy requirements
A responsible practitioner commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $30,000 if they knowingly provide electroconvulsive therapy other than in accordance with the requirements of section 51(1) or (2).
A responsible practitioner commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $5,000 if they fail to record the use of electroconvulsive therapy in accordance with section 51(3).
194 Assisting person under community care order not to attend for care
Subsection (2) applies to a person who is employed in or about a place at which a person under compulsory care who is subject to a community care order is required to attend for care.
The person commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000 if they—
intentionally permit a person under compulsory care not to attend, or to attempt not to attend, at the place; or
intentionally assist in allowing an unauthorised absence or attempted unauthorised absence.
A person commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000 if they—
intentionally instigate or assist a person under compulsory care who is subject to a community care order not to attend, or to attempt not to attend, at any place at which the person under compulsory care is required to attend for care; or
intentionally assist a person under compulsory care who is so absent to avoid, or to attempt to avoid, being taken to the place.
195 Assisting person under hospital care order to be absent without leave
Subsection (2) applies to a person who is employed in or about a hospital in which a person under compulsory care who is subject to a hospital care order is detained.
The person commits an offence and is liable to a fine not exceeding $10,000 if they—
intentionally permit a person under compulsory care to become, or to attempt to become, absent without leave from the hospital; or
intentionally assist in allowing an absence or attempted absence.
A person commits an offence and is liable to a fine not exceeding $10,000 if they—
intentionally instigate or assist a person under compulsory care who is subject to a hospital care order to become, or to attempt to become, absent without leave from the hospital specified in the order or to which the person has been transferred under section 227; or
intentionally assist a person under compulsory care who is absent without leave to avoid, or to attempt to avoid, being returned to hospital.
196 Unlawful publication of reports of proceedings before tribunal
A person who publishes a report of proceedings before a Mental Health Review Tribunal or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal without the leave of that tribunal commits an offence against this Act and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding,—
for an individual, $10,000; and
for any other person, $30,000.
In proceedings against a person for an offence against this section, it is not necessary to prove that the person intended to commit the offence or had any other state of mind in relation to any element of the offence.
197 Obstruction of inspection
The Director of Area Mental Health Services of any service, a responsible practitioner, or an employee in that service commits an offence, and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $30,000, if a district inspector or an official visitor is visiting the service and the Director of Area Mental Health Services, responsible practitioner, or employee—
conceals or attempts to conceal from the district inspector or official visitor, or refuses or wilfully neglects to show to the district inspector or official visitor, any part of the service or any person detained or being given care in it; or
in any other manner wilfully obstructs or attempts to obstruct the district inspector or official visitor in the conduct of their official duties.
198 False or misleading records
A person commits an offence against this section if they—
include or cause to be included in any record under this Act any information that they know to be false or misleading in any material respect; or
negligently include or negligently cause to be included in any such record any information that is false or misleading in any material respect.
A person who commits an offence against subsection (1)﻿(a) is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding,—
for an individual, $30,000; and
for any other person, $50,000.
A person who commits an offence against subsection (1)﻿(b) is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding,—
for an individual, $5,000; and
for any other person, $15,000.
199 Further offences involving false or misleading documents
A person commits an offence against this section if they—
intentionally omit, or intentionally cause any other person to omit, in any notice, statement, or entry under this Act any information prescribed or required by or under this Act to be included in the notice, statement, or entry; or
include or cause to be included in any such notice, statement, or entry any information that they know to be false in any material respect; or
negligently include or negligently cause to be included in any such notice, statement, or entry any information that is false or misleading in any material respect.
A person who commits an offence against subsection (1)﻿(a) or (b) is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding,—
for an individual, $30,000; and
for any other person, $50,000.
A person who commits an offence against subsection (1)﻿(c) is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding,—
for an individual, $5,000; and
for any other person, $15,000.

the Director or another person authorised in writing by the Director in a particular case; or
any constable.
 Infringement offences
201 Infringement offences
A person who is alleged to have committed an infringement offence may—
be proceeded against by the filing of a charging document under section 14 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2011; or
be issued with an infringement notice under section 203.
Proceedings commenced in the way described in subsection (1)﻿(a) do not require the leave of a District Court Judge or Registrar under section 21(1)﻿(a) of the Summary Proceedings Act 1957.
See section 21 of the Summary Proceedings Act 1957 for the procedure that applies if an infringement notice is issued.
202 Who may issue infringement notices
The Director may, in writing, authorise any person to issue infringement notices under this Act.
203 When infringement notice may be issued
The Director or a person authorised by the Director may issue an infringement notice to a person if the Director or person authorised by the Director believes on reasonable grounds that the person is committing, or has committed, an infringement offence.
204 Revocation of infringement notice before payment made
The Director or a person authorised by the Director may revoke an infringement notice before—
the infringement fee is paid; or
an order for payment of a fine is made or deemed to be made by a court under section 21 of the Summary Proceedings Act 1957.
The Director or a person authorised by the Director must take reasonable steps to ensure that the person to whom the notice was issued is made aware of the revocation of the notice.
The revocation of an infringement notice before the infringement fee is paid is not a bar to any further action as described in section 201(1)﻿(a) or (b) against the person to whom the notice was issued in respect of the same matter.
205 What infringement notice must contain
An infringement notice must be in the form prescribed in the regulations and must contain the following particulars:
details of the alleged infringement offence that fairly inform a person of the time, place, and nature of the alleged offence:
the amount of the infringement fee:
the address of the Director:
how the infringement fee may be paid:
the time within which the infringement fee must be paid:
a summary of the provisions of section 21(10) of the Summary Proceedings Act 1957:
a statement that the person served with the notice has a right to request a hearing:
a statement of what will happen if the person served with the notice neither pays the infringement fee nor requests a hearing:
any other matters prescribed in the regulations.
206 How infringement notice may be served
An infringement notice may be served on the person who the Director or a person authorised by the Director believes is committing or has committed the infringement offence by—
delivering it to the person or, if the person refuses to accept it, bringing it to the person's notice; or
leaving it for the person at the person's last known place of residence with another person who appears to be of or over the age of 14 years; or
leaving it for the person at the person's place of business or work with another person; or
sending it to the person by prepaid post addressed to the person's last known place of residence or place of business or work; or
sending it to an electronic address of the person in any case where the person does not have a known place of residence or business in New Zealand.
Unless the contrary is shown,—
an infringement notice (or a copy of it) sent by prepaid post to a person under subsection (1) is to be treated as having been served on that person on the fifth working day after the date on which it was posted; and
an infringement notice sent to a valid electronic address is to be treated as having been served at the time the electronic communication first entered an information system that is outside the control of the Director.
207 Payment of infringement fees
All infringement fees paid for infringement offences must be paid into a Crown Bank Account.
208 Reminder notices
A reminder notice must be in the form prescribed in the regulations and must include the same particulars, or substantially the same particulars, as the infringement notice.
209 Certain sections of Crimes Act 1961 apply to powers to take and return
Sections 91(1), 120(3), 121(3), 131, 180(2), 181(3) and (5), 182(3) and (7), 186(2), 187(4), and 189 contain a power to take or return a person, a person under compulsory care, or a proposed person under compulsory care. In respect of each of those powers, sections 30, 31, and 34 of the Crimes Act 1961 apply—
as if the power were a power of arrest; and
with any necessary modifications.
210 Use of force
A person exercising a power specified in subsection (2) may not use force unless—
they are exercising the power in an emergency; and
there is no reasonable alternative to the use of force; and
they use the minimum force that is reasonably necessary in the circumstances.
The powers are—
a power to take or return a person, person under compulsory care, or proposed person under compulsory care under any of sections 91(1), 108, 109, 120(3), 121(3), 131, 180(2), 181(3) and (5), 182(3) and (7), 186(2), 187(4), and 189:
a power to give emergency care under section 48:
a power to restrain a person under compulsory care under section 112(4):
a power to detain a person, person under compulsory care, or proposed person under compulsory care under any of sections 181(3) and (5), 182(3) to (6), 186(2), 187(4), and 188:
a power to enter premises under either of sections 181(2) and 186(1).
If force has been used under this section,—
the circumstances in which the force was used must be recorded as soon as practicable; and
a copy of the record must be given to the Director of Area Mental Health Services as soon as practicable; and
the Director of Area Mental Health Services must report the use of force and record the circumstances in accordance with section 216(2).
Every person performing a function under this Act must use their best endeavours to minimise the use of force.
211 Protection from criminal liability
A person is not subject to criminal liability if they—
believe on reasonable grounds that a notice has been properly given under section 58 or 61 or a mental health care order has been properly made; and
act relying on that belief; and
act in good faith.
A person is not subject to criminal liability if—
they use force while exercising a power or performing a function in accordance with section 210; and
it was reasonably necessary to use such force in the circumstances; and
the use of force was undertaken in accordance with any guidelines issued by the Director-General under section 214.

220 Fees of mental health practitioners
A mental health practitioner who issues a record, notice, or statement in writing for the purposes of, and in accordance with, this Act must be paid the prescribed fee.
If no fee is prescribed, such a mental health practitioner must be paid the fee that the Minister, with the concurrence of the Minister of Finance, directs from time to time.
In any particular case to which subsection (2) applies, the Minister may withhold the whole or any part of the fee.
Fees payable under subsection (2) are met from any appropriation by Parliament for the purpose.
No fees are payable under this section to a mental health practitioner employed by a service, or by the Ministry of Health, for anything done in the course of that employment.
 Reporting on operation of Act
221 Annual report on implementation of Act
The Director must publish a report annually on the implementation of this Act.
The report must—
include the information prescribed by regulations; and
be published on an internet site that is accessible to the public free of charge.
222 Director-General must review Act every 5 years
The Director-General must conduct a review of the policy and operation of this Act within 5 years after the commencement of this section, and afterwards at intervals of no longer than 5 years.
A review under this section must include a review of progress towards the reduction and elimination of seclusion.
223 Minister to establish advisory committee
The Minister must, within 6 months of the commencement of this section, establish an advisory committee to advise on the operation of this Act.
When appointing members of the advisory committee, the Minister must be satisfied that the membership of the committee has, collectively,—
lived experience of being subject to compulsory mental health care; and
knowledge of tikanga and mātauranga Māori.
The advisory committee must be treated as being established under section 87 of the Healthy Futures (Pae Ora) Act 2022 and sections 87 and 88 of that Act apply with all necessary modifications.
 Miscellaneous provisions
224 Application of other Acts
Except as expressly provided in this Act, nothing in this Act limits or affects in any way the provisions of any other Act.
225 Use of audiovisual links
This section applies if a person other than a Judge or a member of a Mental Health Review Tribunal or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal (person A) performs a function or exercises a power under this Act that requires access to, or the presence of, 1 or more other persons (person B).
Person A may use an audiovisual link to access person B to exercise a power under this Act if—
it is not practicable for person B to be physically present; and
person A has considered the views of person B on the use of an audiovisual link; and
the use of an audiovisual link is appropriate in the circumstances.
Person A must apply any relevant guidelines and standards for compulsory care issued by the Director-General under section 214 when deciding whether—
it is practicable for person B to be physically present; and
the use of an audiovisual link is appropriate in the circumstances.
If a mental health practitioner uses an audiovisual link under subsection (2), the mental health practitioner must—
record in writing the reason that—
it was not practicable for the person to be physically present; and
the use of an audiovisual link was appropriate in the circumstances; and
provide the record to the Director of Area Mental Health Services as soon as practicable after the use of the link.
226 Use of audiovisual link by Judge or member of tribunal
A Judge or a member of a Mental Health Review Tribunal or the Forensic Mental Health Review Tribunal may use an audiovisual link to examine a person under compulsory care if—
it is not practicable for the person to be physically present for an examination; and
they have considered the views of the person on the use of the audiovisual link.
A Judge may determine that all or any participants may appear at a hearing by audiovisual link if—
it is not practicable for the participant or participants to be physically present for the hearing; and
they have taken into account—
the views of the person under compulsory care on the use of an audiovisual link; and
the potential impact of the use of the technology on the effective maintenance of the rights of the person under section 96, and on the right to assess the credibility of witnesses and the reliability of evidence presented to the court.
In this section,—
audiovisual link means facilities that enable both audio and visual communication,—
in relation to an examination of a person under compulsory care, with that person:
in relation to a hearing, between all participants
participant means any of the following persons in a hearing:
a party:
the person under compulsory care:
counsel:
a witness:
the presiding Judge.
To avoid doubt, an examination may not be carried out under this section by audio link.
227 Transfer of persons under compulsory care
The Director may—
direct that any person under compulsory care other than a person under forensic compulsory care be transferred from any service in which that person is detained to any other service specified in the direction (see section 111 for the transfer of persons under forensic compulsory care); and
vary the direction.
A Director of Area Mental Health Services must comply with any direction given under subsection (1) at the time or within the period stated in the direction or, if no time or period is stated, within 14 days after the date of the direction.
However, the person in charge of any service (not being a secure mental health facility)—
may arrange with the person in charge of any other service (not being a secure mental health facility) for the transfer of any person under compulsory care other than a person under forensic compulsory care or a person on a restricted order to that other service; and
may carry out the transfer in accordance with that arrangement.
A direction given under subsection (1) or an arrangement made under subsection (3) is sufficient authority for the transfer of the person under compulsory care and for the person to be received and detained by the service to which it is directed or arranged that they be transferred.
However, if the person under compulsory care is not in a fit state to be removed within the period in subsection (2), the person in charge of the service in which the person under compulsory care is detained must—
provide the Director with a record to that effect; and
transfer the person under compulsory care within 14 days after the person has become fit to be removed.
If the person under compulsory care to be transferred under this section is under the age of 18 years,—
that person or a member of their support network may request the Director of Area Mental Health Services to permit a support person to accompany the person for the duration of the transfer; and
the request may not be unreasonably denied.
The transfer of a person under compulsory care under this section is not completed until the person is received into the service to which the person is transferred, and the responsibility for the person’s care and control must be determined accordingly.
When a person under compulsory care is transferred from one service to another, certified true copies of the following documents must be delivered to the person in charge of the service to which the person under compulsory care is transferred:
the mental health care order:
the record of assessment:
the application that accompanied the mental health care order, or that accompanied any other instrument of authority under which the person was admitted or detained:
the person’s care plan:
sufficient clinical records of the person under compulsory care to provide an adequate summary of the clinical condition of the person immediately before the person’s transfer.
The mental health care order or other instrument of authority remains in force as if the person under compulsory care had been ordered to be received in the service to which the person is transferred.
228 Notice of death
If a responsible practitioner becomes aware of the death of a person under compulsory care (whether within or outside the hospital), they must—
immediately notify a constable; and
notify the person’s nominated person, primary support person, or next of kin as soon as practicable; and
within 14 days after the death, notify the Director of—
the death; and
the apparent cause of death; and
any other circumstances relevant to the death.
229 Removal from New Zealand
The Minister may, if the Minister is satisfied, having considered the advice of the Director under subsection (2), that it is appropriate for a person under compulsory care to be removed from New Zealand,—
by warrant, authorise and direct the removal of the person to any place outside New Zealand; and
make an order concerning the person’s custody pending their removal.
The Director must advise the Minister on the following:
the will and preferences of the person:
the views of the person’s support network:
the care that the person will receive once removed from New Zealand:
any other matters that the Director considers relevant.
The Minister may,—
as a condition of issuing the warrant, direct that sufficient security be given, in any manner that the Minister thinks fit, for the safe custody and maintenance of the person after their removal from New Zealand; and
give directions for the payment by or on behalf of the person or out of the person’s estate of any expenses incidental to their removal from New Zealand; and
direct any person who is, by virtue of a property order made under Part 3 of the Protection of Personal and Property Rights Act 1988, the manager of any property belonging to the person under compulsory care to pay or transfer any property to a person or persons in the country to which the person under compulsory care is being removed, or to deal with the property in any other manner that the Minister decides.
A person removed from a hospital under the authority of this section must be treated as having been released from compulsory care status.
