Veterinarians Act 2005
1 Title
This Act is the Veterinarians Act 2005.
2 Commencement
Section 104 comes into force on 1 January 2006.
The rest of this Act comes into force on the day after the date on which it receives the Royal assent.
 Part 1 — Preliminary provisions
3 Purpose
The purpose of this Act is to protect the public interest by aiming to ensure that veterinarians are competent to practise; and, for that purpose,—
to continue the Veterinary Council of New Zealand; and
to state the Council's functions; and
to provide the Council with the powers to—
register people qualified to practise as veterinarians; and
issue practising certificates to specialists and people qualified to practise as veterinarians; and
set and implement standards for veterinary performance; and
monitor performance and, if necessary, discipline veterinarians and certain other people.
4 Interpretation
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—
certificate of registration—
means a certificate of registration issued by the Council under section 12(2); and
includes—
a certificate of limited registration issued by the Council under section 14(3); and
a certificate of provisional registration issued by the Council under section 15(3)
chairperson means the chairperson of the Council elected under clause 7 of Schedule 1
competence assessment means a competence assessment conducted under section 58
complaints assessment committee means a complaints assessment committee appointed under section 82
conditions of practice means conditions of practice that the Council may impose on a specified person under section 61
Council means the Veterinary Council of New Zealand continued under section 73
disciplinary hearing means a hearing by the Council conducted under sections 47 to 49
health practitioner has the same meaning as in section 5(1) of the Health Practitioners Competence Assurance Act 2003
institution means a university, veterinary college, or other institution providing tertiary education in veterinary science
judicial committee means a judicial committee appointed under section 83
layperson means a person other than a veterinarian, a registered person, or a person entitled to be registered under this Act
limited registration means limited registration under section 14
medical assessment means a medical assessment conducted under section 55
medical practitioner means a health practitioner who is, or is deemed to be, registered with the Medical Council of New Zealand continued by section 114(1)﻿(a) of the Health Practitioners Competence Assurance Act 2003 as a practitioner of the profession of medicine
member means a member of the Council
mental or physical condition includes, without limitation,—
any mental or physical impairment; and
any condition or impairment caused by alcohol or drug abuse
minimum standards for practising means the minimum standards for practising as a veterinarian prescribed by the Council under section 75(i)
Minister means the Minister of the Crown who, under the authority of a warrant or with the authority of the Prime Minister, is for the time being responsible for the administration of this Act
oversight means professional support and assistance provided to a veterinarian by another veterinarian for the purposes of professional development
practising certificate means a practising certificate issued by the Council under section 26(1)
prescribed means prescribed by the Council (using the procedure in section 88(1))
provisional registration means provisional registration under section 15
recognised, in relation to an institution, degree, or diploma, means recognised by the Council under section 8(1)
register means the register established and maintained by the Council under section 22(1)
registered means registered under this Act; and registration has a corresponding meaning
registered person—
means a person registered under section 12(2); and
includes a person registered under section 14 or section 15
Registrar means the Registrar for the Council appointed under clause 20 of Schedule 1
specialist means a veterinarian who satisfies the requirements of section 7(2)
specified period includes a period ending on the occurrence of a specified event
specified person has the meaning set out in section 34
supervision means the monitoring of, and reporting on, the performance of a veterinarian by another veterinarian
veterinarian means a person—
who is a registered person; and
who holds a current practising certificate.
5 Act binds the Crown
This Act binds the Crown.
 Part 2 — Registration and practising certificates
 Subpart 1 — Registration
6 Entitlement to registration
A person, on payment of the prescribed fee, is entitled to be registered if the person satisfies the Council that he or she is a fit and proper person to be registered and has, or is eligible to have,—
a recognised veterinary science degree or diploma from a recognised institution; or
a veterinary science degree or diploma of not less than 4 years of study and 1 or more of the following:
a pass mark in prescribed examinations:
a pass in an assessment programme approved by the Council:
a pass in post-graduate study or training approved by the Council.
Despite subsection (1), no person is entitled to be registered unless the Council is satisfied that the person can communicate in and understand English to an appropriate standard for practising as a veterinarian in New Zealand.
7 Entitlement to registration as specialist
The Council may—
designate any branch of veterinary science as a branch in respect of which a veterinarian may practise as a specialist:
amend or revoke any designation under paragraph (a).
A veterinarian, on payment of the prescribed fee, is entitled to be registered as a specialist if the veterinarian satisfies the Council that, in relation to the branch of veterinary science in respect of which he or she seeks to be registered as a specialist, he or she—
is appropriately qualified; and
has had sufficient training and experience to justify practising as a specialist in that branch of veterinary science.
8 Recognition of institutions and academic qualifications
The Council must, by notice under section 88, declare—
the institutions and the degrees or diplomas offered by those institutions that are recognised for the purposes of section 6(1)﻿(a); and
the assessment programmes, and post-graduate study or training approved by the Council for the purposes of section 6(1)﻿(b).
Before making a declaration under subsection (1)﻿(a), the Council must satisfy itself that an institution provides a course of instruction in veterinary science that, in relation to each degree or diploma,—
provides tuition at a level that is acceptable to the Council; and
provides assessment or examination at a level that is acceptable to the Council; and
leads to a degree or diploma in veterinary science that is acceptable to the Council.
If the Council considers that an institution, or a degree or diploma offered by the institution, included in a declaration under subsection (1)﻿(a) no longer satisfies the criteria in subsection (2), the Council must, by notice under section 88, amend or revoke the declaration or relevant part of the declaration.
Subsection (3) does not affect the registration or continued registration of a person that the Council has accepted for registration on the basis of a degree or diploma from an institution prior to the revocation or amendment of the recognition status of the institution or the degree or diploma.
 Disqualification from registration
9 Disqualification from registration
A person is not entitled to be registered—
if he or she—
has been convicted by any court in New Zealand or elsewhere of any offence punishable by imprisonment for a term of 3 months or longer; and
does not satisfy the Council that, having regard to all the circumstances, including the time that has elapsed since the conviction, the offence does not reflect adversely on his or her fitness to practise as a veterinarian; or
if the Council is satisfied that registration of the person would be inappropriate because, in New Zealand or elsewhere, he or she—
is the subject of professional disciplinary proceedings; or
is under investigation, or has been struck off the relevant register of veterinarians, or has been otherwise disciplined by a veterinary professional organisation; or
if the Council is satisfied that the person cannot practise satisfactorily as a veterinarian because of a mental or physical condition.
 Procedure for registration
10 Application for registration
An application for registration must be made to the Council and must—
be made in a form determined by the Council; and
contain the information determined by the Council; and
be accompanied by the prescribed fee.
11 Consideration of application
When considering an application for registration, the Council may, in relation to the application,—
examine the applicant or any other person:
require any person to verify, by statutory declaration, any statement made by that person:
require the applicant to provide any document or information that the Council considers, on reasonable grounds, is necessary to assess his or her application.
 Registration (other than limited or provisional registration)
12 Council to register applicant or decline application
Subsection (2) applies if the Council is satisfied that—
the applicant—
is entitled under section 6 to be registered; or
is a veterinarian who is entitled under section 7(2) to be registered as a specialist; and
the applicant has paid the prescribed application fee.
The Council must—
register the applicant and enter his or her name in the register; and
notify the applicant in writing that he or she is registered; and
issue the applicant with a certificate of registration.
If the Council is not satisfied that the applicant is entitled to be registered, the Council must—
decline the application; and
notify the applicant in writing of—
its decision (with reasons); and
his or her right of appeal against the decision.
 Limited registration
13 Scopes of practice for limited registration
The Council may—
prescribe the elements of veterinary practice, in terms of 1 or more scopes of practice, in respect of which a person may be registered as a person with limited registration; and
amend or revoke any notice published for the purposes of paragraph (a).
A scope of practice may be described in any way the Council thinks fit, including, without limitation, in any 1 or more of the following ways:
by reference to a name or form of words that is commonly understood by veterinarians:
by reference to 1 or more species of animal:
by reference to an area of science or learning:
by reference to tasks commonly performed:
by reference to illnesses or conditions to be diagnosed, treated, or managed.
Before the Council publishes a notice, the Council must—
consult about the proposed notice with—
veterinarians, or people who the Council considers represent the views of veterinarians; and
organisations that the Council considers will be affected by the proposed notice; and
statutory bodies responsible for the administration of the Animal Products Act 1999, the Animal Welfare Act 1999, and the Biosecurity Act 1993; and
take into account the effect of any proposed scope of practice in relation to—
New Zealand's economic interests; and
maintaining consistency with and support of New Zealand's food safety, biosecurity, and animal welfare regimes.
14 Application for limited registration
A person may apply to the Council for limited registration in terms of 1 or more scopes of practice notified under section 13.
Subsection (3) applies if the Council is satisfied that the applicant—
is appropriately qualified to practise within the scope or scopes of practice in the notice; and
is a fit and proper person to be registered; and
can communicate in and understand English to an appropriate standard for practising as a veterinarian in New Zealand; and
has paid the prescribed application fee.
The Council must—
register the applicant and enter his or her name in the register—
subject to the conditions of practice that the Council considers appropriate (if any); and
subject to the time limits on registration that the Council considers appropriate (if any); and
notify the applicant in writing that he or she is registered; and
issue the applicant with a certificate of limited registration.
If the Council is not satisfied that the applicant meets the requirements of subsection (2), the Council must—
decline the application; and
notify the applicant in writing of—
its decision (with reasons); and
his or her right of appeal against the decision.
 Provisional registration
15 Provisional registration
If an applicant is applying for the first time under this Act for registration (other than for limited registration), the Council may offer to the applicant a certificate of provisional registration pending a decision by the Council on the person's registration application.
Before making an offer, the Council must be satisfied on reasonable grounds that the applicant appears to be entitled under section 6 to be registered.
If the applicant accepts an offer by the Council, the Council must—
register the applicant and enter his or her name in the register for a period not exceeding 3 months; and
notify the applicant in writing that he or she is provisionally registered; and
issue the applicant with—
a certificate of provisional registration; and
a practising certificate for the period of the provisional registration (subject to the conditions of practice that the Council considers appropriate, if any).
Despite subsection (3)﻿(a), the Council may renew a person's certificate of provisional registration and practising certificate, once only, for a further period not exceeding 3 months.
The Council may, at any time, cancel the registration of a person under this section, but must give the person written notice of and reasons for the cancellation.
16 Change from provisional to full registration
If the Council is satisfied that a person with provisional registration is fit for registration under section 12(2), the Council must—
register the person; and
notify the person in writing of his or her change in registration status; and
issue the person with a certificate of registration.
 Cancellation of registration
17 Cancellation of registration
The Council must cancel a person's registration if—
the person requests the Council in writing to cancel his or her registration; or
the Council is notified of the death of the person—
under subsection (2); or
by any other means; or
the person has been registered as a result of an error and the Council is informed of or discovers the error.
On the registration of the death of any person who is stated to be a veterinarian, or who is registered under this Act, every Registrar (within the meaning of section 4 of the Births, Deaths, Marriages, and Relationships Registration Act 2021)—
must notify the Council in writing of the death of the person and particulars of the date and place of death; and
may charge the Council a reasonable fee for a notification under paragraph (a).
This section does not limit the powers of the Council under Part 3.
18 Amendment to specialist's registration
The Council must amend a specialist's registration (and certificate of registration) to that of a registered person only, if—
the specialist requests the Council in writing to amend his or her registration in this way; or
the specialist has been registered as a specialist as a result of an error and the Council is informed of or discovers the error.
This section does not limit the powers of the Council under Part 3.
19 Surrender of certificate of registration if registration cancelled or suspended
A person whose registration is cancelled or suspended must deliver his or her certificate of registration to the Registrar no later than 10 working days after the date on which the notice of cancellation or suspension has been given to the person.
20 Effect of cancellation or suspension of registration
A person is not a registered person for the purposes of this Act if his or her registration is cancelled or suspended.
At the end of a period of suspension, a person's registration is immediately reinstated and the Council must reissue to the person his or her certificate of registration.
Subsection (2) applies unless the Council cancels the person's registration before the end of the period of suspension.
 Offences relating to registration
21 Offences relating to registration
Every person commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000 who, for the purpose of obtaining registration (either for himself or herself or for any other person),—
either orally or in writing makes a declaration or representation knowing it to be false or misleading in relation to a material particular; or
provides to the Council or makes use of any document knowing it to contain a declaration or representation that is false or misleading in relation to a material particular; or
provides to the Council or makes use of any document knowing that it is not genuine.
Every person commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000 who claims to be registered under section 12(2) if he or she is registered under section 14.
Every person who breaches section 19 without reasonable excuse commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $2,000.
 Register of veterinarians and other registered persons
22 Register of veterinarians and other registered persons
The Council must establish and maintain a register of veterinarians and other registered persons.
The register must—
be in the prescribed form; and
contain the prescribed information; and
contain any other information that the Council considers necessary or desirable for the purpose of the register; and
include, for each veterinarian,—
details of any conditions of practice imposed on the veterinarian; and
whether his or her registration or practising certificate is suspended and, if so, the following information:
the grounds under this Act for the suspension; and
the period of suspension; and
any conditions for termination of the suspension; and
contain in separate parts the prescribed information in relation to—
specialists:
veterinarians with limited or provisional registration (including the period of time for which each veterinarian is registered in either category):
registered persons.
The Registrar must ensure that the register is available to members of the public to inspect it free of charge.
Subsection (3) is subject to any prohibition order made by the District Court under section 71.
The Registrar must, on payment of the prescribed fee,—
issue a certificate of registration to any registered person:
supply a certified copy of an entry in the register to any person requesting it.
Information entered in the register under subsection (2)﻿(c) and (e)﻿(iii) does not form part of the register for the purposes of subsection (3) or subsection (5)﻿(b).
23 Alterations to register
The Registrar must promptly amend the information in the register,—
in relation to a registered person, if—
the person's registration is cancelled or amended:
the person's registration is suspended and the period of suspension has expired or the suspension is lifted by the Council, the District Court, or the High Court (as the case may be):
in relation to a veterinarian, if—
the Council has cancelled the veterinarian's practising certificate:
the Council has suspended the veterinarian's practising certificate and the period of suspension has expired or ceased to apply, or the suspension is lifted by the Council, the District Court, or the High Court (as the case may be):
the Council has imposed conditions of practice on the veterinarian and the conditions have expired or ceased to apply, or are removed by the Council, the District Court, or the High Court (as the case may be):
in relation to a specified person, in accordance with section 51(4).
The Registrar may at any time amend the information in the register—
after receiving any information under subsection (3); or
if the amendment is necessary for the purposes of section 22(2); or
if the Registrar considers, on reasonable grounds, that an amendment is necessary.
A veterinarian must—
notify the Registrar if he or she changes his or her name, contact address, or practice name (whether the practice name is the veterinarian's own or his or her employer's); and
provide the Registrar with the relevant current information.
A notification under subsection (3) must be in writing (which may include by way of fax, email, or other electronic means) and made no later than 1 month after the information in the register is no longer current.
Every person who breaches subsection (3) without reasonable excuse commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $500.
 Subpart 2 — Practising certificates
24 Application for practising certificate
The following people may apply to the Council for a practising certificate:
a registered person who wishes to practise as a veterinarian:
a veterinarian or a specialist who wishes to renew his or her practising certificate.
An application under subsection (1) must—
be made in a form determined by the Council; and
contain the information determined by the Council; and
be accompanied by the prescribed fee.
25 Consideration of application
When considering an application for a practising certificate, the Council may, in relation to the application,—
examine the applicant or any other person:
require any person to verify, by statutory declaration, any statement made by that person:
require the applicant to provide any document or information that the Council considers, on reasonable grounds, is necessary to assess his or her application.
26 Council to issue certificate or decline application
The Council must issue to an applicant under section 24 a practising certificate if the Council is satisfied that—
the applicant is appropriately registered; and
the applicant satisfies the practising requirements of the Act; and
the application fee has been paid.
A certificate is effective from the date stated on the certificate, and expires at the close of the practising year to which the certificate relates, unless stated otherwise on the certificate.
If the Council is not satisfied that the applicant meets the requirements of subsection (1), the Council must—
notify the applicant in writing of its decision (with reasons); and
give the applicant a reasonable opportunity to make submissions and be heard on the matter (either personally or by counsel or agent); and
confirm, reverse, or modify its decision (for example, by issuing a practising certificate to the applicant subject to conditions of practice); and
notify the applicant in writing of its further decision (including, if relevant, with reasons, and a statement of the applicant's right of appeal against the decision).
In this section, practising year means the 12-month period ending on 31 March in any year.
27 Content of certificate
A practising certificate must state—
the date of its expiry; and
the section of this Act that the person is registered under; and
whether any conditions of practice are currently imposed on the person; and
if paragraph (c) applies, where the public can find out, free of charge, what conditions are imposed on the person.
28 Expiry of certificate
A practising certificate continues in force until the close of the date of its expiry unless it is sooner cancelled or suspended under this Act.
28A Extension of expiry date of certificate on application for new certificate
This section applies where—
a person who is the holder of an existing practising certificate applies for a new practising certificate before the expiry of his or her existing practising certificate; and
the application complies with section 24(2).
Despite section 28, the applicant's existing practising certificate continues in force until the earlier of—
the date that a new certificate is issued under section 26(1); or
the date that the Council notifies the applicant under section 26(3)﻿(d) that it is not satisfied that he or she meets the requirements for the issue of a new certificate.
29 Surrender of certificate if certificate is cancelled or suspended
A person whose practising certificate is cancelled or suspended must deliver his or her practising certificate to the Registrar no later than 10 working days after the date on which a notice of cancellation or suspension has been given to the person.
30 Effect of cancellation or suspension of practising certificate
A person is not a veterinarian for the purposes of this Act if his or her practising certificate is cancelled or suspended.
At the end of a period of suspension, a person's practising certificate is immediately reinstated and the Council must reissue to the person his or her practising certificate.
Subsection (2) applies unless the Council—
cancels the person's practising certificate before the end of the period of suspension; or
reinstates the person's practising certificate before the end of the period of suspension.
 Offences relating to practising certificates
31 Offences relating to practising certificates
Every registered person who practises as a veterinarian without holding a current practising certificate commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000.
Every person who breaches section 29 without reasonable excuse commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $2,000.
 Subpart 3 — Unlawful use of title
32 Unlawful use of title of veterinarian
No person, other than a veterinarian, may use in connection with his or her business, trade, employment, calling, or profession—
the title veterinarian; or
any words, initials, or abbreviations that are intended to cause, or that may reasonably cause, any person to believe that the person using those words, initials, or abbreviations is a veterinarian; or
the word veterinary with any words, initials, or abbreviations that are intended to cause, or that may reasonably cause, any person to believe that the person using the word veterinary with those words, initials, or abbreviations is a veterinarian.
Every person who breaches subsection (1) commits an offence, and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000.
33 Unlawful use of title of specialist
No person, other than a specialist, may use in connection with his or her business, trade, employment, calling, or profession any words, initials, or abbreviations of the title specialist that are intended to cause, or that may reasonably cause, any person to believe that the person using those words, initials, or abbreviations is a specialist.
Every person who breaches subsection (1) commits an offence, and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000.
 Part 3 — Disciplinary proceedings and medical and competence assessments
 Subpart 1 — Preliminary provisions
34 Interpretation
In this Part, specified person—
means a veterinarian or a person who was a veterinarian at the time of the relevant conduct; and
unless the context otherwise requires, includes—
a registered person:
a registered person whose registration is suspended:
a person who was registered at the time of the relevant conduct.
35 Procedure for making decisions
In the performance and exercise of its decision-making functions and powers under this Part, the Council or a complaints assessment committee must observe the rules of natural justice.
36 Legal assessors
The Council and a complaints assessment committee may each appoint a legal assessor who—
may be present at any proceedings of the Council or the committee under this Part; and
may advise the Council or the committee on matters of law, procedure, and evidence at—
the proceedings; or
any time before or after the proceedings.
A legal assessor must not be present when the Council or the committee deliberates.
37 Evidence and privilege
For the purposes of this Part, every person has the same privileges as a witness in a court of law in relation to—
providing documents, things, or information to the Council in relation to a disciplinary hearing or a medical or competence assessment; or
giving evidence or answering questions at a disciplinary hearing or a medical or competence assessment.
For the purposes of this Part, every counsel appearing before the Council in relation to a disciplinary hearing or a medical or competence assessment has the same privileges and immunities as counsel in a court of law.
 Subpart 2 — How disciplinary process works
38 Complaints about specified person
Any person may make a complaint to the Council about the conduct of a specified person.
A complaint must be in writing.
As soon as practicable after receiving a complaint, the Council must refer the complaint to a complaints assessment committee and the committee must investigate the complaint under section 40.
39 Council-referred matter about specified person
The Council may refer to a complaints assessment committee a matter about the conduct of a specified person that arises—
out of its own inquiries; or
from information it receives.
The complaints assessment committee must investigate the matter under section 40.
 Complaints assessment committee investigations
40 Investigation by complaints assessment committee
A complaints assessment committee must promptly investigate—
every complaint referred to it under section 38(3); and
every referral it receives under section 39(1).
However, if at any time after receiving a complaint a committee considers, on reasonable grounds, that no further action is necessary in relation to the complaint, the committee—
is not required to investigate the complaint; and
must notify in writing (with reasons)—
the complainant; and
the Council, which may, if it thinks fit, determine the matter in any event using its powers under this Part.
Before investigating a complaint or referral, a committee must inform the specified person against whom the complaint or referral has been made in writing of the general nature of the complaint or referral.
For the purpose of carrying out an investigation a committee may appoint or employ any person to make inquiries on its behalf, provide advice, or otherwise assist the committee.
The committee may require a complainant to support his or her complaint with any statutory declarations that the committee thinks fit.
Subsection (1) is subject to section 46(1).
41 Power to call for information or documents
If the conditions set out in subsection (2) are satisfied, a complaints assessment committee may, by written notice, require any person to provide to the committee any papers, documents, records, or things.
The conditions are that—
the committee believes, on reasonable grounds, that the exercise of the powers conferred under subsection (1) is necessary to enable the committee to carry out its investigation; and
the person to whom a notice under subsection (1) is to be given has failed to comply with a previous request to provide to the committee, within a reasonable time, the papers, documents, records, or things required by the notice; and
the committee believes, on reasonable grounds, that—
it is not practicable to obtain the information required by the committee from another source; or
for the purposes of the investigation, it is necessary to obtain the papers, documents, records, or things to verify or refute information obtained from another source.
42 Compliance with requirement to provide information or document
A person who receives a notice under section 41 must, without charge, comply with the requirement stated in the notice in the manner and within the period (being not less than 10 working days after the notice is given to the person) specified in the notice.
Subsection (1) does not require a person to provide any information or produce any document that would be privileged in a court of law.
No person is required to produce to a committee any papers, records, documents, or things if compliance with that requirement would be in breach of an obligation of secrecy or non-disclosure imposed on the person by an enactment (other than the Official Information Act 1982 or the Privacy Act 2020).
Every person commits an offence, and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000, who, when required to comply with a notice given under section 41,—
refuses or fails without reasonable excuse to comply with the notice; or
knowingly or recklessly provides information that is false or misleading in any material particular.
43 Decision of complaints assessment committee
After investigating a complaint or a referral, the complaints assessment committee must provide to the Council a written statement,—
if the committee considers that the matter is a medical matter, recommending—
that the specified person be required to undergo a medical assessment; and
if necessary, that the Council exercise its powers under section 57:
if the committee considers that the matter is a competence matter, recommending—
that the specified person be required to undergo a competence assessment; and
if necessary, that the Council exercise its powers under section 60:
if the committee considers that the matter can be resolved by agreement with the specified person, recommending that the Council impose conditions of practice on the specified person:
if the committee considers that the matter requires a disciplinary hearing, stating that the committee intends to lay 1 or more charges against the specified person:
if the committee considers that the matter is any combination of medical, competence, and disciplinary matters,—
recommending that the Council take 2 or more of the actions set out in paragraphs (a) to (c) as specified by the committee; or
stating that the committee intends to lay 1 or more charges against the specified person and recommending that the Council require the specified person to undertake a medical assessment or a competence assessment, or both (and, if necessary, that the Council exercise its powers under section 57 or section 60, or both):
if the committee considers that the matter requires no further action, stating that it will be taking no further action in relation to the specified person.
However, if during the investigation the committee has acted under section 46, the committee must provide to the Council a written statement (with reasons)—
stating whether—
the complaint was settled between the parties and, if not, the matters on which agreement was reached between the parties (if any); or
conciliation occurred between the parties, and the result of the conciliation; and
recommending what, if any, further action the Council should take under this Part in relation to the specified person who was the subject of the complaint.
Despite subsections (1) and (2), the complaints assessment committee may make a recommendation to the Council without investigating, or fully investigating, a complaint or referral if the committee considers that the complaint or referral—
relates to the competence of the specified person; or
relates to a medical issue; or
requires urgent action by the Council.
The Council may determine a matter referred to it under this section—
in accordance with the committee's statement; or
if it thinks fit, in any other way using its powers under this Part.
 Interim suspension
44 Interim suspension pending medical assessment
A complaints assessment committee may, in addition to providing a statement about a specified person to the Council under section 43, recommend to it that until the Council has conducted a medical assessment, the Council—
suspend the specified person's practising certificate; or
impose conditions of practice on the specified person.
The committee may make a recommendation under subsection (1)﻿(a) only if it is satisfied on reasonable grounds that—
the specified person cannot practise satisfactorily as a veterinarian because of a mental or physical condition; and
because the specified person may continue to practise as a veterinarian, it is necessary in the public interest to suspend the specified person's practising certificate.
The committee may make a recommendation under subsection (1)﻿(b) only if it is satisfied that—
the specified person cannot practise satisfactorily as a veterinarian because of a mental or physical condition; but
despite this it would not be contrary to the public interest to allow the specified person to continue to practise subject to conditions of practice.
The committee must immediately notify the specified person in writing (with reasons) that a recommendation has been made.
After considering the committee's recommendation, the Council must—
suspend the specified person's practising certificate, if it is satisfied that—
the specified person cannot practise satisfactorily as a veterinarian because of a mental or physical condition; and
because the specified person may continue to practise as a veterinarian, it is necessary in the public interest to suspend the specified person's practising certificate; or
impose conditions of practice on the specified person if it is satisfied that—
the specified person cannot practise satisfactorily as a veterinarian because of a mental or physical condition; but
despite this it would not be contrary to the public interest to allow the specified person to continue to practise subject to conditions of practice; or
refuse to suspend or impose conditions of practice on the specified person, and require the committee to continue its procedure under section 43.
However, if the Council acts under subsection (5)﻿(a) or (b), it must complete the medical assessment no later than 50 working days after the suspension or conditions take effect.
The suspension or conditions of practice, as the case may be, cease to apply—
if the Council does not complete the medical assessment before the end of the 50-working day period; and
without any further action being taken by the Council or the specified person.
A decision of the Council under subsection (5) takes effect immediately, and the Council must ensure that the specified person, the complaints assessment committee, and the complainant (if any) are notified in writing (with reasons) as soon as practicable after the decision is made.
45 Interim suspension pending disciplinary hearing or competence assessment
A complaints assessment committee may, in addition to providing a statement about a specified person to the Council under section 43, recommend to it that, until the Council has conducted a disciplinary hearing or a competence assessment, or both, the Council—
suspend the specified person's practising certificate; or
impose conditions of practice on the specified person.
The committee may make a recommendation only if it is satisfied on reasonable grounds that it is necessary in the public interest to suspend the specified person's practising certificate or impose conditions of practice on the specified person.
The committee must immediately notify the specified person in writing (with reasons) that a recommendation has been made.
After considering the committee's recommendation, the Council must—
suspend the specified person's practising certificate or impose conditions of practice on the person, if it is satisfied on reasonable grounds that it is necessary in the public interest to do so; or
refuse to suspend or impose conditions of practice on the specified person, and require the committee to continue its procedure under section 43.
However, if the Council acts under subsection (4)﻿(a), it must complete the disciplinary hearing or the competence assessment, or both, no later than 50 working days after the suspension or conditions take effect.
The suspension or conditions of practice, as the case may be, cease to apply—
if the Council does not complete the disciplinary hearing or competence assessment, or both, before the end of the 50-working day period; and
without any further action being taken by the Council or the specified person.
A decision of the Council under subsection (4) takes effect immediately, and the Council must ensure that the specified person, the complaints assessment committee, and the complainant (if any) are notified in writing (with reasons) as soon as practicable after the decision is made.
 Mediation
46 Resolution of complaint by mediation
When a complaint comes before a complaints assessment committee, or at any time during the investigation of a complaint, the committee may, if it thinks fit, consider—
whether resolution of the complaint or part of the complaint may be reached by agreement between the complainant and the specified person and, if so, assist in such a resolution; and
whether resolution of the complaint may be reached by conciliation between the complainant and the specified person and, if so, discuss the possibility with them.
For the purposes of subsection (1), the committee may appoint or employ a mediator or conciliator to act on the committee's behalf.
No evidence of any admission, information, or statement made to any person acting as a mediator or conciliator under this section is admissible in any proceedings before the Council, a court, or a tribunal.
To avoid doubt, any resolution or part resolution of a complaint under this section does not preclude the Council exercising, in relation to the specified person, its powers under this Part.
 Disciplinary hearings
47 Procedure for hearing
The Council must, by written notice, inform the specified person concerned of its intention to hold a disciplinary hearing.
The notice must include—
a statement that a complaints assessment committee has reason to believe that 1 or more grounds exist entitling the Council to exercise its powers under section 50 or section 61(1); and
sufficient information to clearly inform the specified person of the grounds referred to in paragraph (a); and
the hearing date on which the Council intends to hear the matter (being a date not less than 20 working days after the date on which the notice is served).
The notice may require the specified person to give written notice to the Council (being not later than a specified date before the hearing date) as to whether he or she will—
attend the hearing; and
speak at the hearing personally or by a representative.
A person who fails to comply with any requirement under subsection (3) may still attend the hearing and be heard. However, in making any order under section 51, the Council may take into account the fact of the non-compliance.
The Council must inform the complainant (if any) in writing of the hearing date.
The Council may authorise any person to appear at a hearing and adduce evidence and make submissions on the matter.
48 Council has powers of Commission of Inquiry
For the purposes of a disciplinary hearing, the Council has the powers of a commission under the Commissions of Inquiry Act 1908 and the provisions of that Act, except sections 11 and 12 (which relate to costs) apply as if the hearing were an inquiry under that Act.
49 Disciplinary hearing to be in public
The Council must hold every disciplinary hearing in public.
However, if the Council considers it appropriate to do so, having regard to the interests of any person and to the public interest, the Council may—
hold a hearing or any part of a hearing in private:
prohibit, by order, the publication of—
any report of a hearing or a part of a hearing (whether held in public or in private):
the whole or any part of any paper, document, or record produced at a hearing:
the name of or any particulars about the specified person who is the subject of a hearing, or any other person:
decide a hearing, including any question arising during the hearing, in private.
An order made under subsection (2)﻿(b) continues in force until the sooner of the date specified in the order (if any) or the date of its revocation. However, a person may apply at any time to the Council for the order to be revoked.
Subsection (2)﻿(b)﻿(iii) does not apply in relation to the publication of the effect of an order under section 54(2).
Every person commits an offence and is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $10,000 who, without lawful excuse, breaches any order made by the Council under subsection (2).
50 Grounds for discipline of specified person
The Council may take 1 or more of the actions set out in section 51 against a specified person if, at the conclusion of a disciplinary hearing, it is satisfied that—
both of the following matters apply:
the specified person has been convicted within the last 7 years, whether before or after he or she was registered, by any court in New Zealand or elsewhere of—
any offence punishable by imprisonment for a term of 3 months or more; or
any other offence against this or any other Act that has a direct bearing on the veterinary profession; or
an offence against the Agricultural Compounds and Veterinary Medicines Act 1997, the Animal Welfare Act 1999, the Medicines Act 1981, or the Misuse of Drugs Act 1975, or an offence against any regulations made by or under those Acts; and
the commission of the offence reflects adversely on the person's fitness to practise as a veterinarian; or
the specified person has been guilty of professional misconduct because of any act or omission (including an unethical act or omission) that, in the opinion of the Council, requires disciplinary sanction; or
the specified person has been guilty of professional misconduct because of any act or omission that, in the opinion of the Council, has brought or was likely to bring discredit to the profession; or
the specified person has, for the purpose of obtaining registration (either for himself or herself or for any other person),—
either orally or in writing, made any declaration or representation knowing it to be false or misleading in a material particular; or
produced to the Council or made use of any document knowing it to contain a declaration or representation referred to in subparagraph (i); or
produced to the Council or made use of any document knowing that it was not genuine; or
the specified person has been struck off the register of veterinarians or has otherwise been disciplined by a veterinary professional organisation in another country; or
the specified person has breached any conditions of practice imposed on him or her; or
the specified person has failed to comply with the minimum standards for practising.
However, the Council must not take any action against a specified person in relation to any matter in subsection (1) that occurred before the specified person was registered, if the Council knew of the matter before the registration.
 Subpart 3 — How specified person may be disciplined
 Penalties
51 Penalties
In any case to which section 50 applies, the Council may—
do either or both of the following things:
cancel the specified person's registration and remove the person's name from the register:
at the same time, or in a subsequent order, order that the specified person may not apply for reregistration before the expiry of a specified period:
if the specified person is registered as a specialist, amend his or her registration (and certificate of registration) to that of a registered person only:
suspend the specified person's registration for a period of no more than 12 months:
cancel the specified person's practising certificate:
suspend the specified person's practising certificate for a specified period:
order that the specified person be ineligible to hold a practising certificate for a specified period:
order that the specified person be censured:
order that the specified person may, for a period not exceeding 3 years, practise only subject to the conditions of practice specified in the order:
order the specified person to pay,—
in any case to which section 50(1)﻿(b) or (c) applies, a fine not exceeding $30,000; and
in any case to which section 50(1)﻿(d) applies, a fine not exceeding $10,000:
if the matter arises from a complaint, order—
that any professional fee paid or owing to the specified person by the complainant be refunded, reduced, or waived:
that the specified person pay the complainant's costs and expenses for the hearing by the Council and any inquiries made by the complaints assessment committee.
No fine may be imposed under subsection (1)﻿(i) in relation to an act or omission that constitutes an offence for which the specified person has been convicted by a court.
The Council may order that the specified person pay the costs and expenses of, and incidental to, the disciplinary hearing and any investigation made by the complaints assessment committee.
The Registrar must note the action taken by the Council in the register, and the Council may publicly notify the action in any other way it thinks fit.
An application by a specified person to whom subsection (1)﻿(a)﻿(ii) applies to have his or her name restored to the register must be treated for the purposes of this Act as if it were an application under section 10.
52 Non-payment of fines or costs
If money payable by a specified person under section 51(1)﻿(i), (1)﻿(j), or (3) remains unpaid for 60 days or more, the Council may—
cancel the person's registration; or
suspend the person's registration until the person pays the money and, if he or she does not do so within 12 months, cancel his or her registration; or
cancel the person's practising certificate; or
suspend the person's practising certificate until the person pays the money and, if he or she does not do so before the practising certificate expires, refuse any subsequent application for a practising certificate until the money is paid.
 Decisions and orders
53 Provisions relating to decisions and orders
A decision or an order of the Council made under section 51 must—
be in writing (with reasons); and
contain a statement of the specified person's right to appeal against the decision or order, and the time within which the notice of appeal must be given; and
be signed by the chairperson of the Council or the Registrar.
A copy of a decision or an order must be served on the specified person to whom it applies and takes effect from the date on which it is served or a later date as may be specified in the decision or order.
The Registrar must inform the complainant (if any) in writing of the effect of any decision or order made under section 51.
54 Publication
This section applies if—
the Council has made a decision or an order under this Part in relation to a specified person and the specified person has not appealed against the decision or order within the time allowed; or
a court has made an order under this Act in relation to a specified person.
If subsection (1) applies, the Council may publish a notice in 1 or more publications it thinks fit stating the effect of the decision or order.
Subsection (2) is subject to sections 49 and 71.
 Subpart 4 — Medical and competence assessments
 Medical assessments
55 Medical assessments
The Council may by notice, on its own initiative or on the recommendation of a complaints assessment committee, require a specified person to undertake a medical examination by a medical practitioner at the Council's expense.
The notice must—
be served on the specified person; and
be in writing signed by the chairperson (with reasons); and
specify—
the name and address of the medical practitioner who is to conduct the examination; and
the date by which the examination is to take place (being a date not earlier than 5 working days after the notice is served on the specified person); and
inform the specified person that—
he or she may attend the examination with another person, who may be a support person or an observer; and
the medical practitioner conducting the examination may consult with any other health practitioner regarding the examination; and
the specified person may refuse to undertake the examination, but that the Council may, despite the refusal, act under section 57(1) in any event.
Before giving a notice under this section, the Council must endeavour to consult with the specified person about the medical practitioner who is to conduct the examination.
The medical practitioner conducting the examination may consult with another health practitioner who the medical practitioner considers is able to assist in the completion of the examination or in the completion of the report under subsection (4).
The medical practitioner must, as soon as practicable after conducting an examination, make a written report to the Council.
The Council must, as soon as practicable after receiving the report,—
send a copy to the specified person; and
give the specified person a reasonable opportunity to make submissions and be heard on the matter (either personally or by counsel or agent).
56 Interim suspension by Council pending medical assessment
In addition to acting under section 55, the Council may, on its own initiative, until it has conducted the medical assessment,—
suspend the specified person's practising certificate; or
impose conditions of practice on the specified person.
For the purposes of this section, section 44(5) to (7) applies, with all necessary modifications, as if the Council were acting under that section on the recommendation of a complaints assessment committee.
57 Consequences of medical assessment
The Council may take 1 or more of the following actions against a specified person who has undergone a medical assessment:
cancel the specified person's registration:
suspend the specified person's registration for a specified period:
cancel the specified person's practising certificate:
suspend the specified person's practising certificate for a specified period:
order that the specified person be ineligible to hold a practising certificate for a specified period:
impose conditions of practice on the specified person.
At any time the Council may cancel the suspension of a specified person's registration or practising certificate or remove any conditions of practice imposed on the specified person under this section.
However, the Council may act under subsection (2) only if it is satisfied that the suspension is no longer required or the specified person can practise satisfactorily as a veterinarian (with or without conditions of practice being imposed).
 Competence assessments
58 Competence assessments
The Council may by notice, on the recommendation of a complaints assessment committee or of its own motion, require a specified person to undergo an assessment of the specified person's competence at the Council's expense.
An assessment must follow the procedure set out in section 55(2) to (5), and those provisions apply with all necessary modifications, as if the medical examination were an examination by a person qualified to assess the specified person's competence.
59 Council may require specified person to make practice records available
For the purposes of a competence assessment, the Council may inspect a specified person's practice records, and the specified person must make the records available, without charge, for that purpose to any person authorised by the Council to receive them.
60 Consequences of competence assessment
The Council may take 1 or more of the following actions against a specified person who has undergone a competence assessment:
cancel the specified person's registration:
suspend the specified person's registration for a specified period:
cancel the specified person's practising certificate:
suspend the specified person's practising certificate for a specified period:
order that the specified person be ineligible to hold a practising certificate for a specified period:
impose conditions of practice on the specified person.
At any time, the Council may cancel the suspension of a specified person's registration or practising certificate or remove any conditions of practice imposed on the specified person under this section.
However, the Council may act under subsection (2) only if it is satisfied that the suspension is no longer required or that the specified person can practise satisfactorily as a veterinarian (with or without conditions of practice being imposed).
 Part 4 — Conditions of practice and appeals
 Conditions of practice
61 Conditions of practice
The Council may impose conditions of practice on a specified person—
as a requirement of the specified person's registration or continued registration; or
as a condition of—
issuing the specified person with a practising certificate; or
allowing the specified person to continue to hold a practising certificate; or
as a result of a recommendation by a complaints assessment committee; or
as a result of a disciplinary hearing or a medical or competence assessment.
However, the conditions imposed must be only those that in the Council's opinion are necessary to ensure that the specified person will comply with the minimum standards for practising if the specified person were (as the case may be)—
to be issued with a practising certificate; or
to continue to hold a practising certificate.
Without limiting subsection (1), conditions of practice may include 1 or more of the following:
a condition that the specified person practise subject to the supervision of 1 or more nominated veterinarians or classes of veterinarians:
a condition that the specified person practise subject to the oversight of 1 or more nominated veterinarians or classes of veterinarians:
a condition that the specified person not perform any work of a stated kind, or that the specified person perform any work of a stated kind only in stated circumstances:
a condition that the specified person practise only in a stated capacity (for example, as an employee of 1 or more nominated persons or classes of persons):
a condition that the specified person practise in association with 1 or more nominated persons or classes of persons:
a condition that the specified person practise only for a specified period:
a condition that the specified person obtain 1 or more further stated qualifications or gain further experience of a stated kind:
a condition that the specified person's practice (or the relevant part of his or her employer's practice) be audited at specified or random intervals:
a condition that the specified person be subject to specified medical tests at specified intervals or from time to time:
a condition that the specified person—
pass any examinations or assessments within a specified period or at specified intervals:
complete a period of practical training:
complete a period of practical experience:
undertake a course of instruction:
undergo an inspection:
a condition that the specified person adopt and undertake a systematic process for ensuring that the services provided by the specified person meet the minimum standards for practising.
62 When conditions effective
The Council must notify a specified person of any conditions of practice it has imposed on the specified person.
A notice must—
be in writing; and
contain a statement—
of the reasons why the conditions have been imposed; and
of the person's right to appeal against the imposition of the conditions, or the nature of 1 or more of the conditions imposed, and the time within which the notice of appeal must be given; and
be signed by the chairperson of the Council or the Registrar; and
be served on the specified person.
Unless expressly provided elsewhere in this Act, the conditions take effect as from the date on which the notice is served.
The specified person may practise as a veterinarian only to the extent permitted by the notice.
63 When conditions expire or removed
Any condition of practice imposed by the Council on a specified person ceases to apply on the earliest of the following dates:
the date stated in the relevant notice under section 62(1):
the date on which the Council is satisfied that the specified person has completed, in relation to the condition, the requirement or requirements stated in the relevant notice under section 62(1):
the date on which the Council is satisfied that the specified person is able to comply with the relevant minimum standards of practice without the imposition of the condition.
If subsection (1)﻿(b) or (c) applies, the Council must give written notice to the specified person that the condition of practice is removed.
The notice must be served on the specified person and takes effect as from the date on which the notice is served.
Subsection (1) is subject to sections 44(7), 45(6), and 56(2).
 Appeals
64 Right of appeal
Any person may appeal to the District Court against any decision of the Council to decline to register the person.
A veterinarian may appeal to the District Court against any decision of the Council to decline to register the veterinarian as a specialist.
A specified person may appeal to the District Court against any decision of the Council to—
decline to issue a practising certificate to the person; or
suspend or cancel his or her registration; or
suspend or cancel his or her practising certificate; or
impose 1 or more penalties on the person under Part 3; or
impose 1 or more conditions of practice on the person.
An appeal—
must be brought to the court by way of originating application in accordance with the rules of court; and
must be lodged within 20 working days after notice of the decision or action is communicated to the appellant, or within any further time a District Court Judge allows on application made before or after that period expires.
65 Notice of right of appeal
When notifying a person under this Act of any decision or action against which section 64 gives him or her a right of appeal, the Council must also notify the person in writing of the right of appeal and the time within which an appeal must be lodged.
66 Actions to have effect pending determination of appeal
A decision or action against which an appeal is lodged under section 64 continues in force unless the District Court orders otherwise.
67 Procedure on appeal
An appeal under section 64 must be heard as soon as is reasonably practicable after it is lodged.
An appeal is by way of rehearing.
On hearing the appeal, the District Court may—
confirm, reverse, or modify the decision or action appealed against:
make any other decision or take any other action that the person or body that made the decision or took the action appealed against could have made or taken:
refer the matter or an aspect of the matter back to the Council for reconsideration under section 69.
The court must not review—
any decision or action not appealed against; or
any part of any decision or action not appealed against.
68 Court's decision final
The decision of the District Court on an appeal under this Part is final.
Subsection (1) is subject to section 72.
69 Court may refer matter back for reconsideration
Instead of determining an appeal under this Part, the District Court may direct the Council to reconsider, either generally or in respect of any specified aspect, the whole or any part of the decision or action.
In giving a direction under subsection (1), the court—
must state its reasons for the direction; and
may give any other directions it thinks just in relation to the matter referred back for reconsideration.
Upon being directed to reconsider a matter, the Council must reconsider the matter and, in doing so, must—
take the court's reasons into account; and
give effect to the court's directions.
70 Orders as to costs
On an appeal under section 64, the District Court may order any party to the appeal to pay to any other party to the appeal any or all of the costs incurred by the other party in respect of the appeal.
71 Orders as to publication of names
On any appeal under section 64, the District Court may, if in its opinion it is proper to do so, prohibit the publication of the name or particulars of the affairs of any person.
In deciding whether to make an order under subsection (1), the court must have regard to—
the interests of any person (including, without limitation, the privacy of any complainant); and
the public interest.
72 Appeal on question of law
A party to an appeal under section 64 may appeal to the High Court against any determination of law arising in the appeal.
The appeal must be heard and determined in accordance with the appropriate rules of court.
Part 6 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2011 applies to the appeal—
so far as it is applicable and with all necessary modifications; but
only so far as it relates to appeals on questions of law.
Subsection (3) overrides subsection (2).
 Part 5 — Veterinary Council of New Zealand
73 Veterinary Council of New Zealand
The Veterinary Council of New Zealand established under section 43 of the Veterinarians Act 1994 is continued.
74 Council is body corporate
The Council is a body corporate with perpetual succession.
 Functions of Council
75 Functions of Council
The Council's functions are—
to advise and make recommendations to the Minister on any matter relating to veterinarians and the practice of veterinary science:
to advise the governing body of any New Zealand university in any matter relating to the education of veterinarians:
to promote and encourage high standards of professional education and conduct among veterinarians:
to consider reciprocal arrangements with registration bodies in other countries:
to register persons, issue practising certificates, assess whether persons meet the requirements for continued registration or eligibility for a practising certificate, and perform the other functions relating to registration and practising requirements in accordance with Part 2, including—
to determine the institutions and the degrees and diplomas offered by those institutions that are recognised for the purposes of section 6(1)﻿(a); and
to approve assessment programmes, post-graduate study, and post-graduate training for the purposes of section 6(1)﻿(b)﻿(ii) and (iii); and
to designate branches of veterinary science as branches in which veterinarians may practise as specialists for the purposes of section 7(1); and
to determine the form and content of applications for the purposes of sections 10(a) and (b) and 24(2)﻿(a) and (b):
to keep and maintain the register and carry out the other functions relating to the register in accordance with Part 2:
to receive, investigate, and hear complaints about, inquire into the conduct of, make assessments of, and discipline specified persons under Part 3:
to investigate offences and bring prosecutions under the Act:
to prescribe minimum standards for practising as a veterinarian, including standards relating to either or both of the following:
a person's fitness to practise as a veterinarian:
the maintenance, examination, or improvement of the overall competence of a veterinarian to practise:
to prescribe—
examinations for the purposes of section 6(1)﻿(b)﻿(i):
the form of the register and the information to be contained in the register under section 22:
to prescribe fees payable for the matters referred to in section 97 in accordance with sections 98 to 100.
76 Capacity and powers
For the purpose of performing its functions, the Council has—
full capacity to carry on or undertake any activity, do any act, or enter into any transaction; and
for the purposes of paragraph (a), full rights, powers, and privileges.
77 Composition of Council
The Council must have 7 members being—
3 veterinarians elected in accordance with regulations made under section 101; and
2 laypersons appointed by the Minister by notice in the Gazette; and
1 veterinarian appointed by the Minister by notice in the Gazette; and
the person for the time being responsible for the veterinary science academic programme at Massey University.
78 Further provisions as to Council and its members
Schedule 1 applies to the Council and its members.
 Committees of Council
79 Council may appoint committees
The Council may, by resolution, appoint 1 or more committees to—
perform any of the Council's functions or duties, or exercise any of the Council's powers, that are delegated to the committee under clause 17 of Schedule 1:
inquire into and report to the Council on any matters within the scope of the Council's functions, duties, or powers that are referred to the committee by the Council.
A committee is subject to the control of the Council and may at any time be discharged, altered, or reconstituted by the Council.
80 Membership and chairperson of committees
A committee must consist of 2 or more people, at least 1 of whom must be a member of the Council.
Unless the Council otherwise decides, the Council must appoint the chairperson of all committees.
81 Committees to regulate own procedure
Subject to the Council's direction, a committee may regulate its own procedure.
82 Complaints assessment committees
The Council may appoint 1 or more complaints assessment committees.
Each committee must consist of 2 veterinarians and 1 layperson, one of whom must be appointed as the chairperson of the committee.
The Council may at any time—
revoke any appointment made under subsection (2); or
reconstitute a committee; or
have more than 1 committee operating from time to time.
A member of a complaints assessment committee must not be appointed as a member of any judicial committee that is considering, or that may consider, a matter that the member considered as part of the complaints assessment committee.
A resolution of a complaints assessment committee is passed if a majority of votes are cast for it.
83 Judicial committees
The Council may appoint 1 or more judicial committees to exercise 1 or more of the Council's functions and powers under Part 3.
A judicial committee must consist of a minimum of 3 and a maximum of 5 members.
A judicial committee must include—
a barrister or solicitor of the High Court of not less than 7 years' practice; and
a layperson; and
a member of the Council; and
a veterinarian with relevant experience.
For the purposes of subsection (2), one member may satisfy 2 or more of the requirements of subsection (3).
A member of a judicial committee must not be appointed as a member of a complaints assessment committee that is considering, or that may consider, a matter that the member considered as part of the judicial committee.
84 Procedure of judicial committees
Except as otherwise provided in this Act, the Council must determine the procedure of the judicial committee, including the number of members required for a quorum.
A resolution of a judicial committee is passed if a majority of votes are cast for it.
 Reporting by Council
85 Obligation to prepare annual report
The Council must prepare an annual report on its operations for each financial year.
An annual report must include the audited financial statements of the Council for the relevant financial year.
The financial year of the Council ends in each year on 31 December or any other day determined by the Minister.
86 Annual report
As soon as practicable after the end of each financial year, the Council must deliver to the Minister the annual report for that financial year.
Within 16 sitting days of receiving the annual report from the Council, the Minister must present a copy of the report to the House of Representatives.
 Part 6 — Miscellaneous provisions
87 Protection for members of Council and others
Neither the Council nor any committee, member, committee member, employee, or agent of the Council is liable for any thing done or omitted to be done, in good faith in the performance or intended performance of their functions or duties, or the exercise of their powers, under this Act.
88 Approved and prescribed matters to be notified and available for inspection
If the Council approves or prescribes any matter under this Act, it must do so by giving notice under this section.
The Council may make the documents or 1 or more of the documents relating to an approved or prescribed matter available (for inspection or supply) at any other place the Council determines.
A person may be charged a reasonable fee if the person is supplied with any documents.
A notice under this section is secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).
89 Application of Legislation Act 2012 to Gazette notices
89A Notification of convictions
A registrar of a court who knows that a person convicted in the court is a veterinarian must send a notice of the conviction to the Council if the conviction is for—
any offence against—
the Agricultural Compounds and Veterinary Medicines Act 1997; or
the Animal Products Act 1999; or
the Animal Welfare Act 1999; or
the Biosecurity Act 1993; or
the Food Act 2014; or
the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act 1996; or
the Medicines Act 1981; or
the Misuse of Drugs Act 1975; or
any other offence that is punishable by imprisonment for a term of 3 months or more.
 Evidence
90 Certificate of Council to be conclusive evidence
A certificate as to any of the following matters, given in writing by any person authorised by the Council in writing to provide certificates of that type, is conclusive evidence of that matter, in the absence of proof to the contrary:
as to whether a person was registered at any time:
as to whether a person is currently registered:
as to whether a person holds a current practising certificate:
as to any entry in the register:
as to any act or proceeding of the Council or of any committee of the Council.
91 Information from institutions may be sufficient evidence of facts
This section applies—
if an institution informs the Council that it has lawfully removed the name of a person from the register in which it records qualifications, and the Council has, on the basis of the qualification,—
registered the person; or
included the qualification in the details of registration of the person; or
if a registering authority outside New Zealand informs the Council that it has lawfully removed the name of a person from a register of veterinarians maintained by the authority.
If subsection (1) applies,—
the Registrar must make a note in the entry of the register in relation to the person of the information provided by an institution or registering authority; and
for the purposes of any assessment, investigation, or disciplinary hearing in relation to the person under this Act, the Council may, in the absence of proof to the contrary, use the findings of fact on which the institution or registration authority based its decision to remove the person's name from its register as sufficient evidence of the facts found.
92 Power of Council to supply evidence for registration overseas
The Council may supply to a veterinarian or other registered person the documents it thinks fit for the purpose of enabling the veterinarian or person to seek registration as a veterinarian outside New Zealand.
A veterinarian who requests documents of this type from the Council must pay the prescribed fee (if any) for the documents.
 Financial
93 Payment and application of charges, fees, fines, and other money
All charges, fees, fines, and other money payable by a person under this Act—
are payable to the Council:
are recoverable as a debt due to the Council from that person, whether or not he or she is or remains a veterinarian or registered person:
may be applied by the Council (as it considers appropriate) in the performance of its functions and duties and the exercise of its powers under this Act.
For the purposes of this section, fine does not include a fine imposed for the commission of an offence against a provision of this Act.
94 Crown not liable for debts of Council
The Crown is not liable to contribute towards the payment of any debts or liabilities of the Council.
 References to veterinarians in other enactments
95 References to veterinarians in other enactments
References in any enactment to a veterinarian or a veterinary practitioner or a veterinary surgeon, or registered veterinarian or veterinary surgeon, or duly qualified veterinarian or veterinary surgeon are, unless the context otherwise requires, to be read as if they were references to a veterinarian within the meaning of section 4 of this Act.
 Service of documents
96 Service of documents
Any document, notification, or summons required or authorised by or under this Act may be served—
by delivering it personally to the person; or
by posting it to the person at that person's usual place of residence.
A summons must,—
if it is to be served under subsection (1)﻿(a), be served at least 48 hours before the attendance of the witness is required:
if it is to be served under subsection (1)﻿(b), be served at least 10 days before the attendance of the witness is required.
A document, notice, or summons that is posted is treated as having been served when it would have been delivered in the ordinary course of post.
 Fees
97 Council may prescribe fees
The Council may from time to time, by notice under section 88, prescribe the fees payable for the following matters:
applying for registration under the Act:
maintaining the register:
making an addition or alteration to the register:
applying for a practising certificate:
issuing any certificate, or a copy of any certificate:
supplying a copy of an entry in the register:
supplying a veterinarian or other registered person with any document (other than a certificate of registration) required by the veterinarian or registered person for the purpose of seeking registration overseas:
the Council setting, approving, or marking examinations:
the additional fee to be paid if a prescribed fee is paid after its due date:
any other matter that relates to anything the Council is required to do in order to carry out its functions and duties under this Act.
A notice prescribing fees—
may prescribe different fees for different classes of veterinarian or registered person:
may exempt any class or classes of veterinarian or registered person from liability to pay the fee:
may provide for the waiver, part payment, or refund of any fee.
A notice under this section comes into force on a date specified in the notice, being not less than 28 days after the date the notice is published under the Legislation Act 2019.
98 Principles guiding prescribing of fees
In prescribing fees under section 97, the Council must be guided by the following principles:
fees must be fair and reasonable:
the income from fees must provide the Council with adequate funding to carry out its functions:
the structure of the fee system should seek to eliminate any cross-subsidisation between fee payers:
fees may provide for the Council to maintain adequate net assets provided the net assets have a value no greater than 1.5 times the Council's average annual revenue in the previous 3 financial years.
99 Consultation with veterinarians and other registered persons on fee increases
The Council must take reasonable steps to consult all veterinarians and other registered persons before gazetting a notice under section 97 that will increase any fees payable to the Council.
Subsection (1) does not apply to a notice under section 97(1)﻿(a) or (h).
100 Further provisions relating to fees
The Council may amend or revoke a notice under section 97, by notice under section 88, as if the amendment or revocation were itself a notice under section 97.
The Council must ensure that a current version of each notice that the Council has published under section 97 is—
available on the Council's website or by other electronic means; and
available so that members of the public may—
inspect the notices free of charge; or
obtain a copy of a notice for a reasonable fee.
If a fee is payable to the Council under this Act, the Registrar may, until the fee is paid, decline to do any act, or to permit any act to be done, or to receive any document in respect of which that fee is payable.
 Regulations
101 Regulations
The Governor-General may, by Order in Council made on the recommendation of the Minister given after consultation by the Minister with the Council, make regulations for 1 or more of the following purposes:
regulating the procedure of the Council:
prescribing the manner of holding elections of members to be elected to the Council:
providing for such matters as are contemplated by or necessary for giving full effect to the provisions of this Act and for its due administration.
Regulations under this section are secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).

 Transitional provisions relating to existing veterinarians
106 People entitled to retain registration
For the purposes of this section and sections 107 to 109, the 1994 Act means the Veterinarians Act 1994.
Every person who is registered under the 1994 Act immediately before the commencement of this Act is to be treated as if he or she were registered under this Act and this Act applies to every such person.
The Registrar must, as soon as practicable after the commencement of this Act, enter on the register the names of the people to whom subsection (2) applies.
107 Annual retention certificates issued under 1994 Act to continue in full force
Every current annual retention certificate issued under section 7 of the 1994 Act to a person who, immediately before the commencement of this Act, was registered as a veterinarian under that Act—
is to be treated as if it were a practising certificate issued to the person under section 26; and
continues in full force and effect until the earlier of the following dates:
the date that the certificate is cancelled under this Act; or
the date that the certificate would have expired under the 1994 Act.
 Other transitional provisions
108 Existing matters and existing proceedings
Despite section 103, the 1994 Act continues to apply in relation to any matters that were commenced, but not completed, under it before its repeal.
However,—
any court action or disciplinary or other proceeding that was commenced under the 1994 Act before its repeal—
must be continued under this Act; but
at the end of the action or proceeding, no order may be made, or action taken, unless—
the same could have been made or taken under the 1994 Act; or
the person who is the subject of the action or proceeding agrees; and
any application for registration made under the 1994 Act but not determined before its repeal must be determined in accordance with the provisions of this Act.
109 Membership of Council
The persons holding office as members immediately before the commencement of this Act continue to hold office on the commencement of this Act until the first of the following events:
the date that the member would have ceased to hold office under the 1994 Act is reached; or
the member dies, resigns, is removed from office, or ceases to be a member in accordance with this Act.
