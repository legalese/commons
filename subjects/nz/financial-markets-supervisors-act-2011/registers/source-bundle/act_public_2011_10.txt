Financial Markets Supervisors Act 2011
1 Title
This Act is the Financial Markets Supervisors Act 2011.
2 Commencement
This Act comes into force on 1 October 2011.
 Part 1 — Preliminary provisions
3 Purpose
The purpose of this Act is to protect the interests of product holders, and of residents of retirement villages, and to enhance investor confidence in financial markets and retirement villages, by—
requiring persons who wish to be appointed as supervisors to be capable of effectively performing the functions of supervisors; and
requiring supervisors to perform their functions effectively; and
enabling supervisors to be held accountable for any failure to perform their functions effectively.
That purpose is additional to the purposes of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 set out in sections 3 and 4 of that Act (which apply to this Act also).
4 Interpretation
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—
debt security has the same meaning as in section 8 of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013
deed of supervision has the meaning given in section 5 of the Retirement Villages Act 2003 and includes every instrument that amends the deed of supervision
deposit taker means an NBDT as defined in section 5 of the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013
financial product has the same meaning as in section 7 of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013
FMA means the Financial Markets Authority established under Part 2 of the Financial Markets Authority Act 2011
FMA appointee has the meaning given in section 37(1)
governing document means, as the context requires,—
a governing document within the meaning of section 6(1) of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013:
a deed of supervision

in respect of a debt security, means the issuer of the debt security within the meaning of section 11 of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013:
in respect of a registered scheme, means the manager of the scheme within the meaning of section 6(1) of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013
issuer obligation means an obligation imposed on the issuer of a financial product by or under any or all of the following:

the terms of any offer of the product:
a court order relating to the product:
this Act:
the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013:
the KiwiSaver Act 2006:
the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013

licensee—
means a supervisor that holds a licence; and

every governing document:
the terms of the offer of the financial product:
a court order relating to a supervised interest:
this Act:
the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013:
the KiwiSaver Act 2006:
the Non-bank Deposit Takers Act 2013:
the Retirement Villages Act 2003
material change of circumstances, in relation to a licensee, means—
a change that adversely affects the licensee's capacity effectively to perform the functions of a supervisor in respect of a debt security or registered scheme, or of a statutory supervisor in respect of a retirement village, covered by the licence; or
a change that means that the licensee no longer meets the requirements referred to in section 16(2)
Minister means the Minister of the Crown who, under the authority of any warrant or with the authority of the Prime Minister, is for the time being responsible for the administration of this Act
operator has the meaning given in section 5 of the Retirement Villages Act 2003

Unless the context otherwise requires, a term or expression that is defined in the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 (in relation to financial products) or the Retirement Villages Act 2003 (in relation to retirement villages) and used, but not defined, in this Act has the same meaning as in those Acts.
5 Act binds the Crown
This Act binds the Crown.
 Part 2 — Regulation of supervisors
 Subpart 1 — Licensing of supervisors
 Requirement to be licensed, etc
6 Supervisor must be licensed
A supervisor in respect of a debt security must hold a licence that covers supervision of the security.
A supervisor in respect of a registered scheme must hold a licence that covers supervision of the scheme.
A statutory supervisor in respect of a retirement village must hold a licence that covers supervision of the retirement village.
A contravention of this section may give rise to a pecuniary penalty under section 41.
7 Licensee must comply with conditions imposed on licence
A licensee must comply with—
every condition imposed on the licence by the FMA under section 11; and
every condition imposed on the licence by regulations made under this Act (if any).
8 Prohibition on holding out
A person must not represent that the person is licensed to be a supervisor in respect of a debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village if the person does not hold a licence that covers supervision of the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village.
A contravention of this section may give rise to a pecuniary penalty under section 41.
9 Requirement to be licensed: exception for certain FMA appointees
Section 6 does not apply to a person appointed as an FMA appointee under section 22 or 37.
 Licences: general
10 FMA may license supervisors
The FMA may license a person to be 1 or more of the following:
a supervisor in respect of debt securities:
a supervisor in respect of registered schemes:
a statutory supervisor in respect of retirement villages.
A licence may cover supervision of all debt securities, all registered schemes, all retirement villages, or any 1 or more of the following:
debt securities of 1 or more classes:
1 or more particular issues of debt securities:
registered schemes of 1 or more classes:
1 or more particular registered schemes:
retirement villages of 1 or more classes:
1 or more particular retirement villages.
A class may be defined (to include or exclude supervision of a debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village) in any way, including, without limitation, by reference to—
a particular issuer or operator; or
a particular class of issuer or operator.
11 FMA may impose conditions on licence
The FMA may, on granting or varying a licence, impose conditions on the licence.
The FMA may impose conditions that—
limit a licence:
apply only if the licensee is appointed as—
supervisor in respect of a particular debt security or class of debt security; or
supervisor in respect of a particular registered scheme or class of registered scheme; or
statutory supervisor in respect of a particular retirement village or class of retirement village.
Subsection (2) does not limit subsection (1).
Examples of the conditions that the FMA may impose on a licence under subsection (2)﻿(a) include, without limitation, conditions—
limiting the number of appointments as supervisor that may be held by the licensee:
setting a maximum value for supervised interests that are debt securities or registered schemes:
limiting the number of retirement villages in respect of which the statutory supervisor may hold a licence:
relating to the matters listed in section 16(3).
12 Duration of licence
A licence must be issued for a fixed period of no more than 8 years.
A licence takes effect on the date stated in the licence.
Subject to section 19(2), a licence expires on the earlier of—
the date on which the licence is cancelled under section 17 or 32(2)﻿(d); and
the date on which the fixed period ends.
13 Information to be stated in licence
A licence must state the following information:
the name of the licensee:
in the case of debt securities, the debt security or debt securities the supervision of which is covered by the licence:
in the case of registered schemes, the scheme or schemes the supervision of which is covered by the licence:
in the case of retirement villages, the retirement village or villages the supervision of which is covered by the licence:
the conditions (if any) imposed on the licence by the FMA under section 11:
the date on which the licence takes effect:
the date on which the fixed period for which the licence is issued ends:
the date by which the first report under section 25 must be delivered.
14 FMA must send licence and details to licensee and others
If the FMA issues a licence, the FMA must send the licence to the licensee.
If the FMA varies a licence, the FMA must send a replacement licence to the licensee.
If the FMA cancels a licence, the FMA must notify the former licensee.
In addition to the requirements of subsections (1) to (3), the FMA must send details of the licence or the replacement licence or notification of the cancellation (as the case may be) to—
the Registrar of Financial Service Providers, if the licence relates to a debt security or registered scheme; and
the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, if the licensee is, or was, the supervisor of a debt security issued by a deposit taker; and
the Registrar of Retirement Villages, if the licensee is, or was, the statutory supervisor of a retirement village.
The FMA may publicly notify any details about a licence (including the information referred to in section 13 and details relating to the variation or cancellation of a licence) as it thinks fit.
 Applications for, to vary, or to cancel licences
15 Application for, or to vary, licence
A person may apply to the FMA—
for a licence; or
to vary a licence.
An application must be—
made in the form required by the FMA; and
accompanied by the fee prescribed under section 67 of the Financial Markets Authority Act 2011.
For the purpose of making a decision on an application, the FMA may require the applicant to supply information in addition to that contained in the application and may, at any time, require the applicant to verify that all, or any specified part, of the information provided in relation to the application (whether at the time of the application or at any later date) remains accurate at the time that verification is required under this subsection.
16 Decision on application for, or to vary, licence
The FMA may issue or vary a licence only if the FMA is satisfied that, having regard to any conditions imposed on the licence, the applicant is capable of effectively performing (or will, after the variation, be capable of effectively performing)—
the functions of a supervisor in respect of debt securities or registered schemes the supervision of which is covered by the licence:
the functions of a statutory supervisor in respect of retirement villages the supervision of which is covered by the licence.
Despite subsection (1), the FMA may not issue a licence unless the FMA is satisfied that the applicant meets the following requirements:
the applicant is—
a body corporate that is incorporated in New Zealand; or
an overseas company (as defined in the Companies Act 1993) registered under that Act:
every director and senior manager of the applicant is of good character:
in the case of an applicant for a licence that covers supervision of a debt security or a registered scheme, the applicant is, or will be, registered under the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008 on and from commencing to perform the functions of a supervisor:
in the case of an applicant for a licence that covers supervision of a retirement village, the applicant complies with section 13(1)﻿(a) of the Financial Service Providers (Registration and Dispute Resolution) Act 2008.
Before making a decision under subsection (1), the FMA must assess the following matters:
the experience, skills, and qualifications of the applicant (including, in particular, of the applicant's directors and senior managers):
the financial resources available to the applicant:
the other resources available to the applicant:
the applicant's procedures for ensuring that the applicant complies with the licensee obligations:
the applicant's procedures for ensuring that,—
in the case of a licence relating to debt securities or registered schemes, issuers of those debt securities or of managed investment products in those schemes comply with the issuer obligations; and
in the case of a licence relating to retirement villages, operators of those retirement villages comply with the operator obligations:
the applicant's independence from issuers, or from operators, covered by the licence:
the applicant's governance structure:
the applicant's professional indemnity insurance:
other prescribed matters that relate to the applicant, to debt securities, registered schemes, or retirement villages the supervision of which is covered by the licence, and to issuers or operators covered by the licence:
any other matter that the FMA considers is material.
In this section,—
director has the meaning given in section 126 of the Companies Act 1993, but also includes, in the case of a body corporate that is not a company, a person (including a delegate) who occupies a position comparable to that of a director
senior manager, in relation to an applicant, means a person (including a delegate) who is not a director but occupies a position that allows the person to exercise significant influence over the management or administration of the applicant (for example, a chief executive or a chief financial officer).
17 Application to cancel licence
The FMA may cancel a licence on the written application of the licensee.
Before cancelling a licence under subsection (1), the FMA must be satisfied that the licensee does not hold an appointment as a supervisor.
18 Notice, consultation, and submissions concerning decision under section 16(1)
Before making a decision under section 16(1), the FMA must consult with, and take into account the views of,—
the Reserve Bank of New Zealand, if the application relates to a licence that covers supervision of a debt security issued by a deposit taker:
the Registrar of Retirement Villages, if the application relates to a licence that covers supervision of a retirement village.
The FMA may decline to issue a licence, or may issue a licence other than as proposed in the application, only if the FMA gives the applicant—
at least 10 working days' written notice of the following matters before the FMA makes a decision under section 16(1):
that the FMA may decline to issue a licence, or may issue a licence other than as proposed in the application; and
the reasons why it is considering taking that action; and
an opportunity to make written submissions and to be heard on the matter within that notice period.
After making a decision on the application under section 16(1), the FMA must notify the applicant, and any person supervised by the applicant, in writing of—
the decision; and
if the FMA declines to issue the licence, or does not issue or vary the licence as proposed in the application, the FMA's reasons for the decision; and
if the FMA issues or varies the licence (whether as proposed in the application or not), any matters that the FMA wishes the licensee to consider for the purposes of section 26(2).
19 Appeal to High Court against FMA's decision on application
An applicant may appeal to the High Court no later than 20 working days, or such further period as the court may allow, after receiving written notice of the decision under section 18(3).
If a supervisor in respect of a debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village lodges an appeal against a decision not to issue a new licence that covers supervision of the debt security, scheme, or retirement village, the court may extend the validity of the supervisor's current licence, to the extent that it covers supervision of the debt security, scheme, or retirement village, until the appeal has been determined or withdrawn.
A decision against which an appeal is lodged remains valid pending the determination of the appeal unless the court orders otherwise.
On appeal, the court may—
confirm, cancel, or vary the decision; or
refer the decision back to the FMA with a direction to reconsider the whole or a specified part of the decision.
 Expiry of licences and temporary appointment powers for expiries and other vacancies
20 Effect of expiry of licence
The expiry of the licence of a person who holds an appointment as a supervisor does not, of itself, terminate the appointment (but, if the person continues to hold the appointment and does not obtain a new licence, the person breaches section 6).
This section is for the avoidance of doubt.
21 Licensee must apply for new licence or notify issuer or operator before licence expires
A licensee appointed as a supervisor in respect of a debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village must, between 9 and 12 months before the expiry date of the licence,—
make an application under section 15 for a new licence that covers supervision of that debt security, scheme, or retirement village; or
notify the issuer of the debt security or managed investment products in the scheme or the operator of that retirement village (as the case may be) and the FMA, in writing, that the licensee does not intend to make the application referred to in paragraph (a).
If the licensee makes an application referred to in subsection (1)﻿(a), the FMA must make a decision on the application no later than 3 months before the expiry date.
In this section, expiry date means the date on which the fixed period for which a licence is issued under section 16 ends.
22 FMA may make temporary appointment
This section applies if—
all of the following are satisfied in relation to a supervisor in respect of a debt security or registered scheme or a statutory supervisor in respect of a retirement village (the existing appointee):
the FMA rejects an application by the existing appointee for a new licence that covers supervision of the debt security, scheme, or retirement village; and
the existing appointee no longer holds a licence that covers supervision of the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village; and
the existing appointee has not been replaced; or
the existing appointee wishes, at any time, to resign, ceases to carry on business, refuses to act as a supervisor, or is for any reason incapable of acting as a supervisor and the existing appointee has not been replaced; or
a debt security, a registered scheme, or a retirement village does not, for any reason, have a supervisor.
The FMA may,—
in the case of subsection (1)﻿(a) or (b), remove an existing appointee as supervisor in respect of the debt security or registered scheme, or as statutory supervisor in respect of the retirement village, by written notice to the existing appointee; and
in any case, appoint a person (the FMA appointee) to the position of supervisor of the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village for a period of 6 months; and
if the FMA considers it necessary or desirable in the circumstances, reappoint the FMA appointee (as an FMA appointee) to the position of supervisor of the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village for a further period that the FMA thinks fit (and a reappointment under this paragraph may be made on 1 or more occasions).
Before making an appointment under subsection (2)﻿(b) or (c), the FMA must, if the FMA considers it practicable to do so, consult the relevant issuer or operator.
The FMA's ability to set preconditions on requests to it under subsection (1)﻿(b) includes (without limitation) a discretion to set preconditions that satisfy the FMA that—
it is appropriate for the replacement of the existing appointee to be done under this section rather than by means of another power or process; and
the FMA appointee’s costs and charges (including for any appointment on a continuing basis referred to in subsection (9)﻿(b)), and any costs of the FMA in connection with the appointment, will be met by the issuer or operator, under subsection (5), or by the existing appointee; and
a suitable bond has been, or will be, paid to secure—
the performance of any indemnity or other obligation in connection with the costs or changes referred to in paragraph (b):
compliance with any other preconditions.
The FMA must, as soon as practicable after making the appointment, notify the issuer or the operator of the appointment.
The issuer or operator must reimburse the FMA for—
the FMA appointee's costs and charges that are paid by the FMA; and
any costs of the FMA in connection with the appointment.
An amount payable under subsection (5) must be paid by the date specified for payment in an invoice given to the issuer or operator by the FMA and is recoverable as a debt due to the FMA.
Subject to subsection (7), the FMA appointee is bound by the terms of the governing document relating to the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village.
The FMA may approve a change to those terms (including a change that increases the charges payable to the FMA appointee) during the period of the FMA appointee's appointment if—
the issuer or operator (as the case may be) and the FMA appointee agree that the change should be made; and
the FMA is satisfied that the change will have no significant adverse effect on the interests of holders of the debt security or managed investment product or on the interests of residents of the retirement village.
The power in subsection (7) may be exercised to approve a change to the terms of the governing document despite anything to the contrary in the document.
Before the appointment of the FMA appointee under subsection (2) finally comes to an end (taking into account any reappointment under subsection (2)﻿(c)), the issuer or the operator must either—
appoint a licensee as a replacement for the FMA appointee; or
appoint the FMA appointee as the supervisor on a continuing basis (provided that the FMA appointee holds a licence that covers supervision of the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village).
Despite sections 6 and 16(2), the FMA may appoint, as an FMA appointee, any person (including a natural person) that the FMA considers appropriate in the circumstances, whether or not that person holds a licence.
If the FMA appointee does not hold a licence, the FMA appointee must, for the purposes of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 and the Retirement Villages Act 2003, be treated as holding a licence that covers supervision of the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village (as the case may be).
23 Issuer or operator may replace existing appointee or FMA appointee
This section applies if—
the licence of a supervisor in respect of a debt security or registered scheme or of a statutory supervisor of a retirement village (the existing appointee) is due to expire; and
the existing appointee will, on the expiry of the licence, no longer hold a licence that covers supervision of the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village.
For the purposes of subsection (1)﻿(a), a licence is due to expire if—
the licensee has given notice in accordance with section 21(1)﻿(b) (notice that the licensee does not intend to apply for a new licence that covers supervision of the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village); or
the FMA has given notice in accordance with section 18(3)﻿(b) (notice that the FMA will not be issuing a new licence as proposed in the application).
This section also applies if an FMA appointee has been appointed by the FMA under section 22(2)﻿(b) or (c).
If the issuer of the debt security or managed investment products, or the operator of the retirement village, appoints a person (the new appointee) as the supervisor in place of the existing appointee or the FMA appointee, and the new appointee accepts the appointment, the issuer or operator may remove the existing appointee or the FMA appointee by written notice to the existing appointee or the FMA appointee.
An appointment under subsection (4) must be made in accordance with the relevant governing document.
The issuer or operator must, as soon as practicable after giving the written notice, give a copy of the notice to the FMA.
The removal of the existing appointee takes effect on the date stated in, or calculated in accordance with, the notice.
But if the notice is given to the FMA appointee, the removal of the FMA appointee cannot take effect until at least 15 working days have passed since the date on which the notice was given to the FMA appointee.
Subsection (8) does not apply if the FMA appointee agrees in writing that the subsection does not apply.
The power in subsection (4) may be exercised to remove the existing appointee or FMA appointee despite anything to the contrary in the governing document.
Nothing in this section limits the issuer's or operator's power to remove the existing appointee apart from under this section.
24 Existing appointee must provide documents
The FMA appointee may, by giving notice in writing to the existing appointee, require the existing appointee to provide the FMA appointee, at the existing appointee's expense, with all documents held by, or in the control of, the existing appointee that relate to the functions of the position to which the FMA appointee has been appointed.
The new appointee may, by giving notice in writing to the existing appointee or FMA appointee (person A), require person A to provide the new appointee, at person A's expense, with all documents held by, or in the control of, person A that relate to the functions of the position to which the new appointee has been appointed.
A notice under this section—
must be given no later than 6 months after the date of the appointment of the person who gives the notice:
ceases to be effective if the person who gives the notice ceases to hold the position referred to in subsection (1) or (2):
must specify a reasonable time within which the documents must be provided.
An existing appointee or an FMA appointee to whom a notice is given under subsection (1) or (2) must comply with the notice.
A document provided pursuant to a notice under this section may be used only for the purpose of performing the functions of the position referred to in subsection (1) or (2).
A contravention of subsection (4) or (5) may give rise to a pecuniary penalty under section 41.
In this section,—
existing appointee—
has the meaning given in section 22(1)﻿(a) or 23(1)﻿(a); and
includes, in relation to an FMA appointee who is appointed in the circumstances referred to in section 22(1)﻿(c), the person who held the position of supervisor of the debt security, registered scheme, or retirement village before the FMA appointee was appointed
FMA appointee has the meaning given in section 22(2)﻿(b)
new appointee has the meaning given in section 23(4).
 Subpart 2 — Monitoring and enforcement
 Reports and investigations
25 Licensee must deliver regular reports to FMA
A licensee must deliver a report to the FMA—
by a date determined by the FMA when a licence is issued, which must be between 6 and 12 months after the date on which the licence is issued; and
at least once every 6 months after that date.
The report must contain—
prescribed information about the following:
the requirements referred to in section 16(2):
the matters referred to in section 16(3):
the licensee's compliance with any conditions imposed on the licence:
the supervised interest:
the licensee's compliance with the terms of every governing document that relates to a supervised interest:
other prescribed matters; and
any information required by a condition imposed on the licence.
26 Licensee must report breach of licensee obligation, etc
This section applies if a licensee believes that—
the licensee has, or may have, breached a licensee obligation; or
a material change of circumstances has occurred, may have occurred, or is likely to occur in relation to the licensee; or
the information on which the FMA based the decision to issue or vary the licence was, or may have been, wrong, misleading, or incomplete.
For the purposes of subsection (1)﻿(b) and (c), the licensee must consider any matters notified by the FMA under sections 18(3)﻿(c) and 30(5)﻿(d).
The licensee must, as soon as practicable after the licensee forms the belief referred to in subsection (1), deliver a report to the FMA containing details of the belief and the licensee's grounds for the belief.
 Action plans
27 FMA may require licensee to submit action plan
This section applies if the FMA is satisfied that—
a licensee has breached, or is likely to breach, a licensee obligation; or
a material change of circumstances has occurred, or is likely to occur, in relation to a licensee; or
the information on which the decision to issue or vary a licensee's licence was based was wrong, misleading, or incomplete in a material respect.
The FMA may, by written notice to the licensee, require the licensee to submit an action plan to the FMA.
The notice must—
give details of the breach or likely breach, material change of circumstances or likely material change of circumstances, or wrong, misleading, or incomplete information; and
specify the date by which the action plan must be submitted to the FMA.
The action plan must specify—
the step or steps that will be taken—
to remedy or avoid the breach or likely breach; or
to avoid any further breach; or
to mitigate or avoid any adverse effects or changes arising, or likely to arise, from the material change of circumstances; or
in light of the wrong, misleading, or incomplete information; and
the date by which each step will be taken.
28 Approval or rejection of action plan
If a licensee submits an action plan, the FMA may—
approve the action plan; or
require the licensee to amend the action plan and resubmit it to the FMA by a specified date for approval or rejection; or
reject the action plan.
If the FMA approves the action plan or amended action plan, the licensee must comply with the plan.
An action plan that has been approved by the FMA may be varied at any time by the licensee with the consent of the FMA.
29 FMA's powers if action plan rejected
If the FMA rejects a licensee's action plan or amended action plan, the FMA may do either or both of the following:
give a written direction to the licensee:
vary the licensee's licence in accordance with section 30.
A direction under subsection (1)﻿(a) must specify—
the step or steps that the licensee must take—
to remedy or avoid any breach or likely breach of a licensee obligation; or
to avoid any further breach; or
to mitigate or avoid any adverse effects or changes arising, or likely to arise, from the material change of circumstances; or
in light of the wrong, misleading, or incomplete information; and
the date by which each step must be taken.
The licensee must comply with the direction.
 Variation of licences
30 FMA may vary licence because of material change of circumstances, etc
The FMA may, by written notice to a licensee, vary the licence—
if the FMA is satisfied that—
a material change of circumstances has occurred in relation to the licensee; or
the information on which the FMA based the decision to issue or vary the licence was wrong, misleading, or incomplete in a material respect; or
if the FMA rejects the licensee's action plan or amended action plan; or
if the licensee—
fails to submit or amend an action plan by the date specified by the FMA; or
fails to comply with an action plan by the date specified in the plan; or
fails to comply with a direction of the FMA under section 29 or 36 by the date specified in the direction; or
fails to comply with a direction of the FMA under section 205 of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 by the date specified in the direction; or
is in administration, receivership, or liquidation, or is subject to statutory management, or enters any other process (whether in New Zealand or another country) under which the assets and affairs of the licensee are administered, or the assets of the licensee are realised, for the benefit of creditors of the licensee.
The FMA may vary a licence only if the FMA gives the applicant—
at least 10 working days' written notice of the following matters before the FMA varies the licence:
that the FMA may vary the licence; and
the reasons why it is considering taking that action; and
an opportunity to make written submissions and to be heard on the matter within that notice period.
Subject to subsection (4), the FMA must exercise the power to vary a licence so that (after the licence is varied) the FMA is satisfied that, having regard to any conditions imposed on the licence, the licensee is capable of effectively performing the functions of a supervisor in respect of debt securities or registered schemes, or of a statutory supervisor in respect of retirement villages, covered by the licence.
The FMA may not vary a licence so that the licence ceases to cover a supervised interest.
The notice under subsection (1) must state,—
if subsection (1)﻿(a) applies, the material change of circumstances or information that the FMA considers was wrong, misleading, or incomplete; and
the FMA's reasons for varying the licence; and
the date on which the variation takes effect; and
any matters that the FMA wishes the licensee to consider for the purposes of section 26(2).
31 Appeal to High Court against FMA's decision to vary licence
A licensee who is dissatisfied with the FMA's decision under section 30(1) may appeal to the High Court no later than 20 working days, or such further period as the court may allow, after receiving written notice of the decision.
A decision against which an appeal is lodged remains valid pending the determination of the appeal unless the court orders otherwise.
On appeal, the court may—
confirm, cancel, or vary the decision; or
refer the decision back to the FMA with a direction to reconsider the whole or a specified part of the decision.

32 FMA's powers if action plan not submitted, etc

fails to comply with a direction of the FMA under section 29 or 36 by the date specified in the direction; or
fails to comply with a direction of the FMA under section 205 of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013 by the date specified in the direction; or
is in administration, receivership, or liquidation, or is subject to statutory management, or enters any other process (whether in New Zealand or another country) under which the assets and affairs of the licensee are administered, or the assets of the licensee are realised, for the benefit of creditors of the licensee.
The FMA may take 1 or more of the following steps:
vary the licensee's licence in accordance with section 30:
give a written notice (a removal notice) to the licensee that applies to 1 or more supervised interests:
by written notice (a variation notice) to the licensee, vary the licensee's licence so that the licence ceases to cover 1 or more supervised interests:
by written notice (a cancellation notice) to the licensee, cancel the licensee's licence.
To avoid doubt, a removal notice may apply to any supervised interest, whether or not the action plan or direction referred to in subsection (1) relates to that interest.
If the FMA varies a licence under subsection (2)﻿(c) so that the licence ceases to cover a supervised interest, the FMA must give a removal notice under subsection (2)﻿(b) that applies to the interest.
If the FMA cancels a licence, the FMA must give a removal notice under subsection (2)﻿(b) that applies to every supervised interest.
The FMA may take 1 or more of the steps referred to in subsection (2)﻿(b) to (d) only if the FMA gives the licensee—
at least 5 working days' written notice of the following matters before the FMA takes the action:
that the FMA may take 1 or more of the steps referred to in subsection (2)﻿(b) to (d); and
the reasons why it is considering taking that action; and
an opportunity to make written submissions and to be heard on the matter within that notice period.
33 Removal notice
A removal notice must—
state the FMA's reasons for giving the notice; and
specify the latest date by which the existing appointee will be removed (the final removal date), which must not be more than 40 working days after the date on which the removal notice is given to the existing appointee; and
specify the supervised interest or interests to which the removal notice applies.
Despite subsection (1)﻿(b), if the removal notice is given in relation to the cancellation of a licence, the final removal date must be no later than the date on which the cancellation takes effect.
The FMA must give a copy of the removal notice to every affected person at the same time as the notice is given to the licensee.
The removal of the existing appointee as supervisor in respect of an affected debt security or scheme, or as statutory supervisor of an affected retirement village, takes effect on the final removal date unless the existing appointee is removed from that appointment before that date (whether under section 38(1) or otherwise).

37 Removal notice: FMA may replace existing appointee
If an affected person does not remove the existing appointee by written notice under section 38(1) or otherwise appoint a person as the supervisor in place of the existing appointee before the final removal date, the FMA may appoint a person (the FMA appointee) to that position for a period of 6 months beginning on the final removal date.
If the FMA considers it necessary or desirable in the circumstances, the FMA may reappoint the FMA appointee (as an FMA appointee) to the position of supervisor for a further period that the FMA thinks fit (and a reappointment under this subsection may be made on 1 or more occasions).
Before making an appointment under subsection (1) or (1A), the FMA must, if the FMA considers it practicable to do so, consult the affected person.
The FMA must, as soon as practicable after making the appointment, notify the affected person of the appointment.
The affected person must reimburse the FMA for—
the FMA appointee's costs and charges that are paid by the FMA; and
any costs of the FMA in connection with the appointment.
An amount payable under subsection (4) must be paid by the date specified for payment in an invoice given to the affected person by the FMA and is recoverable as a debt due to the FMA.
The FMA appointee is bound by the terms of the governing document under which the existing appointee was appointed (subject to subsection (6)).
The FMA may approve a change to those terms (including a change that increases the charges payable to the FMA appointee) during the period of the FMA appointee's appointment if—

Before the appointment of the FMA appointee under this section finally comes to an end (taking into account any reappointment under subsection (1A)), the affected person must either—

 Pecuniary penalty and compensation orders
41 Pecuniary penalty orders
The High Court may, on application by the FMA, order a licensee or other person to pay a pecuniary penalty to the Crown if the court is satisfied that—
the licensee or other person has contravened section 6, 8, 24(4) or (5), or 39(5) or (6); or
the licensee has contravened a licensee obligation.
In determining whether or not to make a pecuniary penalty order and (if an order is to be made) the amount of the penalty to be imposed, the court must have regard to all relevant matters, including—
the nature and extent of the contravention:
in the case of a contravention relating to a debt security or registered scheme, the likelihood, nature, and extent of any damage to the integrity or reputation of New Zealand's financial markets as a result of the contravention:
the nature and extent of any loss or damage suffered by product holders or residents because of the contravention:
the circumstances in which the contravention occurred:
whether or not the licensee or other person has previously contravened this Act or a licensee obligation:
the public benefit in encouraging prompt and honest self-reporting of breaches or possible breaches of licensee obligations:
any other circumstances that the court considers relevant.
The maximum amount of a pecuniary penalty under this section is $600,000.
If conduct by a person constitutes a contravention of 2 or more provisions referred to in subsection (1) or licensee obligations, proceedings may be brought against that person for the contravention of any 1 or more of the provisions or obligations, but a person is not liable to more than 1 pecuniary penalty order for the same conduct.
42 Compensation orders
The High Court may, on application by the FMA, a product holder, or a resident, order a licensee to pay compensation to any product holder or resident (the aggrieved person) if the court is satisfied that—
the licensee has contravened a licensee obligation; and
the aggrieved person has suffered, or is likely to suffer, loss or damage because of the contravention.
The court may make a compensation order whether or not any aggrieved person is a party to the proceeding.
The court may make any order it thinks just to compensate an aggrieved person in whole or in part for the loss or damage.
43 Further provisions relating to pecuniary penalty orders and compensation orders
A licensee may be liable for both a pecuniary penalty order and a compensation order in respect of the same contravention of a licensee obligation.
Once criminal proceedings against a licensee for an offence relating to the contravention of a licensee obligation are determined, the High Court may not make a pecuniary penalty order against the licensee in respect of that contravention.
Once civil proceedings against a licensee for a pecuniary penalty order in respect of the contravention of a licensee obligation are determined, the licensee may not be convicted of an offence in respect of that contravention.
Proceedings under sections 41 and 42 are civil proceedings and the usual rules of the court and rules of evidence and procedure for civil proceedings apply (including the standard of proof).
In this section and in sections 41 and 42,—
compensation order means an order made under section 42
licensee includes a former licensee
pecuniary penalty order means an order made under section 41.

the manager has appointed another person who holds a licence under the Securities Trustees and Statutory Supervisors Act 2011 that covers the unit trust to the position, and the new trustee has accepted the appointment; or
the High Court consents.
Despite anything to the contrary in the trust deed, a manager may not discharge or remove a trustee except with the approval of the High Court.
Subsection (2) does not apply to the removal of a trustee by the manager under Part 2 of the Securities Trustees and Statutory Supervisors Act 2011.

83 New section 19A inserted
The following section is inserted after section 19:
19A Trustee may apply to High Court for orders relating to unit trust
This section applies if a trustee is satisfied that—
there is a significant risk that the interests of unit holders will be materially prejudiced; or
the provisions of the trust deed are no longer adequate to give proper protection to unit holders.
The trustee may apply to the High Court for an order under this section.
The court may direct that an application be served on any person that the court thinks fit.
On an application, the court may, after giving the manager and any other persons that the court thinks fit the opportunity to be heard, by order—
amend the provisions of the trust deed:
impose restrictions on the activities of the trustee or the manager (including, in the case of the manager, restrictions on advertising) that the court thinks are necessary for the protection of the interests of unit holders:
direct the manager or trustee to convene a meeting of unit holders for the purpose of—
having placed before them by the trustee any information relating to their interests, and any proposals for the protection of their interests, that the court or the trustee considers necessary or appropriate; and
obtaining their opinions or directions in relation to the information and proposals referred to in subparagraph (i):
give directions in relation to the conduct of any meeting convened in accordance with paragraph (c):
stay all civil proceedings before any court by or against the trustee or the manager:
restrain the payment of money by the trustee or the manager to unit holders or a class of unit holders:
remove a person as manager and appoint another person as manager (with any powers that the court orders):
give any other directions that the court considers necessary to protect the interests of unit holders, or the public.
The court may vary or cancel an order made under this section.
In exercising its powers under this section, the court must have regard to the interests of all creditors in respect of the unit trust.
