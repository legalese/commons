Written from BRIEF.md and the source text (`../registers/source-bundle/act_public_1993_91.txt`, version as at 24 January 2026) alone, before any `.l4` module was opened.
Each scenario gives the facts, the answer I expect, and the words of the Act that give it.
Paragraph labels are reconstructed from each provision's place in its list (BRIEF.md).
"Inputs" in BRIEF.md (reasonableness, substantiality judgements, "ordinarily acquired for personal use", remediability, supplier refusal) are taken as given facts in each scenario.

A section at the end ("Encoding notes") was added in phase 2: it records which scenarios the encoding cannot express. No expected value was changed after reading the encoding.

## A. Who is a consumer (s 2 "consumer")

The definition has two limbs joined by "and": (a) acquires from a supplier goods or services *of a kind ordinarily acquired for personal, domestic, or household use or consumption*; and (b) does not acquire them, *or hold himself or herself out as acquiring them*, for the purpose of (i) resupplying in trade, (ii) consuming in a process of production or manufacture, or (iii) for goods, repairing or treating in trade other goods or fixtures on land.

| # | Facts | Expected | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Individual buys a fridge for her home; fridge is of a kind ordinarily acquired for household use. | Consumer. | s 2 consumer (a), (b). |
| A2 | Company buys the same fridge for the staff kitchen; not for resale, production or repair. | Consumer. The test is the *kind* of goods, not the buyer's personal purpose; a business can be a consumer. | s 2 consumer (a) "of a kind ordinarily acquired"; (b) only excludes the three purposes. |
| A3 | Individual buys an industrial lathe, a kind not ordinarily acquired for personal/domestic use. | Not a consumer. | s 2 consumer (a). |
| A4 | Retailer buys fridges to resell in trade. | Not a consumer. | (b)(i) "resupplying them in trade". |
| A5 | Bakery buys flour (a household kind of good) to bake bread for sale. | Not a consumer. | (b)(ii) "consuming them in the course of a process of production or manufacture". |
| A6 | Appliance repairer buys a household fuse to fit into a customer's toaster in his repair business. | Not a consumer. | (b)(iii) "in the case of goods, repairing or treating in trade other goods". |
| A7 | Plumber buys household taps to install in a customer's house (fixtures on land) in trade. | Not a consumer. | (b)(iii) "fixtures on land". |

## Encoding notes (phase 2)

Written after reading cga-types, cga-scope, cga-goods, cga-services and cga-goal. No expected value above was changed.
`tests-independent.l4` carries 153 assertions: 145 satisfied, 6 failed, 2 refused (run of 2026-10-15).

### Findings (failed or refused assertions)

1. **Duplicate rejection (G5').** Remediable, substantial, supplier refused, right to reject intact: s 18(2)(b)(ii) and s 18(3)(a) both give rejection; the encoding lists "reject" twice. Expected each remedy once.
2. **Duplicate cancellation (services).** Same pattern under s 32(a)(ii)(B) and s 32(b)(i): "cancel" listed twice.
3. **s 32(a)(ii)(B) cancel without a contract (K13').** The encoding checks "there is a contract with the supplier" only for s 32(b)(i). Cancelling "the contract for the supply of the service" presupposes one; a consumer who is not party to any contract (s 2 supplier (b): "whether or not the consumer is a party") cannot cancel. Ambiguous; I think the encoding should apply the contract condition to both limbs.
4. **s 36(c) "where section 29 applies" (I12').** The encoding requires the failure complained of to be the s 29 guarantee. My reading: s 29 "applies" when a purpose or result was made known and relied on, whatever guarantee failed (e.g. a s 28 lack of care that leaves the product unfit for the made-known purpose). The goods analogue, s 21(c) "where section 8(1) applies", is encoded without such a condition, so the two are inconsistent. Ambiguous; I prefer my reading.
5. **s 41(2) charity bars the manufacturer too.** The encoding folds s 41(2) into "the Act gives redress", so manufacturer remedies become EMPTY. s 41(2) only removes redress "against a charitable organisation"; Part 3 redress against the manufacturer is untouched. Clear error.
6. **Consumer limb (b)(iii) applied to services (A13).** `is a consumer` applies the repair-in-trade exclusion regardless of whether goods or services were acquired; the Act confines it to "in the case of goods". Real but small (an input-field question).
7. **Refused: s 6 claim on reticulated electricity (B19).** The Act answers this (s 7B(4): s 7A is the only guarantee, so no redress under s 6); the encoding refuses. Defensible as an input-validity guard; not a REFUSE case under the brief's rule ("where the source does not answer").
8. **Refused: work and materials (K24, ss 15, 34).** A supply recorded as `services` cannot carry a goods failure: the encoding refuses. `Thing supplied` is a single choice, so a work-and-materials contract has to be split by the user into two cases. Representational limitation.
