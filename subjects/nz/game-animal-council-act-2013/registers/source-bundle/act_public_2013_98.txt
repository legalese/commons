Game Animal Council Act 2013
1 Title
This Act is the Game Animal Council Act 2013.
2 Commencement
This Act comes into force on the day after the date on which it receives the Royal assent.
 Part 1 — Preliminary and key provisions
 Subpart 1 — Preliminary provisions
3 Purposes
The purposes of this Act are—
to establish the Game Animal Council and provide for its functions; and
to create powers for the Minister to manage herds of special interest; and
to allow the Minister to delegate his or her powers to the Council; and
to provide for fees and levies to fund the Council and its functions.
4 Interpretation
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—
conservation board means a conservation board established under section 6L of the Conservation Act 1987
Council means the Game Animal Council established under section 6
Crown entity means a Crown entity within the meaning of section 7 of the Crown Entities Act 2004
Customs has the meaning given to it in section 5(1) of the Customs and Excise Act 2018
Customs officer means a person holding office as a Customs officer under the Customs and Excise Act 2018
Director-General means the Director-General of Conservation
enforcement officer means—
a constable; or
a person appointed under clause 1(1) of Schedule 1
game animal—
means—
any chamois, deer, or tahr:
any pig that is living in a wild state and is not being herded or handled as a domestic animal or kept within an effective fence or enclosure for farming purposes; and
includes the whole or any part of the carcass of the animal
game trophy—
means any part of a chamois, deer, tahr, or pig that is a hunting souvenir; but
does not include—
velvet or velvet antlers:
the hide of a deer
game trophy export levy means the levy that is—
payable for the export of a game trophy; and
imposed by regulations made under section 34
government department means a public service agency as defined in section 5 of the Public Service Act 2020
herd of special interest means a species of game animals in a specified area designated by the Minister as a herd of special interest under section 16
honorary enforcement officer means a person appointed under clause 2(1) of Schedule 1
Minister means the Minister of Conservation
New Zealand Conservation Authority means the authority established under section 6A of the Conservation Act 1987
overriding considerations means—
the welfare and management of public conservation land and resources generally:
any statement of general policy that is made, or has effect as if it were made, under—
section 17B of the Conservation Act 1987:
section 44 of the National Parks Act 1980:
section 15A of the Reserves Act 1977:
section 14C of the Wildlife Act 1953:
any conservation management strategy made under section 17D of the Conservation Act 1987:
any conservation management plan made under—
section 17E of the Conservation Act 1987:
section 40B of the Reserves Act 1977:
any management plan made under—
section 47 of the National Parks Act 1980:
section 41 of the Reserves Act 1977:
any wild animal control plan made under section 5 of the Wild Animal Control Act 1977:
any pest management strategy, pest management plan, pathway management plan, or operational plan made under the Biosecurity Act 1993
public conservation land means land that is—
held, managed, or administered by the Department of Conservation under the Conservation Act 1987 or an enactment listed in Schedule 1 of that Act; and
owned by the Crown
regional council—
means a regional council within the meaning of section 5(1) of the Local Government Act 2002; and
includes a unitary authority within the meaning of section 5(1) of that Act.
Unless the context otherwise requires, terms used and not defined in this Act but defined in the Wild Animal Control Act 1977 have the same meaning as in that Act.
5 Act binds the Crown
This Act binds the Crown.
 Subpart 2 — Game Animal Council
6 Establishment of Council
The Game Animal Council is established as a body corporate with perpetual succession.
The Council is legally separate from its members, its employees, and the Crown.
For the purpose of performing its functions, the Council has—
full capacity to carry on or undertake any activity, do any act, or enter into any transaction; and
for the purposes of paragraph (a), full rights, powers, and privileges.
Subsection (3) is subject to this Act, any other enactment, and the general law.
7 Functions of Council
The Council has the following functions in relation to game animals:
to advise and make recommendations to the Minister:
to provide information and education to the hunting sector:
to promote safety initiatives for the hunting sector, including firearms safety:
to advise private landowners on hunting:
to develop, on its own initiative or at the direction of the Minister, voluntary codes of practice for hunting:
to raise awareness of the views of the hunting sector:
to liaise with hunters, hunting organisations, representatives of tangata whenua, local authorities, landowners, the New Zealand Conservation Authority, conservation boards, and the Department of Conservation to improve hunting opportunities:
to conduct research, including research on the hunting of game animals:
in respect of herds of special interest for which the Minister has delegated management powers under section 20 to the Council,—
to undertake management functions that are compatible with the management of public conservation land and resources generally; and
to exercise its powers for the effective management of the herd:
to operate voluntary certification schemes for professional hunting guides and game estates:
to promote minimum standards and codes of conduct for certified hunting guides and game estates:
to investigate complaints and take disciplinary action in relation to certified hunting guides and game estates:
to provide any other services to hunters that the Minister is satisfied are ancillary to the Council's other functions:
to perform any other functions conferred on it under this Act or any other enactment:
to assess the costs of managing herds of special interest and make recommendations to the Minister on ways to recover those costs.
In performing functions other than the functions in subsection (1)﻿(a) and (f), the Council must have regard to any views expressed in writing by the Minister to the Council.
8 Council membership
The Minister must appoint no fewer than 9, and no more than 11, persons as members of the Council.
The Minister must appoint members to the Council who the Minister considers have, collectively, knowledge of, and experience in relation to, matters relevant to the functions of the Council.
Knowledge and experience that are relevant include knowledge of, and experience relating to,—
in the case of game animals,—
recreational hunting:
commercial hunting:
deer farming:
safari park operations:
meat processing and exporting:
hunting-related tourism:
Māori hunting interests:
research on hunting in New Zealand:
farming generally:
forestry:
kaitiakitanga (being guardianship of natural and physical resources in accordance with tikanga Māori):
nature conservation:
scientific disciplines.
Before appointing a member to the Council, the Minister must publish a notice—
stating the number of appointments the Minister intends to make; and
calling for nominations to be sent to the Minister; and
stating a date after which the Minister may decline to accept nominations, being a date not less than 28 days after the date of first publication of the notice.
The notice—
must be published at least twice in a daily newspaper circulating in each of the cities of Auckland, Hamilton, Wellington, Christchurch, and Dunedin; and
may be published in any other media, and on any other occasions, that the Minister thinks appropriate.
An appointment of a member of the Council—
must be made by notice in the Gazette; and
takes effect from the date of the notice, or a later date specified in the notice.
The Council's powers are not affected by any vacancy in its membership.
9 Restriction on membership
An employee of the Department of Conservation must not be appointed to the Council.
10 Terms of office of Council members
A member of the Council holds office for a term, not exceeding 3 years, that the Minister specifies in the notice of appointment under section 8(6).
The Minister may remove a member from office at any time for—
bankruptcy; or
inability to perform the functions of the office; or
neglect of duty; or
misconduct.
A member may, at any time, resign from office by giving written notice to the Minister.
Except where a member dies, resigns, or is removed from office, the member continues in office after the expiry of his or her term until the member is reappointed or a successor is appointed.
If any member of the Council dies, resigns, or is removed from office, the Minister may fill the vacancy created—
in the manner provided in section 8; or
by appointing a person, in accordance with section 8(6), from among the nominations received in response to the most recent call for nominations under section 8(4).
A person may not serve as a member of the Council for more than 6 consecutive years and a period of 3 years must elapse following the end of that period before the person is again eligible to be a member.
A member is not entitled to any compensation or other payment or benefit relating to his or her ceasing, for any reason, to hold office as a member.
11 Remuneration and expenses
Members of the Council are entitled to receive from the Council, in accordance with the fees framework referred to in section 10 of the Crown Entities Act 2004,—
remuneration for services as a member at a rate and of a kind determined by the Minister:
reimbursement for actual and reasonable travelling and other expenses incurred in performing functions and duties as a member.
12 Chairperson of Council
The Minister must, by notice in the Gazette, appoint a member of the Council to be the chairperson of the Council for a term that the Minister specifies in the notice of appointment.
The Minister must appoint as chairperson a member of the Council whom the Minister considers has the appropriate management and conflict resolution skills to chair the Council.
If there is a vacancy in the office of chairperson, the members may elect one of their number to act as chairperson until an appointment is made under subsection (1).
The chairperson must preside at all meetings of the Council at which the chairperson is present.
If the chairperson is absent from a meeting of the Council, the members present must appoint one of their number to chair the meeting.
13 Meetings of Council
Meetings of the Council may be held at the times and places that the Council or the chairperson appoints.
A special meeting must be called by the chairperson whenever 3 or more members, in writing, request a meeting.
A meeting of the Council may only be held if a quorum of the members is present at the time and place appointed for the meeting.
A majority of the members in office forms a quorum.
At any meeting of the Council,—
decisions may be made by a simple majority of members present and voting on the matter; and
the chairperson of that meeting has a deliberative vote and a casting vote; and
proper minutes must be kept of proceedings.
Part 7 of the Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987 applies to the Council as if the Council were a local authority.
Subject to the provisions of this Act, the Council may regulate its procedure in any manner it thinks fit.
The Council must notify the Minister of the adoption of any procedure as soon as is reasonably practicable after it is adopted.
The Council must make its procedures available for inspection free of charge to any person.
14 Minister and Director-General entitled to attend meetings of Council
The Council or the chairperson must give no less than 7 days' notice to the Minister and the Director-General of—
every meeting of the Council; and
the proposed business to be transacted at that meeting.
The notice—
must be in writing, and state the time and place of the meeting; and
may be given by post, delivery, email, or other electronic communication.
The Minister and the Director-General (or the Minister's or Director-General's nominee) are entitled to attend and speak at any meeting of the Council, but are not entitled to vote on any question.
Any other Minister of the Crown may nominate a representative who is entitled to attend any meeting of the Council.
This section applies despite anything to the contrary in Part 7 of the Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987.
15 Members not personally liable
A member of the Council is not personally liable for any act done or omitted to be done in good faith in the performance or exercise or intended performance or exercise of the functions or powers of the Council.
 Subpart 3 — Herds of special interest
16 Minister may designate herds of special interest
The Minister may, by notice, designate any species of game animal in a specified area on public conservation land to be a herd of special interest if—
the Minister considers that—
the animals are of special interest to hunters; and
the animals can be managed for hunting purposes; and
management of the animals for hunting purposes is consistent with the overriding considerations; and
the Minister has regard to—
the purposes of the Wild Animal Control Act 1977; and
the status of the land, particularly the designation of the land as a conservation area, national park, or reserve; and
the overriding considerations; and
a herd management plan has been made for the herd under section 19.
In determining whether a species of game animal is of special interest to hunters and can be managed for hunting purposes, the Minister must have regard to the advice, if any, of—
the Minister for Primary Industries; and
the Council; and
the Department of Conservation; and
any relevant regional council; and
the New Zealand Conservation Authority; and
any relevant conservation board; and
any representative organisations that the Minister considers appropriate.
The notice under subsection (1) must specify—
the species of game animal that constitutes the herd; and
the specified area of public conservation land where the animals are located; and
the date from which the designation takes effect; and
the reasons why the Minister considers the animals to be of special interest to hunters; and
the expected benefits to be gained by managing the animals for hunting purposes.
If a herd of special interest is designated in a national park, section 4(2)﻿(b) of the National Parks Act 1980 does not apply to that herd.
To avoid doubt, section 4(2)﻿(b) of the National Parks Act 1980 does not limit the Minister’s power to make a designation under subsection (1).
A notice under this section is secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).
17 Ownership of herds of special interest
A game animal that is part of a herd of special interest—
is the property of the Crown; but
if it is lawfully killed pursuant to this Act, ceases to be the property of the Crown and becomes the property of the person who has lawfully killed it.
This section does not impose any obligation or liability on the Crown for any damage done by any game animal that is part of a herd of special interest.
18 Minister's powers
The Minister may do any or all of the following for the purpose of managing herds of special interest, and game animals that are part of a herd of special interest:
issue authorisations for hunting, subject to any terms that the Minister thinks fit:
determine who may receive authorisations, whether by balloting or otherwise:
cancel or suspend authorisations (for breaches of the terms or conditions of hunting, or otherwise):
specify, by notice, the conditions under which the animals may be hunted, including the periods and times at which they may be hunted:
capture, convey, or liberate the animals:
carry out surveys and other assessments regarding the animals and the means of managing them:
prepare publications relating to the animals and their management:
collect and disseminate information relating to the animals and their management:
promote standards (including behavioural and industry standards) for game animal hunting and game animal management:
authorise or carry out operations for managing and controlling the size of the population, including the payment of bounties, grants, or subsidies.
The Minister must not specify a condition under subsection (1)﻿(d) without first consulting with the Director-General and any persons or representative organisations that the Minister considers appropriate.
A notice under subsection (1)﻿(d)—
is secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements); but
despite section 26 of that Act, takes effect 28 days after the publication of the notice or on a later date (if any) specified in the notice.
19 Herd management plans
The Minister must make a herd management plan for any herd of special interest (herd management plan).
The Minister may make a herd management plan for any game animals that the Minister proposes to designate as a herd of special interest.
The purpose of a herd management plan is to set out objectives and strategies for the management of a herd of special interest to achieve the expected benefits provided for under section 16(3)﻿(e).
Herd management plans must be consistent with the overriding considerations.
Before making or amending a herd management plan, the Minister must—
publish a notice of his or her intention—
in the Gazette; and
in each of the daily newspapers published in Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, and Dunedin; and
make a copy of the draft plan available to any person on request; and
give interested persons a reasonable time to make submissions on the draft plan; and
consult—
the Minister for Primary Industries; and
the Council; and
the Director-General; and
any relevant regional council; and
the New Zealand Conservation Authority; and
any relevant conservation board; and
any other person that the Minister considers appropriate.
Herd management plans must be reviewed at intervals of not more than 5 years.
A herd management plan binds the Council.
A herd management plan does not restrict or affect the exercise of a legal right or power by any person unless that person agrees to be bound.
To avoid doubt, subsection (8) does not affect any power to make any statement, strategy, or plan in paragraphs (b) to (e) of the overriding considerations.
20 Delegation of Minister's powers to Council
The Minister may delegate in writing to the Council all or any of his or her powers under section 18 except the power under section 18(1)﻿(e) to capture, convey, or liberate animals that are part of a herd of special interest.
The Council may exercise any powers delegated under this section, subject to any general or special directions given or conditions attached by the Minister, in the same manner and with the same effect as if the powers had been conferred directly by this section and not by delegation.
Every delegation made under this section is revocable in writing at will.
No delegation made under this section prevents the exercise of any power by the Minister.
Every delegation made under this section continues in force until it is revoked, despite the fact that the Minister who made it has ceased to hold office, and the delegation continues to have effect as if made by his or her successor in office.
The Minister must not delegate any power under this section to the Council unless the Minister—
first consults—
the Director-General; and
the New Zealand Conservation Authority; and
any conservation board having jurisdiction over land affected by the delegation; and
is satisfied that management by the Council (of the herd to which the delegation relates)—
is consistent with the overriding considerations; and
will improve the management of the herd.
21 Notification and exercise of delegated powers
If the Minister delegates any powers specified in section 18 to the Council,—
the Minister must publish a notice in the Gazette specifying the herd or herds of special interest to which the delegation relates, together with the powers delegated and any conditions of the delegation; and
the Council must exercise the powers that have been delegated—
consistently with the overriding considerations; and
in accordance with the terms of the delegation.
 Subpart 4 — Game trophy export levy
22 Person must pay game trophy export levy
A person must not export a game trophy from New Zealand that is a souvenir of an animal hunted and killed in New Zealand on or after the day this Act comes into force unless the person pays to the Council the game trophy export levy prescribed (if any) in respect of that trophy.
23 Export levies payable to Council
Game trophy export levies are payable to the Council and the funds received form part of the Council's funds.
Any unpaid game trophy export levy is recoverable as a debt owed to the Council in a court of competent jurisdiction.
Before bringing any proceedings to recover a debt under subsection (2), the Council must write to the exporter at his or her last known address and give the exporter not less than 10 working days to pay the amount in full.
Payment of a debt under this section is not admissible as evidence in a prosecution for an offence under section 29.
24 Customs officer may detain game trophies
A Customs officer may detain any goods that he or she has reasonable grounds to suspect is a game trophy whose exportation is prohibited by section 22.
The Customs officer may, in detaining the goods, use any reasonably necessary force.
A game trophy detained under subsection (1) cannot be seized or forfeited under subpart 9 of Part 3 of the Customs and Excise Act 2018 until the close of the 20th working day after the detention.
The chief executive of Customs may, at any time before the goods are forfeited, deliver the goods to the owner or other person from whom they were seized on production of proof of payment of prescribed game trophy levy.
Customs must transfer any condemned game trophy to the Council to be dealt with under section 25.
Section 189(2) of the Customs and Excise Act 2018 does not apply to game trophies.
25 Disposal of condemned game trophies
The Council may destroy, sell, or otherwise dispose of any game trophy transferred under section 24(5).
The Council must not sell or dispose of any game trophy to any member or employee of the Council or any of his or her immediate family members.
Any funds from any sale or other disposition of any game trophy form part of the Council's funds.
26 Customs to provide information about game trophies
The Council may make a request in writing to the chief executive of Customs to provide information Customs holds about the export of suspected game trophies in order to enable the Council to—
determine compliance with the requirement in section 22:
recover unpaid game trophy export levies.
The information that Customs may provide under subsection (1) is—
the full name and contact details of the exporter:
a description of the goods exported:
the stated value of the goods:
the date the goods were exported:
any other information that is reasonably necessary for the purposes specified in subsection (1).
The chief executive of Customs may, but need not, provide the information to the Council.
The Council may use the information obtained only for the purposes specified in subsection (1).
The Council must, within 20 working days after receiving it, destroy any information that it has obtained under this section that is not necessary for the purpose of recovering any game trophy export levy.
No request may be made to, or information provided by, Customs under this section unless there is a current memorandum of understanding between the Council and Customs that—
relates to the provision, storage, and destruction of information in a manner that is consistent with the Privacy Act 2020; and
contains requirements for reviewing the effectiveness of the information sharing.
The Council must provide details in its annual reports on the operation of information sharing under this section.
 Subpart 5 — Codes of practice for hunting
27 Compliance with codes of practice condition of concession, permit, or authorisation
Compliance with codes of practice made by regulations under this Act is a condition of every existing or new—
concession granted under section 22 of the Wild Animal Control Act 1977:
hunting permit issued under section 38 of the Conservation Act 1987:
authorisation to hunt given under—
this Act:
section 50 of the Reserves Act 1977:
section 8 of the Wild Animal Control Act 1977.
28 Code of practice for aerial hunting
The Council must, by 15 December 2014,—
develop an initial code of practice for aerially assisted trophy hunting that addresses the following:
hunting and shooting from helicopters; and
hazing of animals during aerially assisted trophy hunting; and
herding of animals for the purpose of aerially assisted trophy hunting; and
any other matter the Council considers appropriate; and
present a copy of the code to the Minister.
In this section, hazing, in relation to an animal, includes persecuting, harassing, molesting, or mistreating that animal.
 Part 2 — Miscellaneous provisions
 Subpart 1 — Offences and penalties
29 Export of game trophies
A person commits an offence if the person exports, or attempts to export, a game trophy without paying the prescribed game trophy levy.
A person who commits an offence against this section is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $5,000.
30 Hunting or killing animals in herd of special interest
A person commits an offence who hunts or kills any game animal that is part of a herd of special interest without—
an authorisation, a licence, or a permit issued for the purpose under this Act or any other Act; or
complying with the terms of any such authorisation, licence, or permit.
A person commits an offence who hunts or kills any game animal that is part of a herd of special interest without complying with a condition specified under section 18(1)﻿(d).
A person who commits an offence against subsection (1) is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $5,000.
A person who commits an offence against subsection (2) is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $2,500.
31 Capturing, conveying, or possessing game animal that is part of herd of special interest
A person commits an offence who, without the Minister's written authority, captures, conveys, or has in his or her possession any live game animal that is part of a herd of special interest.
A person who commits an offence against this section is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $5,000.
32 Restrictions on liberation of game animal that is part of herd of special interest
A person commits an offence who, without the Minister's written authority, liberates any game animal that is part of a herd of special interest or allows any game animal that is part of a herd of special interest to escape.
A person who commits an offence against this section is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $50,000.
33 Failing to produce authorisation, licence, or permit
A person commits an offence who—
is stopped by an enforcement officer or honorary enforcement officer who observes the person hunting or killing animals in a herd of special interest; and
fails, within 5 working days, to produce an authorisation, a licence, or a permit issued for the purpose under this Act or any other Act after being requested to do so by an enforcement officer or honorary enforcement officer.
A person who commits an offence against this section is liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $2,500.
 Subpart 2 — Other matters
34 Funding regulations
The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, on the recommendation of the Minister, for the purposes of funding services provided, powers exercised, or functions performed by the Council under this Act, make regulations prescribing any or all of the following fees or levies payable to the Council:
fees for hunting any animal that is part of a herd of special interest:
game trophy export levies.
Regulations made under subsection (1)﻿(b)—
may impose different rates of levies for different classes of game trophies; and
may exempt the payment of levies in respect of export to any country or countries; but
must be consistent with New Zealand’s international obligations.
Regulations under this section are secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).
35 Minister’s recommendation
Before making a recommendation under section 34, the Minister must—
determine, in consultation with the Minister of Finance, the amount that the Minister considers reasonable to raise by hunting fees, or game trophy levies (as the case may be), having regard to—
the Council's financial statements; and
the Council's forecast budget or budgets; and
any income the Council is likely to receive from sources other than levies; and
the likely impact of the fees or levies on the affected persons, goods, and industries; and
publish a notice of his or her intention to recommend a levy—
in the Gazette; and
in each of the daily newspapers published in Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, and Dunedin; and
make details of the proposed fee or levy available to any person on request; and
give interested parties a reasonable time, which must be specified in the notice, to make submissions on the proposal; and
consult the Council; and
consult any other persons, representative groups, government departments, and Crown entities that the Minister in each case considers appropriate.
36 Other regulations
The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, also make regulations for any or all of the following purposes:
prescribing forms for the purposes of this Act:
prescribing administrative fees and charges for the purposes of this Act:
prescribing codes of practice for hunting game animals for the purpose of section 27:
prescribing requirements for voluntary certification schemes for professional hunting guides and game estates:
providing for any matter that is reasonably contemplated by this Act as necessary—
for giving it full effect; or
for its administration.
Regulations under subsection (1)﻿(b) may prescribe that fees are to be payable to the Council.
Regulations may be made under subsection (1)﻿(c) only on the recommendation of the Minister, given after—
the Minister is satisfied that the code will be consistent with—
the purposes of the Wild Animal Control Act 1977; and
the overriding considerations; and
the Minister has had regard to—
the likely effects of the code on hunting generally or on any group of hunters or any hunting sector specifically; and
New Zealand's reputation as a hunting destination for international tourists.
Regulations under subsection (1)﻿(d) may, in relation to certified persons, prescribe—
minimum standards:
codes of conduct:
complaints and disciplinary processes.
Regulations under this section are secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).
37 Fees and levies payable to Council
If fees or levies are payable to the Council,—
the funds received form part of the Council's funds; and
any unpaid fee or levy is recoverable as a debt owed to the Council in a court of competent jurisdiction.
38 Enforcement officers and honorary enforcement officers
The provisions of Schedule 1 apply to enforcement officers and honorary enforcement officers.
39 Relationship of this Act with other enactments
This Act does not affect the exercise of the Minister's, or the Director-General's, powers under any other Act.
40 Review of operation of Act
The Minister must, not later than 3 years after the commencement of this section,—
review the operation of this Act; and
prepare a report on the review.
The report must include recommendations on whether any amendments to the Act are necessary or desirable.
As soon as practicable after receiving the report, the Minister must present a copy of that report to the House of Representatives.
41 Consequential amendments
The Public Finance Act 1989 is amended in the manner set out in Schedule 2.
The enactments specified in Schedule 3 are amended in the manner set out in that schedule.
