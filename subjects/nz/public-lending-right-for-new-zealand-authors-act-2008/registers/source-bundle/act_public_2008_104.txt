Public Lending Right for New Zealand Authors Act 2008
1 Title
This Act is the Public Lending Right for New Zealand Authors Act 2008.
2 Commencement
This Act comes into force on 1 January 2009.
 Part 1 — Preliminary provisions
3 Purposes
The purposes of this Act are—
to establish the public lending right for New Zealand authors scheme; and
to disestablish the New Zealand Authors' Fund.
4 Interpretation
In this Act, unless the context requires another meaning,—
advisory group means the advisory group established under section 17
author is defined in section 5
book is defined in section 6
chief executive means the chief executive of the department of State that administers this Act
Minister means the Minister of the Crown who is responsible for the administration of this Act—
under the authority of a warrant; or
under the authority of the Prime Minister
New Zealand author is defined in section 7
New Zealand library is defined in section 8
register means the register kept under section 13(1)
regulations means regulations under this Act
scheme means the public lending right for New Zealand authors scheme that this Act establishes.
5 Meaning of author
Author means a person who—
satisfies subsection (2); and
satisfies whichever of subsections (3) to (5) is applicable to him or her; and
is not excluded from being an author by any of subsections (6) to (8); and
satisfies conditions applicable to him or her added to this definition by regulations, if there are any such regulations.
Author means a natural person.
If a book's intellectual content is expressed only in text, author means a person who created the text.
If a book's intellectual content is expressed only in illustrations, author means a person who created the illustrations.
If a book's intellectual content is expressed in text and in illustrations, author means both a person who created the text and a person who created the illustrations.
Author does not include a person who writes or illustrates a book as part of his or her duties under a contract of service or a contract for services.
Author does not include a person who receives payment for writing or illustrating a book wholly by way of a fee.
Author does not include a person who paid a third party to publish the person's book.
6 Meaning of book
Book means a publication that—
is listed in the national bibliographic database of the National Library of New Zealand Te Puna Mātauranga o Aotearoa; and
is not excluded from being a book by any of subsections (2) to (5); and
satisfies conditions applicable to it added to this definition by regulations, if there are any such regulations.
Book does not include a serial publication.
Book does not include a text book intended for use in schools.
Book does not include charts, maps, plans, or tables.
Book does not include sheet music.
7 Meaning of New Zealand author
New Zealand author means an author who—
is a New Zealand resident as defined in section YD 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007; and
satisfies conditions applicable to him or her added to this definition by regulations, if there are any such regulations.
8 Meaning of New Zealand library
New Zealand library means a library in New Zealand that—
makes its books available for use in New Zealand; and
satisfies conditions applicable to it added to this definition by regulations, if there are any such regulations.
9 Act binds the Crown
This Act binds the Crown.
 Part 2 — Public lending right for New Zealand authors scheme
10 Establishment of scheme
This Act establishes the public lending right for New Zealand authors scheme.
11 Purpose of scheme
The purpose of the scheme is to provide for New Zealand authors to receive payments in recognition of the fact that their books are available for use in New Zealand libraries.
12 Payments under scheme
The chief executive must make annual payments under the scheme to New Zealand authors in accordance with regulations.
If a New Zealand author dies after entering his or her name in the register for the year, the chief executive must make the annual payment to which the author is entitled to the administrator of the author's estate.
13 Register for purposes of scheme
The chief executive must keep a register in accordance with regulations.
A person who is eligible under regulations for payments under the scheme, and who wants to receive payments under the scheme, must enter his or her name in the register annually in accordance with regulations.
14 Matters that may be dealt with in regulations
Regulations may deal with the matters described in this section.
Regulations may add conditions to the definitions of author, book, New Zealand author, and New Zealand library in sections 5 to 8.
Regulations may specify the eligibility criteria for both books and New Zealand authors that entitle authors to payments under the scheme.
Regulations may specify the kind of New Zealand library in which a New Zealand author's book must be available so as to entitle the author to payments under the scheme.
Regulations may specify how New Zealand libraries are to be surveyed or sampled to establish the availability in them of a New Zealand author's book.
Regulations may describe the method by which payments under the scheme are calculated.
Regulations may prescribe requirements for the keeping of the register.
Regulations may specify who may enter his or her name in the register and how he or she is to do it.
Regulations may describe the process that a person must follow to challenge a decision affecting him or her made in the course of the scheme's administration.
Regulations may provide for any other matters that are contemplated by this Act, necessary for its administration, or necessary to give it full effect.
15 Consultation on proposal for regulations
The chief executive must consult the advisory group on a proposal to make regulations.
The chief executive must—
give the advisory group notice of the proposal; and
give the advisory group an opportunity to give its advice; and
consider the advice.
The chief executive must advise the Minister of the results of the consultation.
The Minister must—
satisfy himself or herself that the chief executive has consulted as required by subsection (2); and
take the results of the consultation into account; and
decide whether or not to recommend the making of the regulations to the Governor-General.
16 Regulation-making power
The Governor-General may make regulations by Order in Council about one or more of the matters described in section 14.
The Governor-General may make the regulations only after being advised by the Minister to do so.
Regulations under this section are secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).
 Advisory group
17 Establishment of advisory group
The chief executive must appoint an advisory group for the scheme.
The advisory group is a statutory board for the purposes of the Fees and Travelling Allowances Act 1951.
The members of the advisory group may be paid, out of public money, remuneration by way of fees, salaries, or allowances, and travelling allowances and travelling expenses, under the Fees and Travelling Allowances Act 1951. The provisions of that Act apply accordingly.
18 Membership of advisory group
The members of the advisory group—
must be persons who have appropriate experience, knowledge, and skills, in the chief executive's opinion; and
must include—
at least 1 representative of organisations of authors:
at least 1 representative of organisations of librarians:
a representative of 1 or more relevant government departments; and
may include any other person to whom paragraph (a) applies.
The following provisions apply to the terms of appointment of members of the advisory group:
the chief executive must fix a term of appointment for each member; and
each term must be for a period of less than 5 years; and
each term may be renewed.
A member of the advisory group ceases to be a member if he or she—
dies; or
gives the chief executive written or electronic notice of his or her resignation as a member; or
is adjudged bankrupt under the Insolvency Act 2006; or
becomes a special patient as defined in section 2(1) of the Mental Health (Compulsory Assessment and Treatment) Act 1992; or
becomes the subject of an order under the Protection of Personal and Property Rights Act 1988; or
is convicted of an offence punishable by a term of imprisonment of 2 years or more.
The chief executive may appoint a member to replace a member who has ceased to be a member.
The chief executive must consult the Minister before appointing members to the advisory group.
19 Functions of advisory group
The functions of the advisory group are—
to advise the chief executive on a proposal to make regulations when consulted under section 15; and
to advise the chief executive on policy and administrative matters affecting the scheme when at a meeting called under section 20.
In addition to the occasions described in subsection (1), the chief executive may ask for the advisory group's advice at any time by any written or electronic means.
20 Meetings of advisory group
The chief executive must call a meeting of the advisory group at least once every 3 years.
21 New Zealand Authors' Fund
When this Act commences,—
section 31 of the Arts Council of New Zealand Toi Aotearoa Act 1994 is repealed; and
the New Zealand Authors' Fund established by section 31 of the Arts Council of New Zealand Toi Aotearoa Act 1994 is dissolved; and
assets and liabilities of the New Zealand Authors' Fund become assets and liabilities of the scheme.
