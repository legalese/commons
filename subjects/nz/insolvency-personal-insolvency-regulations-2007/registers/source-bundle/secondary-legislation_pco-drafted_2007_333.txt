Insolvency (Personal Insolvency) Regulations 2007
Regulations
1 Title
These regulations are the Insolvency (Personal Insolvency) Regulations 2007.
2 Commencement
These regulations come into force on 3 December 2007.
3 Interpretation
In these regulations, unless the context otherwise requires,—
Act means the Insolvency Act 2006
New Zealand Business Number means the number allocated to an entity under the New Zealand Business Number Act 2016.
In these regulations, unless otherwise stated, a reference to a section is a reference to a section of the Act.

 Part 2 — Bankruptcy
6 Debtor's statement of affairs
For the purposes of section 46(1), a statement of affairs must be—
signed by the debtor; and
dated.
The statement of affairs must contain the following information:
the debtor's full legal name:
the debtor's current address, telephone number, and any other contact detail (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
any previous address of the debtor at which the debtor has resided in the previous 5 years, but, if there are more than 2, only the 2 most recent:
any other name used by the debtor in the previous 7 years, including any alias but excluding any trading name:
any trading name used by the debtor in the previous 5 years (solely or with other persons):
the debtor's date of birth:
whether the debtor is male or female:
the debtor’s New Zealand Business Number (if any):
the debtor's tax file number (IRD number):
if the debtor has a current passport, the nationality and number of the passport:
if the debtor is employed—
the employer's name and address:
the debtor's occupation:
the following details for each account held by the debtor (solely or with other persons) with any bank in the previous 5 years:
the account name:
the account number:
the bank and bank branch:
the name of each signatory:
a statement of the debtor's income:
an itemised statement of the debtor's expenses:
if the debtor has included his or her partner's expenses under paragraph (j), a statement of the partner's income:
a statement of the debtor's current assets, including the description, value, and location of those assets:
a statement of any assets that the debtor has disposed of in the previous 5 years:
a statement of the debtor's liabilities, including any contingent liabilities, with the following details for each liability:
the amount:
each creditor’s name, address, telephone number, and any other contact detail (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
if it is a joint liability, the following:
a statement that it is a joint liability:
each other debtor’s name, address, telephone number, and any other contact detail (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
how it was incurred:
whether it is secured:
if it is secured, a description of the security:
a statement of all financial transactions by the debtor during the previous 3 years:
the following details for any person who, as a lawyer or an accountant, provided services to the debtor in the previous 5 years:
the person’s name, address, telephone number, and any other contact detail (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
the name of the body corporate, partnership, or other organisation (if any) through which the person provided the services (whether as an employee or otherwise):
the following details for any person not covered by paragraph (p) who, as a professional adviser, provided services to the debtor in the previous 5 years in respect of the debtor’s financial affairs (including the valuation of any of the debtor’s assets or liabilities):
the person’s name, address, telephone number, and any other contact detail (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
the name of the body corporate, partnership, or other organisation (if any) through which the person provided the services (whether as an employee or otherwise):
a statement of the debtor’s involvement with any trust in the previous 5 years as a settlor, trustee, or beneficiary, including details of the following:
any money or other assets paid or transferred by the debtor to the trustee, or by the trustee to the debtor, in the previous 5 years:
any liability currently owed to the debtor by the trustee:
a statement of any agreement entered into by the debtor in the previous 5 years under section 21, 21A, or 21B of the Property (Relationships) Act 1976, including the following:
the name of the other party to the agreement:
details of the assets and liabilities dealt with by the agreement:
a statement of any lump sum contributions made by the debtor in the previous 5 years to a retirement scheme.
In subclause (2), references to the previous 3, 5, or 7 years are to the period of 3, 5, or 7 years that ends with—
the date of the statement of affairs; or
the date of adjudication, if this regulation is being applied in accordance with regulation 8.
In subclause (2)﻿(ha), bank means—
a registered bank, or an overseas bank, as defined in section 2(1) of the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989; or
a building society as defined in section 2(1) of the Building Societies Act 1965 or a body corporate or other organisation of the same, or substantially the same, nature operating outside New Zealand; or
a credit union as defined in section 2 of the Friendly Societies and Credit Unions Act 1982 or a body corporate or other organisation of the same, or substantially the same, nature operating outside New Zealand.
In subclause (2)﻿(p), lawyer includes a person practising law outside New Zealand (in addition to a person covered by the definition of lawyer in section 3).
In subclause (2)﻿(t), retirement scheme—
means a retirement scheme within the meaning of section 6(1) of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013; and
includes a scheme registered before 1 December 2014 in the KiwiSaver schemes register under the KiwiSaver Act 2006 or a scheme registered before that date under the Superannuation Schemes Act 1989.

10 Bankrupt's application for Assignee's consent to enter into business
A bankrupt's application to the Assignee for consent under section 149(1) to enter into a business or employment must—
be in writing; and
be signed by the bankrupt or the bankrupt's authorised representative; and
be accompanied by an affidavit by the bankrupt verifying the information required under paragraph (d); and
contain the following information:
the bankrupt's full name:
the bankrupt's current address, telephone number, and any other contact detail (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
the reasons for the application:
whether the business is a new business:

any other information that the Assignee may require.
In deciding the application, the Assignee must have regard to the interests of the bankrupt, the bankrupt's creditors, and the community, but may otherwise decide it in his or her absolute discretion.
The Assignee may—
refuse the application; or
grant it without conditions; or
grant it subject to any conditions that the Assignee thinks fit.

A bankrupt's application to the Assignee for consent to leave New Zealand must—
be in writing; and
be signed by the bankrupt; and
contain the following information:
the bankrupt's full name:
the bankrupt's current address, telephone number, and any other contact detail (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
the intended date of departure:
whether the departure is temporary or permanent:
if the departure is temporary, the intended date of return:
the reasons for departure:
the bankrupt's address while overseas:
any other information that the Assignee may require.
In deciding the application, the Assignee must have regard to the interests of the bankrupt and the bankrupt's creditors, but may otherwise decide it in his or her absolute discretion.
The Assignee may—
refuse the application; or
grant it without conditions; or
grant it subject to any conditions that the Assignee thinks fit.

 Examinations
13 Expenses of attending examination
A person who is summoned for examination by the Assignee is entitled to be paid an allowance and travelling expenses as if that person were a witness for the purposes of the Witnesses and Interpreters Fees, Allowances, and Expenses Regulations 2023.
A bankrupt who is required to attend a public examination is entitled to be paid travelling expenses as if the bankrupt were a witness for the purposes of the Witnesses and Interpreters Fees, Allowances, and Expenses Regulations 2023.
 Assignee
14 Assignee's records and accounts
The Assignee must keep accounting records in the following form:
an estate ledger:
a cash book:
a register of unrealised assets.
The estate ledger must be balanced with the cash book at the end of each calendar month.
Subject to any directions of the Auditor-General as to trading accounts, all receipts and payments made by the Assignee must be entered in the cash book.
15 Assignee's bank account
The Assignee must pay all money received by him or her into a bank account to his or her own credit as Assignee.
16 Publication and advertisement of Assignee's final statement of receipts and payments
17 Assignee not required to incur expense if bankrupt has no available assets
The Assignee is not required to incur any expense in relation to the estate of a bankrupt who has no available assets.
However, the Assignee may incur expense in that case (but cannot be required to do so) if the Assignee has obtained a guarantee from the creditors or some of them.
18 Assignee's remuneration
Except when subclause (2) applies, the rates of remuneration for the Assignee and his or her staff are the following (in each case, exclusive of goods and services tax):
the Assignee and Deputy Assignee: $230 per hour:
legal and accounting staff: $230 per hour:
insolvency officers: $160 per hour.
When the Assignee carries out his or her duties, and exercises his or her powers, under subpart 3 of Part 5 of the Act, the Assignee's rate of remuneration is 2.5% of the value of the assets of the debtor recovered by the supervisor.
 Discharge
19 Time for creditor's notice of opposition to discharge on ground not specified in Assignee's report
For the purposes of section 297(1), a creditor must give notice not less than 5 working days before the application is to be heard.
 Part 3 — Compositions
20 Dispute as to claim under composition
This regulation applies if, under a composition, provision is made for the payment of any money to the creditors entitled to it, and a claim for which a creditor's claim form has been lodged is disputed.
The court may, if it thinks fit, order that the money that would be paid if the claim were established be secured as the court directs while the claim is determined, and on determination of the claim the money so secured must be paid as the court orders.
21 Creditor's claim after approval of composition
A person claiming to be a creditor under a composition who has not proved his or her debt before the approval of the composition must lodge a creditor's claim form with the trustee.
A creditor's claim form must comply with regulation 12.
A creditor cannot enforce payment of any sum or part of a sum payable under a composition unless the creditor has lodged a creditor's claim form with the trustee and the trustee has admitted the claim.
 Part 4 — Proposals
22 Resolution accepting proposal
The resolution of the creditors at a meeting accepting the proposal must be in the form of a statement that contains the following information:
the name of the insolvent; and
the date of the proposal; and
a statement that the meeting of creditors resolved that the insolvent's proposal annexed to the resolution be accepted; and
a copy of the proposal as an annexure; and
a record of any modifications to the proposal (if any) agreed at the meeting; and
the name of the person confirmed or appointed as trustee; and
the signature of the chairperson of the meeting of creditors; and
the number of creditors voting in favour of the resolution and the total amount of their proven claims; and
the number of creditors voting against the resolution and the total amount of their proven claims.
23 Notice of hearing of application for approval of proposal
A notice under section 333(1)﻿(b) must be in form 2 of the Schedule.
 Meetings
24 Chairperson
The chairperson of the meeting is the person elected by the creditors to be the chairperson.
25 Chairperson may adjourn meeting
The chairperson may adjourn the meeting from time to time and place to place.
26 Quorum at meetings
Two creditors or their representatives present at the meeting constitute a quorum.
A creditor is present at a meeting for the purposes of subclause (1) if the creditor is a person who has voted by postal vote in accordance with section 330(2).
If no quorum is present at the meeting, the proposal is deemed to be not accepted by the creditors.
27 Insolvent must attend meetings
The insolvent must attend every meeting of the creditors under subpart 2 of Part 5 of the Act.
However, subclause (1) does not apply to the extent that the insolvent is prevented from attending a meeting by illness or some other sufficient cause.
The insolvent must answer to the best of his or her knowledge and ability every question put to him or her by the chairperson or any creditor in respect of the insolvent's conduct, dealings, property, and affairs.
The failure of an insolvent to attend a meeting does not affect the validity of any resolution passed at the meeting.
28 Attendance by non-creditors
A person who is not a creditor may attend a meeting of creditors with the approval of the majority of the creditors.
29 Trustee must report to meeting
If the trustee attends a creditor's meeting or an adjournment of the meeting, the trustee must—
report on the conduct of the proposal to date; and
give any creditor any further information that the creditor may properly require; and
if reasonably required, produce for the meeting (or its adjournment) all accounting records, deeds, and papers in the trustee's possession that relate to the insolvent's property.
30 Minutes and record of meetings
The chairperson must ensure that minutes are kept of each meeting, and must sign the minutes.
The chairperson may record the meeting, but only with the consent of each creditor attending.
31 Who may vote at creditors' meeting
A creditor whose claim has been admitted may vote at creditors' meetings.
32 Admission or rejection of claims for purposes of voting
The provisional trustee has the power to admit or reject a claim for the purposes of voting at a creditors' meeting, but his or her decision is subject to appeal to the court.
If the provisional trustee is uncertain whether a claim may be admitted or rejected, he or she must allow the creditor to vote subject to that vote being declared invalid in the event of the claim being rejected for purposes of voting.
33 Voting by preferential creditors
This regulation applies when it is necessary to compute the requisite majority of creditors for passing a resolution accepting a proposal that contains an offer to compromise at less than 100 cents in the dollar.
The preferential creditors must not be counted either in number or value except as to any part of their claim that is not preferential.
34 Form of postal vote
For the purposes of section 330(1)﻿(e), the prescribed form of postal vote is form 3 of the Schedule.
 Creditors' claims
35 Creditor must submit claim form
A creditor's claim form for a claim under a proposal must comply with regulation 12.
A creditor may amend a claim form if the trustee agrees.
A creditor who has submitted a claim form may, at any reasonable time, inspect the claim form of any other creditor.
36 Trustee must notify creditor whether claim accepted or rejected
The trustee must notify in writing each creditor who has submitted a claim form whether the claim has been accepted or rejected, with reasons in writing if the claim has been rejected.
 Trustee
37 Summary of receipts and payments
A summary of receipts and payments under section 338(3)﻿(a) must be in form 4 of the Schedule.
The Registrar may direct that the summary, once filed, must be audited by a chartered accountant.
38 Audit of trustee's accounts and records
The Registrar must obtain an audit by a chartered accountant of the trustee's accounts and records relating to the insolvent if the Registrar considers that an audit is desirable.
The Registrar may act on his own initiative or on the application for an audit by a creditor.
39 Trustee must produce accounts, etc, for purposes of audit
For the purposes of an audit under regulation 37(3) or 38, the trustee must produce to the auditor the trustee's accounts, records, documents, and other information relating to the insolvent as and when the auditor requires them.
The cost of an audit under regulation 37(3) or 38 must be paid out of the money available under the proposal.
40 Trustee's fee
The trustee is entitled to a fee, in addition to payment of the trustee's reasonable expenses, for the work of the trustee after acceptance of the proposal by the creditors.
The fee must be paid at the following rates on the net value of the property realised by the trustee after deducting any sums paid to secured creditors out of the proceeds or in respect of their securities:
20% of the first $3,000 or part of it, with a minimum of $200:
10% of the next $7,000 or part of it:
5% of any amount in excess of $10,000.
41 Appointment of new trustee by creditors
The creditors may appoint a new trustee to replace a trustee who has died, or is incapable of acting, or wishes to be replaced.
The appointment must be made by a resolution of the creditors at a meeting of the creditors convened to appoint a new trustee.
A meeting to appoint a new trustee may be called by the existing trustee (if capable) or any creditor.
42 Replacement of trustee by court
The court may, on the application of the insolvent or any creditor, remove a trustee and appoint another trustee in his or her place.
43 Effect of appointment of new trustee
Any property vested in the first trustee appointed, whether vested under the proposal or under any deed entered into in terms of the proposal, vests in a trustee appointed under regulation 41 or 42 without the necessity of any conveyance, assignment, or transfer.
A trustee appointed under regulation 41 or 42 must complete all matters and transactions left uncompleted by the previous trustee.
Any payment made, any thing done, any transaction entered into, and any liability incurred by a trustee in good faith and in exercise of his or her powers is binding on every subsequent trustee.
 Part 5 — Debt repayment orders
44 Application for debt repayment order
An application for a debt repayment order under section 341 must be—
signed by the person making the application; and
dated.
If the application is made by the debtor, the application must state whichever of the following applies:
that the debtor proposes to pay the creditors in full; or
the amount in the dollar that the debtor proposes to pay.
In all cases, whether the application is made by the debtor or a creditor, the application—
must—
state the name and address of the debtor's proposed supervisor and annex the written consent of that person to be supervisor; or
if the debtor considers that a supervisor is not necessary, state the debtor's reasons; and
must be accompanied by a statement of affairs.
In all cases, whether the application is made by the debtor or a creditor, the application must contain the following information:
the debtor's full name:
the debtor's current address, telephone number, and any other contact details (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
the debtor's occupation:
the debtor's date of birth:
the debtor’s New Zealand Business Number (if any):
a detailed statement of the debtor's property:
each creditor's current address, telephone number, and any other contact detail (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
the amount and nature of each of the creditors' debts:
whether any of the debts are secured and the value of the security:
whether any of the debts are guaranteed by any person:
a statement of the debtor's earnings:
if the debtor is employed, the employer's name, current address, telephone number, and any other contact detail (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address).
The fee that is payable for making the application is $100 inclusive of goods and services tax.
45 Notice of application for order
The applicant for a debt repayment order must, as soon as practicable, notify the following persons that an application for a debt repayment order has been made:
the debtor, unless the debtor is the applicant; and
each known creditor.
The notice must—
state—
the name of the applicant; and
the name of the debtor; and
the amount proposed to be paid under the order; and
the name of the proposed supervisor; and
state that the debtor and the creditors have the right to make representations opposing or supporting the order to the Assignee in writing within 10 working days after the date of the notice.
46 Time for debtor or creditor to make representations to Assignee
For the purposes of section 343(2), the time for the debtor or a creditor to make representations to the Assignee is 10 working days after the date of the notice under regulation 45.
47 Time for sending notice of debt repayment order
For the purposes of section 353, the supervisor must send the notice of the debt repayment order within 15 working days of the order being made.
 Creditors' claims
48 Creditor must submit claim form
A creditor who wishes to make a claim must submit a claim form to the supervisor within 15 working days after receiving notice that the debt repayment order has been made.
The claim form must comply with regulation 12.
A creditor may amend a claim form if the supervisor agrees.
A creditor whose claim has been admitted may, at any reasonable time, inspect,—
if the debtor applied for the debt repayment order, the debtor's application; and
the debtor's statement of affairs; and
the claim form of any other creditor.
49 Late claim in respect of debt incurred before debt repayment order
This regulation applies if, after a debt repayment order has been made, a person (A) claims that the debtor has incurred a debt before the order was made.
The supervisor may accept A's late claim for the debt and in that event the debt is included in the administration of the debtor's estate.
If the claim is not accepted, A may apply to the Assignee under regulation 53.
A creditor whose claim is admitted late under this regulation is entitled to be paid a dividend from the date of the order, but this regulation must not affect the distribution of any dividend declared before the date on which the claim is admitted.
50 Claim in respect of debt incurred after debt repayment order made
This regulation applies if, after a debt repayment order has been made, a person (A) becomes a creditor of the debtor.
A may submit a claim form to the supervisor in respect of the debt and the supervisor may accept the claim.
If the supervisor accepts the claim, A may be included as a creditor in the administration of the debtor's estate, but must not be paid a dividend under the order until all creditors included in the administration as creditors before the making of the order have been paid.
If the claim is not accepted, A may apply to the Assignee under regulation 53.
51 Variation of order as result of admission of claim under regulation 49 or 50
The supervisor, or the debtor if no supervisor has been appointed, may apply to the Assignee for a variation of the order if the admission of a claim under regulation 49 or 50 would substantially affect the performance of the order.
52 Supervisor must notify creditors as to acceptance or rejection of claim
The supervisor must as soon as practicable notify each creditor in writing whether that person's claim has been accepted or rejected.
If the supervisor rejects a creditor's claim, whether in whole or in part, the supervisor must also, in the same notice,—
give that person the reasons for rejecting it; and
advise that person of the right to apply to the Assignee to modify or reverse the supervisor's decision concerning the claim.
53 Assignee may modify or reverse supervisor's decision to reject creditor's claim
A creditor whose claim has been rejected by the supervisor may apply to the Assignee for the Assignee to modify or reverse the supervisor's decision.
The creditor must make the application within 15 working days after the date of a notice given under regulation 52.
The Assignee must, as soon as practicable, notify the supervisor, the debtor, and the creditor that—
the application has been received by the Assignee; and
each of the supervisor, the debtor, and the creditor has the right to make submissions to the Assignee concerning the claim within 15 working days after the date of the notice that the application has been made.
The Assignee may, in his or her absolute discretion after considering any submissions made in accordance with subclause (3)﻿(b), modify or reverse the supervisor's decision.
54 Advice to creditors of dividend
The supervisor must ensure that remittances of dividends to creditors are accompanied by an advice note identifying the payment and the period to which it relates.
55 Notice to employer to pay debtor's earnings to supervisor
A direction by a supervisor to the debtor's employer to pay all or part of the debtor's earnings to the supervisor must—
be in writing; and
state the date on which payment to the supervisor must begin; and
be served on the employer personally or posted to the employer's place of business.
56 Notice of default under order
The supervisor must as soon as practicable notify in writing the Assignee and each creditor of a default by the debtor under a debt repayment order.
57 Variation or discharge of debt repayment order
An application under section 350 to vary or discharge a debt repayment order must—
be in writing; and
state the reasons why the order should be varied or discharged.
The applicant must send a copy of the application to every person who will be adversely affected by the variation or discharge.
58 Notice by supervisor of variation or discharge of order
The supervisor must, as soon as practicable, give the debtor and each creditor written notice of any variation, or the discharge, of the order.
 Supervisor
59 Supervisor's obligations
The supervisor must perform diligently and competently the duties of a supervisor set out in the Act, these regulations, and the terms of the order appointing the supervisor.
60 Supervisor and debtor must render accounts
The Assignee may require the supervisor or, if no supervisor has been appointed, the debtor to file 6-monthly accounts.
The first 6-month period begins on the date of the order.
The accounts must—
be filed not later than 20 working days after the end of each 6-month period; and
record the receipts and payments under the order during that period; and
be accompanied by the relevant bank statements.
The Assignee may inspect the accounts filed under this regulation and may appoint an auditor to audit the accounts.
For the purposes of an audit, the supervisor or debtor must, as and when required, produce to an auditor appointed under subclause (4) all accounts and relevant documents relating to the administration of the debtor's affairs.
61 Supervisor must provide Assignee with statement of receipts and payments after discharge of order
Within 20 working days after the discharge of the debt repayment order, the supervisor must provide the Assignee with a statement of receipts and payments.
This regulation does not apply if the supervisor has used the Assignee's trust account for receipts and payments under the order.
62 Money received by supervisor
For the purposes of section 351, instalments paid to the supervisor under a debt repayment order must be paid into—
the Assignee's trust account; or
a bank account in the names of the debtor, the supervisor, and any other person that the supervisor thinks appropriate.
All payments out of the trust account or bank account must be by cheque or electronic funds transfer.
If the supervisor elects not to use the Assignee's trust account for payments to the supervisor, the supervisor must personally bear the cost of the fees and other expenses incurred in maintaining the bank account that is used for the purpose.
63 Payments to creditors
Unless the order provides otherwise, payments to creditors must be made as follows while the debt repayment order is operative:
every 4 months, if the order is made before 24 March 2016:
every 6 months, in any other case.
However, if a dividend (other than a final dividend) is less than $20, the supervisor may hold that and subsequent dividends over until the accumulated dividend to be paid exceeds $20.
Despite anything in these regulations, the supervisor may, if he or she thinks fit, liquidate a debt if the total of the debt payable to the creditor under the order is less than $200, in preference to other admitted debts of greater amount.
64 Supervisor's remuneration
For the purposes of section 346(2), the amount that the supervisor may charge the debtor is 7.5% of the value of the assets of the debtor that are recovered by the supervisor.
 Part 6 — No asset procedure
65 Application for entry to no asset procedure
An application for entry to the no asset procedure under section 362(1) must—
be addressed to the Assignee; and
state that the debtor applies for entry to the no asset procedure; and
be signed by the debtor; and
be dated.
The application must contain the following information:
the debtor's full name:
the debtor's current address, telephone number, and any other contact details (such as a mobile telephone number or an email address):
the debtor's occupation:
the debtor's date of birth:
the debtor’s New Zealand Business Number (if any).
The application must be accompanied by a statement of affairs that complies with regulation 6.
66 Means test for entry to no asset procedure
The prescribed means test for the purposes of section 363(1)﻿(e) is whether, taking into account the income of the debtor personally and that of any relative with whom the debtor lives, the debtor has a surplus of money after paying the household's usual and reasonable living expenses.
67 Assignee's notice to debtor of admission to no asset procedure
A notice under section 367(1) must—
be signed by the Assignee; and
state that the debtor has been admitted to the no asset procedure; and
be dated; and
contain the following information:
the debtor's full name and address:
the debtor's date of birth:
the date of admission to the no asset procedure:
a summary of the debtor's obligations under the provisions of the Act relating to the no asset procedure.
68 Assignee's notice to debtor of termination of debtor's participation in no asset procedure
A notice of termination under section 373(2) must—
be signed by the Assignee; and
state that the debtor's participation in the no asset procedure has been terminated; and
be dated; and
contain the following information:
the debtor's full name and address:

 Part 7 — Miscellaneous
69 Additional reasons for refusing access to public register or suspending operation of public register
The Assignee may refuse access to a public register maintained under section 62, 354, or 368, or suspend the operation of it, for any of the following additional reasons:
for the maintenance of the register:
in response to technical difficulties in the maintenance or operation of the register:
to ensure the security or integrity of the register.
70 Additional information in public registers
For the purpose of section 449(1)﻿(o), a public register maintained under section 62, 354, or 368 must contain the following information in relation to a person who is or has been bankrupt, or who is subject to a current summary instalment order, or who is currently admitted to the no asset procedure, or who has been discharged from that procedure under section 377:
the year and month of birth of the person; and
the person’s New Zealand Business Number (if any).
70A Search criteria for public registers
For the purpose of section 453(1)﻿(g), the public registers may be searched by reference to the New Zealand Business Number (if any) of any of the following:
a person who is or has been bankrupt:
a person who is subject to a current summary instalment order:
a person who is currently admitted to the no asset procedure:
a person who has been discharged from the no asset procedure under section 377.
71 How Assignee must advertise document, proceeding, or other step
When, under the Act or these regulations, the Assignee must advertise a document, proceeding, or other step to be taken, the Assignee must advertise it by publishing it—
once in the Gazette; and
on the Insolvency and Trustee Service website maintained by the Ministry for 4 years after the date of discharge.
An advertisement by the Assignee may advertise a document, proceeding, or other step in relation to more than 1 bankruptcy or other administration of an estate.
72 How document or notice must be filed or lodged with, or submitted to, the Assignee
This regulation applies when a person (A) must file, lodge, or submit a document or notice with or to the Assignee.
In the case of an application under section 49 (debtor's application for automatic adjudication), 341 (application for a debt repayment order), or 362 (application for admission to no asset procedure), A must either—
complete the application online at the website of the Insolvency and Trustee Service, except that A must also sign and return the printed version of the application generated by the Insolvency and Trustee Service website; or
print off a blank application form from the Insolvency and Trustee Service website, and take or post that form after completion to the Assignee.
In all other cases, A must take, fax, or post the document or notice to the Assignee.
73 Service of document or notices by Assignee
A notice or document that must, under the Act or these regulations, be served on or sent to any person by the Assignee, and is not by the Act or these regulations directed to be served personally, may be sent by ordinary letter post addressed to the last known place of business or abode of the person.
