Infrastructure Funding and Financing Act 2020
1 Title
This Act is the Infrastructure Funding and Financing Act 2020.
2 Commencement
This Act comes into force on the day after the date on which it receives the Royal assent.
 Part 1 — Preliminary provisions
3 Purpose
The purpose of this Act is to provide a funding and financing model for the provision of infrastructure for urban development, that—
supports community needs; and
appropriately allocates the costs of infrastructure.
In this section, urban development includes—
development of housing, including public housing and community housing, affordable housing, homes for first-home buyers, and market housing:
development and renewal of urban environments, whether or not this includes housing development:
development of related commercial, industrial, community, or other amenities, infrastructure, facilities, services, or works.
4 Outline of this Act
This Part contains preliminary provisions.
Part 2 describes how a levy to fund eligible costs relating to eligible infrastructure is authorised.
Part 3 describes how and by whom a levy is administered when the RLA is a territorial authority.
Part 3A describes how and by whom a levy is administered when the RLA is a water organisation.
Part 4 provides for operational and financial controls and powers.
Part 5 provides for monitoring, enforcement, remedies, and some miscellaneous matters.
5 Transitional, savings, and related provisions
The transitional, savings, and related provisions (if any) set out in Schedule 1 have effect according to their terms.
6 Act binds the Crown
This Act binds the Crown.

8 Meaning of eligible infrastructure
In this Act, eligible infrastructure means infrastructure that is—
of the type set out in subsection (2); and
described in a levy order as infrastructure in relation to which a levy is authorised.
The type of infrastructure for which a levy may be authorised is new or upgraded—
water services infrastructure; or
transport infrastructure; or
community infrastructure or community facilities; or
environmental resilience infrastructure.
In this section,—
community facilities has the same meaning as in section 197(2) of the Local Government Act 2002
community infrastructure has the same meaning as in section 197(2) of the Local Government Act 2002, except for the following:
the land is not required to be owned or controlled, or to be acquired, by the territorial authority:
the development assets on the land are not required to be owned or controlled by the territorial authority
environmental resilience infrastructure means infrastructure for—
managing risks from natural hazards, including by avoiding or mitigating those hazards and reducing those risks; and
environmental restoration generally
natural hazard has the same meaning as in section 2(1) of the Resource Management Act 1991
transport infrastructure means—
infrastructure that facilitates transport on land by any means; or
infrastructure that facilitates coastal shipping (including transport by means of harbour ferries, or ferries or barges on rivers or lakes).
9 Meaning of eligible costs
In this Act, eligible costs means costs that are—
of a type set out in subsection (2); and
described in a levy order as the costs that may be met by the levy.
The types of costs for which a levy may be authorised are—
the costs of the construction of eligible infrastructure that are set out in subsection (3); and
the costs of financing the construction of eligible infrastructure that are set out in subsection (4); and
the costs incurred in operating and maintaining eligible infrastructure after it is constructed (to the extent that those costs are not referred to in subsection (3)﻿(c)); and
the costs of the administration of a levy that are set out in subsection (5); and
any further costs incurred by a responsible SPV in complying with this Act and the levy order; and
the general operating costs of a responsible SPV.
The costs of the construction of eligible infrastructure are—
establishment costs; and
construction costs; and
costs incurred in operating, repairing, and maintaining eligible infrastructure after it is constructed and—
before it is vested (to the extent that it will vest in a responsible infrastructure authority); or
before it is transferred to another person (to the extent that it is transferred).
The financing costs are—
the costs of meeting a responsible SPV’s commitments under any loan or obligations under any incidental arrangement; and
the costs of refinancing; and
a return on the capital provided by holders of debt or equity.
The levy administration costs are—
costs incurred by an RLA in assessing and collecting a levy; and
costs incurred by an RLA or a responsible SPV in recovering unpaid levy; and
costs incurred by the recommender, the monitor, or a Crown Manager in performing their functions or duties or exercising their powers under this Act, if a responsible SPV is liable to fund or reimburse those costs.
In this section,—
construction costs includes—
direct construction costs, insurance relating to construction, and project contingency costs incurred by a responsible SPV; and
construction costs incurred by any other person in relation to any matters set out in paragraph (a), if the responsible SPV is contractually liable to fund or reimburse that person’s construction costs
establishment costs includes—
costs incurred in the establishment of a responsible SPV; and
costs incurred in relation to the acquisition and preparation of land on which eligible infrastructure is to be constructed, including the costs of purchasing or otherwise acquiring land and the costs of procuring designations under the Resource Management Act 1991; and
costs incurred by a responsible SPV in relation to planning and arranging for the commencement of construction work and in relation to any levy proposal; and
costs incurred by any other person (including levy proposal costs) in relation to any matters set out in paragraphs (a) to (c), if the responsible SPV is contractually liable to fund or reimburse that person’s costs
general operating costs means general management and administration costs associated with the construction of eligible infrastructure, the financing of that construction, the administration of a levy, or any support or service received from the Crown
incidental arrangement and loan have the same meanings as in section 112 of the Local Government Act 2002, except that each reference to a local authority must be read as a reference to the responsible SPV that is party to the loan or arrangement
levy proposal costs means costs incurred by—
the proposer of a levy that is authorised by a levy order; or
the recommender or any other person acting on behalf of the Crown in relation to any levy proposal.
9A Meaning of responsible infrastructure authority
A responsible infrastructure authority, in relation to eligible infrastructure, means an agency—
in which the eligible infrastructure is, or is proposed to be, vested (to the extent of the vesting); or
that owns, or is proposed to own, the eligible infrastructure (if the eligible infrastructure is to be both constructed and owned by the agency without any vesting agreement).
In subsection (1), an agency means—
the RLA:
if the RLA is a territorial authority, a council-controlled organisation of the RLA:
a territorial authority:
a water organisation:
the relevant regional council:
a government agency:
a State enterprise.
9B Responsible levy authority that is water organisation
This section applies if a levy is authorised only for water services infrastructure that is, or will be, used to provide water services for which a water organisation is responsible through a transfer agreement as set out in section 12 or 15 of the Local Government (Water Services) Act 2025.
The responsible levy authority or RLA, in relation to the levy area, is—
the water organisation for the service area in which the levy area is located; or
if the levy area is located in more than 1 service area, each water organisation in whose service area any part of the levy area is located.
For the purposes of subsection (2)﻿(b), each RLA’s responsibility under this Act relates only to the part of the levy area that is in the water organisation’s service area.
This section does not apply to an Auckland water organisation.
In this section, service area, in relation to a water organisation, means the area specified in the transfer agreement between that organisation and a territorial authority.
9C Responsible levy authority that is water organisation: Auckland water organisation
This section applies if a levy is authorised only for—
infrastructure that is, or will be, used to provide water supply or wastewater services (as defined in section 4(1) of the Local Government (Auckland Council) Act 2009) for which an Auckland water organisation is responsible; or
water services infrastructure that is, or will be, used to provide water services for which an Auckland water organisation is responsible through a transfer agreement as set out in section 12 or 15 of the Local Government (Water Services) Act 2025.
The responsible levy authority or RLA, in relation to the levy area, is the Auckland water organisation.
9D Responsible levy authority that is territorial authority
This section applies if,—
despite section 9B or 9C, a levy is authorised only for infrastructure described in either of those sections but the territorial authority and the water organisation have agreed that the territorial authority will be the responsible levy authority; or
a levy is authorised in a circumstance other than that described in section 9B or 9C.
Example 1
A levy is authorised for both water services infrastructure and another type of infrastructure.
Example 2
A levy is authorised only for infrastructure that is not water services infrastructure.
Example 3
A levy is authorised only for water services infrastructure, but a territorial authority is responsible for the water services provided by that infrastructure.
The responsible levy authority or RLA, in relation to the levy area, is—
the territorial authority for the district in which the levy area is located; or
if the levy area is located in more than 1 district, each territorial authority in whose district any part of the levy area is located.
For the purposes of subsection (2)﻿(b), each RLA’s responsibility under this Act relates only to the part of the levy area that is in the territorial authority’s district.
10 What is an SPV
A company, limited partnership, Crown entity, or other person may be a special purpose vehicle (an SPV) under this Act only if its governing documents state that—
it is established and operates for the sole purpose of acting as a responsible SPV under this Act; and
the governing documents cannot be amended without the consent of the monitor.
An SPV may be established and operate only in relation to 1 levy order.

as if any reference to—

 Part 2 — Authorisation of levy
16 Outline of this Part
This Part provides for a levy to be authorised to fund eligible costs relating to eligible infrastructure.
Subpart 1 describes how a proposal to have a levy authorised is considered and recommended.
Subpart 2 describes the Order in Council that authorises the use of a levy in a levy area.
Subpart 3 describes the basic features of levy liability and how the levy is set for each levy year.
 Subpart 1 — Consideration of levy proposal
 Levy proposal
17 Who may propose levy
Any person may propose that a levy be authorised to fund eligible costs relating to eligible infrastructure.
The proposer must give the proposal to the recommender.
18 Content of levy proposal
A levy proposal for the authorisation of a levy must include the following information:
how the proposed levy supports community needs and appropriately allocates the costs of eligible infrastructure, including consideration of—
the extent of expected benefits outside the levy area compared with expected benefits within the levy area; and
the distribution of expected benefits in the levy area as a whole or any identifiable part of the levy area, and to persons in the levy area; and
the extent to which the actions or inaction of particular persons or a group contribute to the need to undertake the construction work (or for the construction work to have been undertaken):
if consent is required under section 24 to include protected Māori land in the proposed levy area, details of any protected Māori land in the proposed levy area:
if consent is not required under section 24 to include protected Māori land in the proposed levy area,—
the estimated proportion of the proposed levy area that is protected Māori land; and
the estimated proportion of protected Māori land that is not leviable land under section 36:
details of any consultation undertaken, including with affected landowners, iwi, or hapū:
the proposed responsible infrastructure authority or authorities (if any):
to the extent that the eligible infrastructure is not proposed to be vested in, or owned by, a responsible infrastructure authority, the person who owns, or will own, the eligible infrastructure:
the proposed RLA or RLAs:
if the proposed RLA is a territorial authority, examples of expected assessments for a representative range of rating units:
if the proposed RLA is a water organisation, examples of expected assessments for a representative range of properties and Māori land:
to the extent that it is proposed to include the costs described in section 9(2)﻿(ba) as eligible costs, the methodology used to estimate those costs:
all of the matters set out in sections 31 to 33A (as applicable):
any of the matters set out in section 34 that are relevant to the levy proposal:
all other information necessary to enable the recommender to assess the levy proposal.
For the purposes of subsection (1)﻿(c), estimates of the proportion of protected Māori land in the proposed levy area may be based solely on information readily available in the rating information database.
18A Eligible infrastructure to which levy proposal relates
The levy proposal must include the proposed eligible infrastructure.
The new or upgraded infrastructure described in section 8(2) may—
be not yet constructed or fully constructed when the levy proposal is submitted; or
have been completed up to 2 years before the levy proposal is submitted.
 Levy proposal assessed by recommender
19 Recommender assesses levy proposal
The recommender must notify the responsible Minister of any levy proposal that the recommender receives.
The recommender—
may assess any levy proposal that the recommender receives; and
must assess a levy proposal on request by the responsible Minister.
A proposer must give to the recommender any additional information required by the recommender for the purpose of assessing a levy proposal.
20 Infrastructure endorsement
A proposed responsible infrastructure authority must endorse the technical specifications of proposed eligible infrastructure if satisfied that—
those specifications are compatible with any wider infrastructure network of which it is to be a part, or will be made compatible with the network if specified conditions included in the vesting agreement for the infrastructure under section 90 are met; and
the proposed responsible infrastructure authority will be able to plan for and meet the necessary operational and maintenance costs of the infrastructure after it is vested in the authority.
The proposed responsible infrastructure authority may give an infrastructure endorsement on request by the proposer, a person acting on behalf of the proposer, or the recommender.
The person who requests an infrastructure endorsement must give to the proposed responsible infrastructure authority all additional information required by the authority for the purpose of giving the endorsement.
For the purpose of subsection (1)﻿(a), the proposed responsible infrastructure authority may require that specified conditions that the authority considers appropriate be included in the vesting agreement relating to the proposed eligible infrastructure.
Subsection (1)﻿(b) does not apply in relation to any operational or maintenance costs that are proposed to be included as eligible costs under section 9(2)﻿(ba).
An infrastructure endorsement under this section is not required—
to the extent that the eligible infrastructure is not proposed to be vested in a responsible infrastructure authority; or
if the proposed responsible infrastructure authority is the proposer.
20A Infrastructure endorsement by territorial authority or water organisation
This section applies if—
proposed eligible infrastructure will connect to the wider infrastructure network of a territorial authority or water organisation; but
the territorial authority or the water organisation is neither the proposer nor a proposed responsible infrastructure authority.
The territorial authority or water organisation must endorse the technical specifications of the proposed eligible infrastructure if satisfied that those specifications are compatible with any wider infrastructure network of which it is to be part.
The proposer, a person acting on behalf of the proposer, or the recommender may request an infrastructure endorsement from the territorial authority or water organisation.
The person who requests an infrastructure endorsement must give to the territorial authority or the water organisation all additional information required by the authority or organisation for the purpose of giving the endorsement.
21 Levy endorsement
A proposed RLA that is a territorial authority must endorse the proposed levy unless the RLA can demonstrate that the proposed levy will compromise its ability to collect rates during the proposed levy period.
A proposed RLA that is a water organisation must endorse the proposed levy unless—
it can demonstrate that the proposed levy will compromise its ability to collect charges under section 86 or 89 of the Local Government (Water Services) Act 2025 during the proposed levy period; or
if the proposed RLA is an Auckland water organisation, it can demonstrate that the proposed levy will compromise its ability to collect charges from its customers during the proposed levy period.
The proposed RLA may give a levy endorsement on request by the proposer, a person acting on behalf of the proposer, or the recommender.
The proposed RLA may specify as a condition of the levy endorsement that levy collection costs be included in the levy order as an eligible cost.
This section does not apply if the proposed RLA is the proposer.
22 Authorities not restricted by endorsement
Giving an infrastructure endorsement or a levy endorsement does not—
require a proposed responsible infrastructure authority or proposed RLA to agree with or approve of any other aspect of the levy proposal or the construction work to which it relates; or
limit or affect the responsible infrastructure authority or the RLA in its performance of any function or duty, or its exercise of any power, under this Act or any other Act.
22A Territorial authority or water organisation not restricted by endorsement
Giving an infrastructure endorsement under section 20A does not—
require the territorial authority or water organisation to agree with or approve of any other aspect of the levy proposal or the construction work to which it relates; or
limit or affect the authority or organisation in its performance of any function or duty, or its exercise of any power, under this Act or any other Act.
23 Endorsements notified
A proposed responsible infrastructure authority or proposed RLA must, as soon as practicable, give the recommender and the person who requested an infrastructure endorsement or a levy endorsement written notification of its decision about the endorsement (including a decision not to give the endorsement) and the reasons for that decision.
A territorial authority or water organisation must, as soon as practicable, give the recommender and the person who requested an infrastructure endorsement under section 20A written notification of its decision about the endorsement (including a decision not to give the endorsement) and the reasons for that decision.
24 Consent to include protected Māori land
Protected Māori land may be included in a proposed levy area only if the owners of that land consent to its inclusion.
The owners must give any consent in writing to the proposer and the recommender.
However, the consent of the owners of protected Māori land is not required under this section if the proposed levy area is expected to encompass 5,000 or more leviable properties over the levy period.
In this section, leviable property, in relation to an RLA that is a water organisation, means any property or Māori land that is leviable.
25 Recommendation
The recommender must not recommend a levy proposal to the responsible Minister unless the recommender has received—
endorsements under sections 20 to 21 (if applicable); and
consent under section 24 (if required).

36 Leviable land
Land within a levy area is leviable under this Act to the extent that it is rateable under sections 7 to 9 of the Local Government (Rating) Act 2002.
37 Who must pay levy
The ratepayer for a rating unit is liable to pay all levy that is due on the unit.
The ratepayer for a separate rating area is liable to pay all levy that is due on the separate rating area.
However,—

the levy liability of trustees in whom Māori freehold land is vested is limited in accordance with section 93 of the Local Government (Rating) Act 2002, which applies subject to subsection (3) and with all other necessary modifications; and
the ratepayer for a rating unit is not liable for any levy due on any separate rating area in the rating unit.
In any calculation of the levy liability of trustees under section 93 of the Local Government (Rating) Act 2002, any rates assessed under that Act must first be deducted from the income referred to in that section.
This section does not apply if the RLA is a water organisation.
37A Who must pay levy if RLA is water organisation
If the RLA is a water organisation, the persons specified in sections 94 and 96 of the Local Government (Water Services) Act 2025 (the 2025 Act) are liable to pay all of the levy that is due on a property or Māori land.

Sections 94, 96, and 98 of the 2025 Act apply for the purposes of this section—
as if a reference to water services charges included a reference to a levy; and

the amount of the levy that must be paid.

the amount of the levy that must be paid.

If the portion of the levy is paid, no further liability for that portion of the levy may be assessed.

This section and sections 95 and 96 apply if, before the levy order comes into force, the RLA—

a development contribution under subpart 5 of Part 8 of the Local Government Act 2002 in relation to any eligible infrastructure (or any infrastructure that includes eligible infrastructure):

in relation to any eligible infrastructure that is water services infrastructure (or any infrastructure that includes that type of eligible infrastructure),—

identify the specific type of costs of the construction of eligible infrastructure to which contributions relate.
96 Levy and previous contribution not to fund same portion of costs of construction
The purpose of this section is to avoid the same portion of the costs of the construction of eligible infrastructure from being funded from both IFF funding and a previous contribution.
If an amount of a previous contribution (an amount) relates to a portion of the costs of the construction of eligible infrastructure that would, but for this section, be funded by a levy,—
the RLA must confirm—
the amount; and
that the amount is allocated towards that portion of the costs; and
the responsible SPV must ensure that the IFF funding that is allocated towards the costs of the construction of the eligible infrastructure is reduced by that amount.
The responsible SPV must notify the monitor that the IFF funding has been reduced.
This section does not prevent eligible costs from including the costs of the construction of eligible infrastructure that are also being funded from a previous contribution if the levy and the previous contribution fund different portions of the costs of the construction of the eligible infrastructure.

This section applies if the landowner does not satisfy a judgment for IFF funding and associated costs (including interest) within 20 working days after the date of the judgment.

the amount of the judgment (including interest); and
any disbursements incurred related to the sale process.
The Registrar’s notice must be served in one of the ways set out in section 69 of the Local Government (Rating) Act 2002.
If all of the amounts set out in subsection (2)﻿(c) have not been paid within 10 working days after the date of the Registrar’s notice, the Registrar may proceed to sell the undeveloped land in accordance with sections 98I to 98P.

A sale to which section 98H applies must be by public auction or public tender.
Without limiting the powers of a Registrar, the Registrar may—
place a reserve on the undeveloped land; or
refuse any tender; or
bid for and buy the undeveloped land at the auction; or
put the undeveloped land up for sale as often as may be required until it is sold.
The purchaser of the undeveloped land is the highest bidder at the auction or the highest tenderer, as the case may be, if the highest bidder or tenderer has complied with all conditions precedent (if any) that are set by the Registrar.
Subsection (3) is subject to any rights reserved by the Registrar under subsection (2).
98J Further powers and duties of Registrar
Despite section 98I, if the Registrar has placed a reserve on the undeveloped land and the land is not sold by public auction or public tender, the Registrar may, with the consent of the responsible SPV, sell the land by private treaty for any consideration that the Registrar thinks reasonable.
The Registrar may include covenants and conditions that the Registrar considers fair and equitable.
98K Execution of documents for sale
In the case of a sale made under section 98I or 98J, when the purchase price has been fully paid, the Registrar must—
execute, under the seal of the High Court, a memorandum of transfer on behalf of the landowner whose interest has been sold; and
note on the transfer that the undeveloped land has been sold under this Act.
When the transfer has been executed, the purchaser is entitled to obtain possession of the purchased undeveloped land.
98L Registration of instruments
An instrument executed under section 98K may be registered under the Land Transfer Act 2017.
98M Presumption of valid sale
A sale made under section 98I or 98J is valid for all purposes, even if there has been an irregularity in the conduct of the sale.
No purchaser is obliged to inquire whether the sale was properly conducted.
98N Application of balance of proceeds
After the proceeds of a sale of undeveloped land have been applied as specified in the levy order, the Registrar must pay the balance (if any) to Public Trust.
Public Trust, on the order of the High Court (which may be made by motion in Chambers), must pay the balance to those persons who are entitled to it, together with interest.
98O No further levies may be charged
If a responsible SPV has recovered the IFF funding from the landowner under this subpart, no further levies may be charged under the levy order referred to in section 98C(1) in relation to the undeveloped land to which the recovery relates.
98P Reduction of maximum levy revenue when funding recovered for undeveloped land
If the responsible SPV has recovered the IFF funding for the undeveloped land under this subpart, the responsible SPV must—
exclude the area of undeveloped land from the levy area; and
reduce the maximum levy revenue by the amount of IFF funding recovered for the undeveloped land.
The maximum levy revenue must be reduced under subsection (1)﻿(b) by reducing either or both of the following:
the levy period:
the amount of the levy set in later levy years.
The responsible SPV must notify the monitor of the changes to the levy area and maximum levy revenue.
The monitor must publish a notice of the changes to the levy area and the maximum levy revenue no later than 5 working days after being notified of the changes by the responsible SPV.

 Powers relating to private land
99 Power to construct eligible infrastructure on private land
This section applies to eligible infrastructure only if a levy order applies this section (see section 34(1)﻿(g)﻿(i)).
A responsible SPV may exercise the powers of a local authority or a territorial authority under section 181 of the Local Government Act 2002 for the construction of eligible infrastructure in a levy area (and, for that purpose, section 181 and Schedule 12 of that Act apply with all necessary modifications).

This section and sections 101 to 105 apply only if applied by a levy order (see section 34(1)﻿(g)﻿(ii)).
For the purposes of providing water services infrastructure in the levy area, the responsible SPV may—
construct, place, and maintain eligible infrastructure that is water services infrastructure in, on, along, over, across, or under any road or public land within or outside the levy area; and
for the purposes of any work carried out under paragraph (a), open or break up any road or public land; and
alter, repair, or remove that infrastructure or any part of that infrastructure.
However, the responsible SPV must exercise the powers under subsection (2) in accordance with any conditions that the person who has jurisdiction over the road or land imposes.

Before a responsible SPV proceeds to open or break up any road or public land under section 100(2)﻿(b), the SPV must give to the person who has jurisdiction over the road or land written notice of the intention to carry out the work.
Subsection (1) is subject to section 104.
Every notice must specify the location of the proposed work, the nature of the work to be carried out, and the reasons for it.

The person who has jurisdiction over the road or land must notify the responsible SPV in writing of any conditions imposed under section 100(3).

103 Failure to notify conditions
If the person who has jurisdiction over the road or land fails to notify the responsible SPV in accordance with section 102, the conditions are not imposed and the SPV may commence work.
104 Urgency
If work is urgent and necessary because of any defective equipment or other emergency, a responsible SPV—
is excused from giving notice under section 101(1) before commencing the work; but
must give the information required by section 101(3) to the person who has jurisdiction over the road or land as soon as practicable after commencing the work.
105 Appeal by responsible SPV to District Court
A responsible SPV may appeal to the District Court against any condition imposed under section 100(3) on the grounds that the condition is unreasonable.
An appeal must be made not later than the day that is 45 working days after the date of notification of the conditions imposed, or within any further time that the District Court may allow.
In its determination of any appeal, the District Court may confirm, modify, or cancel the condition.
The decision of the District Court in the determination of an appeal under this section is final.

 Accounting records and reporting

In this subpart,—

registered bank has the same meaning as in section 2(1) of the Banking (Prudential Supervision) Act 1989.
107 Levy account
A responsible SPV must—
pay levy revenue, as soon as practicable after it is received, into an account with a registered bank; and
until the levy revenue is used to pay an eligible cost or otherwise paid as permitted by this Act, ensure that the levy revenue is held separate from other money held by or on behalf of the responsible SPV.
108 Accounting records
An SPV must ensure that there are kept at all times accounting records that—
correctly record the SPV’s transactions; and
will at any time enable the SPV’s financial position to be determined with reasonable accuracy; and
will enable the SPV to ensure that its financial statements comply with this Act; and
will enable the SPV’s financial statements to be readily and properly audited.
The SPV must establish and maintain a satisfactory system of control of its accounting records.
The accounting records must be kept—
in written form; or
in a manner in which they are easily accessible and convertible into written form.

An SPV must ensure that, within 4 months after the end of each levy year, financial statements that comply with generally accepted accounting practice are completed in relation to the SPV and that levy year.

An SPV must ensure that its financial statements are audited by a qualified auditor.
An auditor must, in carrying out an audit for that purpose, comply with all applicable auditing and assurance standards.

An SPV must,—
as soon as practicable after the end of each levy year, prepare an annual report on the operations of the SPV; and
provide the annual report to the monitor within 4 months after the end of each levy year.

112 Content of annual report
An SPV’s annual report must contain—
the information that is reasonably necessary to enable an informed understanding of the SPV’s operations during the levy year to which it relates; and
audited financial statements for that levy year for the SPV; and
the auditor’s report on those financial statements.
The annual report must include the following information for the levy year to which it relates:
eligible costs incurred, itemised as in the levy order:
all other costs incurred by the SPV:
if the SPV is entitled to levy revenue,—
the annual levy:
the actual levy revenue collected:
in relation to the levy account required by section 107,—
the opening balance of the account for the levy year; and
interest earned on funds held in the account; and
the closing balance at the end of the levy year:
how the closing balance referred to in paragraph (c)﻿(iii)﻿(C) will be applied to eligible costs in the future:
in relation to forecast excess levy,—
every forecast of excess levy; and
every decision to reduce the maximum levy revenue by reducing the levy period or the levies payable in later levy years:
any additional information required by the monitor under section 113.
113 Additional information required by monitor
For the purposes of section 112(2)﻿(f), the monitor may (by written notice at least 4 months before the start of a levy year) require an SPV to include additional information in its annual report for that year.
The monitor must publish any requirement given under this section.

In this section, lend money or provide credit—

to defer payment for any goods or services supplied or works constructed for any person, organisation, or government; and

to enter into finance lease arrangements or arrangements that are of the same or a substantially similar nature; and
to subscribe for any debt securities or uncalled capital; but
does not include to sell or supply goods or services on credit in the ordinary course of the RLA’s performance of its lawful responsibilities and on terms and conditions generally available to other parties of equivalent creditworthiness.

This section does not apply to a liability for any sum of a kind described in section 49(2) of the Public Finance Act 1989.

 Subpart 1 — Role of monitor
118 Appointment of monitor
The Governor-General may, by Order in Council made on the recommendation of the responsible Minister, appoint a government agency as monitor for the purposes of this Act.
An order under this section is secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).
119 Functions, duties, and powers
The monitor’s functions are to—
publish information about the levy scheme and about specific levy areas, levy orders, and SPVs; and
determine any objection by a levypayer to an assessment of the levypayer’s levy liability; and
monitor an SPV’s compliance with this Act and the levy order; and
inquire into, and intervene in, an SPV’s operations in accordance with this Act.
In addition, the monitor may exercise all powers that are—
conferred on the monitor by this Act; or
reasonably necessary for the performance of the monitor’s functions and duties.
120 Monitor must publish information
The monitor must publish general information about the levy scheme established by this Act to enhance public understanding of—
how a levy order applies to a levy area; and
how levy liability applies—
to rating units; or
if the RLA is a water organisation, to a property or Māori land.
In addition, the monitor must publish information about a specific levy area, levy order, and responsible SPV, including—
the annual levy resolution of the SPV for each levy year:
the SPV’s annual reports:
information to assist levypayers to understand their levy liability and potential levypayers to understand how levy liability is assessed under this Act.
121 Information and assistance
The monitor may direct a responsible SPV or an RLA to give the monitor any information or other assistance that the monitor considers to be reasonably necessary for the performance of the monitor’s functions and duties and the exercise of the monitor’s powers under this Act.
In relation to an RLA that is a territorial authority, information under subsection (1) includes all information requested by the monitor from the RLA’s rating information database and rates records.
The SPV or RLA must comply with the direction.
122 Restricted change of control of SPV
This section applies if a restricted change of control occurs.
The monitor may direct the SPV not to pay any specified distributions to prohibited persons, and the SPV must comply with that direction.
In this section,—
distribution means any distribution by a company or limited partnership, or other payment of a similar nature to a distribution
prohibited person means a person who, as a result of a restricted change of control occurring, becomes a shareholder, partner, or other person with an interest or a right to participate in any capital, assets, earnings, or other property of the SPV
restricted change of control means a change of control as defined by the levy order for the purposes of this section (see section 34(1)﻿(e)).
123 Inquiry into SPV’s operations
The monitor may inquire into any aspect of an SPV’s operations if the monitor—
reasonably believes that a significant problem relating to the SPV could exist; or
has any concern relating to any matter that could potentially become a significant problem relating to the SPV.
124 Monitor may give direction or request remedial plan
This section applies whether or not the monitor has conducted an inquiry under section 123.
If the monitor reasonably believes that a significant problem relating to a responsible SPV exists, the monitor must direct the SPV—
to address the significant problem; or
to prepare a remedial plan for addressing the significant problem.
A direction must—
be in writing; and
describe the significant problem to be addressed; and
state the date by which the significant problem must be addressed; and
in the case of a direction under subsection (2)﻿(a), state how the significant problem must be addressed.
The responsible SPV must comply with a direction given under this section.
If the monitor is satisfied that a remedial plan prepared by the SPV will address the significant problem,—
the monitor may accept the plan; and
the SPV must address the significant problem in accordance with the plan that is accepted.
If the monitor is not satisfied that a remedial plan prepared by the responsible SPV will address the significant problem, the monitor must give a direction to the SPV under subsection (2)﻿(a).
The preparation of a remedial plan does not prevent the monitor from giving a direction under subsection (2)﻿(a).
125 Monitor may recommend appointment of Crown Manager
If a responsible SPV fails to address a significant problem in accordance with section 124, the monitor may recommend to the responsible Minister that a Crown Manager be appointed to the SPV.
 Subpart 2 — Role of Crown Manager
126 Responsible Minister may appoint Crown Manager
The responsible Minister may, by notice, appoint a person as Crown Manager to a responsible SPV if—
the responsible Minister reasonably believes that a significant problem with the responsible SPV exists; and
the monitor has recommended that a Crown Manager be appointed.
The notice of appointment must include—
the purpose of the appointment, including—
an outline of the problem in relation to which the Crown Manager has been appointed; and
the extent of the Crown Manager’s authority; and
the start and end dates of the Crown Manager’s appointment; and
the start and end dates of the management period.
In addition, the notice of appointment may include limits or conditions on—
the functions and duties to be performed, and the powers to be exercised, by the Crown Manager; and
how those functions and duties are to be performed, and how those powers are to be exercised.
The responsible Minister must notify the appointment of a Crown Manager—
to the responsible SPV; and
in the Gazette.
127 Functions, duties, and powers of Crown Manager
A Crown Manager must perform the functions and duties and exercise the powers of the responsible SPV under this Act—
in accordance with this Act and the levy order; and
subject to any limits or conditions on the Crown Manager’s role set out in the notice of appointment under section 126.
The functions and powers of a Crown Manager include all matters for which the responsible SPV is responsible under this Act.
The Crown Manager may—
work with the board of directors or other governing body of the SPV or act to their exclusion; or
give directions to the board or other governing body.
The board of directors or other governing body of the SPV—
must co-operate with the Crown Manager, including by complying with any directions given by the Crown Manager; and
remains responsible for the general operation of the SPV.
128 Information to responsible Minister and monitor
A Crown Manager must inform the responsible Minister and the monitor about—
the steps being taken to address the significant problem; and
the progress of those steps.
The monitor, if satisfied that the significant problem has been addressed, must inform the responsible Minister.
129 Termination of appointment
The responsible Minister may terminate the appointment of a Crown Manager at any time.
No compensation is payable to the Crown Manager in relation to the termination of their appointment.
130 Protection from liability
The Crown Manager is not liable for any act done in good faith in the performance or intended performance of their functions or duties or the exercise of their powers.
131 Costs of Crown Manager
A responsible SPV owes as a debt to the Crown any costs that the Crown incurs for the appointment of a Crown Manager to the SPV, including the payment of remuneration and expenses to the Crown Manager.
The SPV must not pay those costs by increasing the levy or extending the levy period.
132 Remuneration and expenses of Crown Manager
A Crown Manager is entitled—
to receive remuneration for services as determined by the responsible Minister in accordance with the fees framework; and
to be reimbursed, in accordance with the fees framework, for actual and reasonable travelling and other expenses incurred in performing their functions.
In this section, fees framework means the framework determined by the Government from time to time for the classification and remuneration of statutory and other bodies in which the Crown has an interest.
 Subpart 3 — Modified rights and remedies for creditors
133 General law applies with some modifications

 Modification of Receiverships Act 1993 for SPVs
134 Application of Receiverships Act 1993
The Receiverships Act 1993 applies to a receiver of an asset of an SPV appointed under this subpart with the modifications set out in this subpart.
135 Receiver appointed under instrument
This section applies if an SPV gives a charge over any asset of the SPV as security for any loan or for the performance of any obligation.
The instrument that creates or evidences the terms and conditions of the charge may provide for the appointment of a receiver of that asset on terms agreed by the parties.
This section applies subject to the Personal Property Securities Act 1999, and does not limit any other rights or remedies of the holder of a charge over any asset of an SPV.
136 Receiver appointed by court
The court may, on application by any creditor of a responsible SPV, appoint a receiver to exercise the powers, and perform the functions, of the SPV under this Act in relation to a levy.
The court must, as it considers appropriate, specify—
the term of the appointment; and
the rights, powers, and duties of the receiver; and
the terms and conditions of the appointment, including terms about security and remuneration.
Before appointing a receiver, the court must have regard to—
the interests of all of the responsible SPV’s creditors (whether secured or unsecured); and
the interests of the levypayers; and
the role of the receiver under this subpart.
This section does not limit the proceedings that may be brought against an SPV or the circumstances in which a court may appoint a receiver of an SPV’s other assets.
This section is subject to sections 140 and 141.
137 Powers of receiver
This section applies if—
a responsible SPV has charged levy revenue as security for any loan or for the performance of any obligation; and
a receiver has been appointed under section 135 or 136 in respect of that loan or obligation.
From the date of the receiver’s appointment until the termination of that appointment, all powers of the responsible SPV that relate to a levy under this Act are conferred on, and may be exercised by, the receiver.
The receiver may exercise the SPV’s powers as agent of the responsible SPV.
Section 13 of the Receiverships Act 1993 is subject to section 139(3) of this Act (which limits the exercise of those powers in relation to assets not charged in favour of the appointor of the receiver).
138 General duties of receiver
The general duties imposed on receivers by section 18 of the Receiverships Act 1993 are subject to the constraints imposed on receivers by section 139(1).
139 Constraints on receiver
A receiver appointed in relation to a responsible SPV or an asset of a responsible SPV must ensure that no action of the receiver prevents any activities of that SPV, any other SPV, or the responsible infrastructure authority that are essential for the maintenance of public health and safety, regardless of anything in—
this Act or the Receiverships Act 1993; and
any instrument providing for or governing the appointment of the receiver.
An action of the receiver is only to be taken to prevent an activity of the responsible SPV, any other SPV, or the responsible infrastructure authority if—
the action necessarily results in that outcome; and
the outcome is not more fairly attributable to the act or omission of a person outside the receiver’s control.
In exercising any powers (including those of a manager), the receiver is not entitled to control, dispose of, or otherwise interfere with the ability of the responsible SPV, any other SPV, or the responsible infrastructure authority to exercise or perform its rights, powers, and duties in relation to assets not charged in favour of the appointor of the receiver.
A receiver appointed under a charge instrument or by the court must distribute the proceeds of collection and recovery of the money and assets the receiver is entitled to collect and recover in the following order of priority:
any amounts required for the receiver’s remuneration, costs incurred by the receiver, and the costs incurred by any person in obtaining the receiver’s appointment:
any amounts payable in respect of claims by law to be preferred to claims under any charge over those assets:
any amounts required to be paid out of the proceeds of collection of the money and assets to enable the receiver to carry out the activities specified in subsection (1):
the amounts secured by any charges over those assets in the order of priority accorded those charges, so as to preserve the respective entitlements of the holders of those charges:
if the receiver was appointed by the court on the application of 1 or more unsecured creditors, any amounts payable, or directed by the court to be paid, to those creditors:
any residue to, or for the benefit of, the responsible SPV as it directs.
140 Protection of receiver
No proceedings lie against a receiver of a responsible SPV for any breach of section 139(1) by—
the receiver; or
any adviser or delegate of the receiver.
No proceedings lie against an adviser or a delegate of a receiver for any breach of section 139, other than proceedings brought by the receiver.
A receiver (and any adviser or delegate) must be indemnified in accordance with subsections (4) and (5) in respect of any liability relating to the exercise, purported exercise, or omission to exercise any right or power of the receiver by the receiver (or the adviser or delegate).
A receiver appointed by the court (and any adviser or delegate) must be indemnified by the responsible SPV.
A receiver appointed under a charge document (and any adviser or delegate) must be indemnified out of the assets subject to receivership, unless the terms of the appointment of the receiver provide otherwise.
Subsections (1) and (3) do not apply to an act or omission of a person that constitutes bad faith or gross negligence.
This section does not limit or affect the operation of sections 19 and 20 of the Receiverships Act 1993.
In this section, adviser or delegate, in relation to a receiver, means an adviser or a delegate of the receiver who has been reasonably selected and whose activities have been reasonably supervised.
141 Exception in relation to Crown Manager
If a Crown Manager is appointed under this Act in relation to a responsible SPV (either before or after a receiver is appointed under a charge instrument or by the court), the court may order that any receiver so appointed may not, until the court so orders, exercise any of the rights, powers, and duties of a receiver.

 Subpart 4 — Enforcement
 Injunctions and compliance orders
143 Court may grant injunction
A court may, on application by the monitor, grant an injunction restraining an SPV from engaging in conduct that constitutes or would constitute a contravention, or involvement in a contravention, of this Act or a levy order.
144 When court may grant injunction
A court may grant an injunction restraining an SPV from engaging in conduct of a particular kind if—
it is satisfied that the SPV has engaged in conduct of that kind; or
it appears to the court that, if an injunction is not granted, it is likely that the SPV will engage in conduct of that kind.
The court may grant an interim injunction restraining an SPV from engaging in conduct of a particular kind if in its opinion it is desirable to do so.
Subsections (1)﻿(a) and (2) apply whether or not it appears to the court that the SPV intends to engage again, or to continue to engage, in conduct of that kind.
Subsections (1)﻿(b) and (2) apply whether or not the SPV has previously engaged in conduct of that kind or there is an imminent danger of substantial damage to any other person if the SPV engages in conduct of that kind.
145 No undertaking as to damages required
If the monitor applies to the court for the grant of an injunction, the court must not, as a condition of granting the injunction, require the monitor to give an undertaking as to damages.
In determining the monitor’s application for the grant of an injunction, the court must not take into account the fact that the monitor is not required to give an undertaking as to damages.
146 Court may make compliance order
The court may, on application by the monitor, make an order (a compliance order) requiring an SPV to do 1 or more of the following:
to comply with a provision of this Act or a levy order:
to remedy non-compliance with the provision by another means:
to avoid or mitigate any actual or likely effect of non-compliance with the provision.
The court may make a compliance order if it is satisfied that, by engaging in any conduct, the SPV has failed, or is likely to fail, to comply with a provision of this Act or the levy order.
The court may make a compliance order on any terms that it considers appropriate, including by specifying any reasonable steps that the SPV must take in order to comply with the order.
 Civil liability and compensatory orders
147 Court may make civil liability order
A court may, on application by the monitor, make a civil liability order if the court is satisfied that a person has contravened or was involved in a contravention of this Act or a levy order.
A civil liability order may direct the person in contravention, or the person involved in the contravention, to refund money or return property to an SPV if the court is satisfied that the SPV has suffered, or is likely to suffer, loss or damage because of the contravention.
148 Court may make compensatory order
A court may, on application by the monitor, make a compensatory order if—
the court is satisfied that—
a person has contravened or was involved in a contravention of this Act or a levy order; and
the SPV has suffered, or is likely to suffer, loss or damage because of the contravention; and
it appears to the court that a civil liability order under section 147 is not practicable in the circumstances.
The court may make any order it thinks just to compensate an SPV in whole or in part for the loss or damage, or to prevent or reduce the loss or damage.
An order under this section may include an order to direct a relevant person to do either or both of the following:
to pay to the SPV the amount of the loss or damage (in whole or in part):
to take other steps to avoid or mitigate any actual or likely loss or damage resulting from the contravention.
Subsection (3) does not limit subsection (2).
In this section, relevant person means—
any person in contravention; or
any person involved in the contravention.
149 Defences
In any proceeding under section 147 or 148 against a person (A) for a contravention of this Act or a levy order, it is a defence if A proves that—
A’s contravention was due to reasonable reliance on information supplied by another person; or
both of the following apply:
A’s contravention was due to the act or default of another person, or to an accident or to some other cause beyond A’s control; and
A took reasonable precautions and exercised due diligence to avoid the contravention.
For the purposes of subsection (1)﻿(a) and (b)﻿(i), another person does not include a member of the board of directors or other governing body, an employee, or an agent of A.
150 Defences for person involved in contravention
This section applies if—
a person (A) contravenes this Act or a levy order; and
another person (B) is involved in the contravention.
In any proceeding under section 147 or 148 against B for involvement in the contravention, it is a defence if B proves that—
B’s involvement in the contravention was due to reasonable reliance on information supplied by another person; or
B took all reasonable and proper steps to ensure that A complied with the relevant provision of this Act or the levy order.
For the purposes of subsections (1)﻿(b) and (2)﻿(a), another person does not include a member of the board of directors or other governing body, an employee, or an agent of A.
151 Meaning of involved in a contravention
In sections 147, 148, and 150, a person is involved in a contravention if the person—
has aided, abetted, counselled, or procured the contravention; or
has induced, whether by threats or promises or otherwise, the contravention; or
has been in any way, directly or indirectly, knowingly concerned in, or party to, the contravention; or
has conspired with others to effect the contravention.
 Offences
152 False statement or information
A person commits an offence if the person makes a statement or gives information for the purposes of this Act that is false or misleading in a material particular, knowing it to be false or misleading.
A person who commits an offence under this section is liable on conviction,—
in the case of an individual, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 12 months or a fine not exceeding $5,000; and
in any other case, to a fine not exceeding $15,000.
153 Refusal or failure to give information
A person commits an offence if the person refuses or fails, without reasonable excuse, to give any information that is in that person’s possession or under that person’s control when required under this Act to give the information.
A person who commits an offence under this section is liable on conviction,—
in the case of an individual, to a fine not exceeding $2,000; and
in any other case, to a fine not exceeding $5,000.
154 Resistance, obstruction, or failure to comply with directions
A person commits an offence if the person, without reasonable excuse,—
fails to comply with a direction given under this Act; or
resists or obstructs any person who is acting in the performance of their functions or duties or in the exercise of their powers under this Act.
A person who commits an offence under this section is liable on conviction,—
in the case of an individual, to a fine not exceeding $2,000; and
in any other case, to a fine not exceeding $5,000.
 Subpart 5 — Miscellaneous
 Recommender
155 Appointment of recommender
The Governor-General may, by Order in Council made on the recommendation of the responsible Minister, appoint a government agency as recommender for the purposes of this Act.
An order under this section is secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).
156 Function of recommender
The recommender’s function is to perform the role and duties and exercise the powers conferred and imposed on the recommender under subpart 1 of Part 2 to support the responsible Minister’s consideration of a levy proposal.
 Delegations
157 RLA may delegate
An RLA may delegate the performance of its functions or duties or the exercise of its powers under this Act in accordance with section 132 of the Local Government (Rating) Act 2002.
This section applies to an RLA that is a territorial authority.
158 Responsible SPV may delegate
A responsible SPV may delegate to an officer of the SPV the performance of its functions or duties or the exercise of its powers—
under this Act; or
in its capacity as a responsible SPV, under any other enactment.
This section does not authorise the delegation of—
the power to delegate; or
the SPV’s functions, duties, and powers relating to the setting of an annual levy.
An officer to whom a function, duty, or power is delegated (the delegate) may, unless the delegation provides otherwise, perform the function or duty, or exercise the power, in the same manner, subject to the same restrictions, and with the same effect as if the delegate were the SPV.
A delegation does not affect or prevent the performance of the delegated function or duty, or the exercise of the delegated power, by the SPV.
In this section, officer, in relation to an SPV, means—
a named person; or
the person who is for the time being the holder of a specified office.
159 Other provisions of Local Government (Rating) Act 2002 applied
The following provisions of the Local Government (Rating) Act 2002 apply in relation to a levy authorised under this Act:
section 133 (in certain cases Governor-General in Council may extend time or validate proceedings):
section 134 (Judge, etc, not interested merely by being ratepayer):
section 135 (evidence of certain matters):
section 136 (notification).
 Courts
160 Jurisdiction of courts
The High Court has exclusive jurisdiction to hear and determine proceedings in New Zealand under this Act, other than—
proceedings to recover unpaid levy under section 86W or 86X; and
appeals under section 105; and
proceedings for offences.
