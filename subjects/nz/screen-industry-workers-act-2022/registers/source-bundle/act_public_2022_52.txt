Screen Industry Workers Act 2022
1 Title
This Act is the Screen Industry Workers Act 2022.
2 Commencement
Section 98 (which contains regulation-making powers) comes into force on the day after the date of Royal assent.
The rest of this Act comes into force on the day that is 3 months after the date of Royal assent.
 Part 1 — Preliminary provisions
3 Purpose
The purpose of this Act is to provide a workplace relations framework for certain participants in the screen industry.
4 Employment status of screen production workers determined by this Act
This Act determines the employment status of an individual who falls within the meaning of screen production worker in section 11.
If an individual is a screen production worker who is a party to, or covered by, a written agreement with an engager stating that the individual is an employee,—
the agreement is conclusive proof for the purposes of this section that the individual is an employee of the engager; and
this Act does not apply to the individual.
If an individual is a screen production worker who is not a party to, or covered by, a written agreement with an engager stating that the individual is an employee,—
that is conclusive proof for the purposes of this section that the individual is not an employee of the engager; and
this Act applies to the individual.
To avoid doubt, a screen production worker cannot invoke section 6(2) of the Employment Relations Act 2000 to determine whether they are an employee.
To avoid doubt, section 6 of the Employment Relations Act 2000 determines the employment status of an individual who works in the screen industry but is not a screen production worker within the meaning of section 11 of this Act.
5 No contracting out
This Act applies despite any contract, agreement, or other arrangement.
A contract, agreement, or other arrangement is unenforceable to the extent that it is inconsistent with this Act.
6 Prohibition on matters relating to bullying, discrimination, and harassment
A contract, agreement, or other arrangement between persons must not prevent a screen production worker (or any person acting on their behalf) from raising a complaint of bullying, discrimination, or harassment under this or any other enactment (see section 25(1)).
The contract, agreement, or other arrangement is unenforceable to the extent that it contains such a prohibition.
7 Commerce Act 1986 prohibitions on restrictive trade practices do not apply
Nothing in section 27 or 30 of the Commerce Act 1986 applies to—
anything done by any person for the purposes of collective bargaining for a collective contract in accordance with this Act; or
anything done by any person to give effect to a collective contract entered into in accordance with this Act.
However, see section 28 of this Act, which prohibits industrial action (within the meaning of that section) during bargaining for a collective contract.
8 Act binds the Crown
This Act binds the Crown.
9 Transitional, savings, and related provisions
The transitional, savings, and related provisions set out in Schedule 1 have effect according to their terms.
10 Interpretation
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—
Authority means the Employment Relations Authority established under section 156 of the Employment Relations Act 2000
bargaining, in relation to bargaining for a collective contract, means all the interactions between the parties that relate to the bargaining, including—
negotiations that relate to the bargaining; and
communications or correspondence (between, or on behalf of, the parties before, during, or after negotiations) that relate to the bargaining
chief executive means the chief executive of the department
collective contract—
means an enterprise contract or an occupational contract; and
includes any variation to the terms of that contract
commencement date, in respect of a collective contract, means the date on which the contract comes into force, which is,—
in respect of an occupational contract, the date calculated in accordance with section 51(1); and
in respect of an enterprise contract, the date on which the bargaining parties sign the contract (see section 67)
court means the Employment Court constituted under section 186 of the Employment Relations Act 2000
coverage clause,—
in relation to an occupational contract, means the provision in the contract that specifies the occupational group who do the work to which the contract applies:
in relation to an enterprise contract, means the provision in the contract that specifies the work to which the contract applies
dispute means, as the case may be,—
any dispute about the interpretation, application, or operation of an individual contract:
any dispute about the interpretation, application, or operation of a collective contract:
any problem relating to or arising from bargaining for a collective contract:
any problem relating to or arising from a workplace relationship
disputed terms has the meaning set out in section 73(1)
engager means a person who engages 1 or more screen production workers (for example, a production company)
engager organisation means an organisation registered under section 84 as an engager organisation
enterprise contract means a collective contract that applies to, and is enforceable by, the persons specified in section 65
individual contract means the contract between a screen production worker and the worker’s engager (in relation to the work to be performed by the worker for the engager)
occupational contract means a collective contract that applies to, and is enforceable by, the persons specified in section 50
occupational group means a category of screen production worker specified in Schedule 2
person in control of the workplace means—
the person who, at the time at which a request to enter a workplace is made under section 90, is in control of the workplace; or
a representative of that person
Registrar means the Registrar of Screen Industry Organisations appointed under section 94
regulations means regulations made under section 98
screen production means a record (or any part of it) captured in any medium from which a moving image may be produced for distribution to the public by any means including, without limitation, cinematic exhibition, television broadcast, Internet streaming, or software download
screen production worker or worker has the meaning given in section 11
worker organisation means an organisation registered under section 84 as a worker organisation
workplace—
means—
a place at which a screen production worker works from time to time; and
a place to which a screen production worker goes to do work; but
for the purposes of sections 89 to 93, excludes any building or any part of a building to the extent that it is occupied as a residence and is not being used as a production set
workplace relationship means any of the relationships specified in section 12.
Subsection (3) applies for the purposes of assisting the interpretation of any provision of this Act that applies 1 or more provisions of the Employment Relations Act 2000, and it does so with any necessary modifications.
Without limiting the ordinary meaning of that term, necessary modifications to a provision include the following:
any reference to an employee must be read as if it were a reference to a screen production worker; and
any reference to an employer must be read as if it were a reference to an engager; and
any reference to any employment agreement must be read as if it were a reference to an individual contract; and
any reference to a union must be read as if it were a reference to a worker organisation; and
any reference to a collective agreement must be read as if it were a reference to a collective contract.
11 Meaning of screen production worker
In this Act, screen production worker or worker—
means an individual—
who is engaged by a person to contribute to the creation of 1 or more screen productions to which this Act applies (see section 13); and
who undertakes the work in New Zealand; but
excludes any individual who, in relation to the screen production or screen productions,—
only provides support services; or
is a volunteer; or
is engaged to do the work by a person that does not primarily engage in work relating to the creation of screen productions.
For the purposes of subsection (1), an individual (I), is engaged by a person (P) if—
I is engaged directly by P; or
I is engaged indirectly through 1 or more interposed persons (for example, a company that I controls in any manner).
For the purposes of subsection (1)﻿(b)﻿(iii), a person (whether a company, partnership, individual, or other entity) does not primarily engage in work relating to the creation of screen productions if the person—
derived less than 50% of the person’s average annual gross income (within the meaning of section BC 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007) from work relating to the creation of screen productions in the previous 3 financial years; and
is not a special purpose vehicle established within those previous 3 financial years principally for the purpose of creating or contributing to 1 or more screen productions.
In this section,—
support services means any of the following services if they make a peripheral (and not integral) contribution to the creation of the particular screen production or productions:
accounting, administration, advertising, auditing, legal, management, or representation services:
any other services of a similar nature
volunteer means an individual who does work without reward and without expecting reward.
12 Meaning of workplace relationship
A workplace relationship is a relationship between any of the following:
an engager and a screen production worker bargaining for an individual contract:
a screen production worker and the worker’s engager:
a worker organisation and—
its members who are screen production workers; and
any other screen production workers to whom a collective contract may apply and for whom the worker organisation is a bargaining party or signatory party (including workers who are members of an organisation referred to in subparagraph (iii)); and
any other worker organisation that is bargaining for, or a party to, the same collective contract:
an engager organisation and—
its members who engage screen production workers; and
any other engager to whom a collective contract may apply and for whom the engager organisation is a bargaining party or signatory party (including members of an organisation referred to in subparagraph (iii)); and
any other engager organisation that is bargaining for, or a party to, the same collective contract:
a worker organisation and an engager organisation bargaining for, or a party to, the same occupational contract:
a worker organisation and an engager.
13 Screen productions to which Act applies
This Act applies in respect of the screen productions described in clause 1 of Schedule 3.
This Act does not apply in respect of the screen productions described in clause 2 of Schedule 3.
 Part 2 — Good faith and freedom of association
14 Duties of good faith for parties in workplace relationship
Parties in a workplace relationship must not, whether directly or indirectly, do anything—
to mislead or deceive each other; or
that is likely to mislead or deceive each other.
Subsection (1) is a complete description of the duties of good faith in relation to workplace relationships, except when parties are bargaining collectively (when they also have the duties set out in section 26).
15 Penalty for breaching section 14
A person who breaches section 14 is liable to a penalty under this Act.
16 Voluntary membership of worker organisation or engager organisation
A contract, agreement, or other arrangement between persons must not require a screen production worker—
to become or remain a member of a worker organisation or a particular worker organisation; or
to cease being a member of a worker organisation or a particular worker organisation; or
not to become a member of a worker organisation or a particular worker organisation.
A contract, agreement, or other arrangement between persons must not require an engager—
to become or remain a member of an engager organisation or a particular engager organisation; or
to cease being a member of an engager organisation or a particular engager organisation; or
not to become a member of an engager organisation or a particular engager organisation.
17 Prohibition on preferential treatment
A contract, agreement, or other arrangement between persons must not confer on a screen production worker, because the worker is or is not a member of a worker organisation or a particular worker organisation,—
any preference for the worker’s engagement as a screen production worker; or
any preference for the worker’s engagement as a screen production worker to continue; or
any preference in relation to—
the worker’s terms of engagement (including terms relating to compensation for termination of their contract); or
any other benefit or opportunity that relates to the worker’s engagement (whether specified in their individual contract or otherwise).
Subsection (1) is not breached simply because a screen production worker’s individual contract is different from those of other screen production workers engaged by the same engager.
A contract, agreement, or other arrangement between persons must not confer any benefit or opportunity on an engager because the engager is or is not a member of an engager organisation or a particular engager organisation (for example, an arrangement offering the engager a more favourable contract on the condition that the engager not join an engager organisation).
18 Undue influence prohibited
A person must not exert undue influence, directly or indirectly, on a screen production worker with the intention of inducing the worker to—
form, join, not join, remain a member of, or cease to be a member of a worker organisation; or
participate or not participate in any aspect of collective bargaining under this Act; or
be covered or not be covered by an enterprise contract; or
terminate their individual contract with their engager on account of the fact that they are or are not a member of a worker organisation or a particular worker organisation.
A person must not exert undue influence, directly or indirectly, on any person with the intention of inducing the person to—
not act on behalf of any screen production worker; or
cease to act on behalf of any screen production worker.
19 Penalty for breaching section 18
A person who breaches section 18 is liable to a penalty under this Act.
Matters relevant to whether a person has exerted undue influence on any other person under section 18 include any developments in the common law relating to the parameters of undue influence under the Employment Relations Act 2000 that are relevant to the context of this Act.
 Part 3 — Individual contracts
20 Engager responsibilities when entering individual contracts
An engager must ensure that an individual contract is in writing.
An engager must do at least the following things before entering into a new individual contract:
give the screen production worker concerned a copy of the contract intended for negotiation (whether contained in a single document or otherwise); and
advise the worker that they are entitled to seek independent advice about it; and
give the worker a reasonable opportunity to seek that advice; and
consider any issues raised by the worker and respond in good faith.
An engager must provide a screen production worker with a copy of their individual contract as soon as practicable after the contract is entered into.
21 Process to vary individual contract
If the parties wish to vary an individual contract, the engager must—
give the worker a copy of the contract with the potential variation; and
advise the worker that they are entitled to seek independent advice about it; and
give the worker a reasonable opportunity to seek that advice; and
consider any issues raised by the worker and respond in good faith.
An engager must provide the worker with a copy of the varied contract as soon as practicable after the contract is varied.
Subsections (1) and (2) do not apply to any of the following kinds of variation:
a variation resulting from a collective contract coming into force that applies to the worker:
a variation resulting from a variation to a collective contract if—
the collective contract applies to the worker; and
the variation to the collective contract will apply to the worker.
22 Individual contract terms
A screen production worker’s individual contract must contain the terms specified in section 23.
A screen production worker’s individual contract must also comply with—
subsection (3), if an occupational contract applies to the worker:
subsection (4), if an enterprise contract applies to the worker:
subsection (5), if both an occupational contract and an enterprise contract apply to the worker.
The worker’s individual contract—
must contain all terms of the occupational contract that are required to be included in an individual contract (except terms otherwise covered by the worker’s individual contract that are the same or more favourable to the worker):
is deemed to contain every other term of the collective contract that is applicable to the worker (except terms otherwise covered by the worker’s individual contract that are the same or more favourable to the worker):
may contain any other terms that the worker and engager agree, except any term that is—
prohibited under section 6; or
less favourable to the worker than a corresponding term of the collective contract and not authorised in accordance with section 54.
The worker’s individual contract—
is deemed to contain every term of the enterprise contract that is applicable to the worker (except terms otherwise covered by the worker’s individual contract that are the same or more favourable to the worker):
may contain any other terms that the worker and engager agree, except any term that is—
prohibited under section 6; or
less favourable to the worker than a corresponding term of the collective contract.
The worker’s individual contract—
must contain all terms of the occupational contract that are required to be included in an individual contract (except terms otherwise covered by the worker’s enterprise contract or individual contract that are the same or more favourable to the worker):
is deemed to contain every other term of the occupational contract that is applicable to the worker (except terms otherwise covered by the worker’s enterprise contract or individual contract that are the same or more favourable to the worker):
is deemed to contain every term of the enterprise contract that is applicable to the worker (except terms otherwise covered by the worker’s individual contract that are the same or more favourable to the worker):
may contain any other terms that the worker and engager agree, except any term that is—
prohibited under section 6; or
less favourable to the worker than a corresponding term of the enterprise contract; or
less favourable to the worker than a corresponding term of the occupational contract and not authorised in accordance with section 54.
The terms of any collective contract that are deemed to be included in a screen production worker’s individual contract in accordance with this section are not required to be separately duplicated in the worker’s individual contract. (However this does not apply to any mandatory terms that must be included under section 23.)
A worker’s individual contract is unenforceable to the extent that it contains terms specified in subsection (3)﻿(c)﻿(i) or (ii), (4)﻿(b)﻿(i) or (ii), or (5)﻿(d)﻿(i), (ii), or (iii).
23 Mandatory terms in individual contracts
An individual contract must contain a term that requires both parties to the contract to comply at all times with their respective obligations under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 and the Human Rights Act 1993.
An individual contract must contain, in plain language,—
an explanation of the process by which the screen production worker may raise, and the engager respond to, a complaint by the worker about bullying, discrimination, or harassment in the workplace; and
a statement that the process does not prevent the screen production worker from raising a complaint under this or any other enactment.
An individual contract must contain an explanation in plain language of the processes available for resolving disputes, which may include those available under subpart 1 of Part 5.
An individual contract must contain a term that sets out—
the period of notice (if any) required if either party to the contract terminates the contract; and
the compensation (if any) payable to a screen production worker if the party who terminates the contract is the worker’s engager.
24 Prohibition on retaliatory termination or non-renewal of individual contracts
An engager must not terminate or refuse to renew a screen production worker’s contract if the decision to do so is motivated, wholly or partly, by the exercise or proposed exercise by the worker of any right, power, authority, or remedy conferred on the worker by—
a term of the worker’s individual contract; or
this Act or any other enactment.
An engager must not terminate or refuse to renew a screen production worker’s contract if the decision to do so is motivated, wholly or partly, by the worker having provided or intending to provide information or evidence in relation to a complaint of bullying, discrimination, or harassment made by another individual under this or any other enactment.
25 Penalties relating to individual contracts
An engager is liable to a penalty under this Act if the engager enters into an individual contract that includes a term that has the effect of breaching section 6.
An engager is liable to a penalty under this Act if the engager does not comply with 1 or more of the requirements in section 20 or 21.
An engager is liable to a penalty under this Act if the engager enters into an individual contract and the contract does not comply with the requirements of section 22.
An engager is liable to a penalty under this Act if the engager terminates or refuses to renew a screen production worker’s contract and is motivated to do so, wholly or partly, by any of the factors set out in section 24.
 Part 4 — Collective bargaining
 Subpart 1 — All collective bargaining
26 Collective bargaining must be carried out in good faith
The parties to collective bargaining must act in good faith during the bargaining process.
For that purpose, the parties must do at least the following:
comply with section 14 (as parties to a workplace relationship); and
as soon as possible after the initiation of bargaining, use their best endeavours to agree a process for conducting the bargaining in an effective and efficient manner (including how they will address any disputes that may arise between them during the bargaining); and
meet each other from time to time for the purposes of the bargaining; and
consider and respond to proposals made by each other; and
continue to bargain (including doing the things specified in paragraphs (c) and (d)) about any matters on which they have not reached agreement even if they have come to a standstill about 1 or more other matters; and
recognise the role and authority of any person chosen by each to be its representative or advocate; and
refrain from undermining or doing anything that is likely to undermine the bargaining or the authority of any other party to the bargaining; and
provide to each other, on request and in accordance with section 30, information that is reasonably necessary to support or substantiate claims or responses to claims made for the purposes of the bargaining; and
comply with section 27.
Matters relevant to whether the parties are dealing with each other in good faith in accordance with this section include—
the provisions of any agreement about good faith entered into by the parties; and
the background circumstances; and
the circumstances of the parties, including—
the operational environment of the parties; and
the resources available to the parties; and
any developments in the common law relating to the parameters of good faith in collective bargaining under the Employment Relations Act 2000 that are relevant to the context of this Act.
Subsection (2)﻿(c) does not require the parties to continue to meet each other about proposals that have been considered and responded to.
Nothing in this section prevents any engager from—
communicating with screen production workers during the bargaining (including, without limitation, the engager’s proposals for the collective contract); or
continuing to negotiate and form individual contracts with workers during bargaining.
Subsection (5) applies provided the engager’s behaviour is consistent with subsection (2)﻿(f) and (g) and any duties imposed on the engager by sections 14 and 18.
27 Bargaining parties must conclude collective contract
The parties bargaining for a collective contract must conclude a collective contract.
28 Industrial action prohibited during bargaining
Engagers and screen production workers are prohibited, during the bargaining for a collective contract, from taking industrial action, if the action is intended to undermine or affect the outcome of that bargaining (irrespective of whether the engager is a party to, or the worker is represented at, the bargaining).
In this section, industrial action means any of the following behaviours:
2 or more workers refusing, in a concerted manner, to fulfil 1 or more terms of their individual contracts with their engagers:
an engager or a person authorised on the engager’s behalf preventing 1 or more workers from carrying out their obligations under their individual contracts (for example, blocking access to a set):
an engager refusing to carry out 1 or more obligations under the engager’s individual contracts with 1 or more workers (for example, not paying the workers).
To avoid doubt, nothing in this section prevents 1 or more screen production workers from exercising any rights under section 83 of the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 to cease or refuse to carry out work.
29 Penalties for failing to comply with collective bargaining requirements
A person who breaches section 26 (other than subsection (2)﻿(i)) is liable to a penalty under this Act.
A person who breaches section 28(1) is liable to a penalty under this Act.
30 Requests for information during bargaining
This section applies for the purposes of section 26(2)﻿(h).
A request for information made by 1 or more parties to another must—
be in writing; and
specify the nature of the information requested in sufficient detail to enable the information to be identified; and
specify the claim or the response to a claim in respect of which information to support or substantiate the claim or the response is requested; and
specify a reasonable time within which the information is to be provided.
A party must provide the information requested—
directly to the other party or parties; or
to an independent reviewer, if the party providing the information reasonably considers that it should be treated as confidential information.
A person must not act as an independent reviewer unless appointed by mutual agreement of the party or parties requesting the information and the party that holds the information.
As soon as practicable after receiving information under subsection (3)﻿(b), an independent reviewer must—
decide whether and, if so, to what extent the information should be treated as confidential; and
advise the parties of the decision in a way that maintains the confidentiality of the information.
If an independent reviewer decides that the information should be treated as confidential, the independent reviewer must—
decide whether and, if so, to what extent the information supports or substantiates the claim or the response to the claim in respect of which the information is requested; and
advise the party that requested the information of the decision in a way that maintains the confidentiality of the information; and
answer any questions from the party that requested the information in a way that maintains the confidentiality of the information.
Unless the parties otherwise agree, information provided under subsection (3) and advice and answers provided under subsections (5) and (6)—
must be used only for the purposes of the bargaining concerned; and
must be treated as confidential by the parties and any person conducting the bargaining on their behalf; and
must not be disclosed to anyone else, including any person to whom the collective contract would apply.
This section does not limit or affect the Privacy Act 2020.
Nothing in the Official Information Act 1982 (except section 6) enables an engager that is subject to that Act to withhold information that is requested under section 26(2)﻿(h).
 Subpart 2 — Requirements for all collective contracts
31 Form and content of collective contracts
A collective contract must—
be in writing; and
after ratification, be signed by each bargaining party.
The contract must contain the terms specified in section 32.
The contract may contain any other terms that the parties agree, except any term that is—
contrary to law; or
inconsistent with this Act.
32 Mandatory terms in collective contracts
A collective contract must contain a coverage clause.
A collective contract must contain the following terms for all screen production workers who do the work to which the coverage clause relates:
the rates of pay:
the entitlement to breaks:
the extent to which, and the manner in which, public holidays are recognised:
the hours of work:
availability for work:
the minimum procedural requirements for raising and responding to a complaint relating to bullying, discrimination, or harassment in the workplace:
a termination clause:
the minimum procedural requirements for resolving disputes relating to an individual contract.
A collective contract must also contain the following terms:
the date on which the contract expires or an event on the occurrence of which the contract expires that complies with the requirement in section 34(1) or (2), as the case may be:
a term providing for how the contract may be varied:
a term authorising exemptions from the terms of the collective contract (as provided for in section 54) if the contract is an occupational contract.
For the purposes of subsection (2)﻿(a), the contract must contain 1 or more of the following terms:
the rates payable (excluding any calculation or other adjustment for royalties and residuals):
the minimum rates payable (excluding any calculation or other adjustment for royalties and residuals):
1 or more methods of calculating the rates or minimum rates payable (excluding any calculation or other adjustment for royalties and residuals).
For the purposes of subsection (2)﻿(b), the contract must specify whether or not there is a minimum entitlement to breaks and, if so, their frequency, duration, and timing.
For the purposes of subsection (2)﻿(c), the contract must specify whether or not workers are expected to work on 1 or more public holidays as defined in section 44(1) of the Holidays Act 2003 and, if so,—
whether they are entitled to receive additional compensation for working on those days; and
the method or methods of calculating the additional compensation.
For the purposes of subsection (2)﻿(d), the contract must specify whether or not there is a maximum number of hours workers may be required to work in a given period and, if so,—
whether they are entitled to receive additional compensation for working beyond the maximum hours; and
the method or methods of calculating the additional compensation.
For the purposes of subsection (2)﻿(e), the contract must specify whether or not workers may be required to be available (with no guarantee for work) beyond the contractually agreed hours and, if so,—
the method or methods of calculating additional compensation (if any) for work done during the period of availability; and
the method or methods of calculating additional compensation (if any) for being available during the period of availability despite doing no work in that period.
For the purposes of subsection (2)﻿(g), the termination clause must specify—
the process by which either party to an individual contract may terminate the contract; and
the period of notice (if any) required by either party; and
the compensation (if any) payable to the worker if the engager terminates the contract.
Terms of a collective contract may differentiate between specified work, or specified types of work, or specified workers, or specified types of worker.
33 Bargaining fees
An occupational contract must not include a bargaining fee term.
An enterprise contract may include a bargaining fee term if—
the enterprise contract allows coverage to a non-member worker in a manner authorised by section 65(2); and
the bargaining fee term applies only to non-member workers who have consented to be covered under that section; and
the bargaining fee is no greater than the lowest fee that a non-member worker would be required to pay if they were a member of any worker organisation that was a signatory party to the enterprise contract.
A bargaining fee term in an enterprise contract has no effect to the extent that it does not comply with subsection (2).
In this section,—
bargaining fee means an amount (however described) payable by a screen production worker to a worker organisation under a bargaining fee term, whether payable as a lump sum or on a periodical basis
bargaining fee term means a term in a collective contract that—
applies to 1 or more non-member workers; and
requires them to pay a bargaining fee; and
specifies the amount of the bargaining fee
non-member worker means a screen production worker who is not a member of a worker organisation that is a signatory party to a collective contract.
34 Duration of collective contracts
An occupational contract—
is in force, as from its commencement date, for no less than 3 years; and
must expire no more than 6 years from its commencement date.
An enterprise contract—
is in force as from its commencement date; and
must expire no less than 3 years and no more than 6 years from its commencement date.
Despite subsections (1) and (2), a contract that would otherwise expire continues in force if—
the contract is an occupational contract and, before the date of expiry, an application is made under section 36 to initiate bargaining to replace it; or
the contract is an enterprise contract and, before the date of expiry, the bargaining process to replace it has begun in accordance with section 60(2).
If subsection (3)﻿(a) applies, and the Authority declines the application made under section 36 to initiate bargaining to replace the occupational contract, the contract expires on the later of the following dates:
the date calculated under subsection (1)﻿(b):
the date on which the Authority declines the application to initiate bargaining.
Despite subsection (2)﻿(b), if the work of all the screen production workers to whom the enterprise contract applies is completed before the date on which the enterprise contract expires, the enterprise contract must be treated for all purposes as having expired on the date on which all the work is completed.
 Subpart 3 — Occupational contracts
35 Preliminary requirements
To initiate bargaining for an occupational contract, a worker organisation or an engager organisation must apply to the Authority in accordance with section 36 and the Authority must decide (under this subpart)—
whether to allow bargaining to be initiated; and
certain matters relating to the conduct of the bargaining.
Before allowing bargaining to be initiated, the Authority must be satisfied that there is—
at least 1 worker organisation registered under subpart 1 of Part 6 that represents screen production workers to whom the occupational contract will apply; and
at least 1 engager organisation registered under subpart 1 of Part 6 that represents engagers to whom the occupational contract will apply.
See section 45 for the potential effect on a bargaining process of a bargaining party being removed.
36 Application for approval to initiate bargaining
A worker organisation or an engager organisation may apply to the Authority to initiate bargaining for an occupational contract.
The application must be made in the manner prescribed by the regulations (if any) and be accompanied by—
a copy of the applicant’s certificate of registration provided under section 84; and
a copy of a bargaining notice that complies with the requirements of subsection (3); and
if the applicant is a worker organisation, the following information:
the number of its members who do the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract; and
a statement that, of those members, a simple majority who voted by secret ballot voted in favour of the applicant initiating bargaining; and
a statement of how the organisation will conduct a ratification vote that will allow any eligible worker who does the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause to cast a vote, irrespective of whether the person is a member of the applicant’s organisation; and
if the applicant is an engager organisation, the following information:
the number of its members who engage workers who do the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract; and
a statement that, of those members, a simple majority who voted by secret ballot voted in favour of the applicant initiating bargaining; and
a statement as to which worker organisation it considers should conduct the ratification vote.
A bargaining notice must be in writing and identify—
the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract; and
all worker organisations whose members do the work of that occupational group; and
all engager organisations whose members engage workers who do the work of that occupational group; and
any occupational contract that applies to some or all of the screen production workers in the occupational group referred to in paragraph (a) or the most recently expired occupational contract that applied to some or all of those workers (if any); and
who will conduct the ratification vote for the purposes of section 48, which must be—
the applicant, if a worker organisation; or
the worker organisation identified under subsection (2)﻿(d)﻿(iii), if the applicant is an engager organisation.
An applicant must, at the same time as applying to the Authority, send a copy (including the accompanying material) to the other worker organisations and engager organisations referred to in subsection (3)﻿(b) and (c).
This section is subject to section 37.
37 When application may be made
If there is no applicable occupational contract in force or an applicable contract expired more than 2 years previously, a worker organisation may apply to initiate bargaining, and may do so at any time.
If there is an applicable occupational contract that expired no more than 2 years previously, a worker organisation or an engager organisation may apply to initiate bargaining, and may do so at any time.
If there is an applicable occupational contract in force,—
a worker organisation may apply to initiate bargaining no earlier than 180 days before the date on which the contract expires; and
an engager organisation may apply to initiate bargaining no earlier than 160 days before the date on which the contract expires.
Despite subsections (1) to (3), if the Authority has published a decision approving the initiation of bargaining for an occupational contract under section 42 and that bargaining has not concluded, no other application to initiate bargaining for the same occupational group may be made.
To avoid doubt, the circumstances described in subsections (1) to (3) are the only circumstances in which a worker organisation or an engager organisation may apply to the Authority for approval to initiate bargaining for an occupational contract.
In this section, applicable occupational contract means an occupational contract that applies to the occupational group identified in a bargaining notice in accordance with section 36(3)﻿(a).
38 Applications must be notified and submissions invited
The Authority must, as soon as is reasonably practicable, give notice, on an Internet site maintained by, or on behalf of, the Authority, of any application to initiate bargaining received under section 36.
The notice must—
include a copy of the bargaining notice referred to in section 36(3); and
invite submissions from any person; and
state the date by which submissions must be received (being no earlier than 28 days after the date on which the Authority gives the notice); and
state the manner in which submissions must be given (which may include by use of a form); and
state any minimum requirements that submissions must comply with; and
give the contact details of the Authority.
The regulations may prescribe the manner in which a submission on an application to initiate occupational bargaining must be made.
However, regulations made for the purposes of subsection (3) may only require the provision of information that is necessary for the Authority to make a decision under section 39.
The Authority may, if it considers it appropriate and in any manner it sees fit, extend the time within which submissions may be made.
39 Authority must decide whether to allow bargaining
Once the submission period has closed, the Authority must decide whether to allow collective bargaining to be initiated.
The Authority must approve an application if it is satisfied that there is sufficient support to do so.
In this section, sufficient support means,—
if the applicant is a worker organisation, that the Authority is satisfied (having regard to the application and any submissions it has received that comply with the requirements of section 38) there are, in total, more workers who do the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract who want to bargain than who do not; and
if the applicant is an engager organisation, that the Authority is satisfied (having regard to the application and any submissions it has received that comply with the requirements of section 38) there are more engagers who engage workers who do the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract who want to bargain than who do not.
For the purposes of determining the total number of workers referred to in subsection (3)﻿(a), the number of members of any relevant worker organisation is the total number of members of the organisation who do the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract.
For the purposes of determining the total number of engagers referred to in subsection (3)﻿(b), the number of members of any relevant engager organisation is the total number of members of the organisation who engage screen production workers who do the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract.
40 Bargaining parties
This section applies if the Authority approves an application to initiate collective bargaining under section 39.
The following organisations are deemed to be bargaining parties, unless subsection (4) applies:
all worker organisations who have members who do the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract; and
all engager organisations whose members engage workers who do the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract.
An organisation that wishes to be excused from being a bargaining party must apply in writing to the Authority for permission.
On receipt of a request under subsection (3), the Authority may excuse an organisation specified in subsection (2) from being a bargaining party.
However, the Authority must not excuse an organisation if—
doing so would mean a substantial number or distinct class of screen production workers or engagers would not have their interests adequately represented during the bargaining process; or
the number of members of the excused organisation affected by the bargaining (because their work falls within the intended coverage clause) would be greater than the number of affected members of all other organisations on the same side of the bargaining.
In making a decision under subsection (4), the Authority—
must have regard to any submissions received that comply with the requirements of section 38; and
may consider any other information or matter that, in its opinion, is relevant; and
may consider any other information from any worker organisation or engager organisation deemed to be a bargaining party.
To avoid doubt, the application of the occupational contract to any screen production worker who does the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract, or any engager who engages such a worker, is not affected by an organisation being excused from participating in the bargaining, even if the worker or engager is a member of the excused organisation.
41 Authority must decide worker organisation responsible for ratification vote
This section applies if the Authority approves an application to initiate collective bargaining under section 39.
The Authority must decide which worker organisation will conduct the ratification vote, if it is not satisfied that the applicant worker organisation or the worker organisation nominated by the applicant engager organisation (as the case may be) consents and has the ability to conduct the ratification vote in the manner referred to in subsection (3)﻿(d)﻿(ii).
Before deciding, the Authority—
must have regard to any submissions received that comply with the requirements of section 38; and
may consider any other information or matter that, in its opinion, is relevant; and
may consider any other information from any other worker organisation or engager organisation deemed to be a bargaining party; and
must be satisfied that—
the organisation it is intending to confer the duty upon agrees to conduct the ratification vote; and
the organisation has the ability to conduct the ratification vote in a manner that will allow any eligible worker who does the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause to cast a vote, irrespective of whether the person is a member of that organisation.
42 Authority must publish decisions on applications
The Authority must publish all its decisions made under section 39 on applications to initiate bargaining for an occupational contract on an Internet site maintained by, or on behalf of, the Authority (and, for successful applications, its decisions made under sections 40 and 41).
Decisions in relation to successful applications must include—
the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract; and
the bargaining parties to the contract; and
the worker organisation that will conduct the ratification vote and the process that it will use; and
any occupational contract that applies to some or all of the screen production workers in that occupational group or the most recently expired occupational contract that applied to some or all of those workers.
43 How bargaining initiated (once approval given)
Bargaining for an occupational contract must be initiated by the worker organisation or engager organisation that applied under section 36(1) to initiate bargaining.
The organisation must initiate bargaining—
by giving a written bargaining notice to each bargaining party specified in the Authority’s decision to allow bargaining (published under section 42); and
on or before the expiry of the 28th day from the date on which the Authority publishes that decision.
The bargaining notice must include the information published by the Authority under section 42(2).
If the organisation fails to comply with subsection (2)﻿(a), bargaining for the collective contract is automatically initiated on the day after the date referred to in subsection (2)﻿(b), in accordance with the terms of the Authority’s decision published under section 42.
44 Removal of bargaining party
The Authority may remove a worker organisation or an engager organisation as a bargaining party for an occupational contract after bargaining has been initiated, but only if—
the organisation applies in writing to the Authority to be removed; or
the organisation’s registration has been cancelled under section 86; or
the organisation was identified as a party as a result of providing false or misleading information to the Authority; or
the Authority is satisfied that the organisation no longer has any members to whom the contract would apply.
For the purposes of deciding whether to remove an organisation under subsection (1)﻿(a), section 40(4) to (6) applies with any necessary modification.
To avoid doubt, the application of the collective contract to any screen production worker who does the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause of the contract, or engager who engages such a worker, is not affected by an organisation being removed from participating in the bargaining, even if the worker or engager is a member of the removed organisation (as the case may be).
The Authority must publish a removal decision on an Internet site maintained by, or on behalf of, the Authority.
45 Removal of bargaining party: effect on bargaining for occupational contract
This section applies if, during bargaining for an occupational contract, 1 or more of the bargaining parties is removed.
Bargaining for the contract ends—
if there is no engager organisation; or
if there is no worker organisation.
46 Addition of bargaining party
The Authority may include a worker organisation or an engager organisation as a bargaining party for an occupational contract after bargaining has been initiated, if—
the organisation applies in writing to the Authority to be included; and
the organisation has members who do the work of the occupational group to be specified in the coverage clause or has members who engage such workers; and
the organisation was not identified during the process leading to the initiation of bargaining (for example, because the organisation was not in existence or registered at that time); and
the Authority is satisfied that—
allowing the organisation to be included will not undermine the bargaining taking place; and
a substantial number or distinct class of screen production workers or engagers (as the case may be) would not have their interests represented were the organisation not permitted to be included in the bargaining process.
In making a decision under subsection (1)﻿(d), the Authority—
may consider any information or matter that, in its opinion, is relevant; and
may consider any information from any worker organisation or engager organisation that is already a bargaining party; and
may, but is not required to, notify the application, and invite and consider submissions on it, in any manner it sees fit.
The Authority must publish all its decisions made under this section on an Internet site maintained by, or on behalf of, the Authority.
47 Authority must assess draft occupational contracts for suitability for ratification
This section applies if—
the parties to occupational bargaining have agreed the terms they wish to put forward for ratification and the party who initiated the bargaining has submitted the draft contract to the Authority for assessment as to its suitability for ratification; or
the Authority has fixed terms in an occupational contract in accordance with section 74.
The Authority must approve or not approve the draft as suitable for ratification.
The Authority must approve the draft if satisfied that it complies with section 31(1)﻿(a), (2), and (3).
If the Authority does not approve the draft,—
it must give the bargaining parties its reasons (by reference to the relevant matters in subsection (3)); and
the parties may revise the draft and resubmit it for approval.
The process in subsection (4) may be repeated 1 or more times.
The Authority’s decision under this section is final.
48 Ratification of occupational contract
This section applies if the Authority has approved under section 47 a draft occupational contract as suitable for ratification.
Before any worker organisation that is a bargaining party signs the contract, it must be ratified by the screen production workers who do the work of the occupational group specified in the coverage clause of the contract.
For that purpose, the worker organisation responsible for conducting the ratification vote must give notice of the following matters:
that the Authority has approved the draft contract to proceed to a ratification vote; and
where a copy of the contract may be obtained; and
the details of the ratification process published under section 42(2)﻿(c), including—
who is eligible to vote; and
the final date by which votes must be cast; and
the method by which votes may be cast.
Notice must be given on an Internet site to which the public has free access in accordance with regulations made under section 98 (if any).
For the purposes of subsection (3)﻿(c)﻿(i), a person is eligible to vote if, at any time in the relevant period, the person is or has been a party to a contract with an engager in respect of work on a screen production and the work is or was that done by the occupational group specified in the coverage clause of the contract.
For the purposes of subsection (3)﻿(c)﻿(ii),—
the final date by which votes must be cast must be no earlier than 14 days from the date on which voting begins; and
voting may begin no earlier than the date on which notice of the ratification vote is given under subsection (3).
In subsection (5), relevant period means the period starting 3 years before the public notice of application (see section 38) and ending on the date on which voting on ratification closes.
The occupational contract is ratified if, of the eligible voters who voted, a simple majority vote in favour of ratification.
49 Occupational contracts must be delivered to chief executive
The worker organisation responsible for conducting the ratification vote for an occupational contract must ensure that, as soon as practicable after the contract is signed, the following are delivered to the chief executive:
a copy of the contract; and
a statement about the ratification vote that includes—
the total number of votes cast; and
the total number of votes in favour; and
the total number of votes opposed.
If the worker organisation referred to in subsection (1) is deregistered before sending the signed contract to the chief executive, any other signatory party to the contract may send the signed contract to the chief executive.
For each contract received, the chief executive must—
give notice in the Gazette of the date on which it will commence under section 51 and where a copy may be obtained; and
publish it on an Internet site maintained by, or on behalf of, the department.
50 Coverage of occupational contract
An occupational contract applies to, and is enforceable by, the following persons:
every screen production worker who does the work of the occupational group specified in the coverage clause of the contract; and
every engager who engages a screen production worker to do that work; and
every worker organisation that is a signatory party to the contract; and
every engager organisation that is a signatory party to the contract.
51 Commencement of occupational contract
An occupational contract comes into force on the date that is 6 months from the date on which the notice is gazetted by the chief executive in accordance with section 49(3)﻿(a).
The terms in the occupational contract apply to a screen production worker’s individual contract on and from that same date, unless subsection (3) applies.
The terms in the contract apply to the worker’s individual contract on and from the date that is 12 months from the date on which the chief executive gives notice of the contract if—
the engager and the worker entered into the individual contract before the occupational contract was gazetted by the chief executive; and
the individual contract is still in force at that later date.
52 Addition of replacement signatory party to occupational contract
A registered worker organisation may apply to the Authority to be added as a signatory party to an occupational contract if all the worker organisations that were signatory parties are deregistered.
A registered engager organisation may apply to the Authority to be added as a signatory party to an occupational contract if all the engager organisations that were signatory parties are deregistered.
An application under subsection (1) or (2) must be in writing.
On receipt of an application under subsection (1) or (2), the Authority may add a signatory party to an occupational contract if—
the contract is in force; and
the Authority is satisfied that the applicant organisation satisfies the criteria in section 46(1)﻿(b) and (c).
In making a decision under subsection (4), the Authority—
must consult all remaining signatory parties to the contract; and
may consider any information or matter that, in its opinion, is relevant; and
may, but is not required to, notify the application, and invite and consider submissions on it, in any manner it sees fit.
The Authority must publish all its decisions made under this section on an Internet site maintained by, or on behalf of, the Authority.
53 Variations to occupational contract
For an occupational contract to be varied, there must be—
at least 1 signatory party that is a worker organisation; and
at least 1 signatory party that is an engager organisation.
A variation to an occupational contract must be—
made in accordance with the term or terms of the contract that provide for how the contract may be varied (see section 32(3)﻿(b)):
assessed by the Authority under section 47; and
ratified under section 48; and
delivered to the chief executive in accordance with section 49.
For the purposes of subsection (2), the processes in sections 47 and 48 apply as if the variation were a draft occupational contract (with any necessary modifications).
54 Exemptions from occupational contract
An occupational contract must contain a term authorising an engager and 1 or more screen production workers to—
enter into individual contracts containing 1 or more terms that are less favourable to the workers than the terms of the occupational contract (less favourable term); or
vary individual contracts between the engager and the workers to include a less favourable term.
However, a less favourable term may form part of a worker’s individual contract only if—
the less favourable term relates only to a particular screen production; and
the less favourable term does not have the effect of allowing less favourable terms relating to the rates payable under the occupational contract in accordance with section 32(2)﻿(a); and
complying with the term in the occupational contract that the less favourable term would replace would result in significant disruption to the production that could not have reasonably been foreseen; and
the engager and the worker agree that the less favourable term be included in the individual contract, after—
the engager has advised the worker that the worker is entitled to seek independent advice about the term; and
the worker has had a reasonable opportunity to seek that advice; and
the engager has considered any issues raised by the worker and responded to them in good faith; and
either—
the engager obtains the consent of all signatory parties to the occupational contract in accordance with the process in section 55; or
the less favourable term is immediately needed and the engager follows the immediate exemption process in section 56.
See section 58 for the effect of a cancelled registration on an exemption process.
55 Process to obtain consent to less favourable term
The process to obtain consent for the purposes of section 54(2)﻿(e)﻿(i) is as follows:
the engager must send a written request to all signatory parties that sets out the following information:
the proposed less favourable term; and
the proposed number of individual contracts that will contain the less favourable term; and
the term or terms of the occupational contract to which the proposed less favourable term relates; and
how the inclusion of the proposed less favourable term satisfies the criterion in section 54(2)﻿(c):
each signatory party to the occupational contract must respond in writing to the request within 14 days:
if a signatory party does not respond in writing within 14 days, the signatory party may be treated as having consented to the less favourable term.
When calculating a 14-day period for the purposes of this section, the days in the period commencing with 25 December in a year and ending with 2 January in the following year must not be counted.
56 Immediate exemption process
For the purposes of section 54(2)﻿(e)﻿(ii), a less favourable term that is immediately needed may be orally agreed between 1 or more workers and their engager but the engager must, as soon as is reasonably practicable, amend each worker’s written individual contract to record the variation.
The engager must also, as soon as is reasonably practicable after agreeing the less favourable term, notify (in writing) all the signatory parties to the occupational contract of—
the less favourable term; and
the number of individual contracts that contain the less favourable term; and
the term or terms of the occupational contract to which the less favourable term relates; and
how the inclusion of the less favourable term satisfies the criterion in section 54(2)﻿(c); and
why the inclusion of the less favourable term was immediately needed.
57 Penalty for breaching section 54
An engager who breaches section 54 is liable to a penalty under this Act.
58 Cancellation of registration: effect on exemptions from occupational contract
This section applies if an exemption from an occupational contract is sought in reliance on section 54 but the registration of 1 or more of the signatory parties has been cancelled.
If 1 or more signatory parties remain,—
the consent of all of the remaining signatory parties must be obtained in accordance with the process in section 55; or
in the case of an immediate exemption, all of the remaining signatory parties must be notified in accordance with section 56.
If no signatory parties remain, the requirements in sections 55 and 56 to notify and seek the consent of signatory parties do not apply.
 Subpart 4 — Enterprise contracts
59 Who may participate in bargaining for enterprise contract
The bargaining parties for an enterprise contract may only be—
1 or more worker organisations; and
1 or more engagers.
To avoid doubt, if 1 or more engagers use an engager organisation to act as their agent in the bargaining, each engager is still a party to the contract (and not the engager organisation).
60 How and when bargaining may be initiated
A worker organisation or an engager may initiate bargaining for an enterprise contract by giving to all the other proposed bargaining parties a bargaining notice in writing that states—
the intended coverage of the contract; and
the time period within which the parties served with the notice may respond in writing to the notice.
Bargaining is initiated only if all parties that receive the bargaining notice consent in writing within the stipulated time period.
Each worker organisation must notify each of the other bargaining parties of its ratification procedure when bargaining is initiated.
61 Withdrawal of consent to bargaining prohibited
Bargaining parties for an enterprise contract are prohibited from withdrawing their consent once bargaining is initiated under section 60(2) (and the parties must conclude a collective contract in accordance with section 27).
62 Limitations on terms that may be agreed
The bargaining parties for an enterprise contract must not agree to any term that would be less favourable to the screen production workers to whom the contract is intended to apply than a corresponding term in an occupational contract that also would apply to those workers (once the enterprise contract came into force).
63 Ratification of enterprise contract
Before a worker organisation that is a bargaining party to an enterprise contract signs the contract, it must be ratified by the organisation’s members who are screen production workers and who do the work specified in the coverage clause of the contract.
This section does not apply if—
the bargaining relates to a screen production for which the engager or engagers have not yet engaged any workers to whom the contract would apply; or
the Authority has fixed the terms of the enterprise contract (see section 75(2)).
64 Enterprise contracts must be delivered to chief executive
The parties to an enterprise contract must ensure that, as soon as practicable after the contract is signed, a copy of the contract is delivered to the chief executive.
A copy of an enterprise contract delivered to the chief executive must include any document referred to, or incorporated by reference, in the contract, unless the document is publicly available.
Nothing in the Official Information Act 1982 applies to a copy of an enterprise contract delivered to the chief executive under subsection (1).
The information contained in a copy of an enterprise contract delivered to the chief executive under subsection (1) must be used for statistical or analytical purposes only.
65 Coverage of enterprise contract
An enterprise contract applies to, and is enforceable by, the following persons:
every worker organisation that is a signatory party to the contract; and
every engager that is a signatory party to the contract; and
every screen production worker—
who is a member of a worker organisation that is a signatory party to the contract (whether at the time the organisation signs the contract or at any later date); and
who does the work specified in the coverage clause of the contract for an engager that is a signatory party to the contract.
An enterprise contract also applies to, and is enforceable by, every screen production worker who does the work specified in the coverage clause of the contract but who is not a member of any signatory worker organisation (a non-member worker), but only if—
the enterprise contract provides for extension of the collective contract to non-member workers; and
a term is included in the contract that sets out—
how the contract will apply to non-member workers who consent to being covered; and
how a non-member worker gives consent; and
the non-member consents in accordance with that term.
66 Screen production worker may choose contract
This section applies if—
2 or more enterprise contracts apply to a screen production worker in respect of the same work; and
the worker is a member of both or all (as the case may be) of the worker organisations that are signatory parties to each enterprise contract.
The screen production worker may choose which enterprise contract applies to them and must notify their engager of their choice in writing.
The worker may change their decision under this section at any time that the section applies to their circumstances by notifying their engager in writing.
67 Commencement of enterprise contract
An enterprise contract comes into force on the date on which the bargaining parties sign the contract.
The terms in the enterprise contract apply to a screen production worker’s individual contract on and from the later of the following dates:
the date on which the bargaining parties sign the enterprise contract:
the date provided in the enterprise contract (which must not be more than 6 months after the signing date).
The terms in the contract apply to a non-member worker’s individual contract on and from the later of—
the applicable date referred to in subsection (2); and
the date on which the non-member worker consents in accordance with section 65(2)﻿(c).
68 Variations to enterprise contract
A variation to an enterprise contract must be ratified under section 63 and delivered to the chief executive under section 64.
For the purposes of subsection (1), section 63 applies as if the variation were an enterprise contract (with any necessary modifications).
 Part 5 — Dispute resolution, challenges, reviews, penalties, and offences
 Subpart 1 — Dispute resolution, challenges, and reviews
69 Disputes
If a dispute arises between persons to whom this Act applies, the dispute must be resolved using—
the dispute resolution processes set out in this subpart; or
any other agreed process (including any varied, additional, or replacement process).
In a dispute,—
a worker organisation may act on behalf of 1 or more screen production workers, if the workers consent; and
an engager organisation may act on behalf of 1 or more engagers, if the engagers consent.
However, see section 73(3) which provides that only the Authority has the power to fix terms in an occupational contract if the bargaining parties cannot agree.
70 Mediation of disputes relating to matters under this Act
The chief executive must employ or engage persons to provide mediation services to support the resolving of disputes in relation to any matter to which this Act applies.
If mediation is provided by the chief executive, sections 144 to 154 of the Employment Relations Act 2000 apply with any necessary modifications.
Nothing in this section prevents any person from seeking and using mediation services other than those provided by the chief executive.
If a dispute cannot be resolved by mediation (whether provided by the chief executive or privately arranged), it may be resolved by a determination of the Authority made under section 72.
Subsection (4) is subject to section 71.
71 Facilitated bargaining: collective contracts
Before seeking a determination, the bargaining parties to a collective contract may attempt facilitation (with a member of the Authority as the facilitator) to resolve a dispute relating to the bargaining.
The Authority may accept a reference for facilitation from any party, or 2 or more parties, to bargaining if the Authority is satisfied that—
the dispute is interfering with the bargaining or the ability of the parties to conclude a collective contract; and
the parties have made sufficient efforts at mediation to resolve their difficulties.
The Authority must carry out any facilitation under this section as if it were a facilitation under the Employment Relations Act 2000, subject to the modifications to the provisions of that Act specified in the following paragraphs:
sections 50A, 50B, 50C(1) and (2), and 50J do not apply; and
sections 50C(3), 50D to 50G, and 50I apply with any necessary modifications; and
section 50H applies, except that the Authority must make 1 or more recommendations on the matters referred to in subsection (1)﻿(a) and (b) of that section; and
the Authority may refer the parties to the facilitation back to mediation if it considers that further mediation would be beneficial to resolving the dispute concerned.
If the dispute remains unresolved at the end of facilitation, any party to the dispute may apply to the Authority under section 72(1) or 73(2) for a determination.
72 Determination of disputes by Authority
A party to a dispute may apply to the Authority for a determination of that dispute by lodging an application in the manner prescribed by the regulations (if any), and paying the fee prescribed by the regulations (if any).
Subsection (1) does not apply to a dispute about an exemption from a term of an occupational contract under section 54.
The Authority must determine the dispute as if it were determining a dispute under the Employment Relations Act 2000, and, for that purpose,—
section 159 of that Act applies (taking into account the concept of good faith adopted under this Act); and
sections 159A to 178 of that Act apply with any necessary modifications.
The Authority has exclusive jurisdiction to make determinations for the purposes of this section.
73 Application to fix disputed terms
This section applies if bargaining parties remain at a standstill in relation to 1 or more terms (disputed terms), with the effect that bargaining is prevented from being completed and a collective contract is unable to proceed to ratification.
A party may apply for the Authority to fix the disputed terms by lodging an application in the manner prescribed by the regulations (if any), and paying the fee prescribed by the regulations (if any).
The Authority has exclusive jurisdiction to make determinations fixing terms in occupational contracts.
74 Use of final offer arbitration process to fix disputed terms
The Authority may fix disputed terms in a collective contract only if—
the circumstances described in section 73(1) exist; and
facilitation under section 71 has taken place and has not resolved the disputed terms; and
the Authority is satisfied that the parties have reached agreement on all terms of the collective contract, other than the disputed terms; and
the Authority is satisfied that the parties have made sufficient efforts to resolve the disputed terms through mediation and facilitation.
The Authority must use the final offer arbitration process set out in Schedule 4 to fix the disputed terms in a collective contract.
Except to the extent that the final offer arbitration process differs, when the Authority is determining an application under section 73(2),—
section 159 of the Employment Relations Act 2000 applies (taking into account the concept of good faith adopted under this Act); and
sections 159A to 178 of that Act apply with any necessary modifications.
Despite subsection (2), the bargaining parties may otherwise settle the disputed terms before the Authority fixes them, provided the manner in which they do so does not involve a third party fixing the terms of an occupational contract (in breach of section 73(3)).
75 Effect of Authority fixing terms
If the Authority has used the final offer arbitration process to fix disputed terms in a draft occupational contract, the Authority must then assess the draft occupational contract for suitability for ratification in accordance with section 47.
If the Authority has used the final offer arbitration process to fix disputed terms in a draft enterprise contract, the contract is binding and enforceable as if it had been ratified as required by section 63 and signed by the parties under section 31(1)﻿(b).
Terms in a collective contract that are fixed by the Authority may be varied by the parties in accordance with section 53 or 68 (as applicable).
76 Challenges to determinations
A party to a matter before the Authority who is dissatisfied with the Authority’s determination of it may elect to have the matter heard by the court, unless subsection (3) applies.
For the purposes of a hearing authorised under subsection (1), the Employment Relations Act 2000 applies subject to the modifications to the provisions of that Act specified in the following paragraphs:
sections 178A, 179, and 180 to 222F apply with any necessary modifications:
sections 179A to 179C do not apply.
A party to a final offer arbitration determination of the Authority may only challenge the decision by way of judicial review under section 77.
77 Review of decisions
Section 194 of the Employment Relations Act 2000 applies, with any necessary modifications, to the exercise, refusal to exercise, or proposed or purported exercise of a statutory power or statutory power of decision (as those terms are defined by section 4 of the Judicial Review Procedure Act 2016) conferred by or under this Act.
 Subpart 2 — Penalties and offence
78 Jurisdiction concerning penalties
The Authority has full and exclusive jurisdiction to deal with all actions for the recovery of penalties for a breach of any provision of this Act for which a penalty is provided under this Act.
An action for the recovery of a penalty under this Act may only be taken in accordance with this subpart.
The action must be brought by way of an application under section 72 to the Authority for a determination.
In determining an appropriate penalty for a breach, the Authority or the court must have regard to all relevant matters, including—
the purpose of this Act; and
the nature and extent of the breach or involvement in the breach; and
whether the breach was intentional, inadvertent, or negligent; and
the nature and extent of any loss or damage suffered by any person, or gains made or losses avoided by the person in breach or the person involved in the breach, because of the breach or involvement in the breach; and
whether the person in breach or the person involved in the breach has paid an amount of compensation, reparation, or restitution, or has taken other steps to avoid or mitigate any actual or potential adverse effects of the breach; and
the circumstances in which the breach, or involvement in the breach, took place, including the vulnerability of any screen production worker concerned; and
whether the person in breach or the person involved in the breach has previously been found by the Authority or the court in proceedings under this Act, or any other enactment, to have engaged in any similar conduct.
The Authority or the court may order that the whole or any part of any penalty recovered must be paid to any person.
Subsection (1) is subject to—
any determination made by the Authority under section 72 to which section 177 or 178 of the Employment Relations Act 2000 applies (which allow for the referral or removal of certain matters to the court); and
any right to have the matter heard by the court under section 76.
79 Recovery of penalties
This section applies for the purposes of a determination under section 78.
An action for the recovery of a penalty may be brought,—
in the case of a breach relating to an individual contract, by anyone to whom the contract applies who is affected by the breach:
in the case of a breach during bargaining for a collective contract, by any party to the bargaining who is affected by the breach:
in the case of a breach relating to a collective contract, by anyone to whom the contract applies who is affected by the breach:
in any other case, by any person in relation to whom the breach is alleged to have taken place:
by a worker organisation or an engager organisation on behalf of any person referred to in paragraphs (a) to (d), if the person consents.
A breach by an engager of the requirements of section 54 must be treated as a breach of a collective contract for the purposes of subsection (2)﻿(c).
A person who is liable to a penalty under this Act is liable,—
in the case of an individual, to a penalty not exceeding $10,000:
in the case of a body corporate, to a penalty not exceeding $20,000.
A claim for 2 or more penalties against the same person may be joined in the same action.
In any claim for a penalty, the Authority or the court (as the case may be) may give judgment for the total amount claimed, or any amount, not exceeding the maximum specified in subsection (4), or the Authority or the court may dismiss the action.
The Authority or the court may order payment of a penalty by instalments, but only if the financial position of the person paying the penalty requires it.
An action for the recovery of a penalty under this Act must be commenced within 12 months after the earlier of the following:
the date on which the cause of action first became known to the person bringing the action:
the date on which the cause of action should reasonably have become known to the person bringing the action.
80 Penalty for obstructing or delaying Authority investigation
A person is liable to a penalty under this Act if the person, without sufficient cause, obstructs or delays an Authority investigation, including by failing to attend as a party before an Authority investigation (if required).
The power to award a penalty under subsection (1) may be exercised by the Authority—
of its own motion; or
on the application of any party to the investigation.
81 Offence to mislead Authority or Registrar
A person commits an offence if the person does or says anything, or omits to do or say anything, with the intention of misleading or attempting to mislead—
the Registrar: or
the Authority, in the performance of its functions under subpart 3 of Part 4.
A person who commits an offence under this section is liable on conviction by the court to a fine not exceeding $5,000.
 Part 6 — Miscellaneous provisions and amendments to other Acts
 Subpart 1 — Registration as worker organisation or engager organisation
82 Incorporated society may apply to register as worker organisation or engager organisation
An incorporated society may apply to the Registrar to be registered as a worker organisation or an engager organisation provided it is entitled to be registered under section 83.
An application must be made in the manner prescribed by the regulations (if any) and must be accompanied by—
a copy of the society’s certificate of incorporation under the Incorporated Societies Act 1908 or the Incorporated Societies Act 2022; and
a copy of the society’s rules as registered under that Act; and
a statutory declaration made by an officer of the society setting out the reasons why the society is entitled to be registered (in terms of section 83(1)﻿(a) or (b)).
83 When society entitled to be registered
An incorporated society is entitled to be registered as a worker organisation or an engager organisation if the society—
is a union that, under the Employment Relations Act 2000 or the Trade Unions Act 1908, is registered and holds a current certificate of registration; or
meets the requirements in subsection (2).
The requirements are that—
the object or an object of the society is to promote its members’ collective work interests; and
the society’s rules are democratic, not unreasonable, not unfairly discriminatory or unfairly prejudicial, and not contrary to law; and
the society’s rules contain a provision relating to the process for holding 1 or more secret ballots for the purposes of this Act; and
the society is independent of, and is constituted and operates at arm’s length from,—
any engager or engager organisation, if the society is seeking to be registered as a worker organisation; and
any worker organisation, if the society is seeking to be registered as an engager organisation.
In deciding whether a society is entitled to be registered, the Registrar may rely on the statutory declaration made by its officer under section 82(2)﻿(c).
84 Registration
The Registrar must register an incorporated society as a worker organisation or an engager organisation, as the case may be, and provide it with a certificate of registration that contains the information prescribed in the regulations (if any), if the society—
applies to be registered in accordance with section 82; and
is entitled to be registered under section 83.
A certificate of registration is conclusive evidence that—
the society is registered as a worker organisation or an engager organisation under this Act (on and from the date stated in the certificate); and
the society has complied with all the requirements of this Act relating to its registration as such an organisation.
The Registrar must—
keep, in any manner that the Registrar thinks fit, a register of registered organisations for the purposes of this section; and
maintain a list of worker organisations and engager organisations on an Internet site maintained by, or on behalf of, the department.
85 Annual return of members
Each worker organisation or engager organisation must, no later than 1 June in each calendar year, deliver an annual return of members to the Registrar for registration.
The annual return must—
state how many members the organisation had as at 1 March in that year; and
be delivered in the manner prescribed in the regulations (if any).
If a worker organisation has delivered, or will deliver, information required under this section to the Registrar of Unions under section 16 of the Employment Relations Act 2000, the organisation may permit the Registrar to obtain that information from the Registrar of Unions.
86 Cancellation of registration
The Registrar may cancel the registration of a worker organisation or an engager organisation, but only if—
the organisation applies to the Registrar to cancel its registration; or
the Authority makes an order directing the Registrar to cancel its registration.
The Authority may make an order for the purposes of subsection (1)﻿(b) only if the organisation has ceased to meet the relevant requirements of section 83.

 Access to workplaces
89 Access to workplaces
A representative of a worker organisation is entitled to enter a workplace for 1 or more of the following purposes:
purposes related to the work of—
the organisation’s members who are screen production workers; or
any other screen production worker to whom a collective contract that the organisation is bargaining for may apply; or
any other screen production worker to whom a collective contract that the organisation is a signatory party to applies:
purposes related to the health and safety of any screen production worker at that workplace who is not a member of the worker organisation if the worker has asked for the organisation’s assistance in relation to the matter:
purposes related to the worker organisation’s business.
Entitlement to access is subject to the representative—
requesting and obtaining consent to enter the workplace under section 90(1); and
satisfying the requirements of sections 91 and 92.
A purpose is within the scope of subsection (1)﻿(a) if it relates to 1 or more of the following:
participation in bargaining for a collective contract:
dealing with matters concerning the health and safety of the workers:
monitoring compliance with the operation of a collective contract:
monitoring compliance with this Act:
with the consent of a worker, dealing with matters relating to that worker’s individual contract, proposed individual contract, terms and conditions of work, or proposed terms and conditions of work.
A purpose is within the scope of subsection (1)﻿(c) if it relates to 1 or more of the following:
discussing the organisation’s business with its members:
seeking to recruit screen production workers as members of the organisation:
providing information about the organisation and its membership to any screen production worker at the workplace.
A discussion in a workplace between a screen production worker and a representative under this section must not exceed a reasonable duration.
An engager must not deduct any amount payable to a worker for the time the worker is in a discussion referred to in subsection (5).
90 Representative of worker organisation must obtain consent to enter workplace
Before entering a workplace, a representative of a worker organisation entitled to enter in accordance with section 89 must request and obtain the consent of the person in control of the workplace.
The person in control may refuse consent only if—
the person reasonably believes the representative does not satisfy the criteria for access under section 89(1); or
the entry would unreasonably impede screen production activities to the extent that—
the entry would prevent production or require it to be temporarily discontinued (for example, if sensitive material is being produced and access to the workplace might require the shooting of a particular scene to be stopped in order to protect the material); and
the impediment could not be overcome by imposing on the representative conditions or further conditions (as the case may be) under section 91(2).
The person in control must advise the representative, in writing, of the person’s decision, including any reasonable conditions imposed on the entry in accordance with section 91(2), as soon as is reasonably practicable, but no later than the day after the date on which the request is received (and must give reasons if the person refuses consent).
The person in control may revoke consent to enter the workplace if—
there is a change in circumstances and entry to the workplace or any part of the workplace might unreasonably impede screen production activities in the manner described in subsection (2)﻿(b); or
the representative is no longer entitled to enter the workplace because the purpose entitling the representative to access under section 89(1) no longer applies; or
the representative does not comply with any reasonable conditions imposed on the entry under section 91(2).
If consent is revoked, the person in control must give the representative written reasons for the revocation no later than the day after the date on which consent is revoked.
91 Conditions relating to access
A representative of a worker organisation entitled to enter a workplace may do so if the representative believes, on reasonable grounds,—
that a screen production worker referred to in section 89(1)﻿(a) or (b) is working or normally works at the workplace; or
that the entry is related to the organisation’s business.
A representative must comply with any reasonable condition of entry that the person in control of the workplace may impose in relation to 1 or more of the following purposes:
minimising disruption to screen production activity:
protecting commercially sensitive or confidential information:
managing risks to the health and safety of any person at the workplace:
maintaining security.
92 Additional requirements relating to access
A representative of a worker organisation entitled to enter a workplace must, at the time of the initial entry and, if requested by the person in control of the workplace, at any time after entering the workplace,—
state the purpose of the entry (in terms of section 89(1)); and
produce evidence of consent to entry; and
produce evidence of the representative’s identity and authority to represent the worker organisation concerned.
A representative who fails to produce any information required under subsection (1) may be refused access.
If a representative with consent to enter a workplace is unable, despite reasonable efforts, to find, at the time of entering the workplace, the person in control, the representative must leave in a prominent place at the workplace a written statement of—
the representative’s identity; and
the worker organisation represented; and
the date and time of entry; and
the purpose or purposes of entry (in terms of section 89).
93 Penalty for certain acts in relation to entering workplace
A person in control of a workplace is liable to a penalty under this Act if the person,—
without lawful excuse under section 90(2), refuses consent for entry to a workplace by a representative of a worker organisation; or
without lawful excuse under section 90(4), revokes consent for entry to a workplace by a representative of a worker organisation; or
imposes conditions on entry to a workplace other than in accordance with section 91(2); or
without lawful excuse, otherwise obstructs a representative of a worker organisation entitled to enter a workplace from entering the workplace or doing anything reasonably necessary for or incidental to the purpose for entering.
A representative of a worker organisation is liable to a penalty under this Act if the representative—
wilfully enters a workplace in breach of section 91(1); or
having lawfully entered a workplace under section 91(1), wilfully fails to comply with any reasonable condition of entry imposed under section 91(2); or
wilfully fails to comply with section 92(1) or (3).

 Matters relating to occupational groups
96 Occupational groups
The Governor-General may, by Order in Council made on the recommendation of the Minister, amend Schedule 2.
An order may insert in or delete from Schedule 2 categories of screen production workers as occupational groups for the purposes of identifying those screen production workers to whom occupational contracts may apply.
If an order has the effect of deleting an occupational group that comprises screen workers to whom an existing occupational contract applies, the order must provide that it comes into force on a date that is after the date on which the existing contract expires.
Before recommending the making of an order, the Minister must—
consult (or be satisfied that the chief executive has consulted on the Minister’s behalf) persons that appear to the Minister or the chief executive to be likely to have an interest in the order; and
be satisfied that the amendment to Schedule 2 will not have the effect that work done by a screen production worker does not fall within any occupational group.
An Order in Council made under this section is secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).
97 Further matters relating to change in occupational groups
This section applies if an Order in Council made under section 96 creates a new occupational group in Schedule 2.
Bargaining for and ratification of an occupational contract in relation to the new occupational group may be carried out (in accordance with subpart 3 of Part 4) at any time after the order comes into force.
Subsection (4) applies to a screen production worker who is covered by an occupational contract (the current contract) but who, once any collective contract referred to in subsection (2) comes into force, will be covered by that contract (the new collective contract).
The new collective contract is deemed to apply to the worker from the date on which it comes into force, irrespective of whether—
the current contract is still in force; and
the terms of the new collective contract are less favourable to the worker than those of the current contract.
 Regulations
98 Regulations
The Governor-General may, by Order in Council, make regulations for 1 or more of the following purposes:
providing for anything this Act says may or must be provided for by regulations; and
providing for anything incidental that is necessary for carrying out, or giving full effect to, this Act.
Regulations made under subsection (1) for the purposes of a section of this Act that requires a thing to be done in a prescribed manner may prescribe—
by whom, when, where, and how the thing must be done:
the form, format, method, or medium that must be used in connection with doing the thing:
what information or other evidence or documents must be provided in connection with the thing:
requirements with which information, evidence, or documents that are provided in connection with the thing must comply.
Regulations made under this section are secondary legislation (see Part 3 of the Legislation Act 2019 for publication requirements).
 Review of Act
99 Review of Act
The Minister must, before the fifth anniversary of the commencement of this Act,—
commence a review of its operation; and
prepare a report on that review.
The Minister must ensure that, during the review, the people and organisations that the Minister thinks appropriate are consulted.
The Minister must present the report to the House of Representatives as soon as practicable after it has been completed.
