## (2) Fork register (the R4 shape; one record `Interpretation of Articles 15 to 20`)

One record, `Interpretation of Articles 15 to 20` (module `g6-interpretation`), carries every fork as one field, `TYPICALLY` the reading taken. Every public decision that depends on a fork takes the record as an ordinary `GIVEN` and delegates by `CONSIDER` to one private implementation per reading; `the readings taken` is the record with every fork read as taken, and `the readings taken but with F6.n read as` r builds the record with fork n read as r and every other fork as taken (L4 has no record-update operator). ### F6.7: which governors Article 18(2) binds

- Provisions: 18(2), 2(1)(i), 2(8); trap T19.
- Reading A (taken): `only the governors of unincorporated bodies within Article 2(1)(i)`. Licensed by: src:468: 'A governor of a registered charity, that is an unincorporated body or association of persons falling within Article 2(1)(i), must in the execution of his or her duties ...'. The words 'that is an unincorporated body ...' qualify 'registered charity', and paragraph (1), which binds every governor, has no such qualification.
- Reading B: `every governor of a registered charity`. Licensed by: The heading of Article 18 ('General duties of governors of registered charities') and the idea that due diligence and the prudent-person standard are general; the relative clause is read as descriptive, not restrictive.
- Why A is taken: It is the text. If every governor was meant the qualification would be surplusage, and Article 18(1) shows the drafter knew how to bind every governor. 'Relevant unincorporated entity' (Article 2(8)) is a narrower class used only for the definition of governor; paragraph (2) says Article 2(1)(i), so every body of that kind is caught.

## (2a) Readings taken that are not forks

- **"Before the date of the giving of the notice" (Article 17(4)) is strictly before:** an act on the day of the notice is not protected, an act on the day before it is. The text says 'before the date', which a day-granular model reads as an earlier day; there is no second reading to offer, so it is not a fork (the boundary is tested on both sides).
- **"Takes effect from the past date" (Article 17(3)) includes the past date itself:** the entity is deregistered on that day, so property acquired on it is not acquired 'before it was deregistered' (F6.4), and the remaining property is that acquired on a strictly earlier day.
- **"After deregistering" and the remaining property are not the same instant as "since a past date":** 'the grounds have existed since a past date' (Article 17(2)(b)) is compared with the date the notice specifies by equality, and with the date of the notice by 'earlier than' (assumption on 'past date' above).
- **The day of a report is inside the ban of Article 19(10) on every reading of F6.10.** Dates have no hour; an act on the day of the report cannot be placed before or after it, and the ban carries a criminal penalty, so the reading that protects the Law's purpose is taken. (Cf. F6.20, F6.22, where the same difficulty is a fork because the two answers are both natural.)
- **A governor who has made both reports is no longer in breach of Article 19(2) on later days.** The duty is to report; once reported, nothing is outstanding. The late report was a breach when it fell due, and Article 19(10) bars the governor from then on. The alternative (a breach that never ends) would bar a permitted governor by Article 19(8) for ever, which no reading of the Article supports, so it is not offered as a fork.
- **"A past date" (Article 17(2)(b)) is a date before the date of the notice.** A date on the day of the notice, or later, is not past, and is not retrospective. The word 'past' is in the text; the comparison is the encoder's.
- **Article 17(4) is vacuously satisfied where the deregistration is not retrospective**: there is no operation of paragraph (3) to save an act from.
- **The effective date of a deregistration without retrospective effect is the date of the deregistration** (the notice, or the decision on an application). The Law does not say more; `Art 17(3) the date deregistered` returns it, and the caller sets the date from G7's rule on the service of notices (IP34).
- **"Must retain for 10 years" (Article 17(1)(a)) is encoded as a prohibition on discarding while the ten years run** (`SHANT ... WITHIN n`), because a retention is a continuing duty whose breach is discarding; the days of the period are computed from the dates, never typed.
- **FULFILLED is used for "nothing is owed / no power arises"** where a duty or power is conditional and the condition is false (the skill's `ELSE FULFILLED`). It is not a finding that something was done. A rule that returns FULFILLED for a false condition does not say the act is forbidden; no prohibition is created by silence.
- **Alternatives are composed with ROR, each unguarded** (Article 15(3)(a) or (b); Article 19(9)(e)(i) or (ii); Article 17(11) withdraw or proceed): an unguarded alternative is not satisfied by default, so the choice is not fulfilled trivially (the skill's ROR gotcha). An alternative that is unavailable on the facts is left out of the ROR, not guarded.
- **The duties of Articles 15(2), 17(6), 18(1), 18(2), 19(7) and 19(9)(a) state no period and carry no `WITHIN`**: a trace leaves them standing, and no breach can be shown for them. Writing a period would invent one (phrasebook 5.9).
- **Persons are identified by name** (a STRING field of each record) where a rule needs 'another governor' or 'the same person' (phrasebook 6.6); names are assumed unique within one charity's board.
- **Article 16(1)(c): a required steps notice served on a governor is not the entity's.** The entity has 'failed to comply with a required steps notice' only for notices served on the charity (`served on` = `the registered charity`).
- **Article 19(1)(a): the link 'led to the service of a required steps notice' is a determination**, supplied as a fact; the encoding also requires that some notice has been served (IP19 not empty).
- **Article 19(5): compliance means the statement AND holding the declarations.** A false statement does not comply with a duty to state that one holds declarations.
- **The period of a disqualification order begins on the first day the court directs, and the 15 years are counted from that day** (normally the date of the order, as the survey reads it); the record has the first and the last day of the period as the court directs them.
- **"Immediately before deregistration" (Article 17(6))**: the caller passes `the registered charity` as the register held it immediately before the date deregistered; the encoding does not choose between the register at the effective date and at the notice (see section (7), question on retrospective effect).

- **`Preliminary notice of Article 16(1)`, `Facts about the grounds of Article 16(1)`, `Route by which the Commissioner deregistered the entity`, `Deregistration of the entity`, `Property of the charity`**: Group-local nouns of Articles 15 to 17. G3 reads the route as the basis of the deregistration (IP32) and G8 the Commissioner's decision (IP27); both come out of functions, not records, so no move is required.
- **Dates of notices: `date of the deregistration`, `date of the preliminary notice`, `given on` of `Preliminary notice of Article 16(1)`**: These are DATEs the caller supplies. G7's `Art 38 the date on which the notice is served` (IP34, fork F7.7) fixes how the date of a notice is determined; the INTEGRATOR should set these three fields from it. G6 does not read `date served` of `Required steps notice` (it reads `steps to be taken by`).
- **`Registered charity`: the field `date deregistered`**: Set by the INTEGRATOR from `Art 17(3) the date deregistered` (IP7 says G6 sets it).
- **`Governor` carries `reportable matters` as an input list; G6 produces it**: `Art 19(1) the reportable matters of the person` fills it (IP21); Articles 19(2), (3), (4) read it. The INTEGRATOR passes the same list to both.
- **The Interpretation record**: `Interpretation of Articles 15 to 20` has one field typed `Unit of a term of imprisonment` (IP35); the INTEGRATOR sets it from the one value shared with G7, G8 and G9. Two fields share the enumeration `How a number of years is counted from a day`; the INTEGRATOR may set them alike.

## (6) Contradictions in the source (found by a separate pass after encoding)

- **Article 19(9)(c) and Article 20(1)(c) against Articles 11(4) and 11(5) (trap T10; G4 owns the collision)**: Article 11(4) says the Commissioner MUST register an applicant who complies and is satisfied of six matters; Article 19(9)(c) says the Commissioner MUST NOT grant the application until a reported fitness issue is determined and any resulting order or notice complied with; Article 20(1)(c) lets the court order that the entity must not be registered. None of 11(4)(a) to (f) mentions either. Article 19(9)(e)(i) even has the Commissioner 'notify the entity that the application will be refused', while 11(5) allows refusal only if 'not so satisfied'. Discriminating case: Every one of 11(4)(a) to (f) is satisfied and a governor's fitness issue is undetermined: MUST register and MUST NOT grant. Handling: G6 produces the two facts as inputs for G4: IP23 (`Art 19(9)(c) bars granting the application`) and IP24 (`Art 20(1)(c) the court orders that the entity must not be registered`); G4 registers the reading (its F4.4). No G6 rule decides the collision.
- **Article 19(9)(e)(i) against Article 28(3) (trap T11)**: The Commissioner must serve a required steps notice requiring a charity to suspend or remove the governor; Article 28(3) makes a required steps notice served on an organized religious charity invalid to the extent that it requires a governor to be suspended or removed. Discriminating case: A governor of an organized religious charity has a reportable matter that the Commissioner finds disqualifying, and the issue is not to be determined by the court. Handling: Fork F6.15 (A taken: Article 28(3) prevails; B: the duty stands, the notice is void to that extent; C: the Law does not say, a named refusal). All three are callable and tested.
- **Article 16(1) beside Article 16(2) (trap T15)**: Both paragraphs reach an entity registered as a charity that has ceased to exist and also no longer meets the charity test; (1) is a power after a preliminary notice, (2) a duty with no notice. Discriminating case: An entity that has ceased to exist and fails the charity test, no preliminary notice given. Handling: Fork F6.2 (A taken: Article 16(2) governs alone).
- **Article 17(2)(c) and 17(4): 'the notice under Article 16(1) or (2)' (trap T15)**: A cross-reference that does not pick out one thing: Article 16(1) has two notices (a preliminary notice giving reasons and inviting objections, and the written notice by which the Commissioner deregisters). Discriminating case: Preliminary notice 1 March 2026, deregistration notice 1 April 2026, past date 1 January 2025; an act on 15 March 2026. Handling: Fork F6.3 (A taken: the deregistering notice).
- **Article 17(3) 'for all purposes' against Article 17(1)(a), 17(5), 17(6) and Article 13(1)**: A retrospective termination dates the deregistration in the past 'for all purposes', but the Commissioner's duty runs 'after deregistering', the remaining property is what was acquired 'before it was deregistered', the preserved purposes are those 'immediately before deregistration', and Article 13(1) says paragraphs (2) to (4) of Article 13 'only cease to apply if the entity is deregistered'. Which instant is 'deregistered' is not stated; with a past date more than ten years before the notice, the ten years of 17(1)(a) would be over before the notice was given. Discriminating case: A donation received between the past date and the notice (17(5)); a past date of 2010 and a notice of 2026 (17(1)(a)); an amendment of the purposes made in the interval (17(6), Article 13(3)). Handling: Forks F6.4 and F6.5 (A taken: the effective date for property; the date of the notice for the retention). The snapshot of the purposes is the caller's input (section (2a)).
- **Article 8(6) and Article 17(1)(a) (trap T14; G3 owns 8(6))**: Two ten-year retention duties on overlapping material (a copy of the constitution, annual returns and so on; and 'the information registered'), neither saying when the ten years start; 17(1)(b) is joined to (a) by 'and' but states no period. Discriminating case: A constitution received in 2019 by a charity still registered in 2030: may the 2019 copy be discarded (the period runs from receipt), or not (it runs from deregistration)? A number released in year 11. Handling: Forks F6.5, F6.6 (here) and F3.1 (G3). The integrator states the pair of readings.
- **Article 19(8) against Article 19(10) and (11) (trap T9)**: Paragraph (8) forbids a person to act as a governor while in breach of (2) or (4); (10) forbids it after reporting, unless permitted; only (10) has an offence, in (11), yet the three are of one kind. Discriminating case: An unreported bankruptcy and the governor acts: barred by (8), no offence under (11). Handling: Fork F6.11 (A taken: no offence under (8)); `Art 19(8) the penalty for contravening Article 19(8)` is callable on the other reading.
- **Article 19(4) 'when notified by the charity' (a trigger the Law does not require anyone to pull)**: Nothing in the Law, including Article 13(7), requires the charity to notify its governors that it is preparing its annual return; yet the duty of each governor to declare is triggered only by the notification, and the charity must state in the return (Article 19(5)) that it has declarations from all governors who have not reported. Discriminating case: A charity files its annual return without ever notifying its governors: no governor is in breach of 19(4), the charity cannot truthfully make the 19(5) statement, and so contravenes 19(5) and must explain under 19(7). Handling: Fork F6.21 records the timing; the missing duty to notify is reported here and in section (7).
- **Article 18(4)(c) against Article 1 'misconduct' and Article 2(10): a definition that uses itself**: Article 1 says a reference to a governor engaging in misconduct is read in accordance with Article 18(4); Article 18(4)(c) makes a governor's misconduct depend on another governor's engaging in misconduct. Discriminating case: Governors who only assist each other and nobody has a base misconduct; or a chain of three. Handling: A least fixed point (the depth is the number of governors) on the reading taken; fork F6.24 for whether the other governor's misconduct is a condition at all. Tests: the chain of three, the cycle of two.
- **Article 17(9) against Article 14(1)**: Article 17(9) lends the Article 14 powers to the court 'as if [the entity] were still registered' but changes who may apply: the Attorney General or a governor, not the Commissioner (Article 14(1) names the Commissioner and the Attorney General). Discriminating case: The Commissioner wishes to apply to protect the remaining property of a deregistered entity. Handling: Encoded as the text says: the Commissioner is not an applicant (a test asserts it). Reported as a possible omission in section (7).
- **Article 19(1)(g) against the commencement of Article 22 (trap T30; the integrator's)**: Article 19(1)(g) refers to Article 2(10)(b), which cites an excepted foreign charity 'within the meaning of Article 22'; Part 5 is in force from 1 May 2018 and Article 22 only from 1 January 2019. Discriminating case: A conviction on 1 July 2018 of an offence in relation to an excepted foreign charity. Handling: Not decided here: Part 5's commencement is the integrator's wrapper (PLAN note 2); `Art 19(1)(g)` takes `offence within Article 2(10)(b)` as a fact.
- **Heading of Article 20 against its paragraphs (3) to (6)**: The heading says 'as to fitness of governor'; paragraphs (3) to (6) concern 'a person', who need not be a governor. Discriminating case: A person who is not a governor is subject to a disqualification order. Handling: The record `Disqualification order` and Article 20(6) are about 'a person', not a governor.
- **Article 15(3)(b) against Article 33(2)(a) and Schedule 1 paragraph 4(3)(b) (traps T12 and T17; G8's and G9's)**: A refusal to deregister on application is not among the decisions Article 33 makes appealable (33(2)(a) lists a decision 'to deregister'), and Schedule 1 4(3)(b) reserves 'whether to refuse' under 15(3) from staff while Article 16 is reserved as 'whether to deregister'. Discriminating case: A charity applies to deregister and is refused on grounds relating to its proposals; a member of staff, under an authorization, deregisters an entity on its own application. Handling: G6 supplies the decision (IP27) as a subject and an outcome (`Art 15(3) the decision of the Commissioner`) so that G8 and G9 can read either outcome; the readings are theirs (F8.4, F9.8).

## (8) What the sources do not answer

1. **The text of the Law before 1 January 2019**, and so what Articles 15 to 20 said when Part 4 and Part 5 came into force on 1 May 2018 (the nine versions begin on 1 January 2019). G7's wrapper decides whether a unit is in force on a day; nothing in G6 does.
2. **Every Order and set of Regulations** that G6's provisions refer to: the circumstances prescribed under Article 16(1)(d) and the further descriptions of reportable matter under Article 19(1)(h) are inputs; the powers to make them are encoded as MAY rules of the Minister. The Orders under the Law (R&O.59/2018 and so on) are not among the inputs.
3. **Any period for the duties of Article 15(2), 17(6), 18(1), 18(2), 19(7), 19(9)(a) and for the Attorney General's power in 17(11)**, and for the Commissioner's decision under 15(3): none is stated, so none is written (phrasebook 5.9).
4. **The number of days that count as 'promptly'** (Article 19(2)), **'a reasonable time'** (16(1), 18(4)(d)(ii), 19(4)); the encoding takes the determination as an input or, for the counting readings of F6.12, a number the caller supplies, and REFUSES by name when it is not supplied.
5. **The maximum fine** for the offences of Articles 19(11) and 20(6): the Law says 'a fine' and no maximum; `a fine with no maximum stated in the Law` is the data. The amount of 'level 3 on the standard scale' is not in the sources and is not used in this scope.
6. **When a deregistration without retrospective effect takes effect**: the date of the notice, of its service, or of the entry on the register. The caller supplies the date; Article 38 (G7) fixes the service of notices.
7. **What 'spent' means for a conviction** (Article 19(1)(f), (g)): it belongs to the Rehabilitation of Offenders rules, not among the sources; a fact per conviction.
8. **The meaning of 'fit and proper person'** (Article 19(9)(a), (13)), **'unfit'** (Article 20(4)), **'expedient in the public interest'** (Article 20(3)), **'proportionate'** (Article 16(1)), **'satisfied'**: determinations of the Commissioner, the Attorney General or the court, supplied as facts.
9. **What a condition on a permission or an order may be** (Article 19(9)(d), (e)(ii), 20(2)(b)), and the content of an order of the court (Article 14(3) lists examples; Article 20(1) is open): an input.
10. **The Interpretation (Jersey) Law 1954**, which would settle how a period is counted: not among the inputs. The counting conventions are forks (F6.13, F6.14, F6.17, F6.18, F6.20, F6.21, F6.22).
11. **Who prosecutes** the offences of Articles 19(11) and 20(6), and the procedure of the court and of any application beyond what the Articles state.
12. **Whether a deregistration decision is suspended by an appeal**, and whether a refusal to deregister is appealable: Part 8 (G8) and Article 34.
13. **What the register held 'immediately before deregistration'** when the deregistration is retrospective (Article 17(6)): the register entry is the caller's input.

## (9b) Mutation sweep (`work/g6/mutsweep.py`)

`work/g6/mutsweep.py` applies ONE mutation at a time to a copy of a rule module (a comparison swapped for its neighbour, AND for OR, TRUE for FALSE, outside names, strings and comments) and runs the test modules that import it; a mutant is *killed* if any assertion fails or any trace leaves its `-- EXPECT:`. A survivor is either an equivalent mutant or logic no test pins down.

Each pointed at logic that only one side of a boundary, or only one of two alternative limbs, had been tested (an `AT MOST` where the day of the order itself is the day asked about; a `NOTHING` branch of a helper never reached; `OR` of two grounds tested only with both true). A test was added for each (each module's sweep copies the deposit when that module's sweep starts, and the tests were edited only after the sweep of the module they test had begun, so each first-sweep survivor was found against the tests as they stood before it), and the sweep was run again from scratch: every one of the 172 mutants is now killed. Before the sweep, a one-off run of `work/g6/mutate.sh` (ten hand-picked changes, one of them a no-op control) killed the nine real mutants, and the control survived, as a control must. What this does not show: that a test's expected value is right (each was decided from the Law's text; see the re-read in the encoder's final report), and nothing about logic the mutation operators do not touch (list functions, record construction, the order of arguments).
