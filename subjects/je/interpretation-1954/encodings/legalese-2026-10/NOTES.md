## Read first: where the text did not say what the brief said

The brief asks for this list first.
`RAW` is `l_17_1954.current.txt`; `V1` is `l_17_1954_20210927.txt`.

- **B1. The proviso to Article 3 is narrower than the brief says.**
  Brief, claim 9: the proviso "protects a body corporate from liability for anything done before the date of the commencement of the Law".
  The text (RAW line 19, the same line in all seven texts) protects a body corporate only against "any criminal proceedings to which that body corporate would not have been liable before that date".
  The encoding follows the text: `the proviso to Article 3 withholds liability` is about criminal proceedings.
- **B2. The "extended or applied" limb of Article 9(3) is not new in V2.**
  Brief, claim 10: V1 says "from time to time"; V2 says "amended, revised or otherwise consolidated ... from time to time" and "includes ... as extended or applied under another enactment".

- **B3. The four-item allowance before 2019 is narrower than the evidence it rests on.**

Findings about the Law itself, not about the brief:

- **F1. A silent or "forthwith" enactment made on day D comes into operation at the start of D, before it was made.**
  V1 Article 1(6) (line 10) construes such an enactment as expressed to come into operation "on the day on which it was passed or made"; Article 1(5) (line 9) says an enactment "expressed or construed as being expressed to come into operation on a particular day" comes into operation "immediately on the expiration of the previous day".
  Read together, which the words of 1(5) ("or construed as being expressed") invite, the result is the start of D.
  Tested: `je54-tests-art01-by-date.l4`, section C (made 27 September 2021, silent and "forthwith").
  The Law gives no other time of day, so a question about any other time refuses (`je54-gaps.l4`).
- **F2. `check.sh` cannot catch a locator that quotes the wrong paragraph.**
  `srcrefs.py check --refs` proves that each quoted line is verbatim and that each locator resolves; it does not prove that the quoted line is the paragraph the `@ref` names.
  A one-off audit on 2026-10-28 (section 11) found four `@ref` blocks of Article 8 that quoted the neighbouring limb or range (the bank limb, the limited liability company limb, the administration order limb, and the range `34-37`), three ranges of Article 8 that left out a limb, and one `@ref` that cited Article 1(5) while quoting Article 1(6).

## 1. What is encoded and what is not

The deposit encodes every paragraph of Articles 1 to 18 and of the Schedule (Part 1 and Part 2) of the Interpretation (Jersey) Law 1954 in each of the seven consolidated texts on jerseylaw.je, from 1 January 2019 to the current text, which has run since 19 June 2026 (V1, V2a to V2f), as 32 modules: 15 rule modules (nouns, the vintage table, the rules by Article, the questions a reader asks) and 17 tests modules, three of which (`je54-tests-independent-1.l4` to `-3.l4`) hold the independent test pass (section 10.3).
The text of the Law that governs is an input chosen from a date by the vintage table, never a name the caller must know, and the versions are never merged: each rule takes the text and answers from that text, and a text that is silent answers with `REFUSE`.
Where the Law says "unless the contrary intention appears" or "unless the context otherwise requires", the rule takes that fact as a `BOOLEAN` input and refuses when it is true; Article 11A says "unless the contrary is expressed" and has an input of that name.
43 `@export` questions in `je54-seat.l4` and `je54-seat-powers-and-penalties.l4` put the rules in the reader's words.
Not encoded: the Legislation (Jersey) Law 2021 (its rule for the commencement of enactments made on or after 28 September 2021 is a refusal that names it); the contents of the other enactments the Law mentions (inputs); any text of the Law before 1 January 2019 except the four items the brief allows, answered from 28 March 2003 and labelled "inferred from the endnotes of the 2019 consolidation, not verified against L.16/2003"; the counting of days, weeks, years or calendar months (nothing in the Law, and no function does it); geometry for Article 15 (the way a distance was measured is a noun and the measured distance an input).
The endnotes (Table of Legislation History, Table of Renumbered Provisions, Table of Endnote References) are not provisions; they are evidence for the vintage table and for the pre-2019 rule, and have no coverage row.

## 2. The seat

The seat is the reader of an enactment: the individual who has to know what an enactment means for them, when it bound them, what may be done to them, and whether a notice reached them in time.
Every `@export` is phrased as that person would ask it ("on what day did the enactment come into force", "is service by post deemed effected", "is the event a person becoming bankrupt", "may the offender be prosecuted and punished under this enactment") and every input carries a `@desc` in facts that person could state.
The Law is addressed to no one in particular: it tells the reader how to read enactments.
Officials (the Royal Court, the Viscount, the Juge d'Instruction) appear only inside Article 8 and the Schedule definitions, as parts of the event or of the definition the reader asks about.
The seat is recorded in `encoding.json`.

Four conventions carry through.
They are the ones the Legislation (Jersey) Law 2021 row uses (`subjects/je/legislation-2021/encodings/legalese-2026-10/NOTES.md`, sections 2, 6 fork L12 and 10.3), and they were adopted here on the team lead's instruction of 2026-10-28, after the independent pass (section 10.3).
They are assumed, not ruled.

- A question whether an Article applies, or whether a thing is one that a list or a definition names, may be answered TRUE or FALSE from the Article's own words.
  The one function here that asks only whether an Article applies, `Article 12: the Article applies`, does not use that allowance: it answers TRUE where the trigger is met and refuses where it is not (an act that is an offence under one enactment only, under the customary law only or under none, or two offences under the same enactment); it is not exported, so the independent pass did not test it, and the allowance is not used elsewhere for an Article's reach (fork I7, section 6).
  The allowance is used for list membership: Article 8 lists the events a reference to a person becoming bankrupt covers as a closed list (assumption A4), so an event that is not on it is FALSE; a Schedule definition that says "shall mean" is closed too, so a thing outside it is outside the word.
- A question that asks for a consequence or a permission in the world (may the power be exercised in this way, do the expressions have the same meaning, may the offender be prosecuted and punished under this enactment) refuses outside the Article's reach.
  Outside means an enactment passed on a day the Article does not cover (Articles 10, 11(4) and 16(1)), or a way, act, power, route, purpose, effect or manner the Article does not name (Articles 11(3), 11(4), 11A, 12, 16(1), 17(2) and 17(3)).
- An "includes" or "including" list does not close the word or the power: a thing it does not name is not shut out, and the Law does not say.
  In Articles 11(3), 11A, 17(2) and 17(3) the question refuses; in the Schedule a definition that says "includes" answers `not named by the Schedule, which says includes and so does not shut it out: the word's own meaning decides`, which is a value that says the Schedule does not decide, not FALSE; fork I6 covers the one definition that says both.
- A contrary-intention input that is true makes the rule refuse in every function that takes one, and so do "unless the context otherwise requires" (Articles 5 and 9(3)) and "unless the contrary is expressed" (Article 11A).
  On 2026-10-28 I read the body of each of the 34 rule-module functions that take such an input and each refuses on it, directly or through the function it calls; the one that did not was the restriction of Article 16(1) (finding P08b, section 10.3).

## 6. The fork register

A fork is a place where the Law's words allow more than one reading and the encoding takes one.
Each fork below is an enum input with the default stated, and each alternative is callable and tested.
The defaults of I1 and I2 are the brief's, which marks them "assumed, not ruled"; the defaults of I3 to I6 are my choices, also assumed, not ruled.

### I1. Which day selects the text of the Law that is read for the commencement of an enactment

- **Question.** To read the commencement of an enactment under V1 Article 1(5) and 1(6), which text of the Law governs: the text in force when the enactment came into force, when it was made, or when the question is asked?
- **Licensing text.** V1 lines 9 and 10 say how commencement is read but not which text of the Law applies to an enactment made when another text was in force; the endnote row for L.8/2021 (RAW lines 187 to 190) says Articles 1(5) and 1(6) are deleted from 28 September 2021; no text says what happens to an enactment made before that day and commencing after it.
  The test for (a) is the one the team lead restated on 2026-10-28 from Article 3(6) of the 2021 Law ("Nothing in this Article affects the commencement of any Jersey legislation that had commenced before the commencement of this Part", Part commencing 28 September 2021): it keys on whether the legislation had commenced before that day, not on the day it was made.
  The 2021 Law is not in the deposit, so I have not seen that sentence and rely on the quotation; no function copies Article 3 into this row.
- **Readings.**
  (a) `whether the enactment had come into force before 28 September 2021` (default; the brief's ruling): find the candidate day under V1; if it is before 28 September 2021 V1 governs; if it is on or after it V1 no longer exists and the 2021 Law's Article 3 governs, so the answer is the candidate day with the marker `governed by the 2021 Law, Article 3(2)`; for an enactment made on or after 28 September 2021 the function refuses and names the 2021 Law, Article 3.
  (b) `the day the enactment was passed or made`: the text in force on the day it was made.
  (c) `the day on which the question about the enactment is asked`: the text in force on the day the question is asked.
- **Reading taken and why.** (a), because the brief rules it; the 2021 Law is not in the deposit, so that its Article 3 gives the start of a named day is the brief's claim, unverified.
- **Discriminating case.** On (a) and on (b) the day is the same in every case of `je54-tests-art01-by-date.l4` (A to E: made 20 September 2021 naming 1 October 2021 and 21 September 2021; made 27 September 2021 silent; made 28 September 2021, which refuses on both; the edge of 28 September 2021), and only the governing provision differs.
  For a named day the 2021 Law is said to give the start of that day too (the brief's claim, stated in the header of that tests module and unverified).
  The first case: an order made 20 September 2021 naming 1 October 2021: on (a) the day is 1 October 2021 with the marker for the 2021 Law; on (b) the day is 1 October 2021 on V1's basis; on (c), asked on 25 September 2021, the same day on V1's basis, and asked on 28 October 2026, a refusal (the text then in force has no Articles 1(5) and 1(6)).
- **Callable alternative and test.** The `reading` input of `on what day did the enactment come into force`, `at what moment did the enactment come into force`, `was the enactment in force on a day` and `was the enactment in force at a moment` (and of the functions in `je54-art01-by-date.l4`); tested in `je54-tests-art01-by-date.l4` (cases A to G) and `je54-tests-crosschecks.l4`.

### I2. Which text of Article 8 governs: the day of the event or the day the question is asked

- **Question.** Is an event a person becoming bankrupt according to the text in force on the day of the event, or on the day the question is asked?
- **Licensing text.** Article 8 (RAW line 29): "references to a person becoming bankrupt shall, unless the contrary intention appears, be construed as references to either" the listed events; the list grew in 2022 and 2026 (lines 36 to 38); nothing says which text of the list governs a question about an old event.
- **Readings.** `the day of the event` (default; the brief's ruling) and `the day on which the question about the event is asked`.
- **Reading taken and why.** The day of the event, as the brief directs; the Law is silent and a rule about the event is naturally one about the Law as it stood when the event happened.
- **Discriminating case.** An administration order made on a company on 15 January 2026, asked about on 28 October 2026: limb (ea) arrived on 19 June 2026, so on the first reading the event is not a person becoming bankrupt and on the second it is.
- **Callable alternative and test.** The `selecting` input of `is the event a person becoming bankrupt`; tested in `je54-tests-art08-bankruptcy.l4`, section "Fork I2".
  The unqualified question "which text governs when the event and the question fall under different texts" is answered by a refusal: `Article 8: the text that governs when the day of the event and the day the question is asked fall under different texts` (`je54-gaps.l4`).

### I3. Article 1(2) (V1 only): does the Article fix the day a Law is passed or only bar an earlier day

- **Question.** Is a Law deemed passed on the later of the day it was confirmed by Her Majesty in Council and the day it was registered by the Royal Court, or does the Article only say it is not deemed passed before both?
- **Licensing text.** V1 line 6: "a Law shall not be deemed to have been passed until it has been confirmed by Her Majesty in Council and registered by the Royal Court".
  "Not ... until" bars an earlier day; whether it also fixes the day is not said.
- **Readings.** `the Law is deemed passed on the later of the day it was confirmed and the day it was registered` (default) and `the Article only bars an earlier day: the caller states the day`.
- **Discriminating case.** Confirmed 1 March 2020, registered 15 March 2020, the caller says 4 April 2020: the first reading gives 15 March 2020 and ignores the caller's day; the second gives 4 April 2020, and refuses for 14 March 2020 or for no day.
- **Callable alternative and test.** The `reading` input of `on what day is a Law deemed to have been passed`; tested in `je54-tests-art01-commencement.l4`, section "Article 1(2)".

### I4. Article 7: what governs once the presumption is rebutted

- **Question.** When the contrary is proved, does the time proved govern, or does the Article give no time?
- **Licensing text.** RAW line 27: "unless the contrary is proved, [service is deemed] to have been effected at the time at which the letter would be delivered in the ordinary course of post".
- **Readings.** `the time that is proved` (default) and `the Article gives no time`.
- **Reading taken and why.** The first: the presumption is about the time at which service was effected, and what rebuts it is proof of that time.
- **Discriminating case.** A letter posted Friday 1 March 2024, ordinary-course delivery Monday 4 March 2024 at 09:00, proved received Saturday 2 March 2024 at 14:05: the first reading gives Saturday 2 March 14:05; the second refuses.
- **Callable alternative and test.** The `reading` input of `at what time is service by post deemed effected` and `on what day is service by post deemed effected`; tested in `je54-tests-art07-service-by-post.l4`, section "The presumption is rebuttable".

### I5. Article 3 proviso: "nothing in this Article"

- **Question.** Does the proviso limit only Article 3, or also the Schedule's definition of "person" (inserted by L.16/2003, applying to every enactment, whenever passed or made)?
- **Licensing text.** RAW line 19: "Provided that nothing in this Article shall render any body corporate liable ... to any criminal proceedings to which that body corporate would not have been liable before that date"; RAW line 108: "'person' shall include any body of persons corporate or unincorporated".
- **Readings.** `the proviso limits Article 3 only` (default) and `the proviso also limits the Schedule definition of person`.
- **Reading taken and why.** The first: the proviso says "this Article", and the Schedule is not Article 3.
- **Discriminating case.** A body corporate, an enactment relating to a punishable offence, an act on 1 June 1950 for which it would not then have been liable: on the first reading `person` reaches it "by the Schedule only"; on the second "not at all".
- **Callable alternative and test.** The `reading` input of `does person include this body`; tested in `je54-tests-art02-05-words.l4` and `je54-tests-seat.l4`.

### I6. The Schedule definition of "Magistrate" means and includes

- **Question.** Where a definition says both "shall mean" and "includes", are the things it names all that the word covers?
- **Licensing text.** RAW line 101: "'Magistrate' shall mean the Juge d'Instruction appointed in pursuance of the Loi (1864) concernant la charge de Juge d'Instruction and includes any person exercising the functions of the Juge d'Instruction".
- **Readings.** `the named things are all that the word covers` (default) and `the named things do not exhaust the word`.
- **Reading taken and why.** The first: with "shall mean" the definition is complete, and "includes" names a person who falls within it.
- **Discriminating case.** A thing the Schedule does not name: outside the word on the first reading; "not named by the Schedule, which says includes and so does not shut it out: the word's own meaning decides" on the second.
- **Callable alternative and test.** The `reading` input of `what does the Schedule definition do for the word`; tested in `je54-tests-schedule-definitions.l4`, section "Both (Magistrate), fork I6".

### Considered and not registered

Where I looked: every paragraph of Articles 1 to 18 and the Schedule was read for an alternative reading; these were considered and are not forks, because the Law's words do not create the alternative or the alternative is not credible.

- **Article 13(2)**, "the amount of the fine or a level on the standard scale is not specified" (RAW line 69): read as "neither is specified".
  The other reading (either unspecified) would make a fine specified by level alone unlimited, which cannot be meant.
- **Article 11(4)** (V1), "passed after 28th March 2003": compared by day, so an enactment passed on 28 March 2003 itself is not after it, and the question whether its power may be exercised in a way refuses (findings N20 and N21, section 10.3).
- **Fork I7, whether an applicability clause is exhaustive (the 2021 row's L12): not registered.**
  None of the 14 findings of the independent pass was an applicability question: each asked for a permission or a consequence in the world (section 10.3).
  No function of this encoding answers "does Article 10 apply" or "is this route one that Article 12 names" on its own, so the allowance that FALSE may stand for such a question is not used for them.
  The one function that asks only whether an Article applies is `Article 12: the Article applies`; it answers TRUE where the trigger is met and refuses where it is not, which is the 2021 row's second reading of L12 and not its default, and its own tests assert those refusals (`je54-tests-art12-15-penalties-forms.l4`).
  So the default here is the refusing reading, there is no alternative to call, and a reviewer who wants FALSE outside Article 12's trigger would be asking for I7 with the exhaustive reading as the alternative.
  If a reviewer holds that Article 10 or Article 12 should answer FALSE outside its reach, I7 would be the fork to register, and its default would then be a ruling and not mine to assume.
- **Article 6(2)** (V1), "until the substituted provisions come into operation": at the instant of operation the repealed provisions are no longer in force (assumption A3).
- **Article 8**: the list is read as the complete list ("construed as references to either", and "construed accordingly"; assumption A4).
- **Article 14**, "not materially affecting the substance nor likely to mislead": both are conditions, both are judgement inputs; when either fails the Article neither saves the form nor says it is invalid, and the function says so.
- **Article 1(4)**: "comes into operation" and "comes into force" are the same event (the brief's ruling; assumption A1).
- **Same-day edges against the Law's own commencement** (Article 3 proviso, Article 10): "before the date of the commencement" compares calendar days, so 21 August 1954 is not before it; a question that turns on the hour of that day refuses.

As the team lead reported it to me (I have not read the working file), a fresh Claude Sonnet 5.5 session decided more than 150 scenarios from the text of the Law before it opened any file of the encoding, kept them in a `decisions.md` in the lead's scratch directory (not deposited), then mapped each onto an exported question and wrote it as an `#ASSERT`.
They are in `je54-tests-independent-1.l4` (Articles 1 to 6 and the Schedule definitions of midnight and month, 158 assertions), `je54-tests-independent-2.l4` (Article 7, Article 8, Articles 12 to 15, the dates before 2019 and the author's unsure scenarios, 148) and `je54-tests-independent-3.l4` (Articles 9 to 11A, Articles 16 and 17 and Part 1 of the Schedule, 214).
The pass first arrived as one file of 1160 lines and was split by Article, with no assertion lost: 520 before, 520 after, and the lines of its sections compare equal as a multiset against the unsplit file.
Sections S and U sit in part 2, out of Article order, because they reuse local definitions of the sections K, L and O, which are in that part.
Its status is the same as the encoding's: one reader's answers, not ruled.

The first run, on the encoding as it stood before the work below (a `check.sh` run of 29 modules that was otherwise green), gave 520 assertions in the independent file: 506 satisfied, 14 failed, 0 warnings.

A failing assertion is a finding, and none was deleted.
No independent expected value was changed, and no independent assertion was pointed at another reading: the only changes are the split (each section moved whole into one of three files) and two comment lines at the head of each part.
After the work below the pass is 520 satisfied, 0 failed, 0 refused.

Nine of the 14 are not independent of the encoding's option names.
Their ids carry a letter suffix: N10b, N18b, N42b, O09t, P08b (two assertions), P13b, P15b and P18b.
They were written while the file was being made, after the exported names and the "some other ..." options had been read (the header of the file says so), so they test the encoding's own list against the author's reading of the Law, not the author's unprompted reading.
The other five (M20, N20, N21, P02 and P03) were decided before the encoding was opened.
All 14 have the same cause: the encoding answered a question that asks for a permission or a consequence in the world with a value where the Article says nothing, and the convention of section 2 is that such a question refuses.

None of the 14 is a fork.
Had a finding been an applicability question, the instruction was to keep FALSE, say so here, and register fork I7 with the refusal as the callable alternative and the exhaustive reading (FALSE) as the default, as the 2021 row does for L12.
None was, so I7 is not registered; `Article 12: the Article applies` is the one function that asks only whether an Article applies, and it refuses outside its trigger (section 6), which the lead should verify.

Two sibling decisions follow from the same convention and were not tested by the pass; the team lead should verify them.

- **S1.** Article 16(1) deals only with a Law passed after the commencement of the Law, so a Law passed before 21 August 1954 now refuses (it answered FALSE).
  `Article 16(1): the Law was passed before the commencement of the Law`; own: 1 corrected, 2 added (`v1-arts-6-16-17`).
- **S2.** Article 12 names the customary law as a route only where the act is an offence under it, so the customary law as a route when the act is not an offence under it now refuses (it answered FALSE), with the same text as row 7.
  Own: 2 corrected (`art12-15-penalties-forms` and `seat`), 2 added (the customary route when the act is an offence under it, which is TRUE, and the refusal in V1).

The first draft's sentence that no expected value was ever fixed to the code's answer is retired (section 11): for these 33 it cannot be shown, and the agreement of test and code is no evidence about the text.
Twenty-four assertions were added, to give both sides of each corrected case (the listed or covered case that answers a value, and the case outside that refuses): 7 in `art11-powers`, 2 in `art09-10-references`, 4 in `art12-15-penalties-forms`, 6 in `v1-arts-6-16-17` and 5 in `seat`.
The assertions of my own moved from 1228 to 1252, and the independent pass adds 520, so the row runs 1772.

The author's unsure scenarios (section U of the independent file, now at the end of part 2) are the places where the words allow more than one reading; each states the reading taken and the other reading in a comment.
Five of the seven groups are covered by a fork and pass on my default, and on the other reading too wherever the author asserted it: U02 (the day for an enactment naming 28 September 2021; only the day is asserted, and it is the same on every reading of I1), U09 (the event or the question day, I2: the author took the day of the event, my default, and the other reading is asserted too), U06 (the proviso and the Schedule definition of person, I5: the literal reading, my default), U (K03, the rebutted presumption of Article 7, I4: the time proved, my default, and the other reading is asserted), and the callable alternatives (b) and (c) of I1.
Two have no fork and pass on my defaults: U (J04), whether the substituted provisions are in force at the instant they are replaced (the author took "not in force", which is assumption A3 and open question Q8), and U14 (D03), "where appropriate" in Article 1(3) (the author took "the reference stands as written" and offers "the Article gives no reading" as the other; the encoding takes the appropriateness as an input, assumption A8, so with FALSE it reads as written and the other reading is not callable).

What the pass did not do.
It did not run before the encoding existed in the sense that matters for the shape: the author decided the scenarios from the text first, but mapped each onto an exported question after reading the seat modules, so the pass is independent of my tests and not of the shape of my exports.
Nine of the 14 findings are not independent of the encoding's option names (above).
Besides the prelude and `daydate`, its imports are the nouns and the two seat modules, so it reaches the rule modules only through the exported questions, and every one of the 43 exported names appears in the three files (checked by searching for each name).
Its answers are one reader's; the triage above followed the convention of section 2, which is also assumed, not ruled, and which a Jersey drafter could rule the other way for Articles 10 and 12 (I7 in section 6).
