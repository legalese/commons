## 1. What is encoded, and what is not

This row encodes the Legislation (Jersey) Law 2021 (L.8/2021) from its two deposited texts: the text in force from 28 September 2021 to 10 January 2024 (version A, `l_8_2021_20240110`) and the text in force from 11 January 2024 to the date of retrieval, 27 October 2026 (version B, `l_8_2021.current`).
It covers the Title, the Long title, Articles 1 to 26, Schedule 1, the placeholders of Articles 24 and 25 and of Schedules 2 and 3, and the endnotes.
Every line of both texts belongs to a row of the coverage table in section 4 (250 rows: 156 encoded, 64 inert, 30 out-of-scope, none refused as a whole, none deferred), and the script that built the table fails if a non-blank line of either text is in no row.

What a reader of an enactment can ask is encoded: what Jersey legislation, a Law and Jersey subordinate legislation are (Article 1); when a Law and subordinate legislation are made and on what day (Article 2); when a provision comes into force, including the edge days around 28 September 2021 (Articles 3 and 26); whether a power may be used before its legislation is in force (Article 4); what a repeal or expiry leaves alone and does not revive (Article 5); how references to re-enacted provisions are read (Article 6); whether an amended instrument can still be amended (Article 7); when the States may amend a Law consequentially on another (Article 8); what a power to make subordinate legislation carries, including whether an Order of a Minister can use the incidental power to amend a Law (Article 9); when a technical document may be incorporated by reference (Article 10); when the States has annulled an instrument by resolution (Article 11); what consolidated, revised and as-enacted legislation are and when a consolidated version must exist (Articles 12 and 13); which editorial changes may be made and whether they have effect (Article 14); what the Law Revision Board's powers and the Schedule 1 paragraphs let a revision do, whether a draft's changes are all within them, and whether the Board's minutes were laid in time (Article 15 and Schedule 1); what must be published and from when (Article 16); which texts are official versions, what they are taken to say, and that they are admissible and noticed judicially (Article 17); what Statute Law Revision Regulations may do (Article 18); and whether a communication with the Legislative Drafting Office is privileged (Article 22).

What is not encoded, each with its reason in the table: the constitution and procedure of the Law Revision Board (15(1) to (3)); the organisation, functions and delegation of the Legislative Drafting Office (Articles 19 to 21, except where 20 only points to a duty encoded elsewhere); the States' powers to amend Articles 2(3)(b), 11, 17(2), Schedule 1 and Part 4 by Regulations (2(5), 11(5), 15(10), 17(6), 23), because the Table of Legislation History records no Regulations made under any of them; titles, headings and definitions that only point at another Article; and Articles 24 and 25 and Schedules 2 and 3, which are spent and omitted.
The Interpretation (Jersey) Law 1954 is not encoded and is not copied; where this Law uses a word that Law defines ("month" in Article 15(9)) the model takes what it needs as an input (section 7).
The Act that brought the Law into force, R&O.112/2021, is cited by the endnotes and is not among the sources.

Shape of the deposit, in dependency order (also in `README.md`): one nouns module; twelve rule modules along the Law's own structure (`art01` to `art26`, `part3a`, `part4`, `schedule1`); a defaults module and three seat modules holding the 63 exported questions; fifteen tests modules, one for each rule module and each seat module (32 modules in all).
No module is over 355 lines (the nouns module, 354 lines, is the longest).

## 2. The seat

The seat is the reader of an enactment: someone who has to know what an enactment means for them, when it bound them, what it lets be done to them, and whether a notice reached them in time.
Facts are ones that person could state.
A provision that addresses only an official (the Principal Legislative Drafter, the Law Revision Board, the Greffier) is encoded for what it means to the reader: whether a consolidated version must exist, whether a change has effect, whether a text is official, whether the Board's minutes were laid in time.
Officials get no seat of their own.

The rule modules answer in the Law's own terms.
The seat modules (`je21-seat-1-…`, `-2-…`, `-3-…`) hold the 63 `@export`ed questions in the reader's words ("Has this Law been made?", "Can this instrument use the incidental power of Article 9(5) to make this provision?", "Is this text an official version of the legislation?", "Are my communications with the Legislative Drafting Office privileged?"), each with an `@ref` and, above it, the quotation of the lines it cites, and each input with a `@desc`.
Three conventions carry through, and each is stated where it matters.

- A rule module's function that says "permits" or "applies" returns FALSE for "this Article does not give the effect", and never for "the Law forbids it". Where the Law says nothing about the rest, the exported question turns that FALSE into `REFUSE` with the reason, so a reader is not told "no" when the answer is "the Law does not say". Where the Law says no in terms (Article 9(6)(b), Article 14(2), Article 17(4) where the contrary is shown), FALSE passes through.
- The exported questions take no reading of a fork. They use the default reading named in `je21-seat-defaults.l4`, and the other reading of each fork is called through the rule module's function, which takes the reading as an input and is tested with both (section 6).
- Only the questions that depend on which text of the Law governs, or on whether the Law was in force, are asked as at a day: the Law-itself questions, the making questions of Articles 2(1) to (4), and the question of the day a silent provision comes into force (asked as at the day the legislation was made). A day before 28 September 2021 is answered with a value, `the Law was not in force on that date`, carrying the commencement as the endnotes record it, never a refusal. Every other exported question is not gated, because the two deposited texts do not differ in any Article those questions rest on (the differences are listed in section 5).

## 4. Coverage table

One row for every paragraph of both deposited texts, keyed by the Article and paragraph number as the source writes it (a definition is keyed by Article 1 and the defined word; a Part heading, the Schedule headings and the endnotes have rows too).
The columns:

- **Lines (B / A)**: the lines of `l_8_2021.current.txt` (B, text from 11 January 2024) and of `l_8_2021_20240110.txt` (A, text from 28 September 2021); `-` where the text has no such paragraph.
  The two texts have the same line numbers from line 1 to line 267; from line 268 the endnotes differ (section 5).
- **Vintages**: `A, B` where the paragraph is in both, `B only` where it is in version B alone (endnote [2]).
- **Disposition**: `encoded` (a rule or a noun carries it), `inert` (quoted; no operative content; the reason says why), `out-of-scope` (a reason of real length), `refused` (none: no paragraph is refused as a whole; the refusals are inside encoded rules and are listed in section 7). None is `deferred`.
- **Rule(s) in the rule modules** and **Exported question(s)**: the functions whose `@ref` locator covers the paragraph's lines, found mechanically from the modules, with the module name without the `je21-` prefix; a long function name is cut at 100 characters with `...`.

Counts: 250 rows, 156 encoded, 64 inert, 30 out-of-scope.
Method: the rows were generated from the text, a disposition was assigned to each by a rule written against the key, the carriers were found from the `@ref` locators, and a script refused to finish while any non-blank line of either text was in no row, or any encoded row had neither a carrier nor a reason.

## 10. Open questions

### 10.1 Where the text does not say what the brief says

The brief's claims were checked against the text of 2026-10-27 (`l_8_2021.current.txt`) and the earlier text (`l_8_2021_20240110.txt`).
Line numbers are lines of `l_8_2021.current.txt` unless a stem is named.
These are the places where the text differs from the brief, in the order of how much they change an answer.

1. **Article 11(4) does not match Article 11(1).** The brief (claim 5) says that for subordinate legislation made before 28 September 2021 "the 3 sittings run from that day's commencement of Part 2". Paragraph (4) (src:113) construes "the reference in paragraph (1) to 3 sittings after it is made" as meaning 3 sittings after the commencement of this Part; but paragraph (1) (src:108) says "no later than 3 sittings after the date on which it is laid" and has no reference to 3 sittings after it is made. The brief's reading is what (4) says, and it is fork L7 (default as the brief reads it, alternative: from the laying).
2. **"Law" and "Jersey legislation" are circular as printed.** "Jersey legislation" means "any Law or Jersey subordinate legislation" (src:11), and "Law" means "Jersey legislation adopted by the States that does not rely on any other legislation to empower its making" (src:15). The model reads a Law as an instrument adopted by the States that relies on no other legislation, and Jersey subordinate legislation as the instruments made under Jersey legislation, UK extended legislation or a registered Order in Council (src:12-14). The brief treats the terms as plain.
3. **Article 4(2) has an "unless" limb the brief's summary omits.** The brief says a power "may be exercised after the making of the legislation, whether or not in force"; the text (src:63) says "unless provision is made to the contrary". Article 4(3) (src:64) begins "However". The contrary provision is an input.
4. **Article 5(3) has no "unless" limb, while 5(2) and 5(4) do** (src:74 against src:67 and src:75). Whether a contrary provision that displaces 5(2) displaces 5(3) is fork L10.
5. **"Made by the States" is not defined.** The brief calls an Order of a Minister "not made by the States, Article 2(3)(b)" and a Law "made by the States". Article 2(3)(b) (src:39-41) says only that other subordinate legislation is made by being signed; "made by the States" appears in 7(2), 9(6)(b) and 11(3) (src:84, src:100, src:112) without a definition. Fork L5 (the brief's reading is the default).
6. **Article 9(6) names Regulations and "legislation not made by the States" only** (src:99-100). An Act of the States that is Jersey legislation is neither on the default reading of L5, so on the text no limit of 9(6) bears on it. The brief's question concerns an Order.
7. **Article 17(2) says "is an official version if"** (src:168), not "only if". The brief (claim 6) states it without that. Fork L11.
8. **Article 22(2) does not say "by or on behalf of" for every body.** The brief says "a department, body, office or unit of administration established by or on behalf of the States". The text says a department "established by the States" (src:214) and a body, office or unit of administration "established on behalf of the States (including under any legislation)" (src:215). Article 22(1) speaks of a "provider of instructions", while 22(2)(a) speaks of "any client of the Legislative Drafting Office", and 22(2)(b) makes unpublished drafts confidential communications although a draft is not necessarily passed between a provider and a Drafter.
9. **The two texts differ in more than the brief lists.** The brief says the only differences are Article 2(1)(b) and the Schedule headings, and that the change is editorial.
