## Social security, benefits and personal income tax (sourced 2026-10-19 UTC)

The set is chosen so a member of the public can get answers to four questions: *how much income tax do I owe?* (including the exemption threshold and marginal relief), *what Social Security and LTC contributions must I pay?*, *what can I get from Income Support, the old age pension and long-term care?*, and *how do these interact?*

Each subject's text is in its `registers/source-bundle/<id>.txt`. The `.meta.json` beside it holds the URL, the point-in-time the page showed, the retrieval time (UTC), sha256 of the download and of the text, the character count and the conversion method. All eight are the Official Consolidated Version on `https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/<id>`.

**How they connect.** One person's year runs through all eight. Earnings attract Class 1 or Class 2 Social Security contributions, set by the Social Security Law and the Contributions Order. Paid and credited contributions build the contribution record behind the old age pension and other contributory benefits. The same income is taxed under the Income Tax Law above the exemption threshold, with marginal relief near it. The LTC contribution is levied by the Social Security Law (Sch 1C) on taxable income and collected with income tax (Income Tax Law, Art 49B and Sch 1A, as cited in the Social Security Law). Its amount is cut where Art 92C marginal relief applies, and it pays for the Long-Term Care Fund under the 2012 Law. Benefit rates come from the Benefits Order. Income Support tops up a household whose calculated income, from earnings, benefits and tariff income from capital, falls below its components. It takes pensionable age from the Social Security Law and maintenance definitions from the Income Tax Law. That income-assessment chain was read only in part, so treat the full chain as unverified (general knowledge).

## Gaps to know about

- **The annual Finance Law is not here.** The 2026 standard rate of income tax is set by the Finance (2026 Budget) (Jersey) Law 2026 (L.12/2026), not by the 1961 Law; Art 1 of the 1961 Law charges tax "Where any Law enacts that income tax shall be charged for any year at any rate". `https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_12_2026` returned 404. Enacted texts sit under `/laws/enacted/`, which jerseylaw.je's robots.txt disallows, so it was not fetched. The 2026 standard rate of 20% is therefore **unverified (general knowledge)**: a web search summary of the States Assembly proposition reported it, but no deposited text says so. The thresholds and marginal-relief rate *are* in the consolidated 1961 Law, because the Finance Laws amend it.
- **Uprated figures are not in the text.** The Social Security Law states base figures and an uprating rule, not the current amounts. That covers the Class 1 and 2 earnings limits (Sch 1A/1B: £776, £3,686, £20,800, uprated each 1 January by the earnings index), the standard rate of benefit (Art 13(2), uprated each October), and the old age pension standard rate (£189.84 to 30 Sep 2013, then uprated by the Sch 1 Part 1A formula). The amounts in force for 2026 are published by the Minister outside these texts. They are **not sourced**, and any figure for them is unverified (general knowledge).
- **Formula images.** The source sets some formulae as images. They are marked `[image in source not converted: …]` and their URLs are listed in each `.meta.json`: 10 in the Income Tax Law (for example the Art 92C(4) credit calculation and the instalment formulae), 2 in the Social Security Law (including the LTC contribution effective rate), and 1 in the Contributions Order. Transcribe them before encoding those provisions.
- **Currency differs between instruments.** The points in time run from 1 January 2023 (Long-Term Care Law) to 19 October 2026 (Contributions Order). Each is the "Current" version the site showed on 2026-10-19. Check the site for later versions before encoding.
- **Not included.** Instruments outside the cap of eight were left out: other Social Security Orders (benefits, home carer's allowance), the Income Support (Special Payments) Regulations, the Social Security (Long-Term Care Contributions) Order 2014 (`ro_212_2014`), the Long-Term Care (Residency Conditions) Regulations 2013, and the Health Insurance Law. Each could be added later as its own subject.
- **Terms.** The OSAL-J licence page (statesassembly.je) sits behind a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) and was not re-read. The terms used are those recorded in this repository on 2026-09-23 (see `charities-2014`). The jerseylaw.je Copyright page asks anyone doing *bulk* downloads to request API access rather than scrape search pages. This deposit fetched eight pages by direct URL, at least 3 s apart, but a larger Jersey intake should use the API (programmedirector@jerseylaw.je).
- **Fractions set as text.** Where the source lays out a fraction as text across lines, the conversion keeps the pieces but loses the layout. For example, Income Support Regulations Sch 1 para 1(1A) reads "B = 1 + C" followed by "C" on its own line. Check every formula against the source.
- **Conversion.** The text is unofficial and keeps the endnotes, which contain the legislative history. The contents list is dropped. Each file's header says so.
