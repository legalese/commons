# Source license

The quoted text of **Magna Carta (1215)** is the English translation printed in William Sharp McKechnie, *Magna Carta: A Commentary on the Great Charter of King John* (Glasgow: James MacLehose and Sons, 1905), taken from an electronic edition of that book.

- **Text deposited**: `registers/source-bundle/magna-carta-1215-mckechnie-translation.txt`, the preamble and the 63 chapters of the translation and nothing else of the book (not the Latin, not the commentary, not the footnotes).
- **Where it came from**: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/65363/pg65363.txt, downloaded 2026-10-29T14:38:38Z. The electronic edition's own header gives release date 2021-05-17 and most recent update 2024-10-18. `registers/source-bundle/magna-carta-1215-mckechnie-translation.meta.json` has the sha256 of the download and of the deposited text, and says how the one was made from the other.
- **Author**: William Sharp McKechnie, born 2 September 1863, died 2 July 1930. The Wikipedia article on him (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Sharp_McKechnie, read 2026-10-29) says: "McKechnie died on 2 July 1930."

## Why this text, and what is claimed about its licence

The aim was an English text of the whole Charter that could be deposited without a licence question.
This book was found by one web search on 2026-10-29 and used; no other translation was looked at or compared.
It is old enough to be out of copyright.
What was read, and what follows from it:

- **United States**: the electronic edition's own licence statement, at the end of the downloaded file, reads: "Creating the works from print editions not protected by U.S. copyright law means that no one owns a United States copyright in these works", and section 1.C says that for an individual work unprotected in the United States the Foundation does "not claim a right to prevent you from copying, distributing, performing, displaying or creating derivative works based on the work as long as all references to Project Gutenberg are removed". The deposited text file carries no such reference. This `SOURCE-LICENSE.md` and the `.meta.json` next to the text do name the source, so that the deposit can be traced; whether naming the source in a provenance note is a "reference" in the sense of section 1.C is not something the licence settles, and it is not ruled here.
- **United Kingdom**: the author died in 1930. The term of United Kingdom copyright in a literary work is generally stated as the life of the author plus 70 years, which would have ended on 31 December 2000. This depositor did not read the statute for this note, so the term is stated as generally understood, not as checked.
- **The 1905 edition is the one deposited.** The preface of the downloaded file is dated 6 February 1905 and its title page 1905. A web search on 2026-10-29 reported a second, revised edition of 1914; it was not looked at.

## What this deposit does not establish

1. **Who made the English.** The book prints the translation beside the Latin and does not, in the pages read, say whose translation it is. It is treated here as the author's own. If it was taken from an earlier work, the question of its copyright moves to that work, and this depositor did not look.
2. **That it is a faithful translation.** The Latin is not deposited and no one has compared the two. The translation contains one bracketed insertion, in the preamble, which is the book's own. In chapter 39 the translation reads "unless by the lawful judgment of his peers and by the law of the land"; chapter 29 of the 1297 text (the sibling subject `uk/magna-carta-1297`), which covers the same ground, reads "but by lawful judgment of his Peers, or by the Law of the Land". Whether the Latin supports one reading or the other has not been checked.
3. **That the electronic text matches the print.** It was produced by volunteers from page images and has not been compared with the print by this depositor.
