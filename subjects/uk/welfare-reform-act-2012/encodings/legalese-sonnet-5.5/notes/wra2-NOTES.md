**Policy for the inert 2 rows.**
Regulation-making powers are inert 2 where they only say what regulations may provide.
Where the text states a limit the person can rely on (a cap on the length of a reduction, a period for a reference), the limit is encoded, not inert: JSA 19(4)(b), 19A(5)(b), 19B(5), 6J(5)(b), 6K(5)(b), WRA2007 11J(4)(b).
Amendments of other Acts are inert 2 when the row is the instruction or the words it substitutes in another Act's existing text; where the row is new operative text the Act inserts (a new section, subsection or definition), it is encoded.
"Includes" lists are encoded as tables, with the member the text names answering `JUST TRUE` and "some other member" answering `NOTHING`.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (wra2)

An answer from a layer-1 rule is one of three things.
`JUST TRUE` means the text says yes.
`JUST FALSE` means the text says no **in its own words**; the code carries a comment `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<quote>"` with the words.
`NOTHING` means the text does not say, and the comment names which of six kinds of not-saying it is:

| kind | meaning | how many places in layer 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `silent` | the text says nothing about this case | 18 |
| `list not closed` | the text names some members and does not close the list | 0 |
| `includes` | "includes" or "in particular" names members and does not close the list | 18 |
| `undefined term` | a term the text uses and does not define | 7 |
| `points outside the text` | regulations, an order, an opinion, another Act | 88 |
| `limited by its own wording` | a grant or a prohibition that covers only the case it states | 129 |

There are 136 `FALSE-kind` places against 260 `NOTHING-kind` places: the Act mostly says "regulations may", "as may be prescribed" and "as the Secretary of State thinks fit", and says "no" rarely.

Two marginal readings decide the border (the brief's rule of 29 October 2026, assumed and not ruled by a lawyer): (a) a failed "if" counts as the text's no; (b) a grant or prohibition limited by its own wording does not.

A rule that returns a plain `BOOLEAN` is a rule where the text closes its own definition (for example "a claimant accepts a claimant commitment if, and only if ..."), so it cannot answer `NOTHING`; its inputs may be `MAYBE`.
In the seat the same three answers appear as a value of the type `Under wra2, a yes, no or not-known answer`: `Under wra2, yes`, `Under wra2, no`, `Under wra2, not known`.
**A seat answer of "not known" is not "no".**
It means the text depends on something the person did not state, or on regulations that are not among the sources.

## What the text turns out to say

1. **The Act says several things twice.**
   Jobseeker's allowance has an interim regime (ss 44 to 46: JSA 9, 10, 19 to 19C) and an after-universal-credit regime (s 49: JSA 6 to 6L); employment and support allowance has the same pair (ss 54 to 56 and s 57).
   The claimant commitment is written five times: JSA 9 (interim), JSA 6A (lines 702 to 709), WRA2007 1C (929 to 935) and 11A (974 to 981), and SSCBA 124A (1147 to 1153).
   The four later copies have the same first five subsections; the interim JSA text differs in that an employment officer agrees the commitment and may refer it to the Secretary of State, who must deal with the reference within 14 days so far as practicable (JSA 9(5) to (7), 10(5) and (6)).
   The encoding keeps each copy as its own labelled rule instead of merging them, because each is a different provision of a different Act.
2. **The caps are three: three years, 26 weeks and 13 weeks.**
   Three years for the higher-level JSA sanction (19(4)(b), 6J(5)(b)); 26 weeks for a fixed-period "other" sanction (19A(5)(b), 6K(5)(b), WRA2007 11J(4)(b)); 13 weeks beginning with the day the claimant's previous entitlement ceased, as the outer limit of a reduction under 19B(5), and 13 weeks for the domestic-violence pause (6H(5), 11H(5)).
   Each is a counting question (F-46-1 to F-46-3).
3. **"No" is rare.**
   Layer 1 has 136 places where the text says no in its own words and 260 places where it does not say.
   Most of the second group are "limited by its own wording" (129) and "points outside the text" (88).
4. **A failure sanctionable under the higher-level section is not sanctionable under the other.**
   JSA 19A(3) (611) and 6K(3) (823) say so in terms; WRA2007 11J has one sanction section and no such rule.
5. **When one member of a joint-claim couple is sanctioned, the allowance is payable to the other member** (JSA 19(7), 19A(10), 19B(8)).
   The answer is a person, so these three rules are in layer 1 and out of the seat.
6. **The three groups of WRA2007 11(3) overlap.**
   A person can be within both 11D (no requirements) and 11E (interviews only), and the text does not say which governs (F-57-1).
7. **Section 63(6) and (7) are shown blank** (lines 1215 and 1216), as rows of dots.
   They are rows of the coverage table with the disposition "repealed or blank".
8. **Section 70(1) repeals SSCBA 138(1)(b) and section 71 amends it.**
   Neither says which comes first (see "What the text does not answer").
9. **Section 50(2) gives retrospective entitlement** (880 to 883), but only in a case with three conditions; outside them the text says nothing, so `NOTHING`.
10. **Most of Part 2 is textual amendment.**
    150 of the 184 inert 2 rows are instructions that amend another Act; the words of the amended provision are not in the sources, so whatever an instruction relies on in the host Act is a borrowed fact, asked as an input.
11. **JSA 19A(7) and 6K(7) joined two limbs that WRA2007 11J(6) keeps apart.**
    The text of all three reads "may be (a) revoked or varied by the Secretary of State; (b) notified ... in such manner as the Secretary of State may determine".
    An earlier draft answered both limbs from one input naming who acts; the final encoding has one rule per limb, as 11J(6) does (`19A(7)(a)`, `19A(7)(b)`, `6K(7)(a)`, `6K(7)(b)`), with the tests rewritten.
12. **The text bounds the one number that WRA2007 11E(1)(a) leaves to regulations.**
    11E(1)(a) covers a single person responsible for a child "aged at least 1 and ... under a prescribed age (which may not be less than 3)" (line 1020).

13. **Sweep for the same shape (4 November 2026).**
    Method: I read every line of my three ranges for "not less than", "not more than", "not exceeding", "at least", "at most", "may not be", "exceed", "more than", "less than" and "aged", and listed every `MAYBE NUMBER` and `MAYBE DATE` input in layer 1.

## What the stable binary does not accept

"Loud" means the binary reports an error and teaches; "silent" means it returns a wrong answer with exit code 0.
Items marked "(brief)" come from the brief's list for the stable binary 1.6.98; the others I met while writing this encoding.

**Loud.**

- A regulative rule (`PARTY ... MUST ...`) cannot be put in an `#ASSERT`; the duty is tested through a paired decision function (a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` over the same facts), and the regulative rule is run with `#TRACE` only.
- No `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` or `BECAUSE` outside `LEST BREACH ... BECAUSE` (brief), so a case outside the encoding returns a constructor or `NOTHING` and the test asserts that.
- A second argument placed on its own line is a parse error; the call is written with `WHERE` locals instead.
- A record built with `WITH` needs one field per line; `WITH` followed by a field and `IS` on one line reports "unexpected IS" (seen in the seat).
- An `#ASSERT` must be on one line (brief).
- `IMPORT` finds only modules in the same folder; a part cannot import another part (brief), so every cross-part fact is an input (seams).

**Silent.**

- `NOT a AND b` parses as `NOT (a AND b)` (brief); every `NOT` in layer 1 and the seat is parenthesised.
- Two counts that are equal on the stable and main builds can hide a different assertion failing on each side, and a refused assertion exits 0 (`check.sh` header); I read the counts per module, not the exit code.
- `l4 run` prints a module's `#TRACE` outcome but no assertion line, so `check.sh` cannot see a trace that differs between builds.
  I ran the two test modules that hold `#TRACE` (`wra2-tests-s44-jsa-commitment.l4`, three traces over JSA 9(7)(a) and 10(3); `wra2-tests-s49-jsa-requirements.l4`, one trace over JSA 6F(1)) with both builds on 4 November 2026 and read the outcome lines: they are the same on both (FULFILLED or BREACH in the same order); the full outputs differ only in the diagnostic `Code:` line, `<none>` on 1.6.98 and `eval/assertion-passed` on 1.6.108.

**Other dialect facts I relied on (all accepted by 1.6.98).**
Names in backticks may contain parentheses; a genitive is parenthesised when it is a function argument; `MAYBE BOOLEAN` with `CONSIDER ... WHEN JUST x` and `WHEN NOTHING`; `BRANCH IF ... THEN ... OTHERWISE`; dates as `Date day month year`.
