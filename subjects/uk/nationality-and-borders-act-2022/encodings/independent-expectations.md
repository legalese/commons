# Independent expectations: Nationality and Borders Act 2022, Part 2

Written from BRIEF.md and the source text (ss 20-22, 26, 30-37) only, before any `.l4`
file was opened. Each scenario: inputs, expected answer, the Act's words.

## Readings adopted

- R1. "Refugee" under s 30-35 = claim within s 30(5) AND stage one (s 32(2)) met AND stage
  two (s 32(4)) met AND not excluded by internal relocation (s 35(1)).
- R2. Stage one needs a characteristic for a Convention reason (actual or attributed by an
  actor of persecution) AND an actual fear in the country of nationality / former habitual
  residence as a result of it (s 32(2)(a),(b); s 32(3)).
- R3. Stage two needs a reasonable likelihood of persecution as a result of the
  characteristic AND that the person would not be protected as in s 34 (s 32(4)(a),(b)).
  Persecution requires an actor of persecution (s 31(1)) and the s 31(2) severity.
- R4. A non-State actor is an actor of persecution only if the State / controlling parties
  (incl. international organisations) are unable or unwilling to give reasonable protection
  (s 31(1)(c)). Otherwise no persecution in law, so stage two fails.
- R5. Protection (s 34(2)) needs BOTH limbs: reasonable steps by an effective legal system
  AND the asylum seeker able to access it. One limb alone: not taken to be protected.
- R6. Internal relocation (s 35(1)) excludes only where BOTH: no well-founded fear in a part
  AND can reasonably be expected to travel to and remain there.
- R7. Particular social group: both s 33(3) (any one of three shared traits) and s 33(4)
  (distinct identity, perceived as different) conditions.
- R8. s 30(5): claim made on or after the in-force date: Act applies; a claim made before:
  the ss 31-35 tests do not govern (expect a refusal / "not determined under this Act", not
  "not a refugee").
- R9. s 37(1): no stop elsewhere: "directly" not excluded (treated as direct). Stop + could
  not reasonably have sought protection there: direct. Stop + could have: not direct.
- R10. s 37(2): three situations by where/when the person became a refugee and lawfulness.
  (b)(i) "before the time when their presence ... became unlawful": claim made while still
  lawful (including where presence has not become unlawful) is without delay.
- R11. s 37(4): penalty for something done in an attempt to leave the UK: not imposed on
  account of illegal entry/presence, so Article 31(1) immunity does not cover it.
- R12. Lateness (ss 20(5), 22(7), 26(4)(b), 26(5)(b)): provided on or after cut-off date =
  late; the cut-off date itself is late; the day before is not.
- R13. s 20(4): material previously provided to SSHD/competent authority is outside the
  requirement, so re-providing it after cut-off does not make it late for s 20(5).
- R14. s 21(1): last day in force = day before the date 12 months after the start date (the
  later of cut-off and appeal-exhaustion); if no corresponding date (29 Feb), the last day of
  that month (28 Feb). No appeal-exhaustion date: start = cut-off.
- R15. s 22(4): must treat as damaging credibility iff material in response to PRN, late,
  relevant decision being made (protection/human rights claim considered, or RG/CG
  decision), and no good reasons.
- R16. s 26(2): minimal weight principle iff evidence late (evidence notice after specified
  date, or PRN evidence supporting the s 120(2)(a)-(c) reasons/grounds statement on/after
  cut-off), considered by a deciding authority in determining claim/appeal, and no good
  reasons. PRN evidence supporting only relevant status information (s 20(3)(a)(ii)) is not
  within s 26(5).

Ambiguities: (A1) s 37(1) is only a negative rule; R9 treats "no stop" as direct. (A2) s 34
deeming is one-way; R5 treats "not both limbs" as not protected. (A3) brief's month-end rule
"last day of that month" read as the month of the missing corresponding date. (A4) s 30(5)
pre-commencement claims: Act silent on what applies, so refusal expected.
