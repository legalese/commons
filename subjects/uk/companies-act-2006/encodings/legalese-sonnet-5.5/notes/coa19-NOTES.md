## Seat

The individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member (shareholder) of a company registered under the Act, or who deals with one (a lender who takes a charge, a person who appoints a receiver, a creditor), asking on facts they can state what the Act requires or lets them do, what rights they have against the company or its directors, what they must disclose, file, approve or vote on and by when, and what follows (liability, offences, penalties) if it is not done. (Assumed, not ruled: the Act is addressed to companies, so the individual is whoever stands in a company's management or ownership.)
There is no seat for the registrar, the court or the Secretary of State: what they do is encoded in layer 1 for what it decides, and in the seat only for what it means to the person (for example the registrar's duties under ss 859A(2) to (5), 859C(2) and (3), and 859L(5), and the court's powers under ss 859F(3), 859M(3) and 859N(2)).

- `JUST TRUE`: the Act says yes: the section applies, the registrar must register, the charge is void against the creditor, the person commits the offence.
- `JUST FALSE` ("a no"): the Act says no in its own words: a "means" definition ("company" means a UK-registered company); a flat negative ("This section does not apply to ...", "is not ... an interest in the land", "It is immaterial ...", "does not affect the validity of the debentures", "Subsection (3) is without prejudice to ..."); an "only if" ("the registrar is required to register it only if a certified copy ... is delivered", "This section applies only to a receiver or manager appointed ..."); a "where", "if" or "when" that is not met ("Where a charge is created outside the United Kingdom ..."); a period fixed in the text's own words that has run out ("before the end of the period allowed for delivery"). Each such branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`.
- `NOTHING` ("not covered"): the Act does not decide the case: it lists without closing the list ("the charge is void against a liquidator, an administrator and a creditor": any other person), says "includes" ("cash" includes foreign currency), is silent, leaves a term undefined, points outside the sources (section 466 of the Companies Act 1985, the regulations under s 1136, s 738 "debenture", s 1121 "in default"), or limits a grant in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit ("on the application of the company or a person interested"). ## What the text turns out to say (findings, numbered)

1. **Three regimes are printed side by side.** Chapter A1 (ss 859A to 859Q, where "company" means a UK-registered company) and Chapters 1 and 2 (ss 860 to 877 and 878 to 892, where "company" is narrower) are all in the text as operative, and the text says nowhere which governs a charge created by a company registered in England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland. The converter's caveat ("may carry more than one extent version of a provision ... which CLML marks but this text does not distinguish") is the likely reason. The only date the text states for the Part is 6 April 2013, in ss 859K(2)(b), 859L(4)(c)-(d) and 859O(3)(b)-(c), where it divides charges for what a notice must contain. The encoding does not resolve this: each Chapter stands on its own words and the seat's questions are named for the section (F-C1).
2. **Offences for not registering exist only in Chapters 1 and 2.** Chapter A1 makes a failure to deliver in time a matter of voidness (s 859H(3)) and not an offence: ss 859A, 859B and 859C create no offence. The offences of Chapter A1 are s 859K(6) (receiver notices) and s 859Q(5) (the place of the copies, a refused inspection). Chapters 1 and 2 make it an offence for the company and every officer in default (ss 860(4), 862(4), 878(4), 880(3)).
3. **Section 859C(2) has no period.** Sections 859A(2) and 859B(2) say "before the end of the period allowed for delivery"; s 859C(2) says only that the registrar must register the charge if a statement is delivered (F-Z1).
4. **The "date of creation" is a table, not a day.** Section 859E(1) gives seven ways a charge is created and the date each takes; a deed held in escrow is created on delivery into escrow and a deed held as undelivered on delivery. The Table prints five sub-rows under one first cell.
5. **s 859I(6) is limited to registration under s 859A or 859B**: a charge registered under s 859C gets a certificate (subsection (3)) that the text does not call conclusive.
6. **The money secured by a void charge "immediately becomes payable"** (ss 859H(4), 874(3), 889(2)), and the voidness is "without prejudice to any contract or obligation for repayment".
7. **Section 859K applies only to some receivers.** Subsection (8) limits it to a receiver or manager appointed by a court in England and Wales or Northern Ireland or under an instrument governed by that law; subsection (9) also excludes a receiver appointed under Chapter 2 of Part 3 of the Insolvency Act 1986 (Scotland). Section 871 (Chapter 1) has no such limit in its words.
8. **Section 868(3)(b) is the only duty of the registrar in this Part that carries a period of its own**: not later than 7 days from the date of delivery of the copies. The registrar's duties in ss 859A(2), 859I and 869 have none; the periods in ss 859A(2) and 860(1) are periods for the company's delivery.
9. **The offence sections give the person no period for the notice of the place** (ss 859Q(3), 877(3), 892(3)): the offence arises "if default is made for 14 days" (F-P2). The Chapter 2 register applies to "every company" (s 891(1)); Chapter 1's applies to "every limited company" (s 876(1)); a company that is not limited answers NOTHING under s 876(1), not JUST FALSE (F-Y1).
10. **The headings of the two Chapters' satisfaction sections differ by a word**: s 872 is headed "Entries of satisfaction and release" and s 887 "Entries of satisfaction and relief"; the encoding follows each section's own words.
11. **The amounts are not in the Act.** Level 3 on the standard scale and the statutory maximum are inputs; the Act says only "level 3", "one-tenth of level 3" and "the statutory maximum". The seat asks for the pounds; the cases use 1,000, 5,000 and 5, which they supply and which are not the Act's figures.
12. **Section 859O(4)** says subsections (1) to (3) do not affect section 466 of the Companies Act 1985 and says nothing of whether that section continues to apply; it is encoded as a flat negative and a NOTHING, not treated as machinery (F-N1).
13. **A Scottish company and the three definitions of "company"**: a company registered in Scotland is within s 859A(7) and s 879(6) and outside s 861(5) (F-C3).

## What the stable binary does not accept, and what it does

Everything here was met while writing this encoding, on 2026-11-13, with `L4_BIN` 1.6.110 (main, e51181d, 2026-11-11) and `L4_MAIN` 1.6.109 (main, 4c887e0, 2026-11-04); the items below were met on 1.6.110, and the four counts that `check.sh` prints agree on both builds for every module (see the end of this note). Failures that give no error are marked silent.

Measured here:
- **`NOT a OR b` is `NOT (a OR b)`** (silent: exit 0, wrong answer). The first test run of `coa19-a1-delivery.l4` had `NOT x OR NOT y` in several functions and 8 assertions failed; every `NOT` in the modules is now written `(NOT x)`. A tool that wraps a bare `NOT atom` in brackets also turned `#ASSERT NOT f x` into `#ASSERT (NOT f) x`, which does not mean the same thing; the tests write `#ASSERT NOT (f x)`.
- **A DEONTIC value cannot be compared in an `#ASSERT`** (`assertion could not be evaluated: Trying to check equality on types that do not support it`, met on `EQUALS FULFILLED`). So the regulative rules of `coa19-regulative.l4` are run with `#TRACE` and read by eye (`## Regulative traces` below), and every duty in it is also a decision function whose tests assert.
- `length` is not a prelude function (`I could not find a definition for the identifier length`); `count` counts a list.
- A positional `Penalty OF a, b, c, d, e` with its arguments on separate lines is mis-indented and rejected; on one line it is accepted. A `LIST (...) , (...)` split over two lines is accepted.
- `#ASSERT f WITH x IS v EQUALS e` reads `EQUALS e` as part of the last `IS` (a type error, loud); write `(f WITH x IS v) EQUALS e`, as `coa19-cases-seat.l4` does.
- `WAITUNTIL 8` (no brackets or backticks) is accepted as a trace event, and `LEST BREACH BY p BECAUSE "..."` is accepted on both builds.
- A `GIVEN` parameter may have the same name as a top-level function of another module without an error (the seat takes the keel's `s 1173(1) ...` as a function while layer 1 takes the same name as an input).
- `@export` on a function whose inputs and result are `MAYBE BOOLEAN`, `DATE` or `LIST OF MAYBE NUMBER` is accepted by `l4 run`.

Taken from the brief and PLAN.md, not re-measured here: `REFUSE` and `#ASSERT REFUSED` do not exist; `BECAUSE` is accepted only in `LEST BREACH ... BECAUSE`; `YMD` is not in the stable `daydate` (use `Date day month year`); `WAIT UNTIL` is spelled `WAITUNTIL`; `TYPICALLY` on a record field is not accepted; an `#ASSERT` must be on one line. None of these is used.

## Open questions for a domain expert

1. Which Chapter governs a charge created by a company registered in England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland (finding 1, F-C1)? The encoding answers each Chapter on its words and cannot say which a real charge falls under.
2. Is the scope list of ss 860(7) and 878(7) closed (F-C2)?
3. Should the missing copy of the order under ss 859A(5) and 859B(7) stop the registrar's duty (F-A1)?
4. Does "before the end of the period" include the last day (F-A4), and does "within 7 days of" count the day of the order (F-K1)?
5. Is "default for 14 days" 14 days or more than 14 (F-P2)?
6. Are the prescribed particulars of ss 860(1), 862(2), 878(1) and 880(2) set somewhere the encoding should have used (F-R1)?
7. For a charge that is not a floating charge, does s 887(2) answer TRUE (nothing is required), FALSE or NOTHING (F-M1)?
8. Is a duty, grant or scope statement limited by a noun qualifier ("every limited company", "any charge registered in pursuance of this Chapter") NOTHING outside the limit, as taken here, or the text's no (F-Y1)?
