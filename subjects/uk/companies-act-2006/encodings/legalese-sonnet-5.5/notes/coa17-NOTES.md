## Edition, method and target

- **The text.** A plain-text rendering of the Act from legislation.gov.uk (Open Government Licence v3.0) made by a converter script; **it is not the official text**, and nothing in this encoding is verified against the official text. `inputs/SOURCES.md` records its provenance: version 2026-07-16 (point in time), `text_sha256` 959c95c8880721e4131be139e0452bdf06e791d6fe8845c51d29b6ecc759a63c; its own caveat is that the text "may carry more than one extent version of a provision, and prospective provisions, which CLML marks but this text does not distinguish". Whether a provision is in force on a given day (the identity-verification sections 790LB and 790LM to 790LT speak of dates "when section 12B(2) came fully into force") is outside the encoding: those dates are inputs.
- **Method.** One agent, no subagents. The rows of the coverage table were generated by `work/units.py` from the text (every non-blank line of the range is in a row: 795 lines, 775 rows). The L4 modules were then written section by section with each rule under a quoted run of the text: the quote lines (`-- src:N | text`) are copied by `work/build.py` from the raw file, never typed, and `python3 -I srcrefs.py check --refs` and `--strict` report 0 problems for every `coa17-*.l4` file. Expected values in the tests are decided by reading the text and written beside each assertion; expected values are written from the text before the code is run, never copied from its output, and a positive control was run once (2026-11-13): one expected value in a copy of `coa17-tests-part21a-ch5.l4` was flipped, and `check.sh` on the copy printed errors 1, satisfied 21, failed 1 for that module and exited 1 (the copy was then deleted).
- **Target.** The stable `l4`: 1.6.110 (main, e51181d, 2026-11-11), with 1.6.109 as the second build that `check.sh` compares. `JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` is unset. No `REFUSE`, no section `GIVEN`, no `YMD`, no sets: dates are `Date day month year`.
- **Where PLAN.md and the brief differ, the brief governs.** PLAN.md section 5 observes that both supplied builds check and run `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` and `YMD` although the brief says the stable line lacks them: this encoding uses none of them (a case outside the text is `NOTHING` or a constructor, and dates are `Date day month year`). Where PLAN.md section 4 names a seam input (`s 1121 the officer is in default`, `s 1110A the individual's identity is verified`, `s 1173(1) the person is a body corporate or a corporation`, `s 1173(1) the entity is a firm`) the input here carries that name; PLAN.md gives no input name for the Schedule 1A and Schedule 1B answers or for the statements under ss 12A and 12B, and the names chosen for those are listed under "Seams (coa17)". PLAN.md section 4.1 marks `the entity is a firm` as "BOOLEAN suffices" and this encoding takes it as a plain BOOLEAN; it takes the Part 21A gate as a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (fork F4).

## Seat

The seat is the brief's: an individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member (shareholder) of a company registered under the Act, or who deals with one, asking on facts they can state what the Act requires or lets them do, what rights they have against the company or its directors, what they must disclose, file, approve or vote on and by when, and what follows if it is not done (**assumed, not ruled**: the Act is addressed to companies, so the individual is whoever stands in a company's management or ownership). In Parts 21 and 21A that person is, in turn, a holder of shares or debentures waiting for a certificate, a transferee or transferor, a personal representative, a director or other officer who may be in default, a person with significant control, or someone who asks a company what it has told the registrar. There is no seat for the registrar, the court or the Secretary of State: they appear only as what they mean for that person (a direction, an order, an exemption, a penalty). `coa17-seat.l4` has 80 questions in 13 groups, every one named "Under section N, ..." and every one with `@export` and `@desc`; `coa17-cases-seat.l4` has 132 cases (see "Files").

Four things the seat layer assumes, each recorded where it matters: (1) whether Part 21A applies to the company (s 790B(1)) is asked as one yes/no in the duty questions (the question "Under section 790B(1), does Part 21A apply to my company?" works it out from the three facts), so a later change to the excluded companies would change those answers; (2) "officer in default" (s 1121, another part's) is asked as a yes/no about the person asking; (3) the pounds a level on the standard scale stands for are not in the Act and are asked (the cases use 1000 as an illustrative figure the person states, not as the Act's); (4) dates the Act does not give (the day section 84 of the Small Business, Enterprise and Employment Act 2015 came into force; the appointed day of ss 790LN and 790LP) are asked, and the cases use illustrative days.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (coa17)

Every decision function of this encoding answers **JUST TRUE**, **JUST FALSE** or **NOTHING** (a `MAYBE BOOLEAN`); a date or number question answers the date or number. In the terms of these two Parts:

- **JUST TRUE**: the text says yes. A company must have the share certificates ready within two months after an allotment (s 769(1)); a person with significant control must tell the company before the end of one month (s 790G).
- **JUST FALSE**, "the text says no in its own words": the case is excluded by words the text uses. "Subsection (1) does not apply" (s 769(2)); a "means" definition that the case does not meet (s 778(2) "financial institution"); an "if" that fails ("A company is not required to give a notice ... if" (s 790D(5)) is JUST TRUE when it holds and JUST FALSE when it does not); a period the text fixes in its own words (a notice given after the fourteenth day is out of time: s 790LA(3)); a flat negative ("Nothing in section 126 ... affects the duty", s 790LA(5)). A listed company asking about Part 21A duties gets JUST FALSE, because s 790B(1) says the Part applies to companies "other than" those.
- **NOTHING**, "not covered": the text does not decide. It is silent; its list is not closed; it says "includes"; the term is undefined here or points outside the sources (Schedule 1A, Schedule 1B, section 1121, section 1110A); or it limits a grant or prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit ("without reasonable excuse", "as the transferee may reasonably request", "A company limited by shares", "In the case of a company registered in Scotland"). **A caller must never turn NOTHING into FALSE**: a "not covered" answer is not a "no".
- Every JUST FALSE branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"` and every NOTHING branch `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, <why> being one of silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording. This encoding has 207 FALSE-kind and 111 NOTHING-kind comments in layer 1.
- The decision functions are named for what the section says (`s 770(1) the bar on registering the transfer applies`, not "may the company register"), so that a JUST FALSE never claims more than the text.

## Jural relations (coa17)

Hohfeld's correlatives: a right (claim) matches a duty; a privilege (liberty) matches a no-right; a power matches a liability (being subject to the power); an immunity matches a disability. Each relation is stated once in layer 1 as a relation between two parties and named neutrally; it is not written again from the other side. Parties the text names (the registrar, the court, the Secretary of State) appear here as the holder or bearer of a relation; the seat layer still has no seat for them. Where the holder of a company's duty is not named in the text (a duty to give notice, owed in effect to the public register and enforced by an offence), the registrar is shown as the holder: **assumed, not stated by the text**. A provision that creates no relation (a definition, a counting rule, machinery) is listed once as `none`. Sections 784 to 789 and 790M to 790ZE are each grouped in one row.

## What the text turns out to say (findings)

1. Section 790LM(1) Case 2(a) says the registrar "is notified under section 790LF that a person has become a registrable person". ## What the stable binary does not accept

Measured on 1.6.110 (main, e51181d) unless marked.

- A DEONTIC value cannot be compared in `#ASSERT`: `#ASSERT rule EQUALS FULFILLED` gives `Trying to check equality on types that do not support it` (error). So the regulative rules in `coa17-regulative.l4` are exercised by `#TRACE` (55 traces in `coa17-tests-regulative.l4`), whose outcome is printed and not counted by `check.sh`; `coa17-check-traces.sh` compares the 55 printed outcomes with the ones the text gives, on both builds, and every duty is also a decision function with asserted tests in the tests file of its module.
- `WITH` inside a `MUST` action is a parse error (`unexpected WITH`): the actions here are nullary constructors.
- `WITHIN` takes a bare number of days counted from entry on this build, with no `OF` anchor; the rules take the day the period begins and work the number out (`Day last MINUS Day first`). A computed `WITHIN (Day ... MINUS Day ...)` is accepted and the deadline is inclusive (an act at day 59 under `WITHIN 59` is in time; at day 60 it is a breach, measured).
- `length` is not a function of the prelude (`I could not find a definition for the identifier length`; use `count`); `A` is a keyword and cannot be a constructor name (a probe with a constructor `A` gave `unexpected A`). Both measured again on 1.6.110 while writing this file.
- Not used, though PLAN.md notes both builds happen to run them: `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED`, `YMD`, `BECAUSE` outside `LEST BREACH`.
- `ROR` of two `MUST` obligations (s 771(1)) is accepted and traced (FULFILLED for either act on day 59, BREACH on day 60 for the first); the trace of the second alternative alone was run and is FULFILLED on day 59.
- The seat layer returns `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (62 questions), `DATE` (14), `MAYBE NUMBER` (3) and one `LIST OF` an enumeration (s 790K(1)); `l4 check` and `l4 run` accept them as the types of `@export`ed questions. Whether Legalese Cloud publishes them was not tried here.
