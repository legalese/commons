## Notes (patterns across the findings; not failed assertions)

1. Seat polarity is a defect class, not a one-off (the seven seat ENCODER rows). A seat question is titled in the person's words and then calls the module function whose answer is the opposite, or a different question. Six are inversions: s.1079(3) (coa23-seat.l4:432-433, "can the company rely" calls "is barred from relying"), s.1079(1) (coa23-seat.l4:412-414, same), s.1137(5)(b) (coa25-seat.l4:144-148, "may my company charge less" calls "is prevented from charging"), s.1198A(1) (coa25-seat.l4:829-839, "may my company carry on" calls "uses the name in contravention"), s.1198B(1) (coa25-seat.l4:859-871, same) and s.1214(1) (coa26-seat.l4:175-177, "am I barred" calls "the person may act"). The seventh, s.1194(1) (coa25-seat.l4:690-700, "do I need approval" calls "contravenes the prohibition"), is a weaker mismatch. The module functions themselves are correct; the defect is the seat's choice of function or its missing negation. How I looked: I listed all 305 `DECIDE` titles in the five seat files with the first module function each calls, screened them with two keyword tests (title says may/can/need and function says barred/prevented/contravenes, or the reverse; and a mismatch in negation words), and read the roughly 150 pairs the screens printed; these seven were the only real ones among them, and I confirmed each with an assertion. I did not open the bodies of the other seat questions, and a mismatch that uses none of the screened words would not show; treat the seven as a lower bound.
