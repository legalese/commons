## Procedure layer: purchase of own shares, the authority for an off-market purchase, the filings after delivery, and payment out of capital by a private company (ss 690 to 723)

The procedure that Companies Act 2006 ss 690 to 723 lays down when a limited company buys its own shares: the contract made available before the vote and the approval that a purchase needs (ss 694 to 696), the copy kept for ten years (s 702), the return and the notice of cancellation after the shares are delivered (ss 707 and 708), and, for a private company paying out of capital, the directors' statement, the special resolution, the notices, the inspection, the objection to the court and the window for the payment (ss 709 to 723), with the solvency-statement route of ss 720A, 720B and 723(1A).
It is written as regulative rules over the Boolean encoding in `coa15-ch4.l4` and `coa15-ch5.l4`.

Files: `coa-proc-purchase-of-own-shares.l4` (the rules) and `coa-proc-purchase-of-own-shares-tests.l4` (83 traces and 15 assertions).
Source lines 9083-9523 of `ukpga-2006-46.txt` (s 723 ends at line 9522; Chapter 6 starts at line 9523).
No existing module is edited; the new module imports `coa-core`, `coa15-nouns`, `coa15-ch4` and `coa15-ch5` and calls their Boolean functions as gates.
It calls none of their DEONTICs.

### How the steps are joined

Where a step's clock runs from the event that ended the step before it, the steps are chained with HENCE.
Measured on both binaries on 25 November 2026 with a probe that imported nothing but the prelude: after `MUST x WITHIN 3 HENCE (MUST y WITHIN 5 ...)`, an `x` on day 2 and a `y` on day 7 is in time, and a `y` on day 8 is a breach; so the clock of the successor starts on the day of the earlier event, not on day 0 of the trace.
That is why the statement, the resolution and the two notices are one chain (the resolution is due within seven days of the statement, the notices within seven days of the resolution), and the application to the court, the applicants' notice, the court's order and the copy of the order are another.
Where several clocks run from the same later event (the resolution, the delivery of the shares), the rules stand side by side and each takes that event as its own day 0; the caller starts each trace on the day its TIMELINE line names.
A trigger that is a date the text counts from (the date of the meeting, the day of the first notice, the date a contract was concluded) is a DATE input of its rule.
The two notices of s 719 are joined by RAND so that both clocks run from the resolution; the keep-available duty of s 720 is a RAND of an opening duty and a bar on stopping, for the same reason.

### What the text leaves silent

- s 714(1) gives the directors no time to make the statement, and s 714(6) gives no time to annex the auditor's report; the chain's first duty has no clock.
- s 694(1) gives no time for the purchase; the bar stays open until the contract is approved.
- s 721(4) gives the court no time for its order; the court's step has no clock and no breach.
- s 702(3) has no end while neither completion of the purchase nor determination of the contract is known; the bar on stopping has no clock then.
- s 723(1) says the payment out of capital, "if made", must be made in the window: the text states a window and no duty to pay, so a company that does not pay is in no breach.
- s 721(2)(a) states no consequence for a late application, and s 722(1) states none for the applicants' notice (the offence of s 722(4) covers subsections (2) and (3) only).
- ss 696(5), 718(3) and 717(3) give invalidity or ineffectiveness, not an offence; the rules carry that consequence in the gate and the breach text.
- ss 702(5) and 720(3) give the duty no period; the 14 days appear only in ss 703(1) and 720(5), as the default that makes an offence.
- s 720B(6) says a late delivery does not affect the validity of the resolution; it says nothing about the duty, which stays a duty.
- s 719(1) and (2) do not say whether the day of the resolution is inside "the week immediately following" it.

### What was not encoded, and why

- ss 690 to 693A and 697 to 701 (the power to purchase, the financing, the authority for a market purchase, the variation and the release): they carry no clock apart from the five-year expiry of an authority, which the existing Boolean functions test; the gate of the bar in s 694 takes the meeting route only, so the employees' share scheme route of s 693A and the written-resolution route of s 696(2)(a) as a gate are not encoded.
- ss 703(2) and (3), 704, 705 and 706: penalties, assignment, the source of other payments and the treatment of the shares; no clock.
- ss 707(2) to (5) and 708(2) and (3): the contents of the return and of the statement of capital.
- ss 710 to 712: the amount of the permissible capital payment and the available profits; the amount is an input of the directors' statement and the helper functions already exist.
- s 713(1): its consequence, that a payment out of capital is not lawful unless ss 714, 716, 719 and 720 are met, is carried by the breach texts and by the bar on the payment; there is no separate rule.
- s 714(2) to (5) and s 715: the contents of the statement and the offence of an opinion without reasonable grounds.
- ss 719(1)(a) to (d) and (3): the contents of the notice and the meaning of an appropriate national newspaper.
- s 720A(2)(a): the solvency statement made not more than 15 days before the resolution is a count back from the resolution, tested by the existing `s 720A(2)`, not a clock after it.
- ss 720B(3), (4) and (6) to (9): the registrar's registration, the effect of registration, the validity of a late delivery and the offence; the offence is carried by the breach text of s 720B(1) and (5).
- s 721(3) and (5) to (7), s 722(4) and (5): the court's other powers, and the penalty; the court's extension of a date is an input of the s 723 rule, and its other powers are not encoded.
- s 1121 (the officer in default) and the penalties: the offences are carried by the breach texts of the duties, which name the section that makes the failure an offence.
