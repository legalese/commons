## Where this brief and PLAN.md differ

1. Imports in layer 1.
   The brief says layer 1 "IMPORTs the keel's modules `coa-core.l4` and `coa-definitions.l4`"; PLAN.md section 1 says import `coa-core`, and section 4.1 says parts "cannot import the keel's definitions module" and take its answers as inputs.
   I followed PLAN.md in the six layer-1 modules (they import `prelude`, `daydate` and `coa-core` only) and the brief where it can be tested: `coa-definitions` is imported by the tests and by the seat layer, which call the keel's `s 4(1)`, `s 4(2)`, `s 3(1)`, `s 1158` and `s 1173(1)` functions to produce the inputs, so the wiring is run, not assumed.
   Reason, measured on 1.6.110: a `GIVEN` named like an imported function (`` `s 4(2) the company is a public company` ``) shadows the function silently, with no warning, so a module that both imports the keel and names its seam inputs as PLAN.md 4.1 asks would carry two things with one name.
   This departs from "where PLAN.md and this brief differ, this brief governs"; the lead should decide, and the cost of the other way is small (add the import and rename the seam inputs).
2. The seam names follow PLAN.md section 4 (`s <answering section> <the question that section answers>`), with `MAYBE BOOLEAN` for answers that can be NOTHING and plain `BOOLEAN` for facts a witness can state.

## Seat

The individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member (shareholder) of a company registered under the Act, or who deals with one, asking on facts they can state what the Act requires or lets them do, what rights they have against the company or its directors, what they must disclose, file, approve or vote on and by when, and what follows (liability, offences, penalties) if it is not done.
(Assumed, not ruled: the Act is addressed to companies, so the individual is whoever stands in a company's management or ownership.)
Part 12 is addressed to the company and its directors and officers, so the seat layer speaks from inside the company ("my company"): the person is a director, secretary or other officer asking what the company must do, what they may do for it, what the Secretary of State may do to it, and what happens to the company and to them if it is not done.
No seat for an official: the Secretary of State and the registrar appear only as the parties the text names (the core's `Party`).

## What is encoded and what is not

Encoded: every provision of lines 3567 to 3716 that creates a rule, a definition, a list or a period, as 153 coverage rows (the table below), in six layer-1 modules in the text's own order (ss 270 to 273, s 274, ss 279G to 279I, ss 279J to 279L, s 279M, s 280), with a tests file for each group (five files) and a seat layer of 59 questions with a case file.
Not encoded, by design: headings (inert, reason 1); the Secretary of State's regulation-making powers and their procedure in ss 279J(5) and (6) and 279K(2) and (3) (inert, reason 2); the bare "..." that stands where a cross-heading was (inert, reason 3); and ss 274A to 279F, twelve sections that this text shows as a heading and a line of dots (repealed or blank in this text).
Not decided, by design: wherever the text hands a judgment to someone else (a director's view of a secretary's capability, "all reasonable steps", "reasonable excuse") the judgment is a plain input; wherever it hands a definition to another section (s 4, s 12, s 9, s 250, s 251, s 1088, s 1121, s 1141, s 1173(1)) the answer is an input (Seams, below).
The sums of money behind "level 4" and "level 5 on the standard scale" are not in the Act and are inputs; the numbers in the tests are illustrations.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (coa6)

  Each such branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`.
- `NOTHING` (null, "not covered"): the Act does not decide the case on these facts: a permission whose "if" condition is not met, or whose subject or grantee falls outside its description ("may be done by or to ..."), an offence limited by "without reasonable excuse" or "who is in default", a scope the section does not reach (s 271 for a private company), a period still running, a question that depends on an answer another section gives and the caller has not supplied.
  Each such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, with one of: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`: null is not "no" (for a permission it is not "may not"; for an offence it is not "no offence").
  The silent failure here is a caller that tests the answer with a plain truth test: JUST FALSE and NOTHING are both falsy in many hosts, and `l4 batch` prints them as `false` and `null`, which a loose consumer can treat alike.
- `REFUSE` is not used: "not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted in the tests.
- Some functions return other types: the three last-day functions (`s 279G(5) the last day of the period` and its sisters) return a `DATE` and the three "days in the period" constants a `NUMBER` (the text has one answer); `s 272(7) the most a person guilty of an offence may be fined on summary conviction, in pounds` returns a `NUMBER` (the level itself); the other pounds functions return `MAYBE NUMBER`, the penalty functions `MAYBE` of a record, and the required-information functions `MAYBE` of a list, each with NOTHING meaning "not covered".

## What the text turns out to say (coa6)

1. **A reference to a section that this text shows as repealed.**
   s 272(4)(b) and (5) (lines 3591, 3593) require "notice of it under section 276", and s 276 is a heading and a line of dots (lines 3625 and 3626).
   The only provision left in this text that makes a company notify the registrar of a change of secretary is s 279G(1)(a) (3648 to 3650).
   The encoding takes the notice day as an input (F-276) and does not substitute s 279G.
2. **Two wordings of s 272(7) run together.**
   Line 3598 ends "... one-tenth of level 5 on the standard scaleone-tenth of the greater of £5,000 or level 4 on the standard scale."
   The rendering carries two versions of the daily default fine (the `text_caveat` of the source says it may); the text does not say where each applies (F-272-7).
3. **A typographical slip in s 279I(1)(b).**
   Line 3664 reads "as one of the proposed joint secretaries of the company become did not become one of the joint secretaries on its incorporation."
   Read as "did not become" (F-279I-1b); the quote line carries the slip as printed.
4. **The register-of-secretaries regime is blank in this text.**
   Twelve sections, ss 274A, 275, 276, 277, 278, 279 and 279A to 279F, appear as a heading and a line of dots (lines 3621 to 3645), and a bare "..." stands where a cross-heading was (line 3633).
   What is left of Part 12 in these lines, besides ss 270 to 274 and s 280, is ss 279G to 279M: the duty to notify (279G to 279I), what the required information is (279J to 279L) and the offence (279M).
5. **No consequence is stated for ss 271 and 273.**
   Within these lines, s 271 creates no offence and no penalty; the enforcement route is the Secretary of State's direction (s 272), and the offence is failing to obey the direction (s 272(6)).
   s 273(1) puts a duty on the directors and says nothing of what follows if they fail, or if the secretary lacks the qualifications.
6. **A vacant office and a private company without a secretary overlap.**
   A private company that has no secretary is "without a secretary" (s 270(2)) and its office of secretary is vacant (s 274); s 270(3)(b)(i) lets "a director" do what the secretary would do, and s 274(a) and (b) let a deputy or an authorised person do it.
   The text does not say which route prevails; each is encoded on its own words.
7. **A director cannot be both halves of a two-person requirement.**
   s 270(3)(b)(i) lets a director act in place of an absent secretary, but s 280 says a provision requiring a thing to be done by or to "a director and the secretary" is not satisfied by one person acting as both.
   A company with a lone director who is also the secretary cannot satisfy a two-person provision, and the encoding does not read s 270(3)(b) as an exception to s 280 (the text does not say so).
   The rule of s 280 is asserted in `coa6-tests-vacancy-and-dual-capacity.l4` (its first s 280 assertion); the interplay with s 270(3)(b) is a reading, not an assertion.
8. **Three 14-day clocks start on three different days.**
   s 279G(5): the day the person becomes or ceases to be the secretary; s 279H(3): the day the change occurs; s 279I(5): the day the company was incorporated.
   Each is "the period of 14 days beginning with" that day, so the last day is the 13th day after it.
9. **Ceasing needs no statements.**
   s 279G(3) applies only to "A notice under subsection (1)(a)": a notice that a person ceased to be secretary needs the date (s 279G(2)) and nothing else.
10. **s 279M(3) names a penalty for each place, s 272(7) does not.**
    England and Wales: "to a fine" (no limit and no daily default fine stated); Scotland or Northern Ireland: level 5 and one-tenth of level 5 daily.
    s 272(7) states one penalty (with two wordings run together) and no place.
