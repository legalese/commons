## What this encoding is

**Source.** `ukpga-2006-46.txt` is a plain-text rendering of the Act and its Schedules from legislation.gov.uk (Open Government Licence v3.0, `STUB-SOURCE-LICENSE.md`) made by a converter script (`SOURCES.md`); it is not the official text, and it "may carry more than one extent version of a provision". Quote lines in the `.l4` files are copied from it by `srcrefs.py quote`, never retyped.

**What is encoded.** Every provision of lines 5418-5894: s 414C (contents of strategic report) to s 414D; Chapter 5, the directors' report (ss 415 to 419A); Chapter 6, the directors' remuneration report of a quoted company (ss 420 to 422A); Chapter 7, publication of accounts and reports (ss 423 to 436); Chapter 8, a public company's laying of accounts before a general meeting (ss 437, 438); Chapter 9, a quoted company's members' approval of the remuneration report and policy (ss 439 to 440).
Regulation-making powers (ss 416(4), 421(1), (2), (2A), 422A(4), 426(3), (6)), headings, a purpose clause and two lead-ins are inert (reasons 1 and 2, below); eighteen units and two lines are blank in the text.

**Seat.** An individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member (shareholder) of a company registered under the Act, or who deals with one, asking on facts they can state what the Act requires or lets them do, what rights they have against the company or its directors, what they must disclose, file, approve or vote on and by when, and what follows (liability, offences, penalties) if it is not done. (Assumed, not ruled: the Act is addressed to companies, so the individual is whoever stands in a company's management or ownership.)
The seat layer has 128 questions, each named "Under section N, ..." and each marked with whose question it is (a director's, a company secretary's, a member's or debenture holder's, or someone who deals with the company).
No seat for an official: the Secretary of State's powers (regulations, guidance), the court's sentencing and the State's prosecution appear only as what they mean for the person (what may be done to them).

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (coa9)

(The rule of 2026-10-29, assumed, not ruled by a lawyer; the rulings of 2026-11-04 and 2026-11-05 are copied word for word at the end of this file.)

Every answer of this part is a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (or a `MAYBE` number, date, list or penalty).

- **JUST TRUE** says the section says yes to the case: the duty applies, the entitlement exists, the offence is committed, the document is what the term means.
- **JUST FALSE, "the text says no"**, is given only where the text says no in its own words: a "means" definition ("“senior manager” means a person who ..."), a flat negative ("Nothing in this section requires the disclosure ...", "It is not a defence to prove ...", "must not be conditional on the payment of a fee"), an exception once it is met ("Except in the case of a company entitled to the small companies exemption"), a period the text fixes ("not later than the end of the period for filing"), or a condition the text introduces with "if", "where" or "provided" that fails ("If default is made in complying with section 423 or 424": no default, no offence by the company). In this part JUST FALSE also stands for one convention that the brief leaves to the encoder: a duty stated as a bare "must" (a report "must contain", a copy "must state") answers JUST FALSE where a stated item is missing, as a compliance answer (the bare-must forks named at the head of the fork register).
  Each such branch carries a comment `-- FALSE-kind (...)` with the quoted words.
- **NOTHING, "not covered"**, is given where the text does not decide: where a scope limit has no "only" and the case is outside it ("In the case of a quoted company ...": a company that is not quoted); where a limb is introduced by "who" and the person is outside it ("every director of the company who knew ..."); where a list is not closed ("The exemption is relevant to ..."); where the text points to a power, a regulation or another section that is not among the sources (section 1121, sections 465 to 467, the regulations of sections 421 and 426); where the text carries two versions and does not choose (s 415A(2), s 438(4)); and where there is no amount or date to give (a fine "without a stated limit").
  Each such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, <why> being silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, or limited by its own wording.
- **A caller must never turn NOTHING into FALSE.** In particular: a director who is outside "every director who knew or was reckless ..." gets NOTHING, not "you commit no offence"; a company that is not quoted gets NOTHING from a rule that says "In the case of a quoted company", not "you are exempt"; and where the Act says "a fine" and states no limit the answer is NOTHING, not zero.
- **Bars** (s 434(2), s 435(2): "must not publish ...") are answered in the direction of the bar: JUST TRUE means "the section bars it"; NOTHING means the bar is not engaged on these facts. Neither is a permission.
- **In the seat layer**, each question is titled in the direction its answer runs ("does the section excuse ...", "am I entitled ...", "do I commit an offence ...", "is the company barred ..."), and an input that the Act's other parts decide (is the company quoted? is this officer in default?) is stated by the person as a plain yes or no.
- **Amounts** the Act leaves to others (the pounds the statutory maximum and each level of the standard scale stand for) and dates the Act leaves to others (the end of the period for filing, section 442) are inputs of the seat questions that need them, with a description saying that the Act does not state them.
