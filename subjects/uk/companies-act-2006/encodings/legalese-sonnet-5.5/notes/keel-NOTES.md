## Seat

The individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member (shareholder) of a company registered under the Act, or who deals with one, asking on facts they can state what the Act requires or lets them do, what rights they have against the company or its directors, what they must disclose, file, approve or vote on and by when, and what follows if it is not done. (Assumed, not ruled: the Act is addressed to companies, so the individual is whoever stands in a company's management or ownership.) Officials and courts appear only as parties the text names (`Party` in the core), never as a seat. This module set has no seat layer: the seat layers belong to the part agents' modules and to the lead's merge; the keel's definitions are ordinary functions over plain facts that a seat layer can call.

## What is encoded and what is not

Encoded: every provision of the keel's two ranges (lines 1-55: Part 1, ss 1 to 6; lines 16446-16624: Part 38, ss 1158 to 1174), as 95 rows in the coverage table below. Nothing outside those lines is encoded. Schedules 6, 7 and 8 (the supplementary provisions to ss 1159 and 1162, and the Act's own index) are in coa29's range; the other definitions the Act scatters through its Parts are the part agents', and PLAN.md lists, from the Act's own index, which part answers each defined expression.

Not covered by design: where a definition is handed to another Act or an EU instrument (ss 1164(3), 1165(7) and (8), 1170, the nine borrowed terms of s 1173(1)), the borrowed answer is a plain input, and `NOTHING` is the answer when it is not known. Amounts, periods and thresholds the Act leaves to regulations or orders are inputs (none occurs in these ranges except "prescribed", s 1167).

## Fork register

Sites the 2026-11-05 ruling leaves to the encoder's judgment (an "unless" met, a bare "must" not done, "listed in Schedule N", a duty whose "if" is unmet, "(other than X)", a missing ledger event) carry one line here for the human reviewer to overrule.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING

The rule of 2026-10-29 (assumed, not ruled by a lawyer) as it reads in this subject. Every definition function answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN`.

- `JUST TRUE`: the Act says the case is within the term.
- `JUST FALSE` ("a no"): the Act says no in its own words: a "means" definition ("“undertaking” means ..."), a condition introduced with "if" or "only if" or "while" or "where" that is not met, a list closed with "means" or "are to", or a flat negative ("does not include an overseas company that has registered particulars", "Neither expression includes a friendly society", "cannot be formed as, or become"). Each such branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`.
- `NOTHING` ("not covered"): the Act does not decide the case: it lists without closing the list, says "includes" ("officer ... includes a director, manager or secretary"), is silent, leaves a term undefined, points outside the sources (the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, an EU Regulation, the Interpretation Act 1978), or limits a grant or a prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit. Each such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, <why> being one of silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`. `REFUSE` is not used: "not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted in the tests.
- A borrowed term (a definition handed to another instrument) is an input: its `JUST FALSE` is the other instrument's no, supplied by the caller, and its `NOTHING` means the caller does not know it.
- Two functions are not `MAYBE BOOLEAN`: `s 4(3)` and `s 5(2)` (a `DATE` for each place of registration) and `s 1173(1) the Gazette ...` (a total table for the three places). In each the text names a value for every place and no other.
