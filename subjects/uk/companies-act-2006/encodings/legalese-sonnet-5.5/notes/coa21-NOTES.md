## How this file was made, and what each part of it rests on

- **The source is not the official text.**
  It is a plain-text rendering of the Act made by a converter script (`inputs/SOURCES.md`), and nothing in this folder is "verified against the official text".
  Where the rendering looks damaged (dotted lines, doubled words, doubled full stops), the finding is recorded under "What the text turns out to say" and the encoding follows the rendering as it stands.
- **Layer 1** is the modules `coa21-ch1-panel.l4`, `coa21-ch2-opting-in.l4`, `coa21-ch3-offers.l4`, `coa21-ch3-squeeze-out.l4`, `coa21-ch3-sell-out.l4`, `coa21-ch4-directors-report.l4` and `coa21-part29-fraudulent-trading.l4`, with tests in the matching `coa21-tests-*.l4` files.
  Every quotation in them (`-- src:` lines and `@ref` locators) was copied from the text by a script, and every phrase quoted in a `FALSE-kind` comment was checked against the text by the same script.
- **Layer 2** is `coa21-seat.l4` and `coa21-cases-seat.l4`.
  The seat is an individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member of a company, or who deals with one; in this range that is a member who is bid for or squeezed out, a director of a company that is bid for or that opts in, an offeror, or anyone dealing with the Takeover Panel.
  That choice of seat is assumed, not ruled.
- **The coverage table** (`## Coverage (coa21)`) is generated by a script from the text and from the generated `.l4` files.
  The script reads the text, splits it into units, takes the disposition of each unit from a hand-written mapping, and then checks that every L4 name it prints is declared in a layer-1 module and that every encoded row has at least one assertion that mentions the name (or one of the type's constructors).
  Every other section of this file is written by hand, from reading the text and the code; each figure in it was taken from a command run on the files in this folder, named beside the figure.
- **Where PLAN.md and the brief differ, the brief governs.**
  (1) PLAN.md section 4.1 says parts cannot import the keel's definitions module, and the brief says layer 1 imports both keel modules: every coa21 layer-1 module imports both, and none uses a name from `coa-definitions` (checked: none of its 91 top-level names occurs in any coa21 file).
  The keel's answers are therefore taken as inputs named for the answering section, as PLAN.md section 4 says, and the lead wires them at the merge.
  (2) The brief says seam names are "as PLAN.md section 8 names them"; PLAN.md has sections 1 to 6 only, so its section 4 (seams) and section 6 (the per-part seam list) were followed.
- **Edit path.** The `.l4` files in this folder are generated from sources in `../../../work/src/` by `../../../work/build.py`; `work/` is scratch and is not shipped.

### F1. s 966(3) and s 966(3A): "or" between the two kinds of transfer or meeting

- **Provision and functions:** s 966(3) (restrictions on transfer) and s 966(3A) (restrictions on voting), `s 966(3) the second condition is met` and `s 966(3A) the third condition is met`.
- **Text:** the articles "provide that they are not to apply to— (a) transfers to the offeror ... during the offer period, or (b) transfers to any person at a time during the offer period when the offeror holds shares amounting to not less than 75% ..."; and for voting, "not to have effect on rights to vote at a general meeting of the company that— (i) decides whether to take any action which might result in the frustration of the takeover bid, or (ii) is held at a time when the offeror holds shares amounting to not less than 75% ...".
- **Reading A (taken):** the restrictions must be disapplied in both limbs; "not to apply to X or Y" says they are not to apply to X and not to apply to Y.
- **Reading B:** disapplying them in either limb is enough.
- **Licence for A:** the negative distributes over "or" in ordinary English, and each limb protects a different moment of a bid, so a company that disapplies only one has not opted its articles into the regime.
- **What moves:** with only one limb disapplied, A makes `s 966(3) the second condition is met` (and, for the voting limbs, `s 966(3A)`) answer `JUST FALSE`; B would answer `JUST TRUE`.
  The one-limb assertions in `coa21-tests-ch2.l4` (the second and third `s 966(3)` cases with restrictions present) show the A answer.
  At the level of `s 966(1) the company may opt in by the resolution`, a failed condition now answers `NOTHING` (fork F12), so the seat question on s 966(1) shows `NOTHING` for such a company, not `JUST FALSE`.

### F11. s 992(6): an application-date limit, for a financial year that began earlier

- **Provision and function:** `s 992(6) the amendments apply to the directors' report` (text line 13924); it takes the first day of the financial year.
- **Text:** "The amendments made by this section apply in relation to directors' reports for financial years beginning on or after 20th May 2006."
- **Reading A (taken):** a scope limit with no "only", so for a financial year that began before 20 May 2006 (for example 19 May 2006) the answer is `NOTHING`: the text says where the amendments apply and is silent on the earlier years (ruling of 2026-11-05 (c), "Periods, 'where' and scope limits").
- **Reading B:** a period the text fixes in its own words, so the answer for an earlier year is `JUST FALSE` (ruling of 2026-11-05 (a)).
- **Licence for A:** "apply in relation to ... financial years beginning on or after" limits the grant of application by its own wording and has no "only"; the question is whether the amendments apply, not whether a time limit on a duty has run.
- **Licence for B:** the date is a boundary the text fixes in its own words, and "on or after 20th May 2006" reads as a period, which ruling (a) answers `JUST FALSE` outside.
- **What moves:** one branch of the function and one assertion (`19 5 2006` in `coa21-tests-ch4-part29.l4`, with the seat case in `coa21-cases-seat.l4`); from 20 May 2006 onward both readings answer `JUST TRUE`.
  Neither reading says anything about whether the inserted words are still in force today.
- **Both readings are defensible, so a lawyer should see this as a question** (also M10 in the marginal readings).

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (coa21)

These are readings that are not forks (the text does not split two ways with different answers) but that a lawyer reading the answers should know about.
Each names where the encoding departs from what a quick reading of the text suggests, or where a result means less than it seems.

- **M1. Section 992 amends another Act, and its inserted text is encoded.**
  The brief lists "amendments of other Acts" as machinery, and subsections (1) and (3) are left inert on that ground.
  The text inserted by subsection (2) (paragraph 13 of Schedule 7 to the 1985 Act), by subsection (4) (section 234ZZA(5)) and by subsection (5) (section 251(2ZB)) is itself an operative duty on directors and companies, so it is encoded as the provision states it.
  The reviewer who prefers machinery can mark the rows inert (reason 2) and drop `coa21-ch4-directors-report.l4`; only the seat layer and its tests import it.
  The text does not say whether the inserted words are still in force.
  `s 992(6) the amendments apply to the directors' report` answers `NOTHING` for a financial year that began before 20 May 2006, and says nothing about the present.
- **M2. Section 964 amends the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and is left inert.**
  Its substance for this Act is the exception in s 948(6) and (7), which is encoded; the text inserted into section 349 of that Act (subsections (3A) and (3B)) is not.
  Section 964 and section 992 are treated differently because the text inserted by s 992 states a duty that the individual in the seat would be asked about, and the text inserted by s 964 states an exception for regulators.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (coa21)

A function in this range answers `JUST TRUE` when the text says yes to the case.
It answers `JUST FALSE` only where the text itself says no in its own words: a "means" definition, a condition the text introduces with "if", "only if", "as long as" or "unless" (and plain variants such as "while", "until" and "except"), a list the text closes with "only", a flat negative ("does not", "shall not", "may not"), a period the text fixes in its own words, or a "where" condition that fails.
Everywhere else it answers `NOTHING`: where the text lists without closing the list, says "includes", leaves a term undefined, is silent, points outside the sources, or limits a grant or prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit.
Each `JUST FALSE` carries a comment `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<quote>"`, whose quotation was checked against the text by script (189 such comments in layer 1).
Each `NOTHING` carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<kind>)` with one of silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording; the counts in layer 1 are: limited by its own wording 122, points outside the text 48, silent 18, includes 13, list not closed 4, undefined term 2.
In the seat layer the same answers read: `JUST TRUE` is yes, `JUST FALSE` is the text's own no, and `NOTHING` is "the text does not answer this on these facts".
The rulings behind these tags are recorded again below, word for word as the brief gives them, because rows in this range carry the `NOTHING-kind` and `FALSE-kind` tags they govern and this part has no per-row ruling entry other than the fork register above.

### Seat audit of 2026-11-12

Every seat question's name and `@export` text were read against the layer-1 function its body calls, for a direction mismatch (a permission asked, a bar answered, or the reverse) and for a subject mismatch ("must I" against "have I", a power against a duty).
All 52 questions were read: 0 direction mismatches and 1 subject mismatch.
The one found is the question on s 979(2): it asked "may the offeror compel me to sell my shares to him", but `s 979(2) the offeror may give notice to the holder of the shares` answers only whether the offeror may give the notice, and compulsion follows from s 981 and can be displaced by an order of the court under s 986.
It is now "Under section 979(2), may the offeror give me notice that he desires to acquire my shares" (the text's own words are "he may give notice ... that he desires to acquire those shares").

## What the text turns out to say (numbered findings)

1. **Two versions of the daily default fine are printed together** in s 980(8)(b)(i) and s 984(7)(b): "one-fiftieth of the statutory maximumone-fiftieth of the greater of £5,000 or the amount corresponding to level 4 on the standard scale for summary offences" (lines 13665 and 13757).
   The rendering does not say which is in force; this is fork F2.
2. **Dotted lines.** Thirteen places in the range show dots in place of words: lines 13161 (s 943(8)), 13232 and 13233 (s 948(7)(b), (c)), 13252 (s 950(1)(b)), 13403 (s 964(3)), 13412 (s 964(5)), 13453 (s 966(6)), 13459 (after "the Defence Council" in s 966(7)), 13500 (after "the Panel," in s 970(1)(a)), 13501 (s 970(1)(b)), 13502 (the end of s 970(2)), and 13509 and 13519 (a line of dots between two definitions in s 971(1)).

## What the stable binary does not accept

Found on 1.6.110 (main) and, where checked, 1.6.109 (main) during this run; the brief's own list is not repeated.
The exact message text was not kept for each; each item is what had to be avoided.

- **Loud (a parse or type error, or a refused construct):**
  - `NOT EQUALS JUST TRUE` is not valid; write `NOT (x EQUALS JUST TRUE)`.
  - `GIVEN` at section level (under a `§` heading) is not accepted for a group of rules; each rule has its own `GIVEN`.
  - A nested `CONSIDER` inline in a branch of another `CONSIDER`, and an `IF ... THEN JUST TRUE ELSE JUST FALSE` written as a function argument, were not accepted; each was replaced by a `WHERE` helper.
  - A `WITHIN` expression in a regulative rule needs an outer pair of parentheses.
  - `AFTER` in a regulative rule is not used; the traces use `(WAITUNTIL n)` (the brief names `WAITUNTIL`, not `WAIT UNTIL`).
  - The core `Party` has no constructor for "a person" or for an offeror, so those rules use their own party types (NEEDS-CORE.md).
- **Silent (exit code 0 and a wrong answer):**
  - `NOT x OR y` parses as `NOT (x OR y)`: `NOT` swallows the rest of the line.
    It produced wrong answers in two functions (s 952(3) and s 961(1)), which failing tests caught.
    In every layer-1 module each `NOT` is now followed by a parenthesised operand, and no `NOT (x)` is followed by `AND` or `OR` on the same line unless the `NOT` is itself inside parentheses (checked by a script run in this session).
  - A `DEONTIC` rule cannot be the subject of an `#ASSERT`.
    Each regulative rule therefore has a decision-function twin that is asserted, and the rule itself has two `#TRACE` directives (one fulfilled, one in breach) whose outcomes are recorded below, in "Regulative rules".
  - `Date` does not reject a day number the month does not have; it rolls forward.
    Measured on 1.6.110 with a probe: `(Date 31 14 2027) MINUS 1` evaluates to 1 March 2028 (31 February 2028 is 2 March), and the same arithmetic for "three months beginning 30 November 2026" gave 1 March 2027 where the calendar says 28 February 2027.
    My first version of the s 980(2) period counted months this way and was wrong at month ends; it now uses `add months`, which clamps to the last day of a shorter month, and it has eight assertions on month ends, a year end and a leap February.
    No test I had written earlier covered a month end, and the function passed every earlier test.
  - `l4 run` exits 0 when an `#ASSERT` fails; the counts in "What check.sh prints" are the evidence, not the exit code.
