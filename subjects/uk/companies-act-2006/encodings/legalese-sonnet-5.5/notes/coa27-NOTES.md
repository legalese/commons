## Seat

The individual to whom the Act is applied: an individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member (shareholder) of a company registered under the Act, or who deals with one, asking on facts they can state what the Act requires or lets them do, what rights they have against the company or its directors, what they must disclose, file, approve or vote on and by when, and what follows (liability, offences, penalties) if it is not done. (Assumed, not ruled: the Act is addressed to companies, so the individual is whoever stands in a company's management or ownership.) In this range that person is: a director or company secretary whose company is directed to have a second audit (s 1248) or is prosecuted (ss 1255 to 1257); an auditor, or a person who calls themselves one (ss 1239, 1241 to 1245, 1250, 1253D to 1253DC); a person served with a notice (ss 1258, 1259); a shareholder or other holder of securities of an issuer, or a director of an issuer, on whom or about whom the competent authority acts (ss 1266 to 1271). There is no seat for an official: the Secretary of State, the registrar, the competent authority, the court and the Tribunal appear only as what they mean to the person.

Files: `coa27-seat.l4` (the questions) and `coa27-cases-seat.l4` (the cases). The amounts a level on the standard scale stands for, the statutory maximum, the general limit in a magistrates' court, every fee the Secretary of State prescribes and the proportions the transparency rules designate are not in the Act: the seat asks them as numbers and says so in each `@desc`.

### Seat audit of 2026-11-12

Each of the 58 seat questions was read, as its `@export` text, its question name and its input `@desc`s, against the body of the layer-1 function it calls (the answers that function can return, not only its name), looking for a question that asks one thing over a function that answers another: a direction mismatch (a question titled as a permission over a function that answers the bar, or the reverse) or a subject mismatch ("must I" over a function that answers "have I"). Result: 1 mismatched, 0 of direction and 1 of subject. It is the question under s 1257(2), which asked "must proceedings ... be brought in the name of the association" while `s 1257(2) the proceedings against the unincorporated association are brought as the section requires` answers whether they were; it is now `Under section 1257(2), are the proceedings against my unincorporated association brought in the name of the association as the section requires`. Four questions put a positive question over a function whose only answers are JUST FALSE and NOTHING (s 1251(1), s 1253D(3), s 1267 FSMA 89H(5), s 1270 FSMA 90A(6)(b)); they were left, because each question asks the same predicate the function evaluates, so JUST FALSE reads as "no", NOTHING as "not decided", and JUST TRUE cannot occur. Two wordings were read and left, being neither direction nor subject mismatches: s 1248(5)(a) asks "the last day on which my company can comply" and the function returns the last day of the 21-day period after which failing to comply is an offence; the `@export` text of s 1251(1) carries the premise "if I have not paid the fee", which the input it asks can contradict (the question name carries no premise). The assertions the rename broke are restated in place in `coa27-cases-seat.l4` with `-- RECONCILED 2026-11-12`.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (coa27)

Every decision function of this part answers `JUST TRUE`, `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING` (a `MAYBE`), except where the question is a date, an amount, a court or a table lookup, whose answer is that value. In this subject:

- `JUST TRUE` means the text says yes to the case: the person is guilty, the duty exists, the power may be used, the definition is met, the claim is made out.
- `JUST FALSE` means the text says no in its own words: a "means" definition not met ("a registered third country auditor means a third country auditor who is entered in the register"); an "if", "where", "only if", "unless" or "when" not met ("A person is guilty of an offence if"; "This section applies only to information ... reasonably required"; "no application is to be regarded as duly made unless this subsection is complied with"); a flat negative ("This section does not authorise"; "Nothing in the sections referred to in subsection (1) authorises"; "does not affect"; "must not be greater"); a defence not shown ("this section gives no defence on these facts", never "no defence at all"); a period or place the text fixes in its own words and the case falls outside ("within the period of twelve months beginning with the date").
- `NOTHING` means "not covered": the text lists without closing the list ("includes" in s 1261(1) "director", "officer" and "transfer"; the persons s 89H applies to); leaves a term to another instrument (Regulation (EU) No. 600/2014, the Transparency Obligations Directive, the Data Protection Act 2018, the Communications Act 2003: the borrowed answer is an input and NOTHING passes through); is silent (a condition that is necessary and not said to be enough); points to a power, regulation, rate or authority outside the sources (what a level on the standard scale stands for, the fee the Secretary of State prescribes); or limits a grant or prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit ("It is an offence for a person whose name does not appear on the register ... to describe himself as ..."; "for a period not exceeding 10 days").
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`; the seat layer passes it through unchanged. `REFUSE` does not exist on the stable binary: "not covered" is `NOTHING` or a constructor, and is asserted (for example the `NOTHING` assertions in every test module).

Layer-1 functions that carry at least one NOTHING-kind tag, by the reason the tag gives (a function with two reasons is counted under each): includes: 6; limited by its own wording: 90; list not closed: 4; points outside the text: 15; silent: 7. The six reasons are the brief's: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.

## What the stable binary does not accept

- **`#ASSERT` on a deontic value.** `#ASSERT <regulative rule> EQUALS FULFILLED` is reported as `eval/unsupported-equality` ("Trying to check equality on types that do not support it") on 1.6.110. So each regulative rule in this part (`s 1248(5)(a)`, `s 1248(7)(a)`, `s 1253B(2)`) has `#TRACE` runs in its test module (the act in time gives `FULFILLED`; the act one day late gives `DEONTIC BREACHED`; I compared the six trace results on 1.6.110 and 1.6.109 by hand and they are identical) and a twin decision function over the same dates whose assertions `check.sh` counts. `check.sh` does not see a different `#TRACE` outcome.
- I used no `REFUSE`, no `#ASSERT REFUSED`, no `YMD`, no section `GIVEN`, no sets and no `TYPICALLY`. PLAN.md section 5 records that both supplied builds nevertheless accept `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` and `YMD`; `check.sh` compares those two builds only, so its "same" column cannot show it. I wrote `NOTHING` and `Date day month year` throughout.
- A seat question that returns a record (`MAYBE Penalty`) or a user-declared type is accepted by `l4 run`; I did not deploy anything, so I have not tested the Interact form on those.
