## Seat

The individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member (shareholder) of a company registered under the Act, or who deals with one (a debenture holder, a creditor, a person offered the company's securities), asking on facts they can state what the Act requires or lets them do, what rights they have against the company or its directors, what they must disclose, file, approve or vote on and by when, and what follows (liability, offences, penalties) if it is not done.
(Assumed, not ruled: the Act is addressed to companies, so the individual is whoever stands in a company's management or ownership.)
The registrar and the court appear only as parties the text names (`Party` in the core) and as what they mean for the person; there is no seat for an official.
No second seat layer was written.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (coa16)

The rule of 2026-10-29 (assumed, not ruled by a lawyer) as it reads in this subject.
Every decision function answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (or a `MAYBE` of a number, a date, an enum or a record where the question asks for one).

- `JUST TRUE`: the Act says the case is within the provision ("yes, the instrument is a debenture", "yes, the company commits the offence").
- `JUST FALSE` ("a no"): the Act says no in its own words: a definition by "means", "is" or "are" that the case does not meet; a condition the text introduces with "if", "unless", "so long as" or "where" that is not met; a period the text fixes ("within two months", "within five working days", "within 21 days") that has run out; a flat negative ("is not invalid by reason only", "does not contravene", "Nothing in this Chapter affects", "does not affect the validity", "does not operate"); and, for a function named for a grant or a power, a where, when or upon kind of gate that fails ("on payment of such fee", "In the case of any such default", "While any trustee ... remains entitled", "Where a remedial order is made against the company itself"), which is listed in the name-test sweep of 2026-11-12 and answers as ruling (b) gives, with `NOTHING` as the other reading. Each such branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`. A gate, an exclusion or a presumption is named for what it does, so its JUST FALSE says "this provision does not apply", never "the opposite substantive result": `s 756(3)` JUST FALSE is "subsection (3) does not exclude the offer", not "the offer is to the public".
- `NOTHING` ("not covered"): the Act does not decide the case: a list not closed with "only" ("an application may be made by ..."), "includes" ("debenture" includes ...), a term the text leaves undefined (whether a share is one of "any other securities"; what is an offer "to the public" beyond (2) to (4)), silence (a company that has not yet registered while the two months run), a power or reference that points outside the sources (s 112, s 250, s 1121, s 1136, Schedule 6 to the Insolvency Act 1986), or a grant, power or prohibition limited by its own wording where the case falls outside the limit ("on request", "knowingly or recklessly", "every officer ... who is in default", a list of who may apply). Each such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, <why> being one of silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`. `REFUSE` is not used: "not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted in the tests.
- A borrowed term (a definition handed to another Act or another part) is an input: its `JUST FALSE` is the other provision's no, supplied by the caller, and its `NOTHING` means the caller does not know it.
- A money answer is a `MAYBE NUMBER`: `NOTHING` where the Act states no ceiling (a fine "to a fine" on indictment). The pounds a level on the standard scale or the statutory maximum stands for are inputs.
- A deadline that has not yet passed, with the duty not yet done, answers `NOTHING` ("no answer yet": silent), not `JUST FALSE`.

## Findings: what the text turns out to say

1. **s 741(1) states two standards.** "As soon as practicable and in any event within two months after the date of the allotment": the second limb is an outer limit, the first a judgment (F11).
2. **s 743(2) states no time**, and s 743(4) makes "default for 14 days" an offence without saying when default begins (F5).
3. **s 749(2) names only officers in default.** Unlike ss 741(2), 743(4), 746(1) and 767(1), it does not say the company commits the offence (F22).
4. **s 762(1)(c) ends "..."** in this rendering: words are omitted after "the consideration for the payment or benefit," and there is no "and" before (d). It is converter output marking omitted words, not a repealed provision, so no row is `repealed or blank`; the application's limbs (a) to (e) are encoded as five conditions all required.
5. **PLAN.md's seam "s 754: sch 4 [coa29]" is a false positive.** s 754(3) names "Schedule 6 to the Insolvency Act 1986 (c. 45) or Schedule 4 to the Insolvency (Northern Ireland) Order 1989", neither a Schedule of this Act (verified against lines 9810-9811).
6. **The "relevant date" is two different things.** In s 751(2) it is 1 July 1948 or 1 July 1961, which regime applied being a fact; in s 754(3) it is the date possession is taken, "for the purposes of those Schedules". Both are encoded, under different names.
7. **The authorised minimum is stated in sterling only.** £50,000 is the Act's own figure (s 763(1)(a)); the euro equivalent is "the prescribed euro equivalent", set by order, so it is an input; an order under s 764 may substitute another sterling amount (the layer-1 functions take it as an input; the seat takes "no such order").
8. **s 765 forbids adding sterling and euro capital**: it "must be met either by reference to allotted share capital denominated in sterling or ... in euros (but not partly in one and partly in the other)", and share capital in any other currency is not counted. 30,000 pounds with 30,000 euros meets neither (tested).
9. **s 767(2)(a) states no ceiling** ("on conviction on indictment, to a fine"), so the seat's question about the greatest fine answers NOTHING for a conviction on indictment; the penalty of s 747(3)(a) likewise has "a fine (or both)" with no ceiling, while its imprisonment term is two years.
10. **s 745(4) says the court's order "must contain" provision to identify the requests**: a requirement on the court's own order, encoded as a function (`s 745(4) the order identifies the requests to which it applies`).
11. **s 757(1) has two routes** (an application, or Part 30 proceedings) but s 757(3) lists applicants only for the application route; Part 30 is another part's and is a plain fact here.
12. **s 758(2)'s exception needs both limbs**: "(a) ... and (b) ...". One limb alone does not lift the court's duty (tested).
13. **s 755(1) and s 4(2) use the same phrase** ("limited by shares or limited by guarantee and having a share capital"); the keel reads the share capital as qualifying both, this part reads it as qualifying the guarantee limb only (F12).
14. **s 766 is entirely machinery.** It lets regulations deal with shares in several currencies, redenominations and new allotments; no regulations are among the sources, so s 765(1) and (3) answer those cases from the text alone (other currencies are not counted).

## What the stable binary does not accept, and what it does

Measured on 2026-11-13 with `L4_BIN` 1.6.110 and `L4_MAIN` 1.6.109, by running small files and from this part's own modules.
- Rejected (loud): `EMPTY : LIST OF T` as an argument (a parse error at the `(`); write `EMPTY` and let the function's type decide.
- Accepted although the brief says the stable line does not have them (a surprise, not a licence): `REFUSE "no"` with `#ASSERT REFUSED`, `YMD 2026 11 13` (printed `DATE OF 13, 11, 2026`), and `NOT TRUE AND FALSE` read as `NOT (TRUE AND FALSE)`, which is `TRUE`; all measured on both builds. None of these is used in this part: `grep -E 'REFUSE|YMD|BECAUSE|TYPICALLY|WAIT UNTIL' coa16-*.l4` finds only `LEST BREACH BY ... BECAUSE "..."`, the form the brief allows, and every `NOT` is bracketed.
- Works on both builds (measured by the modules): forward references at the top level (`s 761(2)` calls `s 765(1)`, defined later); `WHERE` blocks with a `CONSIDER` inside; a `LIST` of records built with `OF`; `add months` and `add years` clamping to the last day of a shorter month; `FLOOR`; `DEONTIC Party <typed action>` rules with a computed `WITHIN`, `SHANT` joined by `RAND`, `ROR` of two obligations, a `LEST` chain, and `(WAITUNTIL n)` in a trace.
- An `@export` question may take an input of an enum type (the core's `Place of registration`, `Liability of members`, `Mode of conviction`) and of `DATE`, and may return a `MAYBE` of a number, a date or an enum.
- An `#ASSERT` cannot compare a `DEONTIC`: regulative rules are tested by `#TRACE`, and the same duties are encoded as decision functions that the `#ASSERT`s test.
