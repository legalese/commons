## Seat

The individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member (shareholder) of a company registered under the Act, or who deals with one, asking on facts they can state what the Act requires or lets them do, what rights they have against the company or its directors, what they must disclose, file, approve or vote on and by when, and what follows (liability, offences, penalties) if it is not done.
(Assumed, not ruled: the Act is addressed to companies, so the individual is whoever stands in a company's management or ownership.)
The seat layer is the two files `coa29-seat.l4` and `coa29-cases-seat.l4`.
There is never a seat for an official: the Takeover Panel, the registrar and the Secretary of State appear only as parties the text names.
Schedule 2 is addressed to the Takeover Panel; its seat questions are asked by a person whose information the Panel holds, under a `§§` heading that says so (a situation the person may not be in).
Schedule 3 and Schedule 9 have no seat question: Schedule 3 is inert (amendments of the Companies Act 1985) and Schedule 9 is blank in this text.
Where the seat is an individual, a limb that needs the recipient to be a company is a fixed placeholder in the question, with a comment saying so (Schedule 5 paras 4(1)(b), 6(b), 7(1)(b)).

### Seat audit of 2026-11-12

Every one of the 40 seat questions was read, by its name, its `@export` text and the `@desc` of each input, against the layer-1 function its body calls and what that function answers.
Direction mismatches (a question titled as a permission whose function answers a bar, or the reverse): none.
The only bar is `Under schedule 7 paragraph 4(1), is the other undertaking barred ...`, and its function answers the bar; the "may" questions (`Under schedule 5 paragraph 9`, `16(3)`, `17(2)`, `17(3)`) call functions that answer a permission, and the "must" question (`Under schedule 5 paragraph 14(1)`, "until what date must ...") returns the last day of the period the duty runs over.
Subject mismatches: one.
`Under schedule 5 paragraph 12(1)` was titled "... in a form and by a means I can read and keep", but its function answers whether the form and means are ones the paragraph allows, which turns on what the company reasonably considered would let the recipient read and retain a copy, not on whether the recipient can in fact.
It is now `Under schedule 5 paragraph 12(1), did my company make the document available in a form and by a means it reasonably considered would let me read it and keep a copy`, with its `@export` text and the two input `@desc`s saying so.
Two input `@desc`s in `Under schedule 5 paragraph 8` that restate the paragraph 9 and paragraph 12(1) answers were tightened to say what those functions answer (the paragraph 9 one now says "may the company send it ... by a website", which includes the revocation limb).
The two seat cases that call the retitled question are restated in place, each with a `-- RECONCILED 2026-11-12: was ...` comment; their facts and expected values did not change.
Questions read twice and kept: `16(3)`, `17(2)` and `17(3)` ask for a "may" grant and take as inputs how the document is sent, so JUST TRUE means the sending is inside the grant and null means it is outside it (a grant limited by its own wording), which is what their functions answer.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (coa29)

The rule of 2026-10-29 (assumed, not ruled by a lawyer) as it reads in Schedules 2 to 9. Every decision function answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (the Schedule 5 date functions answer `MAYBE DATE`, the paragraph 9 and 10 reductions `MAYBE NUMBER`, the Schedule 8 lookup `MAYBE (LIST OF STRING)`).

- `JUST TRUE`: the Schedule says the case is within the provision: a document is validly sent, an address may be used, a person is a specified person, a right is treated as held by the other company.
- `JUST FALSE` ("a no"): the Schedule says no in its own words: a definition by "means" or "are to" (a "lawyer", a "control contract", "voting rights"), an "only", "may only" or "only if" (Sch 4 paras 4A(2), 6, 7(1); Sch 5 paras 6, 7(1), 9, 12(2)), a flat negative ("Nothing in paragraph 6 or 7 shall be construed as requiring ...", "This paragraph shall not be read as affecting ..."), a "where" or "until" that is not or no longer met (Sch 5 paras 4(2), 17(3)), and, by the status-quo reading below, an unmet "if" ("is validly sent or supplied ... if it is sent ... in accordance with this Part") and a bare "must" not done. In Schedule 2 it also means "no head in the list holds", because s 948(3)(b) and (c) hand the question to the list. Each such branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`.
- `NOTHING` ("not covered"): the Schedule does not decide the case: a "may" grant limited by its own wording and the case outside it (Sch 4 paras 3(1), 4; Sch 5 paras 4(1), 16(3), 17(2)), a scope limit with no "only" ("This Schedule applies to documents or information sent or supplied to a company", "in relation to an undertaking which does not have general meetings"), "includes" (Sch 5 para 17(5)), an expression the index does not list (Sch 8), a meaning borrowed from another Act or a designation made outside the sources that the caller does not know (Sch 2 Parts 1 and 2), a seam input that is itself NOTHING, "subject to anything in the company's articles" when the articles do provide otherwise (Sch 5 paras 16 and 17: the articles are not among the sources), or a period that has not yet ended (Sch 5 paras 10(3)(b) and 11(3)(b)). Each such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, <why> being one of silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`. `REFUSE` is not used.
- Two kinds of answer are not `MAYBE BOOLEAN`: the three regulative rules' results (a `DEONTIC`, read with `#TRACE`) and the Schedule 5 `sch 5 para 14(1) the period throughout which ...` record.
- A question of the seat layer says in its `@export` text what null means for it.
- The Schedules say when a communication is VALIDLY sent or is TAKEN to be agreed; they do not say what follows from an invalid communication, and neither does this encoding.

## What the text turns out to say (numbered findings)

Each finding is something found while encoding, with the line of the text and the place in the code that shows it.
No assertion fails and none is refused or edited: a failing assertion would be listed here as a finding, and there is none.

1. **`NOT (x) AND y` bit once.** In a first draft of Schedule 6 para 2 the condition was written `NOT (x) AND y`, which parses as `NOT ((x) AND y)`; a test caught it, and it is now `((NOT (x)) AND y)` (the same trap as the guide's section 5 rule; measured again on 2026-11-13: `NOT TRUE AND FALSE` EQUALS `TRUE` is satisfied on 1.6.110). Every `NOT` in the files of this folder is bracketed.
2. **The same sentence is a grant in Schedule 4 and a duty in Schedule 5.** Sch 4 para 3(1) says hard copy "may be sent or supplied by hand or by post" (line 19403); Sch 5 para 3(1) says it "must be" handed over or sent (line 19439). 3. **Sch 5 para 9 ends with a clause that fits either limb.** "and has not revoked that agreement" (line 19475) follows both (a) and (b); it is read as closing both (fork F2), so a revoked agreement under (a) is JUST FALSE too.
4. **Sch 5 para 4(1)(d) says "the register" and defines nothing.** Limb (c) says "the company's register of members" (line 19447); limb (d) says "as shown in the register" (line 19448). The Act's own index (Sch 8, line 19888) says "the register | section 1080", and s 1080(2) (line 15235) calls the records the registrar keeps "the register". ## What the stable binary does not accept, and what it does

Measured on 2026-11-13 with `L4_BIN` (1.6.110, main, e51181d) by running one-file probes (`l4 run`); the `DEONTIC` probe, the `NOT` and date-arithmetic probe, and the `REFUSE` and `YMD` probes were also run on `L4_MAIN` (1.6.109, main, 4c887e0) with the same result; the other probes were run on 1.6.110 only.
The binary governs, so the modules use none of the constructs that are rejected or surprising.

Rejected (loud, `l4 run` prints a `DiagnosticSeverity_Error`):

- `#ASSERT` on a `DEONTIC` value (`#ASSERT duty EQUALS FULFILLED`): `eval/unsupported-equality`, "assertion could not be evaluated: Trying to check equality on types that do not support it" (both builds). So the four regulative rules (`sch 2 part 3 para 2 ...`, `sch 5 para 12(1) ...`, `13(1)`, `14(1)`) are exercised by two `#TRACE` directives each (the duty done: the trace prints `FULFILLED`; nothing done and no deadline: the trace prints the standing `MUST` with its `LEST BREACH`), which are read by eye, and each has a decision-function twin that IS asserted. A `#TRACE` is not counted by `check.sh`, so a changed trace outcome is invisible to it.
- A continuation line of an `#ASSERT` (`#ASSERT TRUE` then `EQUALS TRUE` on the next line): `parse/unexpected-EQUALS`. Every `#ASSERT` is on one line.
- A `WITH`-named call with several arguments in an `#ASSERT` without commas (`(f WITH a IS 1 b IS 2) EQUALS 3`): `parse/unexpected-IS` at the second `IS`; written `(f WITH a IS 1, b IS 2) EQUALS 3` it is satisfied. With one argument and no brackets (`f WITH a IS 1 EQUALS 2`) the line is read as `a IS (1 EQUALS 2)` and fails with `check/type-mismatch` (the assertion is a NUMBER). The tests bracket every `WITH` call; in a function body the arguments go on separate lines, as in `coa29-seat.l4`.

Accepted although the brief says the stable line does not have them (a surprise, not a licence; the keel's notes record the same on both builds): `REFUSE "no"` (`l4 run` prints "The model refuses to answer") and `YMD 2026 11 12` (prints `DATE OF 12, 11, 2026`), both measured here on both builds. The modules use `Date day month year`, `NOTHING` and `MAYBE` only.

Silent (exit code 0, wrong answer if not known):

- `NOT TRUE AND FALSE` is read as `NOT (TRUE AND FALSE)`, which is `TRUE` (measured on both builds; finding 1). Every `NOT` is bracketed.
- `Date 29 2 2025` is `Date 1 3 2025` (an impossible date rolls forward, as the guide says); `` `add months` (Date 29 2 2024) 12 `` is `Date 28 2 2025` (it clamps; fork F5); `(Date 1 3 2026) PLUS 27` is `Date 28 3 2026` (`PLUS` on a `DATE` adds days) (measured on both builds).
- `check.sh`'s comparison of the two builds sees four counts per module (errors, satisfied, failed, refused). It does not see a different `#TRACE` outcome, nor any difference no assertion exercises; for this folder that means the six `#TRACE` outcomes of `coa29-tests-sch5.l4` and the two of `coa29-tests-sch2-part3.l4` rest on the 1.6.110 reading alone.
- `srcrefs.py quote` prints a raw line that has no word character (a lone `.` or a row of dots), but `srcrefs.py check` then rejects the quote line: "quotation has no text" (measured on line 19224, with `--refs` and with `--strict`, 2026-11-13).
