# Notes: piece uc1 of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013 (S.I. 2013/376)

Piece uc1 covers the title block and regulation 1, the rest of Part 1 (regulations 3 to 6), Part 2 (regulations 7 to 19A), Part 3 (regulations 20 to 22A) and Part 4 (regulations 23 to 37): lines 1-11 and 118-742 of `uksi-2013-376.txt`.
Regulation 2 and the shared vocabulary are the keel's (`uc-core*.l4`, see KEEL-NOTES.md).

**The source is a plain-text rendering, not the official text.**
`uksi-2013-376.txt` was made by a converter script from the revised text on legislation.gov.uk (version 2026-07-16); it is not marked up and it is not the official text.
Nothing here has been verified against the official text.
Every `-- src:N | ...` line in the modules copies line N of that file, mechanically, with `srcrefs.py quote`.

## Seat

The seat is a person who claims or receives Universal Credit, alone or as one of a couple, asking whether they are entitled to it, how much, and what they must do or tell the Secretary of State.
The Regulations speak to that person throughout Parts 1 to 4 ("a person", "the claimant", "joint claimants", "a member of a couple").
They say who may claim and on what basis (regulations 3 to 5), which basic conditions must be met (7 to 16), what limits and bars apply (17 to 19), how the months of the award run (21 to 21A), how income is taken off (22 to 22A) and what the award is made of (23 to 37).
Each of those is a question the person asks, so the layer is phrased as the person asks it: "May my partner and I claim jointly?", "Is my absence from Great Britain disregarded?", "How much Universal Credit am I awarded for an assessment period?".

`uc1-seat.l4` holds 116 questions, each with `@export` and `@desc`, taking plain inputs (yes or no, a number, a date, or a choice from the core's lists), under twelve `§§` headings in the order of the text.
The records the layer-1 modules expect are built inside each question, in its `WHERE`, from those plain inputs, and the same module rule is then called; no rule is restated in the seat.
A record field that the rule called never reads is given a fixed placeholder, with a comment saying so.
Where a question would otherwise need a very long list of inputs, it takes instead the yes-or-no answers of the questions it depends on (named in each `@desc`): regulations 3, 4, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 19, 21, 27, 29, 31, 33 and 36 are built this way.
`uc1-cases-seat.l4` holds 670 assertions, at least one for every question, including the cases outside the encoding (`NOTHING` or a constructor that says "not covered").
The expected values were derived by hand from the text before the question was run, and the file itself was assembled by a script from those hand-written lists.

There is no seat for the Secretary of State.
Where the text turns on a judgment the Secretary of State makes, the question asks the person whether that judgment has been made, as a plain fact the person can state: regulation 4(5) (who has the main responsibility), 11(2) and 11(4A)(c) (unreasonable to expect a return), 15(3) (a reasonable request, and the period extended), 16(1) (lack of capacity, exceptional circumstances), 21A(1)(c) (unnecessary disruption), 22A(3) (good reason for the delay), 29(3) and 29(6)(b) (a determination, or satisfaction after consulting the Scottish Ministers), 33(2) (late reporting accepted) and 34(2)(a) (an amount considered excessive).
What the Secretary of State owes the person, or can do to them, appears as what the person is owed or exposed to: "Does my award include ...?", "On what day is my claimant commitment treated as accepted?", "By what day must I tell the Secretary of State about my childcare charges?".

Facts that belong to other pieces are plain inputs, never imports (see "Cross-references out of my ranges"): the Schedules (uc3), the rules on income and capital (uc2), the capability-for-work assessments (uc4) and the Act.
The figures the Regulations themselves state (the amounts of regulation 36, the capital limit of regulation 18, the minimum amount of regulation 17) come from the layer-1 modules, so a later amendment would change those answers.

For four regulation 2 definitions the question asks the facts the keel's record holds and calls the keel's rule: `reg 2 — terminally ill` (regulations 16, 27, 28, 36), `reg 2 — prisoner` (3(3), 4(6), 19(1)), `reg 2 — looked after by a local authority` (4(6), 8(3)) and `reg 2 — foster parent` (4A(2), 14(1)(d) and (e)).
The places that keep a plain yes or no are listed under "Needs from the core".

   It says what Part 2 "contains provisions about" (src:181-184), and the "only if" that entitlement needs the basic conditions, the financial conditions and no regulation 19 bar is read from its limb (c) and from regulation 19(1), which do not say it either (fork F-7-JOIN).
3. Regulation 8(1)(f) has no upper end for a pregnancy.
   The first limb covers a person who is pregnant and "11 weeks or less before her expected week of confinement" (src:193); a person still pregnant after the expected week began is read as still inside it (fork F-8-OVERDUE).
   Asserted.
4. Regulation 15(4) is the only sentence in this range that tells a person to do something: "A person must accept a claimant commitment by one of the following methods, as specified by the Secretary of State" (src:390).
   The other uses of "must" in lines 118-742 state conditions of a status (regulations 5(2), 9(2), 25(2) to (4)), and regulation 29(6) says B's award "shall not include" an element; none of them tells a person to act.
   Regulation 15(4) itself states no consequence of not doing it.
   The rule checks that the method used is the method the Secretary of State specified, for each of the three methods, and does not hold when the two differ.
   Asserted.
5. Regulation 18(1) states the same figure for joint claimants as for a single claimant: £16,000 each (src:403-405).
   Under the reading taken, in which the two members' capital is set together against it, a couple has the limit of one person (fork F-18-JOINT).
   Asserted.
6. Three amounts cannot be calculated because the text shows the variables of a formula and not the formula: regulation 21A(2)(b) (src:460-463), regulation 22A(2) (src:492-495) and regulation 34A(2) (src:674-678).
   Each sentence introduces a formula ("is to be calculated as follows—", "produced by the following formula—", "Step 2 Apply the formula—"), defines N and A (or PA, D and AP), and then nothing combines them.
   The rules return `NOTHING`, and the seat questions return the same (fork F-FORMULA).
   Reconstructing a formula from the variable names would be an invention; the official text will have it.
   Asserted.
7. Regulation 22 does not floor the award at nothing.
   It says the amounts to be deducted from the maximum amount "to determine the amount of an award" (src:466) and does not say what an award is when the deductions exceed the maximum amount, which section 8 of the Act (out of range) may answer.
   The rule gives the difference, so a deduction of 650 against a maximum of 400 gives minus 250, and a case records that this is what the rule returns, not what is paid.
   Asserted.
8. Regulation 25(2) lists three kinds of accommodation payment: (a) rent payments, (b) dots, and (c) service charge payments, with no "or" or "and" between them (src:527-530).
   The two that remain are read as alternatives (fork F-25-2).
   Asserted.
9. Regulation 26 leaves a gap for a renter on an ordinary tenancy who pays both rent and a service charge.
   Paragraph (2) applies Schedule 4 where the payments are rent payments "whether or not service charge payments are also payable", paragraph (3) applies Schedule 5 where they are service charge payments (its limb (b)(i) is dots), and only paragraph (4), for a shared ownership tenancy, says what to do when both apply (src:544-554).
   Outside shared ownership the text does not say, so the rules return their own "not covered" value and the amount is `NOTHING` (fork F-26-OVERLAP).
   So does a claimant to whom neither paragraph applies.
   Asserted.
10. Regulation 28(7) pays one penny, not nothing, during the wait for the LCWRA element.
    Where, because of regulation 28, the financial condition in section 5(1)(b) or (2)(b) of the Act (the amount payable is not less than the minimum) is not met, income is treated as leaving "the prescribed minimum" payable (src:596), and regulation 17 fixes that minimum at "one penny" (src:401).
    Asserted.
11. Regulation 36 gives every child the same child element, £303.94.
    The first-child row is printed as "{repealed: first child or qualifying young person}" with "{repealed: £351.88}", and in the next row the words "second and each subsequent" are marked repealed, leaving "each child or qualifying young person" at £303.94 (src:723-724).
    So the amount does not depend on a child's place in the family, and the rule for it takes no birth order (fork F-36-REPEALED).
    Asserted.

## Before this is worth relying on

This is a reading of a plain-text rendering, checked by one reader, and it should not be relied on for a decision about a real claimant until the points below are closed.

1. **The source is not the official text.**
   It is a converter's rendering of the revised text on legislation.gov.uk (version 2026-07-16); nothing here was compared with the official text, and the three formulas that are missing (21A(2)(b), 22A(2), 34A(2)) and the dotted limbs (25(2)(b), 26(3)(b)(i)) are exactly where a comparison is needed.
2. **No independent check of the expected values.**
   The expected values in the tests and cases were derived by hand from the text by the same reader who wrote the rules; no second reader, no worked example from the Department for Work and Pensions and no existing calculator was run against them.
   A shared misreading would pass.
3. **Twenty-eight readings were taken and none ruled.**
   Each is in the Fork register with the text that licenses it.
   The ones that most change an answer for a person: F-FORMULA (three amounts unavailable), F-26-OVERLAP (a renter paying rent and a service charge on an ordinary tenancy gets no amount), F-3-BASIS, F-18-JOINT (whose capital is set against £16,000), F-36-AGE (the day at which "aged under 25" is tested), F-8-OVERDUE and F-29-SAME.
4. **Judgments of the Secretary of State are facts the person states.**
   Where the text turns on the Secretary of State's opinion, satisfaction or determination, the seat asks the person whether it has been made; a person who answers yes when it has not is not caught.
5. **Facts from other pieces are plain inputs.**
   The Act, the Schedules (uc3), income and capital (uc2) and the capability-for-work assessments (uc4) are asked as yes-or-no or numbers; a real claimant's answer needs those pieces run first, and nothing here checks that the names match theirs.
6. **The Legalese Cloud form was not tried.**
   `@export` and `@desc` are on every question and the files run on the binary; how the form shows a `MAYBE` return (21 questions), a choice among constructors or a long list of inputs is untested.
7. **Dates and amounts follow this version of the text.**
   The amounts of regulations 18, 22, 24, 36 are as printed; a later amendment would change those answers and the tests that fix them.
8. **The keel was used in part.**
   Four regulation 2 definitions are applied in the seat and not in the modules, and two places keep a plain yes-or-no fact (see "Needs from the core").
