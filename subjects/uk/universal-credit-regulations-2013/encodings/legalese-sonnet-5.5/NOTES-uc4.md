## Seat

The seat is the brief's: a person who claims or receives Universal Credit, alone or as one of a couple, asking whether they are entitled to it, how much, and what they must do or tell the Secretary of State.
For this piece that person asks four kinds of question, in the text's order: whether they count as having limited capability for work, or for work and work-related activity, or as a severe conditions criteria claimant (Part 5, Schedules 6 to 9); what they must do for the Secretary of State when asked for information or to attend an examination (regulations 43 and 44); which work-related requirements bind them, how many hours they must look for, what limits those requirements, and when none may be imposed (Part 8 Chapter 1); and what happens to their award if they fail a requirement, how long and how much, and whether they can have a hardship payment (Part 8 Chapters 2 and 3, Schedule 11).
There is never a seat for an official: the Secretary of State's powers appear as what may be done to the person, and the Secretary of State's judgments are plain yes-or-no facts the person is asked to state or to take from a decision letter.
The questions are listed in `uc4-seat.l4`, each with `@export` and `@desc`; the worked cases are in `uc4-cases-seat.l4`.

How the questions are made, all of it in `uc4-seat.l4` and held to by `uc4-cases-seat.l4`:

- **68 questions, each with `@export` and `@desc`.** Each is asked as that person would ask it ("Do I have ...", "May my partner and I ...", "How many days ..."), each input is a plain fact worded for them, and each `@desc` is the sentence the form would ask. The questions sit under `§§` headings in the text's order, and under one `§` heading for the whole seat.
- **The seat calls the layer-1 rules and restates none of them.** Where a layer-1 rule takes a record, the seat builds the record from plain inputs and passes it in; where a rule takes a list, the seat builds the list from a yes-or-no input and the facts of one item.
- **A date a rule is tested at is an input,** named `the date I am asking about` (or the date of the failure, of the claim, of the notification). Nothing reads a clock.
- **The Secretary of State's judgments are inputs the person states or takes from a decision letter:** "is satisfied that", "considers", "approved by", "specified by". Where the judgment is a number (the lesser number of hours in regulation 88, the number of days in regulation 97(5)), the input is a number and 0 means none.
- **Where the text gives no answer the question returns an explicit value, never FALSE or 0:** a `MAYBE` result left `NOTHING` (the "–" cells and blank "(e)" rows of the sanction tables, a reduction period whose end event has not happened, regulation 99(1)(b), (2B) and (2C) when their paragraph does not apply), or a constructor (`What regulation 99 excuses me from on work availability`). Each such case is asserted.
- **A fact that may not exist is a `MAYBE` input,** left blank by the person (the date a compliance condition was met, the date the award ended, the date regulation 98 last applied). A list input is used where the person chooses all that apply (the descriptors of Schedules 6 and 7, the benefits they are entitled to, the purposes of an interview, the essential needs of regulation 116(3)) and for a run of dates or amounts (the earlier months' breaks of regulation 99(4B), the earlier reduction periods of regulation 101, the monthly earnings of regulation 109).
- **Where the answer differs for a couple,** the question asks for the partner's facts too (the partner's group, hourly rate and expected hours in question 19) or whether the claim is a joint one (questions 56 to 58 and 65), with 0 or no where it does not apply.

What the seat leaves out or simplifies, each of it on purpose:

- **The causes in regulation 39(4) are asked once for each Part of Schedule 6, not once for each descriptor.** The text words them for "any descriptor listed in Part 1" (a specific bodily disease or disablement, or treatment for a physical one) and "in Part 2" (a specific mental illness or disablement, or treatment for one), and question 1 asks each once, so a person whose limitations in two activities of the same Part have different causes cannot say so.
- **A partner's fostering is flattened to yes-or-no inputs** (is the partner also a foster parent of the child, is anyone else, is the person the nominated member), where the core's records hold more (questions 18 and 20).
- **Regulation 98(1)(a) and (b) are one question** (29): the two paragraphs have the same conditions and the same 13 weeks, and differ only in whether a requirement was already imposed (a) or would be a new one (b), and the answer says whether the person is free of requirements on the date. Paragraph (1A) is the next question (30), asked as the negation of the layer-1 rule ("may the Secretary of State impose ..."), because a person asks whether they can be required, not whether the Secretary of State is barred.
- **Regulation 99 is eleven questions (31 to 41), not one:** each of the paragraphs (3), (4), (4A) with (4C), (4B), (5), (5A) with (5B), (6), (2) with (2A)(b), (1)(b), (2B) and (2C) has its own facts. A person excused by any one of them is excused, and the seat leaves them as separate questions.
- **Regulation 42(3) has no question:** it adds nothing to what question 4 and question 3 already ask, and its overlap with regulations 39(4)(c) and 40(3) is fork F-42-1.
- **The partner's threshold is carried inside question 19** (regulation 90): the person who claims jointly gives their partner's group, hourly rate and expected hours, with 0 if not applicable, and the question calls the layer-1 threshold rule for both.
- **An earlier failure is asked as the most recent one only** (questions 45 to 47): a yes-or-no, its date, its reduction period and (for 102) whether it was a pre-claim failure. The layer-1 rules for regulations 102 to 104 take a list; question 47 therefore cannot express a person whose most recent earlier failure fell inside the last 14 days while an older one fell between 14 and 364 days back (fork F-104-1).
- **Regulation 119's six-month test is asked as a count** (question 68): the person says in how many earlier assessment periods since the last reduction they met paragraph (2)(a), (b) or (c), and the seat feeds the layer-1 rule one period that meets (2)(a) for each, which counts the same for a test that only adds up periods.
- **Facts that belong to rules outside my ranges are plain inputs** (the hourly rates of the National Minimum Wage Regulations, the benefits a person is entitled to, whether someone is a member of a couple): see "Cross-references out of my ranges".
- **Regulations 38 and 86(2) and (3) have no question of their own, because they only fix what is asked elsewhere:** regulation 38 says which question Part 5 decides, and regulation 86(2) and (3) say that only one of joint claimants is nominated and that the nomination applies to a child; the one thing a person asks about the nomination, whether they may change it, is question 15, and who is nominated is an input to questions 18 and 20. Regulation 84, regulation 100(2) to (5) and regulation 115 are pointers and are `inert` in the coverage table.

## Coverage table

One row per provision of the four ranges: a row for each regulation or schedule paragraph, and a separate row for each numbered paragraph where the paragraphs differ in what they do.
A regulation's heading line sits in the row of its first paragraph (`reg 39 heading, reg 39(1)`), or has a row of its own where the first paragraph is a run of dots.
Parts, Chapters, cross-headings and Schedule headings each have a row, disposition `inert` (a pure title: it states no rule).
The `where` column is read from the modules by script: it lists the declarations whose `@ref` locators overlap the row's lines, so it follows the code.
`encoded` rows whose `where` names only a record or a type are rows whose rule sits in the neighbouring row's rules (a paragraph that sets the scope of the next).
The dispositions used are the brief's four: `encoded`, `inert` (with its reason), `repealed or blank in this text` and `fork` (the reading taken is in the fork register).
There is no commencement, making or in-force date provision anywhere in these ranges: the only dates the ranges carry are periods that the rules count in (days, weeks, months), so there is no dated value to define.

23. Regulation 119(3) counts "a period of, or more than one period where the total of those periods amounts to, at least 6 months" and tests monthly earnings, while paragraph (2) tests assessment periods.
    An assessment period is taken as one month and the periods need not run together (fork F-119-1).
    Asserted: question 68 (five earlier periods leave payments recoverable, six end the duty to repay).
24. Schedule 11 paragraphs 1(3) and 2(3) take from the days of the ESA (JSA) sanction "any days falling after the date of that termination and before the date on which the award of universal credit starts", that is the days strictly between the two dates, and the text does not say that the result stops at nought.
    The modules take it as 0 when more has been served than applies, and take nothing off where universal credit starts before the termination or the day after it (fork F-sch11-1).
    Asserted: question 62 (52 days: 91 less 30 served less 9 between), question 63 (17 days: 28 less 7 less 4), and `uc4-tests-sch11-sch12.l4` (the 0 results).

## What the stable binary does not accept

Each item was met in this piece and has a way round.

1. **A warning that is easy to read as an error: "AND and OR operators appear at the same indentation level".** It is raised when a group in parentheses that holds an OR starts a line at the column of an AND, even though the parentheses already settle the grouping. Write the OR-group as a `WHERE` helper with a backticked name and AND the helper in.
2. **`WHERE` helper names.** A multi-word helper name must be backticked, or it is read as a mixfix call; a helper name containing digits or parentheses fails to parse unless backticked. All helpers here are backticked, with digit-free names.
3. **A one-line `GIVEN a IS A BOOLEAN, b IS A LIST OF Benefit, c IS A BOOLEAN` fails** at the `IS` after the list type ("unexpected IS"): the comma is read as part of the type. Put one input per line when any of them is a `LIST OF`.
4. **A type name of several words needs backticks everywhere it is used,** including after `WITH` in a record literal and in `LIST OF`.
5. **`YMD` is not in the stable prelude**; the core supplies the shim `YMD y m d MEANS Date d m y`, used here.
6. **The module cannot be checked by `l4 check` alone for its quotations:** `python3 -I srcrefs.py check --refs` is what holds the `-- src:N |` lines and the `@ref` locators to the text.
7. **Positional application is arity-checked and type-checked, but cannot tell two inputs of the same type apart.** A rule applied to two NUMBERs or two DATEs in the wrong order is accepted and silently gives a wrong answer (exit 0, no diagnostic). The seat passes dozens of same-typed inputs by position, so the seat file was generated from a signature table that orders the arguments by the rule's own signature; the cases then pin down the results.
8. **`l4 run` prints each satisfied `#ASSERT` twice** (two `assertion satisfied` lines per assertion), so a count of satisfied lines is twice the count of assertions; the gate that compares written with satisfied counts the lines that follow `Message:`.
9. **A rule call used as an argument of another rule call needs its own parentheses.** In the seat, `reg 116(1)(g) ...` applied to an unparenthesised call of the regulation 116(2) rule was read as a call with three arguments and failed with "expects 2 arguments, got 3"; parenthesising the inner call fixed it.

## Before this is worth relying on

1. A lawyer who knows the Universal Credit Regulations should read the rows marked `fork` in the coverage table against the fork register, and the Secretary of State's judgments (the BOOLEAN inputs worded "is satisfied that" and "considers"), before any answer is relied on.
2. Nothing here is verified against the official text: the source is a converter's plain-text rendering of the revised text at 2026-07-16 (`inputs/SOURCES.md`).
   Two places where the rendering looks damaged are read as the only sensible reading and are not checked against an image of the official page: the formula of regulation 118 (fork F-118-1) and the points cells of Schedule 6 (fork F-points).
3. The hourly rates of the National Minimum Wage Regulations, the benefit entitlements, and every fact about another Act are inputs: they are only as good as the person who states them.
4. The regulations are tested at the dates the callers give; this piece carries no commencement or in-force date (finding 1).
5. The expected values in the seat cases (`uc4-cases-seat.l4`, 511 assertions) were worked out by hand from the text, each with its reasoning in the `-- Qn.` comment above it, and then run.
   On the first run one expected value was wrong, an arithmetic slip of mine in question 57 (it is now -87, which is 300 less the reduction of 387 used in the case before it); it was corrected from the text, not from the output, and no other value was changed.
   As a positive control, ten expectations in a scratch copy of the file were deliberately changed and produced twenty `assertion failed` lines; the copy was deleted.
   The layer-1 tests (`uc4-tests-*.l4`, 1301 assertions in 13 files) carry their expected values beside each assertion, written from the text; I did not keep a record of whether any was changed after a first run, so I do not claim that none was.
6. The seat is a simplification of the text, and the simplifications are listed under "Seat": the most important is that a question about reduction periods takes only the most recent earlier failure, so question 47 cannot express a person whose most recent earlier failure fell within 14 days while an older one fell between 14 and 364 days back (fork F-104-1).
7. The generators are not in this folder.
   The Schedule 6 and 7 modules, the quotation lines, the seat and the cases were produced by scripts that live in a scratch directory outside the deposit (`uc4-work/`); the folder holds only their output, and the generated files carry no mark saying which lines were generated.
   To change one, edit the file here and run `check.sh`; do not expect the scripts to be available.
8. The worked cases and the seat questions are examples of the text applied to invented facts; they are not advice to a claimant.
