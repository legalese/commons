## Seat

The seat is a person who claims or receives Universal Credit, alone or as one of a couple, asking how their capital and income are counted, how much capital and income they are treated as having, whether the benefit cap applies to them, and what they must do or tell the Secretary of State.
That is the individual to whom Parts 6 and 7 and Schedule 10 are applied (COMMONS-CLAUDE.md, ruled 2026-10-18); the Secretary of State, the court and the department get no seat.
Where a provision addresses only the Secretary of State (the estimate of reg 54(2), the reallocations of reg 61(5) to (7), the apportionment of reg 54A(4)(b), the termination of a start-up period under reg 63(3), the determination of reg 64, the extension of the 6 months under reg 48(2)), the seat encodes what it means for the person and asks "Has the Secretary of State decided ...?" as a conditional input; it never guesses the judgment.

**The files.**
`uc2-seat.l4` has 130 questions and `uc2-cases-seat.l4` has 666 worked cases with hand-written expected values (at least one case for every question, true and false for every yes-or-no question, and cases that expect `NOTHING` where the text gives no answer).
Every question has `@export` and `@desc`.
Questions take plain inputs (yes/no, numbers, dates, or a choice from a list that a module declares) and are grouped under `§` headings in the order of the text: Part 6 Chapter 1 (regs 46 to 50), Chapter 2 (52 to 64), Chapter 3 (66 to 74), Chapter 4 (75 to 77), Part 7 (78 to 83), and Schedule 10 in five groups (premises, business assets, rights in pension schemes, amounts earmarked, other payments).

**How a question is built.**
The seat adds no law and restates none.
Each question builds, in its `WHERE`, the record that a layer-1 rule expects from the person's answers, then calls that rule.
A record field that the rule called never reads is given a fixed placeholder, with a comment above the placeholders saying so.
A `MAYBE` is built inside the question (`IF flag THEN JUST x ELSE NOTHING`), so the person never has to supply one.
Two rules in `uc2-seat.l4` are not questions: `seat uc2 — the last day of a start-up period, if there is one` and `seat uc2 — the last day of a grace period, if there is one`, which read a field out of a `MAYBE` record so that the answer is a date.
Because the generator that wrote the seat checks the placeholders against the fields each rule reads, it warns when a placeholder could be read; the warnings that remain are explained at the questions concerned (the benefit-cap total, the exemptions of reg 83(1), and the single-benefit question of reg 66(1)(b)): the fields are not read for the kind of benefit asked about, or FALSE is the neutral value of a disjunction whose other limbs are answered by another question.

**What the seat asks less of than the modules can take.**
The modules decide the general case; the seat asks one slice of it.

- A trade, profession or vocation is asked about one at a time (reg 57, reg 77(4)); the assets of reg 57(5) have a question of their own; unused business losses are one figure.
- A statutory payment that counts as employed earnings (reg 55(4)) is asked as one kind and one amount.
- Earlier claims since the old award (reg 54A) are asked up to four, which is as far as the text goes with a fifth claim (fork F-A2).
- A grace period (reg 82(2)) is asked with one candidate day, and each of its 12 months with its own rate; the seat can say whether a period begins on that day, when it ends, and whether an assessment period falls in it.
- The benefit-cap total (reg 80) takes universal credit, housing benefit and three other welfare benefits.
- The exemptions of reg 83(1)(b) to (k) are asked one payment at a time, for one member of the household; they are alternatives, so asking again for another payment loses nothing.

**What is stated by the person, because the text does not decide it.**
The thresholds of reg 90 (piece uc4); the nil UC threshold of reg 54A(6) (the formula is missing from this rendering); the hourly rate of the National Minimum Wage (reg 82); the occupation condition (Schedule 3, piece uc3), asked here only as "my accommodation for the occupation condition is in Greater London"; whether the LCWRA element or the carer element is in the award (piece uc4); the maximum student loan, the maximum postgraduate loan and the amount of a grant; the provision under which a payment is made (to decide whether it is an "enactment").

**Layer-1 rules with no question of their own, and why.**

- `reg 48(1) — the capital is disregarded` is only the fact that a paragraph of Schedule 10 applies; the Schedule 10 questions answer it.
- `reg 79(2) — the reference period` says the reference period is the assessment period; it is an identity and asks the person nothing.
- `reg 54A(6) — total earned income` is a definition; the surplus-earnings question takes the earned income of the last month as its input.
- `reg 57A — the unused losses carried to the next assessment period` and `reg 58 and 59 — the expenses allowed for an assessment period` take lists and records of payments; the seat asks for the loss of the period and for each deduction (miles, hours at home, premises used personally, a payment) one at a time.

9. **A student loan and a postgraduate loan add, and 30 per cent applies once.** Reg 69(1A) and reg 71 Step 1(aa) both say 30 per cent of the loan; applied together they would give 9 per cent. (9,000 + 600) + 30 per cent of 10,000 over 12 periods, less 110, is 940 (forks F-U2, F-U3). Asserted.
10. **Annuity and trust income count unless reg 75 or 76 disregards them.** An annuity that is retirement pension income is within reg 66(1)(a) and is not counted again under (1)(i); trust income left out by either regulation is not counted (fork F-U9). Asserted.
11. **"A period of no less than one month" for the long vacation** is counted by the core's calendar addition with the last day counted: 1 to 31 July is a month and 1 to 30 July is not (fork F-U5). Asserted.
12. **Reg 80 does not say which paragraph governs when more than one fits.** I read (2), (2A) and (3) as governing their own benefit and (4) then (5) as the general rule for every other benefit (fork F-C1); housing benefit is nil even if it is taken into account as unearned income. Asserted.
13. **Reg 80A(2) names only "a single claimant" and "joint claimants", while reg 78(2) puts a single claimant who is a member of a couple in "couple".** I give that claimant the single claimant's limit (14,753 where not in Greater London, no child) and total the couple's benefits (fork F-C2). This is the reading most in need of a human's confirmation. Asserted.
14. **The monthly level of the cap is rounded to the penny before the comparison.** 16,967 divided by 12 is 1,413.9166..., and the rule compares with 1,413.92 (fork F-C3). Asserted.
15. **Reg 81 does not say what the award is when the reduction exceeds it.** The rule answers `NOTHING` (fork F-C4); the reduction itself is not capped. Asserted.
16. **A 9-month grace period ends the day before the ninth monthly anniversary.** A day of 1 March 2026 gives 30 November 2026; a day of 31 May 2026 gives 27 February 2027, because the core clamps 31 May plus 9 months to 28 February and one day is taken off (forks F-C5, F-C6). Only days not later than the last day of the assessment period asked about are candidates. Asserted.
17. **The earnings threshold disregards a fraction of a pound.** 12 pounds an hour gives 832 a month; 12.21 gives 846.56 and so 846 (reg 6(1A), via the core). Earned income of 831.99 against 832 does not qualify. Asserted.
18. **Four of the eight reasons in reg 83(1)(h) are tied to a payment ("in the case of ...") and four are not.** A section 85 or 86 reason is accepted only for personal independence payment; a care-home or hospital reason under the Attendance Allowance Regulations is accepted with any payment listed in (b) to (g), (ha) or (hb) (fork F-C7). Asserted.
19. **Reg 83(1)(i) and (ia) say "entitled to"; (b) to (g) say "receiving".** A qualifying young person entitled to carer's allowance but not receiving it is within (i); a child receiving personal independence payment is not within (g), which names claimants and qualifying young persons. Asserted.
20. **Reg 83(2)(c): "terminated before 31st March 1973".** Service that ended on 30 March 1973 qualifies and service that ended on 31 March 1973 does not. Asserted.
21. **Reg 59(2)(a) is per assessment period.** 1,000 miles in a car are 833 at 45 pence and 167 at 25 pence, 416.60 (fork F-S5); under 25 hours at home gives nothing and 25 to 50 hours gives 10 (fork F-S6). Asserted.
22. **Reg 63: a start-up period is 12 months from the first day of the assessment period.** From 5 March 2026 it ends on 4 March 2027, or on the day the Secretary of State terminates it if earlier (fork F-A6). Asserted.
23. **Reg 54A runs to the fifth claim and stops.** The surplus of the first claim is the excess over the relevant threshold (the nil UC threshold plus 300); each later claim adds the earned income of the first month of the previous claim and takes off the threshold; a sixth is not covered (fork F-A2); a recent victim of domestic violence has none (reg 54A(5)). Asserted.
24. **Reg 72(1) counts a part-£250 as a whole.** 6,001 yields 4.35 a month, 6,250 yields 4.35, 6,251 yields 8.70, 16,000 yields 174. Asserted.
25. **Reg 77(2) and (3)(a) take the share after the trading assets.** A half share of a company worth 100,000 with 40,000 of trading assets, while engaged in the trade, is 30,000; not engaged, 50,000; a sole owner with no stated share has the whole (fork F-M4). Reg 77(5) takes the person out of reg 77 altogether where they are paid through an intermediary for work that is their main employment. Asserted.
26. **Reg 75(6): 12 months from the day the sum was paid, the last day counting.** Paid 15 January 2026, disregarded on 15 January 2027 and not on 16 January (fork F-M1). Asserted.
