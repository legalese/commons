# Magna Carta (1297), 1297 c. 9 (25 Edw. 1): L4 encoding `legalese-sonnet-5.5`

**Status: draft, last checked 2026-10-30.**
Everything below that states a count was read off a run made on 2026-10-30, after the reconciliation recorded under F-SAVING-VOID (the `check.sh` output is pasted verbatim in "What check.sh prints").
Nothing here has been read by a domain expert.
The encoding is a hybrid, as the brief rules: rules for the text that shows no repeal annotation, and one status entry per other chapter.

## Seat

**A person asking what Magna Carta (1297) secures them, on the parts of its text that show no repeal annotation.**
That is: whether the Church of England is free (chapter 1); whether the City of London and the other cities, boroughs, towns and the Barons of the Five Ports keep their liberties and free customs (chapter 9); whether a person may be taken, imprisoned, disseised, outlawed, exiled or in any way destroyed, or have the King proceed against or condemn them, other than by lawful judgment of their Peers or by the Law of the Land, and whether Justice or Right may be sold, denied or deferred (chapter 29); and what the three paragraphs under the "General Saving" heading promise about the observance of the Liberties.

Why this seat: the brief fixes it, because Commons asks every body of law from the position of the individual it is applied to, and the Legalese site turns the seat layer into a question form.
The seat is two files, `mc97-seat.l4` (19 `@export` questions, each input with an `@desc` worded as the person would be asked) and `mc97-cases-seat.l4` (61 worked assertions, one persona or more per question, plus the subsidy rule called directly because it has no seat question).
There is no seat for the King, for his Heirs or for any official: where the text speaks of the King's own acts ("We will not ...", the promise in the first paragraph), the question is what that means for the person.
The seat IMPORTs the layer-1 modules and restates no rule.
The answers are the layer-1 answers in the layer-1 words, so `not covered by the text` means the text is silent, and never means "no".

## What this encoding does not claim

- **No claim is made that any provision is, or ever was, in force or applied.**
  The extract says the site shows the preamble paragraphs and the three General Saving paragraphs without a repeal annotation, and that this is not a statement about in-force status (extract line 86).
  No function, constant or comment in the modules says otherwise.
  The status entries say only what the extract's table says the site shows.
- **The text is a translation and an extract, not the official text.**
  It is the site's "traditional translation", bracketed insertions included, as another session transcribed it from the rendered XML of legislation.gov.uk.
  `../../registers/source-bundle/aep-Edw1cc1929-25-9-extract.meta.json` records that the transcription is not byte-exact, and that nobody has checked the status table or the variant-readings table against the site.
  Nothing here is "verified against the official text".
- **No domain expert has read this encoding against the source.**
- **No claim is made about how this text relates to any other charter or reissue**, beyond what the text itself says: preamble paragraph 1 says it has seen the Great Charter of the Lord Henry “in these words” (F-RECITE).
  The extract's own observation on that question (its last section) is the extract author's, is not the source, and is not relied on.
- **The words of the repealed chapters are not supplied.**
  They are not in the source.
  A status entry is the tsv string and nothing more.
- **The attestation date is carried as the text gives it** and is not converted to a modern calendar date.
- **Words the text does not define are not defined here** (section "Terms the text does not define").

Hohfeld's correlatives are used for the kind of relation: a claim and the duty correlative to it; an immunity and the disability correlative to it; a power and the liability correlative to it.
Where the text names the holder but not the bearer (or the reverse), the cell says "not named", and the encoding returns `NOTHING` or `not covered`, never an invented party.
Where one provision yields two rules, one says who holds the benefit and the other who is bound; neither restates the other.

- **Readings**: (a) the "if" is the text's own condition, so a thing not procured by any person, or procured but not contrary to the premises, is not made void by this sentence, which is a no-valued answer; (b) silence: an "if" without an "only if" says what follows when the condition holds and nothing when it fails.
- **Taken**: (a), by the rule that a condition introduced by "if" is the text saying no when it fails (the team lead's reconciliation, 2026-10-30), and as the final clause of the second paragraph already was.
  The answer is `not made void by the first unnumbered paragraph`, a constructor of `Effect` beside `of no force nor effect`.
  The old value `not covered by the first unnumbered paragraph` is removed from `Effect`: both inputs of `first paragraph: a thing procured` are addressed by the sentence, so no input is left for it.
- **Changed**: three assertions in `mc97-tests-saving.l4` and one in `mc97-cases-seat.l4` were restated, each with a `-- RECONCILED 2026-10-30` comment above it quoting the original.
- **Where**: `first paragraph: a thing procured`; `Effect` in `mc97-nouns.l4`.

#### F-SAVING-APPERTAIN
- **Ambiguity**: a Liberty that does not appertain to the King, under “as much as appertaineth to Us and our Heirs”.
- **Readings**: (a) “as much as” is a limiter equivalent to "only": the King is bound only so far as it appertains to him, so for a Liberty that does not appertain the text says he is not bound; (b) the words are not "only", the sentence says what the King shall observe, and it says nothing of the rest.
- **Taken**: (b), as the safer answer: `not covered by the text`.
  A marginal reading for a lawyer, not a ruling.
- **Where**: `first paragraph: the King shall observe`, on its `ELSE` branch.

#### F-SAVING-INTHEM
- **Ambiguity**: a Man of the Realm who is not able, under “as much as in them is”.
- **Readings**: (a) “as much as” is a limiter equivalent to "only": a Man is bound only so far as it lies within his power, so a Man who is not able is not bound; (b) the words are not "only", and the sentence says nothing of the rest.
- **Taken**: (b): `not covered by the text`.
  A marginal reading for a lawyer, not a ruling.
- **Where**: `first paragraph: a Man of the Realm shall observe`, on its `ELSE` branch.

#### F-SAVING-WHEREBY
- **Ambiguity**: an act that does not infringe, under “neither We nor our Heirs shall procure or do any thing whereby the Liberties in this Charter contained shall be infringed”.
- **Readings**: (a) the "whereby" clause is the whole of what the King and his Heirs promise, so an act that does not infringe is free; (b) it is a flat prohibition whose "whereby" clause marks what is forbidden and says nothing of the rest, so a non-infringing act is neither forbidden nor permitted by this sentence.
- **Taken**: (b): `not covered by the text`.
  A marginal reading for a lawyer, not a ruling.
- **Where**: `first paragraph: the King and his Heirs shall not procure or do`, on its `ELSE` branch.

#### F-SAVING-GIFT
- **Ambiguity**: “have given unto Us the Fifteenth Part of all their Moveables”.
- **Readings**: (a) a past gift, stated; (b) a duty still to be performed.
- **Taken**: (a): who gave (nine classes), and the ratio one fifteenth.
  The text says nothing about a debt still to be paid.
- **Where**: `first paragraph: the Fifteenth Part of Moveables was given by`; `first paragraph: the Fifteenth Part of`.

#### F-SAVING-AFORESAID
- **Ambiguity**: which gifts and grants “these Gifts and Grants aforesaid” are.
- **Taken**: the four this text shows (the grant to the classes of preamble paragraph 2; the grant to the Freemen of chapter 1; the gift of the Fifteenth Part; the promise not to infringe), and `not covered` for any other.
- **Where**: `second paragraph: what is confirmed, made strong and renewed`; `Gift or grant` in `mc97-nouns.l4`.

#### F-SAVING-NOAGENT
- **Ambiguity**: who is to observe the Charter and its Articles: “shall be stedfastly, firmly, and inviolably observed”.
- **Taken**: no one is named, and none is invented: `NOTHING`.
- **Where**: `second paragraph: the party the text names to observe`.

## What the stable binary does not accept

The binary is the stable build `1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`; the brief says leave `JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` unset, and it was (checked in the shell that ran `check.sh`, 2026-10-29).

**Loud failures (the binary says so, with an error diagnostic):**

- **A parameter with the same name as a keyword segment of its own mixfix name.**
  A rule named `the range from` lowest `to` highest, whose first parameter is also called `to`, fails with "In a definition, the defined term and all its parameters must have distinct names."
  Reproduced on 2026-10-29 with a small probe.
  The cure is to rename the parameter; `mc97-c01.l4` does (`lowest`, `highest`, segment `up to`).
- **A GIVEN order that does not match the order of the parameters in a mixfix head** fails with "The names in a type signature must match those in the definition."
  Reproduced on 2026-10-29 with a small probe.

- **A DEONTIC cannot be asserted.**
  `#ASSERT <rule> EQUALS <rule>` on a regulative rule reports "assertion failed", even with the same rule on both sides (probe, 2026-10-29, build 1.6.98).
  Build 1.6.102 reports "assertion could not be evaluated" for the same probe.
  `check.sh` counts both as failed (it greps for both strings), so an attempt to assert one turns the run red.
  The brief's instruction is followed: every duty is encoded twice, as a regulative rule that is traced and as a decision function over the same facts that is asserted.

**Silent (the run stays green):**

- **A `#TRACE` prints its outcome as an Information message, not as an assertion line.**
  `check.sh` therefore counts nothing for it, and cannot see two builds disagree about one (the script says so in its last line).
  `check-traces.py` reads each trace's outcome back on both builds and compares it with the `-- expect:` line written above it from the text.
  On 2026-10-29: 19 traces (11 in `mc97-tests-c29.l4`, 8 in `mc97-tests-saving.l4`), 19 as expected, the two builds agree.
  Positive control, same day, on a scratch copy of the chapter 29 tests: one expectation flipped from BREACHED to STANDING and another removed, and the script reported "expected STANDING, got BREACHED" and "trace with no `-- expect:` line", with exit status 1.

**A limit of my read-back, not of the binary:** `check-traces.py` classifies any printed obligation as STANDING.
It cannot tell a prohibition that stands because nothing was done from one that stands because the event never reached it; each `-- expect:` comment is written beside a trace whose event is in the trace itself.
