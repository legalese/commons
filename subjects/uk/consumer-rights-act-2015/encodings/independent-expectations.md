# Independent expectations — Consumer Rights Act 2015 (goods ss 19(14)-(15), 20(15), 22, 24; services ss 54-56)

Written from BRIEF.md and the Act's text (revised, 6 April 2026) only, before any `.l4` file was opened.

## Readings adopted

1. **s 22(3)** "the end of 30 days beginning with the first day after these have all happened": E = the latest of transfer, delivery and (if the contract requires installation etc.) the trader's notice. Limit = E + 30.
2. **s 22(4)** perishables: "the end of that shorter period". The Act does not say when the shorter period starts. Reading: it runs from the same start as the 30 days (E + 1), so a shorter period of N days ends on E + N. It applies only if N < 30 ("shorter"); otherwise the 30-day limit stands.
3. **s 22(6), (8)** waiting period W = received − requested + 1 (both days counted). The limit moves to (original limit) + W (brief).
4. **s 22(7)** if the repaired/replacement goods do not conform: limit = later of (received + 7) and (original limit + W). If they conform, (7) does not apply and the limit is original + W (s 22(6) alone).
5. **Request after the limit** (s 22(1)): the right was already lost when the limit passed; "the period ... stops running" presupposes it is still running. Reading: no extension; the limit stays the original one (an encoding that REFUSEs here is defensible; one that extends is a finding). A request on the last day is still within the period and extends it.
6. **Six months "beginning with" day X** (ss 19(14), 24(11)): ends on the day before the corresponding date six months later. **Month ends:** where the corresponding date does not exist (e.g. X = 31 Aug → "31 Feb"), I read the period as ending on the last day of that month (28/29 Feb) — the period cannot end before the month it runs into is complete; this is the usual UK reading of "beginning with" periods (e.g. employment tribunal limits: EDT 31 Aug, three months ends 30 Nov). The brief's "day before the corresponding date" does not address the non-existent date; the alternative "clamp to month end, then minus one" gives 27 Feb. Scenarios where the two readings differ are flagged **[month-end]**.
7. **s 19(14)** applies where the goods fail to conform "at any time within" the six months: input = the day the fault appeared. Presumed iff that day is within the period and neither s 19(15)(a) nor (b) applies.
8. **s 24(10)-(11)** "exercised in the first 6 months": the first 6 months begin with E + 1 and end the day before the corresponding date (reading 6). Exercising on that last day is within; no deduction unless motor vehicle (or SI-specified goods).
9. **Refund** on final rejection = price − deduction where a deduction is allowed, else price. A deduction exceeding the price is not answered by the Act (not tested).
10. **ss 20(15), 56(4)** refund by agreement day + 13.
11. **s 24(5)**: available iff any of (a) after one repair or replacement the goods still do not conform, (b) neither repair nor replacement can be required because of s 23(3), (c) repair/replacement required and trader in breach of s 23(2)(a).
12. **Services**: repeat performance available iff the service does not conform and completing it is not impossible (s 55(3)); price reduction available (where both rights exist) iff the service does not conform and (impossible, or repeat performance required and trader failed within a reasonable time) (s 56(3)). Repeat performance does not cease to be available merely because the trader failed (Act says nothing to that effect).

## Ambiguities recorded

- s 22(4) start of the shorter period (reading 2).
- Request for repair after the limit (reading 5).
- Month-end six-month periods (reading 6).
- Deduction above price; s 24(9) failed-to-collect period (only tested if the encoding has an input for it).
- Whether, once the trader fails under s 55(2)(a), repeat performance remains "available" (read: yes).
