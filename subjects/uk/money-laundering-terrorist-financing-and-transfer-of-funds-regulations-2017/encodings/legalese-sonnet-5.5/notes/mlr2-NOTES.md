## Merge note (mlr2)

How this part answers "the duty does not arise", for whoever reconciles the parts.
In this range the duty function ("must the relevant person ...?") answers `JUST FALSE` when the condition that triggers the duty is not met (a failed "where", "when" or "if": for example `reg 39(2) the relevant person must obtain the information and enter into the arrangements`, `reg 43(1)`, `reg 44(2)`).
The compliance check ("has the duty been discharged?", for example `reg 39(2) the relevant person has met the requirements` and `reg 33(1) the relevant person has applied the enhanced measures in addition to the regulation 28 measures`) answers `NOTHING` when the duty did not arise: it asks only whether a duty that arose was discharged.
It does not answer `JUST TRUE` for "no duty arises" through the compliance function.

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (mlr2)

The keel's three readings, applied throughout (they are conventions of this corpus, which L4 itself does not take a position on):
- (a) a failed "if" or "provided that", or a flat negative, gives `JUST FALSE`;
- (b) a limited grant or prohibition gives `NOTHING` outside its limit;
- (c) an undischarged "must" gives `JUST FALSE` in my compliance checks (for example `reg 39(2) the relevant person has met the requirements`).

And the rulings of 2026-11-05: a period the text fixes in its own words gives `JUST FALSE` outside it; a failed "where" (and "when", "upon", "whenever") gives `JUST FALSE`; a scope limit with no "only" gives `NOTHING`; a definition the text hands to a list gives `JUST FALSE` when no head holds; a defence that is not proved gives `JUST FALSE`; an "until" bar is its own yes/no question.
Where a clause could be read either way, it is a fork below.
The places where a lawyer's answer is most likely to differ from the one given here, in the order the text meets them:

1. **Regulation 33(1), the list of cases.**
   When no limb of paragraphs (a) to (g) holds, and limb (b) (the connection with a FATF call for action country) is answered no, the answer is `JUST FALSE` (F33-1): limb (g) catches every other higher-risk case.
   `NOTHING` is left only where limb (b) cannot be answered because a seam (regulation 4 or 27) gives `NOTHING`.
   A lawyer who reads the list as open will want `NOTHING`.
2. **Regulation 33(4A) and (4B), "Where".**
   A business that provides no life insurance policy gets `JUST FALSE` to "must it consider the beneficiary?" (ruling (b), a failed "where"), not `NOTHING`.
3. **Regulation 34(2) and 37(8), the flat negatives.**
   "May the institution have a correspondent relationship with a shell bank?" is `JUST FALSE` (ruling N2); the question "may it with some other party?" is `NOTHING`, because the text prohibits only the shell-bank case.
4. **Regulation 35(9), the twelve months.**
   The twelve months end on the anniversary of ceasing, inclusive, and after the later of that day and the longer period the answer is `JUST FALSE` (F35-2).
   A person who ceased on 29 February 2020 has until 28 February 2021: `add months` clamps to the end of the shorter month.
5. **Regulation 38(1), the amounts.**
   "The maximum amount ... is GBP 150" is read as "no more than GBP 150" (F38-2); the UK-only words attach to the reloadable instrument (F38-1); "Subject to paragraph (3)" adds no condition (F38-3).
6. **Regulation 39(3), who may be relied on.**
   A person within none of (a), (c), (d) is `JUST FALSE` for "does the person fall within paragraph (3)?" (F39-1), because regulation 39(1) hands "a person who falls within paragraph (3)" to the list.
   `reg 39(1) the relevant person may rely on the third party` is a limited permission and stays `NOTHING` outside the list.
7. **Regulation 40(1), outside the five years.**
   Before the five years begin and once they have run, "must the person still keep the records?" is `JUST FALSE` (F40-1), because the text fixes the period in its own words.
   The other reading ("at least" is a floor that says nothing of later days) gives `NOTHING`, and paragraph (4), a ten-year ceiling for one kind of record, reads best on that reading; paragraph (5), which requires deletion of personal data once the period expires, reads best on the first.
8. **Counting days.**
   "Within fourteen days from the date" and "within 90 days of ..." run to the 14th or 90th day after the day of the event, and the last day itself is in time (F43-1, F45-1).
   "Five years beginning on the date" (regulation 40(3)) ends the day before the fifth anniversary; "a period of five years after the date" (regulation 44(9)(a)) ends on the fifth anniversary (F40-2, F44-1).
   The Interpretation Act 1978 is not staged and is applied nowhere; a lawyer should check these against it and the cases.
9. **Regulation 45(10J).**
   "Must the Commissioners keep the information?" is `JUST TRUE` up to the fifth anniversary of the trust ceasing and `JUST FALSE` after it; "may they keep it?" is `JUST TRUE` up to the tenth anniversary and `JUST FALSE` after it (F45-3).
10. **Regulation 45ZA(5)(aa).**
    The citation of "paragraph (1)(b)(iii) or (c)(ii)" is read as paragraph (2)(b)(iii) and (c)(ii) (F45-2).
11. **The three "comes into force" dates** (regulations 42(2)(b)(iii)(aaa), 45ZA(2)(b)(iii), 45ZA(2)(c)(ii)) and the "31st January after the tax year" dates (regulations 45(3)(a), 45(9)) are not in the text or need the tax year's calendar; each is a plain date input.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (mlr2)

- **`JUST TRUE`** and **`JUST FALSE`** are answers the Regulations themselves give, in their own words: a "means" or "is" definition, an "if", "unless" or "provided that", a flat negative ("must not", "is not required"), a period the text states, a "where" that is not met.
- **`NOTHING`** means the Regulations do not answer on these facts: the text is silent, a list is not closed, a clause says "includes", a term is defined elsewhere (in another regulation, another Act, or a document the text does not contain), or a scope limit ("when the customer is a branch ...", "who provides ...", "who is relied on") is not met.
  Never read `NOTHING` as "no", and never read `JUST FALSE` as "the law forbids it": for a compliance check it can mean "the duty has not been discharged".
- Every branch in the layer-1 files carries a comment, `-- FALSE-kind (...)` or `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, giving the words of the text that license the value.
- **An unanswered seam gives `NOTHING`, not `JUST FALSE`.**
  A rule that turns on an answer from another part (regulation 8, regulation 27, Schedule 3A, ...) and is given `NOTHING` for it answers `NOTHING` itself, except where the rule's other facts already settle it (a `JUST FALSE` from a failed condition holds whatever the seam says).
- **Failures, sorted.**
  *Loud* (the checker or `check.sh` stops): a misspelt name, a wrong constructor, a missing import, a parse error, a failing assertion, a module the second main build reads differently.
  *Silent* (exit code 0, a wrong answer): a seam given `JUST TRUE` instead of `NOTHING` (a rule then looks settled); a date that does not exist (`add months` clamps, so 29 February 2020 plus twelve months is 28 February 2021, not an error); a `#TRACE` outcome (neither `check.sh` nor the assertions see it); a `NOTHING` from a limited rule read as "not required".

## What the main binary does not accept

Found while writing, each with the form that works:

1. **A deontic value cannot be asserted.**
   `#ASSERT <obligation> EQUALS FULFILLED` is refused with "Trying to check equality on types that do not support it".
   Use `#TRACE <obligation> AT 0 WITH PARTY ... DOES ... AT n`, read the outcome by hand, and assert the companion decision function (`... was given within 90 days`) at the same boundary.
   The 14 traces are listed under "Traces read by hand".
2. **`x NOT EQUALS NOTHING` is a parse error**, reported at a bracket elsewhere on the line; write `(NOT (x EQUALS NOTHING))`.
3. **`WHEN \`ctor\` WITH body THEN` is a parse error;** write `WHEN \`ctor\` body THEN`.
4. **A record built inline inside parentheses as an argument is a parse error** ("unexpected ("); build it in a `WHERE` and pass the name.
5. **`APPEND` is not an infix;** use `concat (LIST ...)` or `append xs ys`.
6. **`HAS` fields on one line misalign;** put each field on its own indented line.
7. **A constructor named `A`** is a reserved word.
8. **No `REFUSE`, no `BECAUSE` except `LEST BREACH BECAUSE`, no section `GIVEN`, no sets, no `TYPICALLY`, no `YMD`;** `Date d m y` and `elem x xs` instead.
9. **`add months` and `add years` clamp to the end of a shorter month** (29 February 2020 plus twelve months is 28 February 2021); this is silent and is recorded as a reading in F35-2.
10. **`sed -i` on macOS needs a suffix** (a tooling note, not a language limit).
11. **`MUST ... WITHIN n` is inclusive:** a trace at time n is FULFILLED, at n+1 a BREACH; a `SHANT` with an event that does not match leaves the obligation standing (the residual obligation prints as `PARTY ... MUST NOT ...`).
