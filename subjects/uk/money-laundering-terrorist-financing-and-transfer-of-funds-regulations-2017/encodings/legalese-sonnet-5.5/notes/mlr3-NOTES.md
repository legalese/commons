## Seat (mlr3)

The seat is a **business that is a relevant person under the Regulations, and its compliance officer** (a bank, an accountancy or legal firm, an estate agent, a money service business, a cryptoasset business and so on).
There is never a seat for an official.
A provision that addresses only a supervisor or a registering authority (for example regs 46(1) to (4), 46A, 49(1)(a) to (c), 49(2) and (3), 50(1), 51) is encoded in layer 1 for what it decides, and the person meets it only through the questions that ask what it means to a supervised business.

The seat layer is `mlr3-seat.l4`: 143 questions, each with `@export` and `@desc`, named "Under regulation N, ..." with the Regulations' own numbering, grouped under 8 `§§` headings in the text's order.
Every question takes plain inputs: yes or no, a number, a date, or a choice from a list.
A fact that another part of the Regulations decides, and that the business may not know (whether there is a business relationship, whether a body is a law enforcement authority, whether an offence is one Schedule 3 lists), is asked as "Yes / No / I am not sure"; "not sure" becomes `NOTHING`, and a rule that cannot answer without it answers `NOTHING`.
The dates the Regulations leave out (the full commencement day of reg 56B(2), the Good Fridays and bank holidays near a three-working-day period) are asked for.
The records and `MAYBE` inputs the modules expect are built inside each question from those answers, and the same module rule is then called; no rule is restated in the seat.
The `@desc` of an input that is one of the module's own statutory conditions quotes that condition ("Is it true that ..."); the long ones read stiffly, and are a finding for the person who edits the form (Finding 12).
`mlr3-seat.l4` is **generated** by `work/seatgen.py` (not shipped) from question specs that name the module rule each question calls; the generator types the inputs and the call, and every expected value in `mlr3-cases-seat.l4` is hand-written in the spec from the text.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (mlr3)

- **`JUST TRUE`** is the Regulations' own yes on the facts given: a duty is discharged, a permission applies, a definition is met.
- **`JUST FALSE`** is used where the text says no in its own words, or where Meng's rulings make an unmet clause a no: a flat negative ("shall not", "does not prevent"), an unmet "if" or "where", a period that has passed without the act, a "must" that has not been done, or a closed definition ("means") that is not met. A prohibition is named for the act it permits and answers JUST FALSE where it bites (ruling N2): `reg 56(1) the person may act as the business` is JUST FALSE for a business that must be registered and has no exception shown. Its twin named for the prohibition (`reg 56(1) the prohibition on acting as the business applies to the person`) is JUST TRUE there and JUST FALSE where an exception holds (F10).
- **`NOTHING`** is "the Regulations do not answer on these facts". It is never a no. It is returned when a provision is limited by its own wording and the facts fall outside the limit (reading (b)); when the answer belongs to another section that has not been given (a seam input that is "not sure", for example whether there is a business relationship); when the text is silent on the facts (reg 56(1) when an exception applies: the prohibition is lifted and the paragraph does not say the person may act); when a list is not closed; when a word is removed from the rendering (F8); and when a prohibition's "where" is unmet and the function is named for the permitted act (F6).
- In this subject: **a "no" is**, for example, `reg 56B(1)` once 28 days have passed without the firm informing the FCA, `reg 64(2)` for a provider that cannot respond fully and rapidly to an enquiry from a person specified in paragraph (3), or `reg 56(1)` for a business that must be registered and is not. **"Not covered" is**, for example, `reg 62(1)` for a person who is not a payment service provider (a provider outside its five classes is JUST FALSE), `reg 56B(1)` for a person who is not an authorised or specified investment cryptoasset firm, `reg 64(2)` for an enquirer paragraph (3) does not specify, and `reg 64C(1)` for a transfer outside Part 7A.
- Reading the seat: a `NOTHING` there means the Regulations are silent or the question is outside them for those facts. "I am not sure" goes in as `NOTHING` and comes out as `NOTHING` when the rule cannot answer without it, but see reading (e) for the rules that read it as "not shown".

## Note for the merge

**How "the duty does not arise" is answered in this range.**
It is answered through the compliance function itself, and neither as JUST TRUE (part 1's convention) nor through a companion function (part 4's).
Where the duty does not arise because a "where", "when", "upon" or "if" is unmet, the compliance function answers JUST FALSE (the rulings of 2026-11-05): for example `reg 64D(2) the cryptoasset business of the beneficiary has done what the paragraph requires` is JUST FALSE for a business that has not become aware of the missing information.
Read that as "the text's condition is not met", not "the business has defaulted" (marginal reading (i)).
Where it does not arise because the person falls outside a relative clause, a class or a bare list, the function answers NOTHING.
The companion functions in this range are not "the duty arises" functions; they are the prohibition-named twins (F10) and the two "must delay" functions (F13).
So a merge that wants part 1's JUST TRUE for "no duty" has to add it; nothing here answers it.

- **`CONCAT` is a string function; `append` joins lists.** (Loud: an error.)
- Nothing in this range needed `REFUSE`, `BECAUSE` (outside `LEST BREACH`), sets, or a `YMD` of my own (`Date d m y` is used).
