# Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
There is no REFUSE in it.
It was checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
**Not for public use as advice.**

Every question is a function named as the taxpayer would ask it (`What is my taxable income`, `How much income tax do I owe`, `Is the gain on my home disregarded`), exported with `@export` and a `@desc` on every input, so it can be deployed to Legalese Cloud.

This baseline replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers everything that encoding covered and keeps its hand-checked values where they still hold. Two values changed: c048's tax is now computed from the Rates Act rather than taken from the scenario, and d071 is now answered through indexation as well as the discount.

- **The rates for 2023-24 (dated arm, REQ-0056, 22 October 2026):** Income Tax Rates Act 1986, Compilation No. 62, compilation date 1 April 2024, including amendments up to Act No. 3, 2024 (register id C2024C00132), the compilation in force at the end of the 2023-24 year of income, as deposited at `../../../income-tax-rates-act-1986/registers/source-bundle/C2004A03348.2024-04-01.txt`. ## Seat

**The taxpayer: an individual Australian resident.** The question is "How much income tax must I pay, and what is exempt or offset?" The Act is written in the second person ("Your assessable income ... includes", "You can deduct"), so the seat is its own.

The 2026-10-18 defaults, and where this encoding departs from them:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** The Income Tax Rates Act 1986 is brought in for the threshold, the resident rates and the company rate. The Regulations set none of the numbers used. Indexed caps and the CPI indexation factor are published figures that are not in the deposit, so they are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** These are you, your spouse, your employer, the company, the Commissioner, and the beneficiary.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** The Commissioner's power to allow a longer period (s 292-102(1)(g)) is an input. So is the Commissioner's longer period under s 118-150 (that one is not modelled; see the comment).
- **The Act's definitions are worked out from facts:**
  - discount capital gain, discount percentage;
  - death benefits dependant;
  - the start-up conditions;
  - the base rate entity (Rates Act);
  - taxable income;
  - tax loss.

  Borrowed or factual terms are asked as facts: ordinary income, aggregated turnover, early stage innovation company, net value, total superannuation balance.
- **One case.** One taxpayer's income year, one asset, one contribution or one benefit.
- **The Act's own terms, each glossed.** Each term is given a plain gloss in a comment beside the quoted words.
- **Departure: the company questions.** Two events are about Jarrah Legal Technologies Pty Ltd, the company Daniel founded: d04a (its rate and its losses) and d049 (its R&D offset). The company is the taxpayer, and no individual is. These questions are asked from the founder's seat ("What rate does my company pay?", "What R&D offset does my company get?"). The facts are the ones the founder would state.
- **Departure: d093.** The CGT-on-death question is the deceased's own liability. It is phrased "Does my death make a capital gain", which the executor answers.

## Why this scope

The Act runs to 12 volumes, about 100,000 lines of deposited text. This baseline keeps to what the individual taxpayer meets in these lives and to the definitions those answers need:

- taxable income and the tax owed, with the resident rates;
- deductions and gifts;
- tax losses;
- the two individual offsets;
- the ESS upfront regime;
- the CGT provisions a home seller, a founder selling shares and an estate meet;
- the superannuation provisions for contributions, pensions and death benefits;
- R&D and the early stage investor offset, only as far as Daniel's events need.

**Left out:**

- **Capital gains machinery:**
  - Subdiv 112-E (the deemed sale and reacquisition on 1 July 2027 of assets held on 30 June 2027);
  - cost base rules (Divs 110, 112) and Subdiv 960-M indexation factors;
  - loss ordering;
  - the foreign and temporary resident discount rules;
  - partial main residence exemptions and the absence rule;
  - the rest of Div 152.
- **Employee shares and investment:** Subdiv 83A-C deferral and Subdiv 130-D; Subdiv 360-B.
- **Gifts:** Subdiv 30-DB (spreading a gift deduction); gifts of property.
- **R&D:** Div 355 beyond the rate.
- **Superannuation:**
  - the non-concessional bring-forward;
  - transfer balance credits and debits in detail;
  - untaxed elements;
  - income streams to dependants under 60.
- **Everything outside these lives:** depreciation, trading stock, primary production, partnerships and trusts, consolidation, international tax, FBT interactions.
- **Other Acts:** the Medicare levy and HELP repayments.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Individuals lose the 50% CGT discount on 1 July 2027, and indexation of the cost base comes back

Section 115-100(aa) gives an individual 50% only for a gain "from a *CGT event happening before 1 July 2027". After that date, only s 115-102 (a new residential dwelling) keeps 50%. Otherwise, para (f) gives "0% if none of the above paragraphs applies".

In its place, s 110-36(1A) indexes the cost base of an individual's or a trust's asset for a CGT event on or after 1 July 2027. The asset must have been held 12 months (s 114-10). A gain worked out with indexation is not a discount capital gain at all (s 115-20).

Daniel's Jarrah shares (d071, held from 2031) and Chloe's ESS shares (c085, from 2034) are both sold on 30 June 2050. On this compilation, each gain is proceeds less an indexed cost base, with no discount. d071 expects `cgt_discount: 0.5`; that is wrong on this text. Asserted.

### 2. The deposited Rates Act cannot answer Chloe's first return (superseded 22 October 2026 by finding 9)

The Rates Act compilation of 1 July 2026 has resident tables only for 2024-25 onward. Chloe's 2023-24 return (c045) therefore has no rate in the deposit, and the encoding answers NOTHING.

(Inference, not asserted: if the 2024-25 table applied, $27,500 would give $1,488 less the $700 offset, $788, not the scenario's nil.) Asserted as NOTHING.

### 3. Chloe's 2024-25 tax is $12,238, not $12,842

Under the 2024-25 table:

- $26,800 at 16% is $4,288.
- $26,500 at 30% is $7,950.
- The total is $12,238.

Her $71,500 is above the $66,667 ceiling of the Low Income offset, so she gets no offset. The scenario's $12,842 matches nothing in the text. Asserted.

### 4. Byford is exempt only by choice

The land contract was signed about 17 months before the family moved in. Section 118-110 needs the dwelling to be the main residence "throughout your *ownership period", so on its own it fails. Section 118-150 lets the owner choose to treat the dwelling as the main residence from acquisition, for up to 4 years, if they moved in as soon as practicable and stayed at least 3 months. c080's nil holds only with that choice. The East Perth apartment (d055) was lived in from purchase and is exempt outright. Asserted.

### 5. A testamentary gift earns no deduction, but its capital gain is disregarded

Section 30-15(2): "A testamentary gift or contribution is not deductible under this section." Section 118-60(1) still disregards the gain on a testamentary gift "that would have been deductible under section 30-15 if it had not been a testamentary gift". Without that, event K3 (s 104-215) would tax the transfer to an exempt entity.

The residue passing to the testamentary trust is disregarded under s 128-10 (d093). Daniel's lifetime gift of $2,000,000 to the private ancillary fund (d072) is deductible in full, subject to the s 26-55 limit. Asserted.

### 6. Start-up ESS shares: no tax on the discount, and the later gain runs from market value

When the s 83A-33 conditions hold, the discount drops out of assessable income entirely. The conditions are:

- unlisted;
- under 10 years incorporated;
- turnover of no more than $50 million;
- a discount of no more than 15%;
- an Australian-resident employer;
- the s 83A-45 conditions.

Section 83A-30(1) then treats the share as acquired for its market value, so the later capital gain (c085) is measured from that value. This does not apply to a start-up right (s 83A-30(2)).

Outside the start-up rules, s 83A-35 takes off at most $1,000, and only if the income test total is no more than $180,000. Asserted.

### 7. Superannuation death benefits: tax-free to a spouse, taxed to adult children

A lump sum paid to a death benefits dependant is not assessable (s 302-60). A dependant is a spouse or former spouse, a child under 18, an interdependent person, or a dependant (s 302-195). This covers Sue (c087), Chloe (c096) and Priya (d093).

Adult children who were not dependent are not dependants: Oliver and Ella (c098) and Ruby (k098). Their taxable component is assessable (s 302-145(1)), with an offset holding the rate to 15% on the element taxed in the fund. Asserted.

### 8. Smaller things

- **Low Income tax offset:** $700 up to $37,500, phasing out to nil at $66,667. Asserted.
- **Working Australians tax offset (Subdiv 61-E):** inserted by Act No. 49, 2026. The year it first applies is set in that Act, not in the compilation, so it is a fact here. If it applies to Jake's 2028-29 year it takes $250 off ($842 owed instead of $1,092). Asserted both ways.
- **Company losses:** a company may choose to deduct nil (s 36-17(2)). An individual must deduct the loss against the excess (s 36-15(2)). Asserted.
- **R&D:** under $20 million turnover, the rate is the company rate plus 18.5 points, and the offset is refundable. At 25% that is 43.5% (d049). Asserted.
- **Early stage investors:** 20%, capped at $200,000 with carry-forwards. A retail investor paying more than $50,000 in the year gets nothing; a sophisticated investor is outside that limit (d081). Asserted.
- **Div 152:** the $6,000,000 maximum net asset value test is far exceeded at a $40,000,000 sale (d071; using the sale price as the net value is an inference). Asserted.
- **Downsizer:** age 55 or over, the home held 10 years, the contribution made within 90 days (or longer if allowed), at most $300,000 each and no more than the proceeds (c094). Asserted.
- **Benefits at 60 or over:** "not assessable income and is not *exempt income" (s 301-10), covering c092 and d083. Asserted.
- **Non-concessional cap:** 4 times the concessional cap, and nil once the total super balance reaches the general transfer balance cap. Jake's $100,000 (k089) fits a cap of at least $25,000. Asserted.

### 9. Chloe's 2023-24 income tax is $1,067, not nil (c045)

Rates Act Compilation No. 62, in force for 2023-24, taxes a full-year resident at 19% on taxable income above the $18,200 threshold, up to $45,000. On $27,500 the basic income tax liability is 9,300 x 19% = $1,767. The Low Income tax offset is the full $700 at that income (s 61-115 item 1: taxable income not over $37,500). The working Australians offset (Subdiv 61-E, inserted in 2026) does not apply to 2023-24. So `How much income tax do I owe` 2023 gives $1,067.

The scenario expects nil tax on the 2023-24 return. That is wrong on the law then in force: the offset of $700 does not cover $1,767. The Medicare levy of $150 (Medicare Levy Act baseline, REQ-0024) and any HELP repayment are on top. The audit's unchecked recollection of $1,767 and $1,067 matches the deposited text. This is for the scenario's author. Asserted.
