# Long Service Leave Act 1976 (Tas) — baseline encoding, the employee's seat

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18, the rulings on negatives of 2026-11-04 and 2026-11-05 (copied below), and the `writing-l4-rules` skill (with `references/regulative.md`).
There is no REFUSE in it; where the text does not answer, a question answers `NOTHING` with a `NOTHING-kind` tag in the source, and the tests assert it.
It was checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98, with `JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` unset.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
**Not for public use as advice.**

It was written as part of a run of baseline encodings of the Acts behind personal injury, compensation and other claims work, here under employment: the Act is one of the state long service leave Acts that the Commonwealth Fair Work Act leaves in place. The Act is short (about 30 sections), so **the whole operative Act** is encoded; only machinery, the Secretary's procedures and transitional provisions of historic reach are left out (listed below). There was no earlier encoding of this Act to keep, and no ledger entry was open for it (Tasmania has no requirement ledger).

Three modules:

- `taslsl-dates.l4` — the date arithmetic (built-in date functions only; no `daydate` import).
- `taslsl-employee.l4` — the rules and the 24 exported questions, each with `@export` and a `@desc` on every input, grouped under `§§` headings in the Act's order: does the Act apply (ss 2, 3, 7); mining employee (ss 2, 2A, Schedule 2); continuous employment (ss 2(2), 5); the age for retirement (ss 2, 8(3A)); how much leave (ss 7A, 8, 8A); taking the leave (s 12(1), (2), (3), (5), (10)); payment instead of leave (s 10); ordinary pay (s 11); when the employment ends (s 12(4) — arises only when it ends); on a death (s 9 — asked by the personal representative); when I must be paid (s 12(4), (6)); disputes, recovery and prosecution (ss 13, 14A, 15, 20, 21 — arises only on a dispute or unpaid money).
- `taslsl-cases.l4` — 154 assertions and 3 traces.

**Regulative rule.** `s 12(4), (6)(a) -- pay the leave in full when it begins`: `PARTY my employer MUST pay my long service leave the amount PROVIDED the amount AT LEAST the amount owed WITHIN the days allowed HENCE FULFILLED LEST BREACH BY my employer BECAUSE ...`, with day 0 the day the duty arose (the day the leave was fixed, or the day the employment ended) and the deadline the first day of the leave (or the last day of employment, which s 12(4) makes the first day of the leave). A part payment does not count. It runs inside the exported question `Was my leave pay paid on time` through `CONSIDER EVALTRACE ... WHEN FULFILLED`; the question takes only dates and amounts. One fulfilment and two breaches are `#TRACE`d with `-- expect:` lines.

## Seat

**The employee** (or former employee) to whom the Act gives long service leave: "Does the Act cover me? Am I a mining employee? Did this break keep my employment continuous, and does it count? Have I reached the age for retirement? How many weeks am I entitled to, and must my employer let me take them? Can I be made to take the leave while I am on workers' compensation? Can I be paid out? What is my ordinary pay, and what must I be paid when my job ends? Was I paid on time? Can I refer a dispute, and can a lawyer represent me? Can I still recover the money, and by when can my employer be prosecuted?" For the two questions a death raises (s 9), the deceased employee's **personal representative**, who asks them on the estate's behalf. No seat is given to the employer, the Secretary, the Commission or the magistrate; the Secretary's exemptions and directions (ss 7, 12(1)(a)) are asked as facts or turned into what they mean for the employee (the earliest day a direction can start the leave).

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone.** The Acts it names (the Long Service Leave (State Employees) Act 1994, the Local Government (Building and Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1993, the three workers' compensation Acts, the Corporations Act, the Veterans' Entitlements Act 1986, the Industrial Relations Act 1984) are named, not encoded; what they decide is asked as a fact.
- **The actors the text names:** the employee, the employer, the personal representative, the Secretary, the Commission and its Commissioners and Full Bench, the magistrate, inspectors.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally:** whether an illness or necessity "justifies" ending the employment and whether misconduct was "serious and wilful" (s 8(3)), whether the employee is "regularly employed" (s 5(3)), "reasonable grounds" (s 5(7)), the Secretary's satisfaction (s 7) are inputs. "As soon as practicable ... having regard to the needs of the employer's establishment" (s 12(1)) is not decided.
- **Definitions:** "employee" (s 2(1)), "mining employee" and "metalliferous mine" (with Schedule 2), "age for retirement", "workers' compensation", continuity (s 5), "ordinary pay" (s 11) are worked from facts; "award", "related corporation", "Australian legal practitioner" and "registered organisation" are asked as facts.
- **One case:** one employment (with one employer, counting the service s 2(2) and s 5 join to it).
- **Terms:** the Act's own words in the identifiers, with plain glosses in the `@desc` lines.
- **Machinery out** (below).

## Scope

In: s 2(1) (employee, employer, mining employee, metalliferous mine, age for retirement, workers' compensation, transmission), s 2(2); s 2A(1) and Schedule 2; s 3; s 5(1)–(10) (s 5(11) in a description); s 7(1) (as an input); s 7A; s 8(1)–(3A); s 8A(1)–(3A); s 9; s 10; s 11(1)–(8) (s 11(9) in descriptions); s 12(1)–(6), (9) (in descriptions), (10); s 13(1) (with (1A)–(4) and s 14 in the description); s 14A; s 15(1), (3); s 17 (in a comment: s 10 and s 12 agreements are the express exceptions); s 20(1) penalty; s 21; s 23 (in a description).

Out, and why:

- ss 1, 2A(2), 4, 19, 22, 24 — title, repeal, the Governor's power to amend Schedule 2, inspectors and their powers, the court, regulation-making: machinery.
- s 6 — employment before 15 December 1976, and the 24-year cap on employment before the Long Service Leave Act 1956: of historic reach only.
- s 7(2)–(8) — how the Secretary grants, renews, reviews and revokes an exemption: an official's procedure; whether an exemption is in force is asked. s 7(3) (the effect of a revocation) is noted in finding 10.
- s 8(4), s 8A(4) — employment that began before the Long Service Leave Act 1964 commenced, or (for mining) before the date proclaimed under the Long Service Leave Amendment Act 1980: neither date is in the deposited text, and such employment would now exceed 46 years.
- s 12(7), (8) — adjusting a payment for a pay rise during the leave, and paying by posted cheque: mechanics of payment, not entitlement.
- s 16 — costs before a magistrate: a discretion.
- s 18 — the employer's duty to keep a leave record in the prescribed form (regulations not deposited); s 20(1)(a), (2) — false records and the good-faith defence: the employer's offence.
- ss 25, 26, 27 and Schedule 3 — application of the 1979 and 1980 amendments and the 2011 transitional arrangements (the 2011 transition period ended on 30 June 2013): of historic reach; finding 9.

## Fork register

Each line: the provision, the function, the answer taken, the other reading.

- **F1** ss 2(2), 5(1)(g), (h), 5(9), 12(1)(a), 15(1), 21 "2 months", "3 months", "6 months", "3 years", "one year" — `the same day ... months after`, `the same day ... years after` — the period ends on the same day of the month that many months later, clamped to the last day of a shorter month (31 August + 2 months = 31 October; 30 November + 3 months = 28 February; 29 February + 12 months = 28 February) — the next day (1 March), or periods of 30 or 365 days.
- **F2** ss 5, 8, 8A, 9 "years' continuous employment", "the number of years" — `s 5 -- my years of continuous employment` — the first and the last day are both counted; whole years by anniversary, and the part year as the days since the last anniversary over the days in that anniversary year; days that are not counted (s 5(2)) push the first day later — completed years only (so the pro rata of s 8(2)(a)(iii) would ignore a part year), or 365-day years.
- **F3** s 2A(1) "The several substances specified in Schedule 2 are mineral substances" — `s 2 -- I am a mining employee` — a substance not in Schedule 2 is not a mineral substance: `JUST FALSE` (the Act hands the meaning of "mineral substance" to the Schedule; ruling of 2026-11-05 on delegated definitions (a)) — `NOTHING`, since s 2A does not say "only".
- **F4** ss 2(2), 5(4)–(9) — `ss 2(2), 5(4), (5), (7), (9) -- my earlier service counts` — with none of the links, the earlier service is not "continuous employment with an employer" (s 7A): `JUST FALSE` — `NOTHING`, since s 5 only deems and does not say what does not count.
- **F5** s 8(3)(a), 8A(3)(a) "an employee who attains the age for retirement" — `ss 8(2), 8A(2) -- the weeks ... I have become entitled to` — paragraph (b) pro rata applies to such an employee while still employed and however the employment later ends, misconduct included (the text ties paras (b)–(d) to a termination, but not para (a)) — para (a) read as a retirement, arising only when the employment ends, and not on a dismissal for misconduct.
- **F6** s 12(1)(a) "no direction ... shall require the long service leave to commence before the expiration of 6 months after the date of the direction" — `s 12(1)(a) -- the earliest day a direction can start my leave` — the 6 months expire at the end of the same day 6 months on, so the earliest start is the next day — the same day 6 months on.
- **F7** s 9(2), (3) "ordinary pay for a period equalling 1/60th [1/40th] of the ... period" — `s 9 -- the weeks of ordinary pay owed to the estate` — a year is 52 weeks, so 1/60th of a year is 52/60 weeks (and 1/60th of 10 years is 8 2/3 weeks, as in s 8) — a year of 365 days (52 1/7 weeks), giving 0.869 weeks a year.
- **F8** s 11(3), (4), (6) "the average rate of the remuneration ... during the period of 3 [12] months"; "the average weekly number of hours ... during the period of 12 months" — `s 11 -- my weekly ordinary pay` — the average rate is per hour (pay in the period over ordinary hours in it), applied to the normal weekly hours; 12 months are 52 weeks — an average weekly rate (pay over 13 or 52 weeks), applied without reference to hours.
- **F9** s 9(1) "an employee who is entitled to long service leave under this Act" — `s 9 -- the weeks ...` — the entitlement is the blocks accrued under s 8(2)(a)(i), (ii) or s 8A(2)(a)(i); a pro rata entitlement under s 8(2)(b) at the age for retirement (fork F5) is not added, because s 9(2)(b) already pays the whole 7-to-10-year period — add both (paying the 7-to-10-year period twice).
- **F10** s 12(4) with s 12(6) — `s 12(4), (6)(a) -- whether my leave pay was paid on time` — on termination the whole of the pay is due on the day the employment ends — paid at the usual pay times (s 12(6)(b)) as though the leave were being taken.
- **F11** s 15(1) "at any time during the period of 3 years immediately preceding the date of the application" — `s 15(1) -- I may apply to a magistrate for the payment` — the period runs from the same day 3 years before the application up to the application day itself — excluding both ends.
- **F12** s 5(1) — `s 5(1), (10) -- my employment stays continuous` — a break of a kind the list does not name answers `NOTHING`; s 5(1)(h) needs both the 6-month limit (measured from the start of the stand-down or the termination to the return) and the 14 days after the offer or posting — the 6 months measured as the stand-down's own length.
- **F13** s 12(2) "a period ... in which he is unable to work in his ordinary employment by reason of the injury" — `s 12(2), (3) -- I need not take my leave during this period` — compensation under a law outside the s 2(1) definition does not give the protection: `JUST FALSE` ("workers' compensation means") — `NOTHING`, on the view that the rest of the Act might still protect such an employee.

> - **Periods, "where" and scope limits** (a question raised on 2026-11-05 while checking an encoding of the Retirement and Re-employment Act 1993, not a bench): (a) a period the text fixes in its own words ("within one month", "begins from ... until the employee attains the age", "on the last day of employment"): outside the period the answer is `JUST FALSE`; (b) a "where ..." condition: when it fails the answer is `JUST FALSE`; (c) a scope limit with no "only" (for example "below" the minimum age): outside the scope the answer is `NOTHING`. ## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The Act says nothing about where the work is done.** There is no provision tying it to employment in Tasmania (contrast s 6(1) of the Northern Territory Act). The encoding asks no location fact; which State's Act applies to an employee who works across borders is a question for the general law and for the Fair Work Act, not answered here.
2. **Awards and agreements are not excluded by the Act itself.** s 3 excludes only State employees and local government employees (s 3(b) has been omitted). Whether a modern award or enterprise agreement displaces the Act is a matter of the Fair Work Act 2009, outside this text.
3. **The default age for retirement differs by sex** (s 2(1): 65 for a male, 60 for a female), and the definition of metalliferous mine counts "2 or more men" at a prospecting site. The text gives no age for anyone else; the encoding answers `NOTHING` between 60 and 65 for such a person, and reads "men" as people. Whether the 60/65 split survives the Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (Cth) is a question outside this text and is flagged for a lawyer.
4. **One rate underlies every head.** 8 2/3 weeks for 10 years, 4 1/3 for each further 5, the pro rata on termination and before 10 years, and 1/60th on death all come to 13/15 of a week a year (mining: 13 weeks per 10 years and 1/40th, both 1.3 weeks a year). So a non-mining employee whose employment ends after 10 years is owed years × 13/15 weeks in total (12 years: 10.4 weeks), and the death payment equals the termination payment (asserted: both $10,400 on the same facts).
5. **Misconduct matters only before 10 years.** s 8(2)(a)(iii) gives the pro rata part on "the termination of his employment" for any reason, so an employee with more than 10 years dismissed for serious and wilful misconduct, or who resigns, still gets it (asserted: 12.5 years, dismissed for misconduct, 65/6 weeks). Between 7 and 10 years (5 and 10 for mining) a resignation for an ordinary reason or a dismissal for misconduct gives nothing, unless the employee has reached the age for retirement.
6. **The workers' compensation protection is narrow.** s 12(2) protects an employee from being made to take long service leave while on "workers' compensation", which s 2(1) defines as compensation under three Tasmanian Acts only. An employee compensated under a Commonwealth scheme (Comcare, Seacare, military) is outside the definition (fork F13). Separately, an absence for illness or injury certified as necessary by a medical practitioner keeps the employment continuous and counts towards the years (s 5(1)(c), (2)); the encoding asserts both.
7. **"Attains the age for retirement" is not tied to leaving** (fork F5). Read literally, an employee with 8 years who turns 65 (or 60) while still employed is entitled to pro rata leave then.
8. **s 9 can pay where termination would not.** A death between 7 and 10 years (5 and 10 for mining) pays the estate 1/60th (1/40th) of the whole period with no s 8(3) reason needed; s 9(1) and s 9(2)(b) could overlap where the employee had reached the age for retirement (fork F9). No time is fixed for the payment to the estate; only the general 3-year reach of s 15(1) applies.
9. **A drafting slip in Schedule 3.** Clauses 2(1), (2) and (4) speak of "the new long service entitlements", a term the Schedule does not define; it defines "new long service leave entitlements" (cl 1(1)). The meaning is clear. Schedule 3 is out of scope.
10. **Revoked exemptions.** s 7(3): when the Secretary revokes or refuses to renew an exemption, the employee's earlier continuous employment counts under the Act, and leave or payments under the scheme count as if under the Act. The encoding asks only whether an exemption is in force; an employee whose exemption has ended should answer "no" and enter scheme leave as leave already taken.
11. **Only one duty has a deadline.** s 12(6)(a) (pay in full when the leave begins) is the only provision that fixes when an employee is to be paid, and it is one of three ways of payment; s 12(4) makes the last day of employment the first day of the leave (fork F10). The time limits that matter to a claim are the 3 years of s 15(1) (recovery before a magistrate) and the one year of s 21 (prosecution); s 23 keeps the employee's rights whatever happens to a prosecution.
12. **Lawyers are kept out.** s 14A bars representation by an Australian legal practitioner at a s 13 hearing or a s 14 appeal, except a lawyer employed by a registered organisation to do work non-lawyers can do. Proceedings before a magistrate under s 15 are not covered by the bar.
