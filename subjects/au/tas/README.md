## Sourced 2026-10-20: social security and benefits, and personal tax

A Priority 3 sample: fifteen instruments, text deposited, on two topics, chosen so that a Tasmanian resident could get useful determinations. Eight are principal Acts or orders; seven are the regulations that set the numbers a person needs.

### The Australian twist: what is Commonwealth and what is Tasmanian

Personal income tax and the main social security payments are Commonwealth law, and are already sourced beside this folder:
[`income-tax-assessment-act-1997`](../income-tax-assessment-act-1997/), [`income-tax-assessment-act-1936`](../income-tax-assessment-act-1936/), [`income-tax-rates-act-1986`](../income-tax-rates-act-1986/), [`medicare-levy-act-1986`](../medicare-levy-act-1986/), [`social-security-act-1991`](../social-security-act-1991/), [`social-security-administration-act-1999`](../social-security-administration-act-1999/), [`a-new-tax-system-family-assistance-act-1999`](../a-new-tax-system-family-assistance-act-1999/), [`veterans-entitlements-act-1986`](../veterans-entitlements-act-1986/) and [`military-rehabilitation-and-compensation-act-2004`](../military-rehabilitation-and-compensation-act-2004/).
Tasmania levies no income tax and pays no pension of its own. So this sample is the state law a resident meets **on top of** the Commonwealth layer: state payments when something goes wrong (a crime, a work injury, a road accident), state concessions for pensioners and low-income households, and the state taxes and grants that fall on an individual buying or owning a home.

How the layers join:

- **Commonwealth cards open Tasmanian concessions.** The council rates remission (Rates and Charges Remissions Regulations 2024, reg 4), the electricity concessions (Concession Order 2016), the land tax exemption for a pensioner's home (Land Tax Act 2000 s 19(a)-(b), with Land Tax Regulations 2021 reg 5) and the pensioner duty concession (Duties Act 2001 Ch 2 Pt 5 Div 2B) turn on a Pensioner Concession Card or Health Care Card under the Social Security Act 1991, or a DVA card or pension under the Veterans' Entitlements Act 1986 or the Military Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 2004. A Tasmanian determination therefore often starts from a Commonwealth one.
- **Tasmanian compensation feeds back into Commonwealth payments and tax.** Workers compensation weekly payments and MAIB disability allowances replace earnings and are dealt with under Commonwealth income tax law, and compensation from either scheme is "compensation" for the Social Security Act 1991's compensation-recovery and preclusion-period rules. A Tasmanian award can change a Commonwealth pension.
- **Commonwealth indices set Tasmanian numbers.** The maximum rates remission and the victims-of-crime maximum awards are indexed by the Hobart CPI published under the Census and Statistics Act 1905 of the Commonwealth.
- **The tax side connects within the State.** The Duties Act 2001 borrows its "first home" terms from the First Home Owner Grant Act 2000 (a previous grant disqualifies a buyer from the duty concession, s 46C), and the "principal residence land" that the Land Tax Rating Act 2000 rates at nil is classified under the Land Tax Act 2000. The Commonwealth's Help to Buy shared-equity scheme is carved out of the joint-owner rules of the land tax and grant instruments alike.

### Gaps and flags

- **Numbers that live outside the text.** The rates remission maximum and the victims-of-crime maxima are indexed each year by the Hobart CPI; only base figures are in the law, so the current year's figure needs the ABS index. Workers compensation lump sums are multiples of "basic salary", defined as $311.30 for 1991 varied each year by a "relevant percentage", so the current figure is not in the text.
- **Formulas shown as images.** Twelve formulas (nine distinct images) in the Workers Rehabilitation and Compensation Act, the Duties Act, the MAIB Regulations and the Rates Remissions Regulations appear in the source only as images; they are transcribed by hand in square brackets with the image URL and should be checked against the image.
- **Layout quirk.** Where an amendment note attaches to a lettered paragraph, the label can sit on its own line followed by the note and then the text (for example, Land Tax Act s 19(a)).
- **Not sourced, deliberately.** The Electricity Supply Industry Act 1995 itself (an industry Act; only its concession order matters here); the Victims of Crime Compensation Act 1994 (offender compensation orders, not a payment scheme); the Taxation Administration Act 1997 (procedure); the Duties Regulations 2021; and the Duties (Concession Extension) Order 2023, whose two operative clauses are already incorporated in the Duties Act consolidation. Concessions given by policy rather than by an instrument on the statute book are outside this sample.
- **A dangling Commonwealth reference.** The Rates Remissions Regulations cite the Aged Care Act 1997 of the Commonwealth, which has no subject under `subjects/au/`.

## Encodings (2026-10-24)

Five baseline encodings were made on 2026-10-24, each at `encodings/baseline-2026-10/` in its subject's folder. They are the first encodings of Tasmanian law here.
Between them they read fourteen of the fifteen texts above. Nine are brought into another text's encoding, because they set its numbers or because the same card or the same home switches them on, and so have no encoding folder of their own:

- the Victims of Crime Assistance Regulations 2021 into the victims of crime encoding;
- the Workers Rehabilitation and Compensation Regulations 2021 into the workers compensation encoding;
- the Motor Accidents (Liabilities and Compensation) Regulations 2020 into the motor accidents encoding;
- the First Home Owner Grant Act 2000 and its Regulations 2021 into the Duties Act encoding, because the duty reliefs borrow the grant Act's "home", "new home" and "owner", and a grant already paid bars them;
- the Land Tax Rating Act 2000, the Land Tax Regulations 2021, and the Local Government (Rates and Charges Remissions) Act 1991 and its Regulations 2024 into the Land Tax Act encoding, so that one seat asks about both yearly charges on a home, each relieved by a Commonwealth card.

The Electricity Supply Industry Concession Order 2016 is read by none, so the electricity, life support and medical heating and cooling concessions are not encoded.

Each encoding is a seat-neutral core in the texts' own actors and terms, with a regulative rule wherever a text states a duty or a deadline, and a seat layer of questions that restates no rule. The workers compensation and motor accidents encodings have a second seat, the employer (read with its licensed insurer) and the Motor Accidents Insurance Board (only for its s 23 duty to pay scheduled benefits), because the texts put real duties on them. No official has a seat. The Commissioner of State Revenue, the Commissioner who decides victims of crime awards, the WorkCover Tasmania Board and the Tribunal appear only as parties to rules or as facts.

Four findings refine this README's account of the sample, above:

- Not every concession in "How the layers join" turns on a Health Care Card. The land tax exemption for a pensioner's home takes a Pensioner Concession Card, the DVA gold card with a war widow(er) endorsement, or the special rate pension with total and permanent incapacity, but not a Health Care Card; the rates remission does take one (land tax, finding 2). The pensioner duty concession takes a Pensioner Concession Card, a Commonwealth Seniors Health Card or the special rate pension, at 60 or over (Duties Act s 46L(1)).
- The Duties Act's first home buyer concession and exemption and its pensioner duty concession, listed above, are all closed to a transfer made today as deposited, unless an extension order was made after 13 October 2026 (duties, findings 1 and 3).
- A grant already paid bars both the concession (s 46C(4)(b)) and the exemption (s 57E(4)(b)). It bars the exemption even where the grant was paid back, though the grant Act itself forgives a repaid grant (FHOG Act s 10(2); duties, finding 14).
- The Aged Care Act 1997 that the Rates Remissions Regulations cite (reg 6(a)) is repealed: the deposited Aged Care (Consequential and Transitional Provisions) Act 2024 repeals the whole of it from the commencement of the Aged Care Act 2024, 1 November 2025. Whether its s 4 carries the reference over to the 2024 Act is not settled, so living in residential aged care is asked as a fact (land tax, finding 18).

### Still inputs, because they are not deposited

Shared by several encodings:

- **The Hobart CPI.** The rates remission maximum (reg 5) and the victims of crime maxima (reg 4) are indexed by it. The case files use illustrative or hypothetical figures, not ABS figures. The 2024-25 rates remission maximum needs none (land tax, finding 23).
- **The Taxation Administration Act 1997.** Objections, interest and penalty tax on duty and land tax are not answered; the breaches name the Act and stop there.
- **The value of a penalty unit.** Penalties are given in penalty units.
- **The Acts Interpretation Act 1931.** The encodings state their own readings of how periods are counted, which that Act may settle (for example, workers compensation finding 18 and victims of crime finding 20).
- **Officials' judgments.** The Commissioner's satisfaction, the MAIB Board's determinations, and the Tribunal's extensions and findings are asked as facts, and the answer holds if the official decides that way.
- **Commonwealth cards and pensions.** Holding a Pensioner Concession Card, a Health Care Card, a Seniors Health Card or a DVA card, or receiving a special rate pension, is asked as a fact (see the Commonwealth links below).

The Tasmanian encodings also meet each other, by links in their `NOTES.md`; each takes the other's answer as a fact. The victims of crime encoding sends any death or injury arising out of the use of a motor vehicle to the MAIB scheme (s 6(1)(a)) and excludes, head by head, what has been paid or is payable under workers compensation (s 6(1)(d)), though not funeral expenses (victims of crime, findings 7-9). The motor accidents encoding excludes every scheduled benefit where workers compensation is payable for the injury (s 24(1)(b)).
