## Seat

**The person (householder or business) asked to answer** the Census or a statistical survey. The Act's operative
provisions are put to that person (ss 10, 11, 14, 15) or protect what that person gives (ss 12, 13, 19, 19A), so the
person asked is the one the law is applied to. Decisions are phrased as that person asks them: `Must I fill this in`,
`Have I committed an offence by not answering`, `Is this question optional`, `Is my false answer an offence`,
`May an officer enter my premises`, `Would an officer commit an offence by passing on what I gave`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed. The Census and Statistics Regulation
  2016 (other Census times, classes of premises, Census topics), the Census-day proclamation, the s 10(2) notifiable
  instrument and the Crimes Act 1914 penalty-unit value are not deposited, so each is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the Statistician, authorized officers, officers, the Minister, the Archives,
  Agencies, courts and tribunals.
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally.** Followed: whether a s 13 determination covers the
  information and whether the Statistician approved in writing are inputs; whether a time is "reasonable" (s 18) is an
  input.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed: "premises" (s 18(2)) and "Census day"
  (s 19A(3)) are worked out; "Agency" (Public Service Act 1999) is not needed by any decision. Whether a company is a
  "person" is not answered by this Act and is left to the reader (see finding 2).
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one approach (one form or question put to one person) is one
  `Asked` record.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed: ss 2, 6, 7, 9 (beyond being a purpose), 12(1), (3), 16, 17, 19B,
  27 out.
- **Provisions addressed only to an official.** ss 12(2), 13(3), 19 and 19A address the Statistician or officers; each is
  encoded as what it means for the person: whether results may identify me, whether my information may be released,
  whether an officer who passes it on commits an offence, and whether my Census form is shielded from agencies and courts.

## The lives

- **Chloe, c049 (2026, Census night; Chloe 23, Liam 27).** `Is a Census taken in the year` 2026 (yes); `Must I fill this
  in` under the Census instrument (required, but not itself an offence); Liam asked at the door (requested only); after a
  written direction giving 14 days, 20 days on (offence, 6 daily offences); religion left blank (no offence); false
  answer on the Census form (offence, s 15); a flat or home cannot be entered (s 18); her personal particulars cannot be
  released identifiably (s 13(3)); her form is shielded until the day before 10 August 2125 (s 19A, on the Census day
  the scenario gives).
- **Jake, k047 (2031, Census night at his sister's).** 2031 is a Census year; if he is outside the instrument's class
  at her address (an input), nothing is required of him; a direction giving 7 days, or lacking the s 14 warning, falls
  short; a proper one giving 21 days, left 35 days, gives 14 daily offences and a joined maximum of 14 penalty units.
- **Daniel, d048 (2032, a business survey to his company Jarrah).** 2032 is not a Census year and the survey is for
  s 9, so the Census instrument is not available; a request (the case file supposes one by email) carries no offence; a direction giving 28 days,
  left 40 days, gives 12 daily offences (1,200 dollars on a hypothetical 100-dollar penalty unit); Jarrah's business
  premises may be entered if in a prescribed class; its business figures may be released identifiably under a s 13
  determination.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names ss 8 and 14 and the three events. The seat adds what a person asked would next want to know:
which questions are optional (s 14(3)), what lying costs (s 15), whether officers can come in (s 18), and who can see
the answers (ss 12(2), 13, 19, 19A, 8A). Everything else in the Act is machinery or addressed to officials with no
consequence for the person asked.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Not filling in the Census form is not, by itself, an offence.** s 14(1) bites only on a person "served a direction
   under subsection 10(4) or 11(2)". The Census form is required by notifiable instrument (s 10(2)) and a collector's
   approach is a request (s 10(3), 11(1)); neither is such a direction. So on Census night Chloe is required to fill it
   in, but commits no offence until a written direction is served and its period (at least 14 days) runs out. Asserted.
2. **A business survey cannot use the Census instrument.** s 10(2) is "for the purposes of section 8" only; a business
   survey is a s 9 collection, so Jarrah can only be requested (no offence) or directed (offence on non-compliance).
   Whether a company is a "person" is not stated in this Act (inference: the Acts Interpretation Act 1901, not
   deposited, would decide it); the encoding treats Jarrah as a person asked. Asserted for the methods; the company point
   is not asserted.
3. **The penalty is one penalty unit, but it repeats daily.** s 14 Note 2 and s 21 make the obligation continue with an
   offence "in respect of each day"; s 22 lets the charges be joined with one penalty up to the sum of the maxima. The
   encoding counts one offence a day after the period runs (an inference about where counting starts). Asserted.
4. **Religion is the one question that may be left unanswered.** s 14(3); the defendant bears an evidential burden.
   Asserted.
5. **Lying is an offence on every method; refusing only on two.** s 15 (10 penalty units) reaches anyone "required,
   requested or directed" under s 10(2), (3) or (4) or s 11(1) or (2). A false Census answer is an offence even though a
   blank one is not. Asserted.
6. **A direction must give at least 14 days and explain s 14.** ss 10(4)-(5), 11(2)-(3). The Act does not say what a
   defective notice does; the encoding reads it as not a direction for s 14 (inference) but still a direction for s 15.
   Asserted on that inference.
7. **Personal information is protected from identifying release; business information is not.** s 13(3) bars only
   "information of a personal or domestic nature relating to a person" from release likely to identify that person; a
   business's figures may be released identifiably under a determination the Statistician approves in writing. Published
   results, by contrast, must never be likely to identify "a particular person or organization" (s 12(2)). Asserted.
8. **Officers who pass on what I give face up to 120 penalty units or 2 years** (s 19), or 20 penalty units or 12 months
   if heard summarily (s 20(3)); the person asked faces no imprisonment under ss 14 or 15. Asserted.
9. **Census forms are shielded from Agencies and courts for 99 years from the Census day** (s 19A), and go to the
   Archives only with consent on forms from 2001 on (s 8A). Homes, flats and rooms used for sleeping cannot be entered
   under s 18. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The compilation is of 1 October 2020; the events run to 2032. Later amendments need checking against the register.
- The Census and Statistics Regulation 2016, the Census-day proclamation and the s 10(2) instrument for each Census
  should be deposited, so that the Census topics, the classes of premises and the class of persons required can be
  worked out rather than asked.
- The Crimes Act 1914 (penalty unit, s 4K), the Criminal Code (ss 6.1, 13.3) and the Acts Interpretation Act 1901
  ("person") were not read.
- The readings of a defective direction (finding 6) and of when daily counting starts (finding 3) are inferences that
  case law might settle; none was searched.
