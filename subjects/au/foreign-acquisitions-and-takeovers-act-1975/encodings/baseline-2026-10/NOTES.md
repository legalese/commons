## Why this scope

The Act is long and mostly about entities, businesses, agricultural and commercial land and national security.
The seat and the one life event need only the individual home buyer: am I a foreign person, must I notify, am I exempt, can I buy an established dwelling, and what follows if I get it wrong.
Left out: limbs (b)-(g) of "foreign person"; entities, businesses, agribusiness, agricultural, commercial and mining land; national security actions; the tests for the exemption certificates themselves (ss 57-63); the other regulation exemptions (devolution, custodians, government vendors, timeshares, retirement villages, student accommodation); interim orders, extended decision periods and the s 133 pause; the Treasurer's orders and conditions (Part 3); fees (Part 6); vacancy fees (Part 6A); the Register of Foreign Ownership (Part 7A); the s 98 capital gain computation (an input).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A permanent resident is a foreign person if she has not been in Australia for 200 days in the last 12 months.** s 5(1) makes ordinary residence turn on 200 days' actual presence and on presence not limited in time. A permanent visa satisfies the second limb but not the first. Priya, living in Perth, is not a foreign person; a permanent resident who arrived 120 days ago is. Asserted.
2. **But that newly arrived permanent resident still need not notify.** Reg 38(2)(a) switches off the "excluded provisions" for an acquisition of residential land by the holder of a permanent visa. Reg 28 keeps the exempt buyer a foreign person, though: the exemption removes the duty to notify, not the status. Asserted.
3. **Joint tenants, not tenants in common.** Reg 38(3) exempts a foreign person who buys with a spouse or de facto partner who is a citizen or holds a permanent or special category visa, only if the interest is held "as joint tenants". A four-year temporary visa holder buying with a citizen spouse as joint tenants need not notify; the same purchase as tenants in common must be notified. Asserted.
4. **A citizen living abroad is a foreign person, but exempt.** s 5 defines ordinary residence only for non-citizens, so a citizen's is asked as a fact. A citizen not ordinarily resident is a foreign person under limb (a); reg 35(1)(a) exempts any acquisition of Australian land by such a citizen. Asserted.
5. **Residential land has no value threshold.** s 52(1) with reg 52(1)(a): any interest in residential land meets the threshold test, at any price, except land that is also non-vacant commercial land with a residential part under 10% (reg 52(2)). Asserted.
6. **Residential land means a dwelling, or room for fewer than 10.** s 4 and reg 19: vacant land on which fewer than 10 dwellings could reasonably be built is residential land; a site for 12 is not. A lease reasonably likely to run 5 years or less is not an interest in Australian land (s 12(1)(a)(i), (c)). Asserted.
7. **A developer's dwelling stays "new" for up to 12 months' occupation.** s 4 "new dwelling" (b)(ii): a dwelling in a development sold by the developer is new if not previously occupied for more than 12 months in total. At 14 months it is established. Asserted.
8. **The Act's established dwelling rules are a one-dwelling limit and a bar.** s 95(1): a foreign person who is a temporary resident must not hold more than one established dwelling (with a 6-month grace while genuinely selling, s 95(2)). s 95(4): any other foreign person must not acquire one. Both yield to an exemption certificate, no objection notification or notice imposing conditions (s 95(5)), and the regulation exemptions switch s 95 off too. Asserted.
9. **The announced two-year ban on foreign purchases of established dwellings (from 1 April 2025) is not in the deposited text.** Neither the Act (compilation of 5 December 2025) nor the Regulation (1 November 2025) contains the ban or its dates. A temporary resident may, on the text, hold one established dwelling after notice and approval; whether approval would be given is the Treasurer's decision and is not encoded. Not asserted (nothing to assert).
10. **After notice, the longest wait is 40 days.** s 77(8)(a) gives a 30-day decision period; s 82(2) bars the action until the earliest of 10 days after it ends, the end of an interim order, or a no objection notification. Extensions, interim orders, the s 133 pause and the s 114 fee rule are not encoded. Asserted.
11. **The civil penalty is half the price, or more.** s 94(4) and s 95(7): the greatest of double the capital gain, 50% of the consideration and 50% of the market value. On the d037 price that is $800,000; with a $500,000 gain it is $1,000,000. Asserted.
12. **Failing to notify a home purchase is also a crime.** s 90 keeps the general civil penalties off residential land, but nothing keeps the s 84 offence off it: imprisonment for 10 years, 15,000 penalty units, or both. Asserted.
13. **Consistent with the WA Duties Act baseline.** That encoding finds a permanent resident is never a "foreign individual" for WA foreign transfer duty (Duties Act s 205A), so d037 owes none. Under this Act the answer is reached differently: Priya is not a foreign person while ordinarily resident, and if she were (fewer than 200 days), reg 38(2)(a) and reg 38(3) would still spare her from notifying. Both encodings agree: no foreign duty, no notice. The tests are not the same, and a permanent resident living abroad would be a foreign person here but not a foreign individual there. Not asserted across encodings.
