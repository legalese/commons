# Taxation Administration Act 1953 — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
There is no REFUSE in it.
It was checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
**Not for public use as advice.**

Every question is a function named as the taxpayer would ask it (`Must tax be withheld from my payment`, `Am I liable to pay PAYG instalments`, `By when must I object`, `Am I liable as a director of the trustee`). Each is exported with `@export`, with a `@desc` on every input, so it can be deployed to Legalese Cloud.

This baseline replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers everything that encoding covered: TFNs, PAYG withholding and PAYG instalments. It keeps that encoding's hand-checked values where they still hold: the instalment quarter, due dates, payer kinds, 620 on 7,750 at 8%, and the withholder sizes. It adds five things:

- Part IVC objections and review;
- the Division 284, 286 and 298 penalties;
- the general interest charge;
- the no-ABN withholding amount from the Regulations;
- private ancillary funds (Subdivision 426-D).

Dates are now calendar dates rather than YYYYMMDD numbers.

## Seat

**The taxpayer.** The question is "What must I quote, have withheld and pay in advance, and how do I challenge it?" The taxpayer takes three forms here:

- **the employee being withheld from:** Chloe and Daniel;
- **the sole trader:** Jake, paid for supplies and liable to instalments;
- **the founder of a private ancillary fund:** Daniel, a director of its trustee company.

Most of Schedule 1 Part 2-5 is addressed to the payer. Its rules are encoded as what they mean for the person paid: whether tax comes out of my pay, how much, by when my payer must pay it over, what my payer owes if it does not, and what I must be given.

The 2026-10-18 defaults, and where this encoding departs from them:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** The Regulations are brought in (top rate, rounding, new declarations), and so are the two rates the "top rate" is made of. The withholding schedules and the PAF Guidelines are not deposited. The schedule amount is therefore an input. The Guidelines' rules are not encoded; they are not guessed.
- **The actors the text names:** you, your payer (an entity, or a medium, small or large withholder), the Commissioner, your registered tax agent, the ART and the Federal Court, the trustee, and its directors.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** The ones asked as facts are the Commissioner's choices: giving or withdrawing a rate, agreeing to a late objection, varying the withholding amount, and treating a late disclosure as early. So is an ART stay order. Remission (ss 298-20, 8AAG) is not computed; only the right to object to a refusal is.
- **The Act's own definitions are worked out from facts:**
  - withholder size;
  - the starting instalment quarter;
  - the base penalty amount;
  - private ancillary fund;
  - reviewable objection decision;
  - the general interest charge rate.

  Borrowed terms are asked as facts. These are the ITAA 1936 s 170(1) 2-year period of review, exempt income, constitutional corporation, notional tax, the monthly payer requirement, and the base interest rate.
- **One case.** One payment, one quarter, one objection, one statement, one late return, or one fund.
- **The Act's own terms, each glossed.** Each term is quoted in the comment above the rule that uses it.
- **Departure: provisions addressed to the payer.** Sections 16-30 and 16-75 are written as "What does my payer owe" and "By when must my payer pay over", because their effect on the person paid is the reason they are here.
- **Departure: the fund's trustee.** Section 426-125(8) gives the objection right to the trustee company, not the founder. Daniel's and his children's questions are asked from their seat as directors and founders. The trustee's objection deadline is asked as "my objection" (d091).

## Why this scope

The Act runs to four volumes, about 2 million characters. This baseline keeps to what the individual meets in these lives. That is:

- the TFN offences;
- PAYG withholding as it reaches the payee;
- PAYG instalments for a sole trader;
- objections and review;
- the two penalties an individual most often meets;
- the interest charge;
- the Act's own provisions on private ancillary funds.

**Left out:**

- **Withholding, beyond the payee's side:** Subdivisions 12-C to 12-J, Divisions 13 and 14, refunds (Subdivision 18-B), and part-year payment summaries.
- **Instalments, beyond the individual:** notional tax (the s 45-320 rate formula itself is encoded, from two inputs), GDP adjustment, benchmark tax, varying the rate, monthly payers, companies, trusts, partnerships and consolidated groups.
- **Objections:** amended assessments, private rulings and the special objection periods.
- **Penalties:** scheme penalties, the reasonably arguable position, significant global entities, the shortfall interest charge, and the other administrative penalties (Division 288).
- **Ancillary funds:** public ancillary funds, community charity trusts, and acting trustees.
- **Machinery:** collection and recovery (Part 4-15), ATO powers, secrecy, and the whistleblower provisions.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **There is no Part VA in this Act.** The scenarios cite "Part VA (tax file numbers)" of the Taxation Administration Act. The deposited compilation has none. Section 2 defines a tax file number by reference to "section 202A of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936", and issuing TFNs and TFN declarations are in that Act. This Act holds only s 8C(2) and the offences in ss 8WA to 8WC. Not asserted (a finding about the citation).
2. **Nothing makes a 15-year-old get or quote a TFN.** Under s 8C(2), not quoting a TFN is not, by itself, a failure to give information (k020). Under s 8WA, asking for a TFN is an offence (100 penalty units or 2 years) unless a taxation law provides for it or one of the other exceptions applies. A video store asking for one as identification commits the offence. Asserted.
3. **Wages must have tax withheld whatever the employee's age, and whether or not a TFN was quoted.** Section 12-35 asks neither question (c020, d012, c041). The amount comes from the withholding schedules (s 15-10), which must have regard to HELP repayments (s 15-30). The HECS line on Chloe's payslip therefore comes from the schedule, not from this Act. Asserted.
4. **A sole trader whose ABN is not quoted has 47% withheld.** Regulations s 38 sets the amount at "the top rate" times the payment. Section 5 defines the top rate as the top Rates Act rate (45%) plus the Medicare levy rate (2%). Both figures are rounded down to whole dollars (s 26). So $400 gives $188. With his ABN on the invoice, nothing is withheld; at $50 or less, nothing is withheld either (s 12-190(4)(b)). Asserted.
5. **Jake owes no instalments until the Commissioner gives him a rate** (ss 45-15(2), 45-50(4)). Nothing is due for a quarter that ended before the rate was given (s 45-125(2)(a)). Because he is not registered for GST, he may choose to pay annually if his notified notional tax is "less than $8,000" (s 45-140). Chloe, on wages alone, is never given a rate (Note 2 to s 45-15). Asserted.
6. **The four formulas lost from the text deposit say what the first pass inferred.** From the sidecar: a quarterly instalment is the applicable instalment rate × instalment income for the quarter (s 45-110(1)); an annual instalment on the Commissioner's rate is that rate × instalment income for the year (s 45-115(1)(a)); the Commissioner's rate is notional tax / base assessment instalment income × 100, to 2 places, and nil if either is nil (s 45-320(1)); the penalty is BPA + [BPA × (increase % − reduction %)] (s 284-85(2)), which is the inferred form rearranged. $7,750 at 8% gives $620; $31,000 at 8% gives $2,480 a year; notional tax of $2,500 on $31,000 gives 8.06%. Asserted (the nil rate is encoded, not asserted).
7. **When the employer pays over what it withheld depends on its size.** "Exceeded $25,000" makes a medium withholder, which pays by the 21st of the next month. A small withholder pays by the 21st of the month after the quarter. Deferred BAS payers get the 28th. Asserted.
8. **Single touch payroll displaces the annual payment summary.** Section 389-20 relieves the payer of s 16-155 "to the extent that it would relate to an amount that the entity has notified" under s 389-5. Chloe in 2024 is owed no payment summary if her pay was so reported. Asserted.
9. **Objection periods.**
   - An objection to an income tax assessment is due 2 years after notice if items 1 to 3A of ITAA 1936 s 170(1) apply, and 4 years otherwise.
   - An objection to a related Division 284 penalty takes the same longer period (s 14ZW(1BB)).
   - Anything else is due in 60 days.
   - The Commissioner has 60 days to decide. After that the taxpayer can give notice, and 60 days later the objection is taken to be disallowed (s 14ZYA).
   - Review in the ART, or an appeal to the Federal Court, must be sought within 60 days of service.
   - A pending review does not stop recovery (s 14ZZM), except under an ART stay for a small business assessment.

   Asserted.
10. **Penalties.**
    - **False or misleading statements:** 75%, 50% or 25% of the shortfall for intentional disregard, recklessness or carelessness. With no shortfall, 60, 40 or 20 penalty units instead. There is no penalty if reasonable care was taken, or under the registered-agent safe harbour (s 284-75(5), (6)).
    - **Increases and reductions:** obstruction or a repeat adds 20%. Telling the Commissioner before an examination cuts the base by 80%, or to nil for a shortfall under $1,000 (s 284-225).
    - **Late lodgement:** 1 penalty unit per 28 days or part, up to 5, multiplied by 2 or 5 for larger entities (s 286-80).
    - **Objecting to a refusal to remit:** possible only where more than 2 penalty units remain (s 298-20(3)).

    Asserted.
11. **The general interest charge compounds daily.** The yearly rate is the base interest rate plus 7 percentage points, divided by the days in the year (s 8AAD). The charge for each day falls on the unpaid amount plus the charge from previous days (s 8AAC(3)). So $620 unpaid for 30 days at a 3.95% base rate costs $5.60, against $5.58 at simple interest. Asserted.
12. **On private ancillary funds, the Act holds only the frame; the answers d072 and d091 seek are in the Guidelines.** Under s 426-105, a trust is a private ancillary fund if every trustee is a constitutional corporation that has agreed, in the approved form, to comply with the Guidelines and has not revoked that agreement. The 5% minimum distribution and the responsible person requirement the scenarios ask about are in the Guidelines (s 426-110), which are not deposited.

    What the Act itself adds for the founder:
    - Directors of the trustee company are jointly and severally liable for the guideline penalty for a false holding-out as a DGR, if it cannot reasonably be recovered from the company. A director has a defence if they were not and could not reasonably have been aware, took all reasonable steps, or could take none (s 426-120(5)).
    - The penalty "must not be reimbursed from the fund" (s 426-120(4)).
    - The Commissioner may suspend or remove all the trustees, and the trustee may object under Part IVC (s 426-125(8)).

    Asserted (fund status, director liability, the 60-day objection).
