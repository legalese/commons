# Fair Work Act 2009 — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent
test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

One module, `fwa-employee.l4`, holds every rule of the first pass; each question is a function named as the
employee would ask it and is published with `@export` and `@desc` on every input. One case
file, `fwa-cases-employee.l4`, is generated by a script (scratchpad
`gen_fair-work-act-2009_baseline.py`) whose expected values were written by hand from the
text.

**Day one (22 October 2026, ledger REQ-0058).** A second module, `fwa-day-one.l4`, adds what
a new employee and their employer meet on the first day: the NES as a floor that a contract
term cannot displace (ss 44, 60, 61), the three information statements (ss 125, 125B, 333K),
employee records (s 535 with regs 3.31 to 3.40 and 3.42) and the reversed burden where they
were not kept (s 557C). It asks the employee's questions and IMPORTs `fwa-employee.l4` only
for the s 23 small business rule. An employer seat layer, `fwa-seat-employer.l4`, IMPORTs
both modules and asks the employer's questions, restating no rule (CLAUDE.md, rulings of
2026-10-21). Each has its own case file, `fwa-cases-day-one.l4` and `fwa-cases-employer.l4`,
written by hand (no generator); the employer's includes mirror cases. `fwa-employee.l4` and
`fwa-cases-employee.l4` are unchanged.

**General protections (7 November 2026).** A third employee module, `fwa-general-protections.l4`,
adds what a dismissed employee needs to choose a route: adverse action and workplace rights
(ss 340-342), discrimination (s 351), temporary absence for illness or injury (s 352 with reg
3.01), multiple reasons and the presumed reason (ss 360, 361), the dismissal dispute at the FWC and
in court (ss 365, 366, 368(3), 369(1), 370), costs (ss 570, 611), and the rules that let only one
dismissal claim run at a time (ss 723, 725-733). It IMPORTs nothing from the other modules and
restates none of their rules. Its case file, `fwa-cases-general-protections.l4`, written by hand,
IMPORTs it with `fwa-employee.l4`, so it also shows that the two define no name twice. The facts
are the first matter of the workbench route chooser (Lena Marsh, a fictional Victorian
warehouse coordinator dismissed after disclosing multiple sclerosis), not a cradle-to-grave life.
No ledger entry asked for it; it was asked for directly.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers everything that
one covered and reuses its hand-checked values where they are still right (the k086 notice
and redundancy figures, the 45-year-old pair, the jury service sum, the leave accruals).

## Seats

**The dismissed employee** (`fwa-general-protections.l4`, 7 November 2026) is the employee's
seat again, at the moment of choosing a route: "Was my dismissal unlawful adverse action? Am I
in time? What do I risk in costs? Which of my claims does this one shut?" Every judgment the
text hands to the FWC or a court ("if the FWC is satisfied ...", "if my employer proves ...",
"if ... was among the reasons") is an input. Questions are grouped under three situations,
marked in the `§§` headings where they arise only on dismissal. No employer seat is written
for this module.

**The employee** — and, for Jake's platform years, **the employee-like worker** (s 15P), a
regulated worker under Part 3A-2. The question is the plan's: "What must my employer give
me and pay me, and what can I claim?" Every life event that cites this Act is asked by the
person working, not by the employer, the Fair Work Commission or the Ombudsman. Daniel's
events as an employer (d044, d066) are answered from the seat of his employees, and d044's
day-one questions also from his own seat (below).

**The employer (second seat, `fwa-seat-employer.l4`, 22 October 2026).** A small business
owner or a company taking on employees: "What must I give a new employee, or a new casual,
on day one? Can the contract give them less than the NES? What records must I keep, and for
how long?" Every name begins "As the employer, ", every input is a plain fact the employer
can state with an `@desc` worded from that seat, and every question calls a rule in
`fwa-day-one.l4` or `fwa-employee.l4`. Questions exported:

- As the employer, can the employment contract give this employee less than the NES (ss 44, 60, 61)?
- As the employer, would I contravene the NES (s 44)?
- As the employer, which information statements must I give this new employee (ss 125, 125B(1)(a), 333K)?
- As the employer, must I give this employee the Fair Work Information Statement (s 125)?
- As the employer, must I give this casual the Casual Employment Information Statement at this point (s 125B)?
- As the employer, what employee records must I make and keep (s 535, regs 3.32-3.40)?
- As the employer, for how many years must I keep each employee record (s 535(1))?
- As the employer, may I stop keeping a record I made this many years ago?
- As the employer, is this record in the form the Regulations require (reg 3.31(1))?
- As the employer, how quickly must I let an employee inspect and copy their record (reg 3.42)?
- As the employer, is the pay slip I gave in time (s 536(1))?
- As the employer, if I did not keep the record, must I disprove what the employee alleges (s 557C)?

The employee's day-one questions, in `fwa-day-one.l4`: What are the National Employment
Standards (s 61(2))? Can my employment contract give me less than the NES? Has my employer
contravened the NES (s 44)? Must my employer give me the Fair Work Information Statement?
Has my employer contravened the NES by not giving it? Must my employer give me the Casual
Employment Information Statement at this point? Must my employer give me the Fixed Term
Contract Information Statement? Which information statements must I be given when I start?
What records must my employer keep about me? For how many years? How quickly must I be
shown them? If my employer kept no records, must it disprove what I allege?

Not answered from the employer's seat: which award covers the employees (not deposited, and
not stated in the life), the minimum wage, whether a worker is a casual (s 15A, a fact),
penalties, and every question about ending employment (asked so far only from the
employee's seat).

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The deposited
  Regulations supply the flexible-leave days, the threshold floors and indexation, the pay
  slip contents and the small claims procedure. The awards and the minimum wage order are
  not deposited, so their rates are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: employer, employee, employee-like worker,
  digital labour platform operator, the FWC, the court, Centrelink.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** Followed. Inputs named
  "if ..." carry them: "if the FWC is satisfied it was harsh, unjust or unreasonable",
  "if the FWC is satisfied the term is appropriate", "if it has reasonable business
  grounds", "if redeploying me would have been reasonable", "if the FWC allows a further
  period".
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed for vocational
  placement, junior employee, employee-like worker, small business employer, genuine
  redundancy, minimum employment period, dismissed, and transfer of employment. Asked as
  facts:
  - the ordinary meaning of employee (s 15AA);
  - "associated entities";
  - whether the superannuation guarantee charge would apply (another Act);
  - whether WA law declares a public holiday.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one employment, or one termination.
- **The Act's own terms, glossed.** Followed in every `@desc`.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. The FWC, the Ombudsman, the courts'
  constitution and regulation-making powers are out.

**Departures.** Two, both small:

- **Service is asked in months or years, except for parental leave.** For parental leave
  the s 67(3) date rule needs real dates, so start dates and the expected birth are asked.
- **Long service leave is left out**, although c056 and c072 mention it. s 113 gives NES
  long service leave only through pre-2010 award terms. Otherwise it leaves the matter to
  State law: the WA Long Service Leave Act 1958.

## Why this scope

The Fair Work Act runs to over 800 sections and four volumes. This encoding takes what an
employee meets in the WA lives:

- who counts as an employee;
- the National Employment Standards those events touch;
- notice and redundancy pay;
- unfair dismissal eligibility, outcome, deadline and compensation cap;
- pay slips;
- underpayment claims;
- for the platform rider, the employee-like worker test, what a minimum standards order may
  contain, and unfair deactivation;
- day one (`fwa-day-one.l4`, with the employer's seat): the NES as a floor (ss 44, 60, 61),
  the Fair Work, Casual Employment and Fixed Term Contract Information Statements (ss 125,
  125B, 333K), employee records (s 535, regs 3.31 to 3.40, 3.42), and s 557C.

Left out:

- enterprise agreements and bargaining;
- industrial action;
- the general protections other than those a dismissed employee meets (in
  `fwa-general-protections.l4`: ss 340-342 item 1, 351, 352, 360, 361, 365-370, 570, 611, 723,
  725-733); out are industrial activity, coercion, misrepresentation and sham contracting (ss
  343-350, 353-359), prospective employees, contractors and platform workers (s 342 items 2-7),
  non-dismissal disputes (s 372), the remedies and penalties (ss 539, 545), and unlawful
  termination itself (Part 6-4);
- right of entry;
- casual conversion;
- long service leave (State law here);
- the road transport provisions;
- civil penalty amounts;
- s 120, s 121(4) and s 122;
- ss 55 and 56 (what an award or agreement may say about the NES), the s 15A casual test (a
  fact), the s 333E limits on fixed term contracts, the contents of the statements (ss 124,
  125A, 333J, reg 2.01), and regs 3.41 (transfer of business), 3.43 and 3.44;
- the institutions themselves.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The Act sets no notice for an employee who resigns.** s 117 binds only an employer
   that terminates. s 118 lets an award or agreement set the notice an employee must give.
   Chloe (c072), Daniel (d042) and Liam (c056) owe nothing under the Act, and are owed
   nothing either. Asserted.
2. **Redundancy pay falls at ten years.** The s 119(2) scale peaks at 16 weeks for 9 to 10
   years, then drops to 12 for "At least 10 years". Jake (k086, 21 years) gets 12 weeks and
   5 weeks' notice, as the scenario expects. A Jarrah employee with 9.5 years (d066) gets
   16. Asserted.
3. **"Over 45 years old" is read as older than 45.** At 45 exactly there is no extra week;
   at 45½ there is. This reading is an inference. Asserted.
4. **A small business employer owes no redundancy pay and has a one-year qualifying
   period.** s 23(1) sets the line at "fewer than 15 employees", and s 121(1)(b) and
   s 383(b) do the rest. Daniel's Jarrah (d044) owes none. Asserted.
5. **The Centrelink notice starts at fifteen.** s 530(1) applies only where the employer
   dismisses "15 or more employees". d066's twelve need none. Asserted.
6. **The high income threshold can never fall below $100,000.** Reg 2.13 starts from
   "$100 000", and every indexation step lifts a ratio below 1 to 1. Anyone earning under
   $100,000 (Chloe at $70,000, c040) is therefore under the threshold without knowing this
   year's figure. The encoding lifts an entered threshold to that floor. Asserted.
7. **The contractor high income threshold can never fall below $175,000.** Reg 1.08AA(2)
   sets $175,000 for 2024-25. Indexation cannot reduce it, and s 15C(2) forbids a
   regulation that would. Jake's unfair deactivation claim (k040) needs no current figure.
   Asserted.
8. **The small claims cap is $100,000.** s 548(2)(b) allows a higher prescribed amount, but
   the deposited Regulations prescribe none. Reg 4.01 deals only with how to choose the
   procedure: endorse the papers, or lodge a paper. Jake's $4,100 (k062) fits. Asserted.
9. **Flexible unpaid parental leave is 130 days, not 100.** s 72A(1) says 100 days "or ...
   that higher number of days" prescribed. Reg 2.00(c) prescribes 130 for a birth on or
   after 1 July 2026, so Chloe and Liam's child (2030) carries 130 flexible days. These come
   out of the 12 months. Asserted (130 for 2030; 100 for a birth before 1 July 2024).
10. **There is no s 72, and no limit on concurrent leave.** c063 cites "s 72 (concurrent
    leave)". The deposit has no s 72 in Part 2-2. The transitional provisions treat old
    "concurrent leave" as flexible unpaid parental leave. Nothing in the deposited Act
    stops Liam taking leave while Chloe is on leave (a reading). Asserted.
11. **Liam qualifies for parental leave by three days.** Two inferences make this work:
    - he started at Sparkies on 2 September 2029 (c056);
    - the baby was born on 5 September 2030 (c063).

    His leave starts at the birth, so the s 67(3) date is the day it starts, and he has 12
    months and 3 days. A start a month later would fail. Chloe's leave starts before the
    birth, so her date is the expected date of birth, inferred as 5 September 2030. Asserted.
12. **c061 cites s 83 for the return to work guarantee; it is s 84.** s 83 is consultation
    while on leave. s 84 is encoded (`What job do I come back to`) but not asserted.
13. **A paid intern is not on a vocational placement.** s 12 requires a placement for which
    the person "is not entitled to be paid any remuneration". Chloe at $25 an hour (c032)
    fails that limb. Whether she is an employee is then the s 15AA ordinary-meaning
    question, an input. Asserted.
14. **A minimum standards order may include superannuation.** s 536KL(1) does not list it,
    but s 536KL(2) makes the list non-limiting. s 536KM and s 536KMA do not bar it, though
    they bar overtime rates, rostering, and deeming the worker an employee. Penalty rates
    and minimum engagement are allowed only if the FWC is satisfied. The answer to k041 is
    a reading. Asserted.
15. **Leave figures are per year and accrue progressively; casuals get none.** c042 expects
    4 weeks and 10 days, the yearly figures. After three months Chloe has accrued 1 week
    (assuming constant hours, an inference). Compassionate leave is 2 days per occasion.
    Family and domestic violence leave is 10 days a year, and s 106A(2)(c) makes it
    available in full to casuals. Asserted.
16. **Jury service: base pay less jury pay, for ten days only.** s 111(5) limits the
    employer's payment to the first 10 days. If evidence is required and not given,
    nothing is owed. Asserted with hypothetical figures.
17. **"Probation" is not in the Act.** c040's question about probation is answered by the
    minimum employment period: 6 months, or one year for a small business employer. The
    s 382(b) test also needs an award, an agreement, or earnings under the threshold.
    Asserted.
18. **Unfair dismissal compensation is capped twice.** s 392(5) caps it at the lesser of
    26 weeks' remuneration and half the high income threshold. Asserted with hypothetical
    figures. The application must be made within 21 days (s 394(2)). Asserted.
19. **Service does not follow the employee to a new employer.** s 22(5) carries it over
    only on a transfer of employment: re-employed within 3 months by an associated entity,
    or as part of a transfer of business. Liam's move (c056) starts from nothing. Asserted.
20. **Underpayments: six years.** s 544 sets the time limit for applications. s 545(5) bars
    orders for underpaid periods more than 6 years before the proceedings began. Asserted.

21. **The Act prescribes no contents for an employment contract.** c040 asks "what must a
    contract contain". No section of the deposited Act requires a written contract or lists
    its terms. What the Act requires on day one is a statement: the Fair Work Information
    Statement for everyone (s 125), the Casual Employment Information Statement for a casual
    (s 125B(1)(a)), and the Fixed Term Contract Information Statement for a contract with an
    end date (s 333K). Chloe, full-time and ongoing, is owed the first only. Asserted.
22. **A contract term cannot take an NES away, but the Act never says so of contracts in
    terms.** ss 55 and 56 deal only with awards and enterprise agreements. For a contract the
    answer comes from s 61(1) (the NES "cannot be displaced") and s 44 (an employer "must not
    contravene" them). A term giving more operates; so does an agreement an NES provision
    itself allows (s 64 averaging, s 94 cashing out), which is asked as a fact. Asserted.
23. **Probation does not cut the NES.** s 123 excludes no employee on probation. Chloe,
    dismissed in her first month, would still be owed 1 week's notice (s 117(3)). Probation
    matters only through the unfair dismissal minimum employment period (finding 17).
    Asserted.
24. **Not giving the Fair Work Information Statement is itself an NES contravention.**
    s 61(2)(j) puts Division 12 among the NES, so a failure under s 125 contravenes s 44
    once "as soon as practicable" has passed (asked conditionally). Asserted.
25. **s 61(2)(j) names only the Fair Work Information Statement, but s 61(3) makes all of
    Division 12 part of the NES**, and Division 12 now also holds ss 125A and 125B. The
    Casual Employment Information Statement is therefore an NES duty too (a reading; the
    matter is labelled for both statements in `What are the National Employment
    Standards`).
26. **A small business employer gives a casual the Casual Employment Information Statement
    only at the start and at 12 months.** s 125B(2) removes (1)(b) (6 months) and (1)(c)(ii)
    (each later 12 months) while the employer is a small business employer, but not
    (1)(c)(i). Jarrah, with 3 employees (d044), owes a casual the statement on day one and
    at 12 months; at 15 employees it would owe all four. Asserted (the casual is
    hypothetical: the life does not say).
27. **"Not more than once in any 12 months" is read as a re-employment rule.** s 125(2) and
    s 125B(3) are applied only at a new start, as their notes say ("relevant if the employer
    employs the employee more than once in the 12 months"). How s 125B(3)'s "apart from the
    operation of paragraph (1)(b)" bears on a 6-month statement followed by a 12-month one is
    not encoded. A reading.
28. **The Fixed Term Contract Information Statement is a third day-one statement, outside
    the NES.** s 333K is in Part 2-9, a civil remedy provision of its own, and not in
    REQ-0058's list. It is encoded because it falls due at the same moment. Asserted with a
    hypothetical contract.
29. **Records: 7 years, two kinds always, ten more by circumstance.** s 535(1) and regs 3.32
    and 3.33(1) apply to every employee; regs 3.33(2) to 3.40 add hours, loadings, overtime,
    averaging, leave, cashing out, superannuation, flexibility arrangements, guarantees of
    annual earnings and termination as they arise. The Act does not say when the 7 years
    start; they are read as running from when the record is made (reg 3.41(6) points the same
    way). Chloe's records (c040) are four kinds; a casual of Daniel's on an hourly rate with a
    casual loading (d044, hypothetical) six. Asserted.
30. **Two slips in the deposited Regulations.** Reg 3.44 begins at subregulation (2): there is
    no (1) in Compilation No. 57. Reg 3.42(4) ends its opening words with a full stop where
    paragraphs (a) and (b) follow; it is read as a colon. Neither changes an answer.
31. **No records, reversed burden.** s 557C puts on the employer the burden of disproving an
    allegation about a matter on which it failed to keep a record or give a pay slip, in
    proceedings under the provisions s 557C(3) lists (the NES, awards, agreements, the
    minimum wage, payment), unless the court accepts a reasonable excuse. Asserted.
32. **What the life leaves open for d044.** The life does not say whether any of Jarrah's
    first three employees is a casual, part-time or on a fixed term, nor which award (if any)
    covers them. The cases asked on those facts are marked HYPOTHETICAL; the award is for the
    scenario's author (REQ-0058's note). If Daniel is himself an employee of his company,
    Jarrah's s 23 count is 4 rather than 3: under 15 either way.

### The general protections, for a dismissed employee (`fwa-general-protections.l4`)

33. **Only one dismissal claim at a time, across Commonwealth and State law.** s 725 bars a
    claim of any kind in ss 726-732 while a claim of another of those kinds "applies", and s 732
    reaches "an application or complaint under ... a law of the Commonwealth (other than this
    Act) ... or a law of a State or Territory". So a complaint to the Australian Human Rights
    Commission, or to a State equal opportunity body, about the dismissal shuts both the unfair
    dismissal and the general protections routes, and either of those shuts the complaint,
    until it is withdrawn or fails for want of jurisdiction. Lena's AHRC complaint about her
    dismissal leaves only that complaint open. Asserted.
34. **A complaint about earlier conduct does not count until it covers the dismissal.** s 732(3)
    dates an AHRC Act complaint that "relates to a dismissal only as a result of an amendment"
    from the amendment. Lena's complaint about her shifts alone bars nothing. A reading: s 732(3)
    names only AHRC Act complaints; a State complaint is asked the same way, through the input
    "it is about the dismissal". Asserted.
35. **The certificate opens the court; agreeing to arbitration shuts it.** s 727(1)(b)(iii)
    stops the general protections FWC application counting once the FWC certifies that the
    dispute could not be resolved, so the court application (s 728) is then open, within 14
    days (s 370(a)(ii)). If the parties instead notify agreement to arbitration, s 727(1A) makes
    it count again and the court is shut (note 1 to s 370). Asserted.
36. **Losing an unfair dismissal claim on genuine redundancy reopens the other routes**
    (s 729(1)(b)(iii)); losing on any other ground on the merits does not. Asserted.
37. **A second claim of the same kind is not reached by s 725.** It bars a claim "if any
    other of those sections applies". Fork line: s 725, `May I make this dismissal claim`,
    answer taken TRUE for a second claim of the same kind; the other reading is that the forum's
    own rules (or abuse of process) bar it, which this encoding does not decide. Asserted.
38. **Both Fair Work dismissal routes close on day 22.** ss 366(1) and 394(2) each allow 21 days
    from when the dismissal took effect. Lena, first advised on day 25, needs "exceptional
    circumstances" for either. The two extension tests differ: s 394(3)(b) lists whether the
    person "first became aware of the dismissal after it had taken effect"; s 366(2) does not.
    Both are asked as one input each. Asserted (the time limits; not the difference).
39. **Each side pays its own costs at the FWC, and nearly always in court.** s 611 lets the
    FWC order costs only for an application made vexatiously or without reasonable cause, or one
    that should reasonably have been seen to have no prospect. s 570 lets a court order costs in
    a Fair Work Act matter only for vexatious or unreasonable conduct, or an unreasonable refusal
    to take part at the FWC on the same facts. That is the cost-risk contrast with a
    discrimination claim taken from the AHRC to court, where costs are at the court's ordinary
    discretion (not encoded here). Asserted for ss 570 and 611.
40. **s 351 turns on other Acts.** s 351(2)(a) disapplies the protection for action "not
    unlawful under any anti-discrimination law in force in the place where the action is taken",
    and s 351(3) lists them: the four Commonwealth Acts and one Act for each State and Territory
    (for Victoria, the Equal Opportunity Act 2010). Whether the dismissal is unlawful under one
    of them is asked as a fact. The Disability Discrimination Act 1992 is deposited and not
    encoded (REQ-0115); the Victorian Act is not deposited (REQ-0116). Asserted, with the input
    set both ways.
41. **The s 361 presumption does not help an interim injunction.** s 361(2). The reason alleged
    is otherwise presumed unless the employer proves it was not among its reasons (s 360).
    Asserted.
42. **Illness: the protection runs out at three months unless the time is on paid personal
    leave, and workers' compensation is not paid personal leave.** Reg 3.01(5) and (6). The
    second rule is folded into the input's `@desc` ("not counting time on workers'
    compensation"). Asserted (the three months; (6) only through the input).
43. **Negatives.** The module keeps the baseline's plain BOOLEAN. Every FALSE it gives is a no
    the text states in its own words: periods it fixes (ss 366, 370, 369(1)(c)), "only"
    (ss 570, 611), "must not ... unless" and "must not ... if" (ss 370, 723, 725), conditions
    introduced by "if" (s 342 table, s 369(1), reg 3.01), and the exceptions of s 351(2) and
    reg 3.01(5). Fork line: s 351(1), `Protected attribute`; the attribute list has no "only",
    so an attribute outside it would be NOTHING, not FALSE; the enum admits only listed
    attributes, so no answer is given for one outside it.

A drafting note, not a finding about the law: in this `l4`, `(NOT a AND b)` on one line
parsed as `NOT (a AND b)`. Every negation in the module is now bracketed.
