# Fair Work Act 2009 — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent
test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

One module, `fwa-employee.l4`, holds every rule; each question is a function named as the
employee would ask it and is published with `@export` and `@desc` on every input. One case
file, `fwa-cases-employee.l4`, is generated by a script (scratchpad
`gen_fair-work-act-2009_baseline.py`) whose expected values were written by hand from the
text.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers everything that
one covered and reuses its hand-checked values where they are still right (the k086 notice
and redundancy figures, the 45-year-old pair, the jury service sum, the leave accruals).

## Seat

**The employee** — and, for Jake's platform years, **the employee-like worker** (s 15P), a
regulated worker under Part 3A-2. The question is the plan's: "What must my employer give
me and pay me, and what can I claim?" Every life event that cites this Act is asked by the
person working, not by the employer, the Fair Work Commission or the Ombudsman. Daniel's
events as an employer (d044, d066) are answered from the seat of his employees.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The deposited
  Regulations supply the flexible-leave days, the threshold floors and indexation, the pay
  slip contents and the small claims procedure. The awards and the minimum wage order are
  not deposited, so their rates are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: employer, employee, employee-like worker,
  digital labour platform operator, the FWC, the court, Centrelink.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** Followed. Inputs named
  "if ..." carry them: "if the FWC is satisfied it was harsh, unjust or unreasonable",
  "if the FWC is satisfied the term is appropriate", "if it has reasonable business
  grounds", "if redeploying me would have been reasonable", "if the FWC allows a further
  period".
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed for vocational
  placement, junior employee, employee-like worker, small business employer, genuine
  redundancy, minimum employment period, dismissed, and transfer of employment. Asked as
  facts:
  - the ordinary meaning of employee (s 15AA);
  - "associated entities";
  - whether the superannuation guarantee charge would apply (another Act);
  - whether WA law declares a public holiday.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one employment, or one termination.
- **The Act's own terms, glossed.** Followed in every `@desc`.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. The FWC, the Ombudsman, the courts'
  constitution and regulation-making powers are out.

**Departures.** Two, both small:

- **Service is asked in months or years, except for parental leave.** For parental leave
  the s 67(3) date rule needs real dates, so start dates and the expected birth are asked.
- **Long service leave is left out**, although c056 and c072 mention it. s 113 gives NES
  long service leave only through pre-2010 award terms. Otherwise it leaves the matter to
  State law: the WA Long Service Leave Act 1958.

## Why this scope

The Fair Work Act runs to over 800 sections and four volumes. This encoding takes what an
employee meets in the WA lives:

- who counts as an employee;
- the National Employment Standards those events touch;
- notice and redundancy pay;
- unfair dismissal eligibility, outcome, deadline and compensation cap;
- pay slips;
- underpayment claims;
- for the platform rider, the employee-like worker test, what a minimum standards order may
  contain, and unfair deactivation.

Left out:

- enterprise agreements and bargaining;
- industrial action;
- the general protections;
- right of entry;
- casual conversion;
- long service leave (State law here);
- the road transport provisions;
- civil penalty amounts;
- s 120, s 121(4) and s 122;
- the institutions themselves.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The Act sets no notice for an employee who resigns.** s 117 binds only an employer
   that terminates. s 118 lets an award or agreement set the notice an employee must give.
   Chloe (c072), Daniel (d042) and Liam (c056) owe nothing under the Act, and are owed
   nothing either. Asserted.
2. **Redundancy pay falls at ten years.** The s 119(2) scale peaks at 16 weeks for 9 to 10
   years, then drops to 12 for "At least 10 years". Jake (k086, 21 years) gets 12 weeks and
   5 weeks' notice, as the scenario expects. A Jarrah employee with 9.5 years (d066) gets
   16. Asserted.
3. **"Over 45 years old" is read as older than 45.** At 45 exactly there is no extra week;
   at 45½ there is. This reading is an inference. Asserted.
4. **A small business employer owes no redundancy pay and has a one-year qualifying
   period.** s 23(1) sets the line at "fewer than 15 employees", and s 121(1)(b) and
   s 383(b) do the rest. Daniel's Jarrah (d044) owes none. Asserted.
5. **The Centrelink notice starts at fifteen.** s 530(1) applies only where the employer
   dismisses "15 or more employees". d066's twelve need none. Asserted.
6. **The high income threshold can never fall below $100,000.** Reg 2.13 starts from
   "$100 000", and every indexation step lifts a ratio below 1 to 1. Anyone earning under
   $100,000 (Chloe at $70,000, c040) is therefore under the threshold without knowing this
   year's figure. The encoding lifts an entered threshold to that floor. Asserted.
7. **The contractor high income threshold can never fall below $175,000.** Reg 1.08AA(2)
   sets $175,000 for 2024-25. Indexation cannot reduce it, and s 15C(2) forbids a
   regulation that would. Jake's unfair deactivation claim (k040) needs no current figure.
   Asserted.
8. **The small claims cap is $100,000.** s 548(2)(b) allows a higher prescribed amount, but
   the deposited Regulations prescribe none. Reg 4.01 deals only with how to choose the
   procedure: endorse the papers, or lodge a paper. Jake's $4,100 (k062) fits. Asserted.
9. **Flexible unpaid parental leave is 130 days, not 100.** s 72A(1) says 100 days "or ...
   that higher number of days" prescribed. Reg 2.00(c) prescribes 130 for a birth on or
   after 1 July 2026, so Chloe and Liam's child (2030) carries 130 flexible days. These come
   out of the 12 months. Asserted (130 for 2030; 100 for a birth before 1 July 2024).
10. **There is no s 72, and no limit on concurrent leave.** c063 cites "s 72 (concurrent
    leave)". The deposit has no s 72 in Part 2-2. The transitional provisions treat old
    "concurrent leave" as flexible unpaid parental leave. Nothing in the deposited Act
    stops Liam taking leave while Chloe is on leave (a reading). Asserted.
11. **Liam qualifies for parental leave by three days.** Two inferences make this work:
    - he started at Sparkies on 2 September 2029 (c056);
    - the baby was born on 5 September 2030 (c063).

    His leave starts at the birth, so the s 67(3) date is the day it starts, and he has 12
    months and 3 days. A start a month later would fail. Chloe's leave starts before the
    birth, so her date is the expected date of birth, inferred as 5 September 2030. Asserted.
12. **c061 cites s 83 for the return to work guarantee; it is s 84.** s 83 is consultation
    while on leave. s 84 is encoded (`What job do I come back to`) but not asserted.
13. **A paid intern is not on a vocational placement.** s 12 requires a placement for which
    the person "is not entitled to be paid any remuneration". Chloe at $25 an hour (c032)
    fails that limb. Whether she is an employee is then the s 15AA ordinary-meaning
    question, an input. Asserted.
14. **A minimum standards order may include superannuation.** s 536KL(1) does not list it,
    but s 536KL(2) makes the list non-limiting. s 536KM and s 536KMA do not bar it, though
    they bar overtime rates, rostering, and deeming the worker an employee. Penalty rates
    and minimum engagement are allowed only if the FWC is satisfied. The answer to k041 is
    a reading. Asserted.
15. **Leave figures are per year and accrue progressively; casuals get none.** c042 expects
    4 weeks and 10 days, the yearly figures. After three months Chloe has accrued 1 week
    (assuming constant hours, an inference). Compassionate leave is 2 days per occasion.
    Family and domestic violence leave is 10 days a year, and s 106A(2)(c) makes it
    available in full to casuals. Asserted.
16. **Jury service: base pay less jury pay, for ten days only.** s 111(5) limits the
    employer's payment to the first 10 days. If evidence is required and not given,
    nothing is owed. Asserted with hypothetical figures.
17. **"Probation" is not in the Act.** c040's question about probation is answered by the
    minimum employment period: 6 months, or one year for a small business employer. The
    s 382(b) test also needs an award, an agreement, or earnings under the threshold.
    Asserted.
18. **Unfair dismissal compensation is capped twice.** s 392(5) caps it at the lesser of
    26 weeks' remuneration and half the high income threshold. Asserted with hypothetical
    figures. The application must be made within 21 days (s 394(2)). Asserted.
19. **Service does not follow the employee to a new employer.** s 22(5) carries it over
    only on a transfer of employment: re-employed within 3 months by an associated entity,
    or as part of a transfer of business. Liam's move (c056) starts from nothing. Asserted.
20. **Underpayments: six years.** s 544 sets the time limit for applications. s 545(5) bars
    orders for underpaid periods more than 6 years before the proceedings began. Asserted.

A drafting note, not a finding about the law: in this `l4`, `(NOT a AND b)` on one line
parsed as `NOT (a AND b)`. Every negation in the module is now bracketed.

  rates live there.

  unpaid leave for redundancy pay but not for notice.

  4A), the general protections, and the reg 3.46(5A) exception for a new employee's fund.
- Take long service leave from the WA Long Service Leave Act 1958 for c056 and c072.
- Ask the scenario authors to correct c063 (s 72) and c061 (s 83 → s 84), and to treat the
  resignation notice questions (c056, c072, d042) as contract or award matters.
- No case law, FWC decision or Fair Work Ombudsman guidance was retrieved. The s 15AA test,
  "harsh, unjust or unreasonable", and "reasonable business grounds" are judgments of the
  FWC and the courts that this encoding only asks about.
