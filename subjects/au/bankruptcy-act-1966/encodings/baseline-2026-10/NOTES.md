# Bankruptcy Act 1966 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the
individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage
table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** Bankruptcy Act 1966, Compilation No. 97, compilation date 21 May 2026 (includes amendments up to Act No. 47,
2026), Federal Register of Legislation register id C2026C00215, deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/C1966A00033.txt`.
With the Bankruptcy Regulations 2021, Compilation No. 5, compilation date 1 July 2026 (includes amendments F2026L00133),
register id F2026C00537, deposited at
`../../../bankruptcy-regulations-2021/registers/source-bundle/F2021L00261.txt`: s 10A (statutory minimum), s 36 (income of a
dependant), ss 82 and 83 (removal from the National Personal Insolvency Index). The Regulations' own licence and attribution
are in `../../../bankruptcy-regulations-2021/SOURCE-LICENSE.md`.

**Checks:** two modules (`bda-debt-agreements.l4`, `bda-bankruptcy.l4`) and two case files
(`bda-cases-debt-agreements.l4`, 55 assertions; `bda-cases-bankruptcy.l4`, 15 assertions): 70 assertions satisfied, 0
errors, 0 warnings, with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. Every decision a debtor would ask is marked `@export`,
with `@desc` on each input; internal helpers (date keys, the vote tallies) are not exported. The case files are generated by
the scratchpad script `gen_bankruptcy-act-1966_baseline.py`; expected values were written by hand.

This encoding replaces the first-pass `naive-2026-10` encoding of Part IX. It covers everything that one covered, reuses its
hand-checked values (the vote split, the $690 arithmetic, the arrears thresholds), and adds the Official Receiver's gate as a
conditional answer, the creditor's petition (ss 40, 44 with Regulations s 10A), the Index retention rules (Regulations ss 82,
83), the 10-year bar's running, and what bankruptcy would mean instead (ss 139K, 139P, 149, 149A, 153).

## Seat

**The debtor**: "May I propose a debt agreement instead of bankruptcy; what does it bind me to, and when am I released?"
Part IX is addressed largely to the Official Receiver, the administrator and creditors, but the person it is applied to is the
debtor, and every decision here is phrased as the debtor would ask it (`May I give a debt agreement proposal`, `What may the
Official Receiver do with my proposal`, `May a creditor petition to make me bankrupt`, `Am I released from the debt`, `From
when must my agreement come off the Index`).

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The Bankruptcy Regulations 2021 are deposited and
  used for the statutory minimum ($10,000), the dependant income limit ($3,741 for 2020-21) and Index retention. The debt
  agreement thresholds are not in the Regulations: the threshold amount is 7 times a Social Security Act 1991 rate, which is
  indexed, so it is an input (finding 7). The Minister's s 185C(4B) instrument is not deposited; whether its percentage is
  exceeded is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the debtor, the Official Receiver, the proposed administrator and administrator,
  affected creditors, the trustee (for bankruptcy), the Court only where it alters a result (s 153(2A)).
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally.** Followed. The Official Receiver's satisfaction that the
  statement of affairs is in order, its view of the creditors' interests, its belief about undue hardship (s 185E), and its
  satisfaction that a 6-month default occurred (s 185QA) are inputs; the answers say what follows if they are so. The
  trustee's objection to discharge (s 149A) is asked as a fact.
- **The Act's definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed for "threshold amount", "unsecured debt", "frozen
  debt", "applicable deadline", "statutory minimum", "3-month arrears default", "designated 6-month arrears default",
  "actual income threshold amount" and "dependant". Departure: "insolvent" (s 5(2)-(3)) is the Act's own term but turns on
  every debt and its due date, so it is asked as a fact; "after tax income" is an Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 figure and is
  asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One debtor and one proposal or agreement.
- **The Act's own terms, each glossed.** Followed in the comments and `@desc` texts.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed: administrators' registration, trust accounts, fees, forms and the
  Index's inspection rules are out.

## Why this scope

The three events ask who may propose, who decides, what the agreement does to the debts, how long it runs and what is left
afterwards; the seat adds the debtor's natural follow-ups: what if I fall behind, can I be made bankrupt meanwhile, and what
would bankruptcy have meant instead. Part IX's procedure for varying and terminating by creditor vote, Court termination and
voiding, and the administrator regime are left out except where they change the debtor's answer. Of bankruptcy, only
duration, release and income contribution liability are in.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Jake's five-year agreement is one the Act does not allow.** s 185C(2AA) forbids payments after "3 years beginning on the
   day the agreement is made", or 5 years only if s 185C(2AB) applies, which needs an interest in the home other than "an
   interest under a lease (other than a long-term lease)". Jake rents in Gosnells, so his limit is 3 years (to 2040-05-31),
   and the Official Receiver cannot accept a five-year proposal for processing (s 185E(2)(a)). Only a later variation with an
   administrator's certificate of an unforeseen substantial change, and no increase in the total, could reach five years
   (s 185M(1DB)). Asserted.
2. **The Official Receiver gates, the creditors decide, by value of replies.** s 185E(2) gives a power ("may accept") that
   exists only if the Official Receiver is satisfied of the s 185C conditions and the statement of affairs; s 185E(2AA) and
   (3) are mandatory bars; (2AB) is a discretion to refuse for undue hardship. The vote is "a majority in value of the
   creditors who reply before the applicable deadline" (s 185EC(1)(b)), disregarding the proposed administrator and its
   related entities, counting a secured creditor only for its shortfall; no replies and it lapses (s 185G(b)). A tie is read
   as no majority. Asserted.
3. **Giving the proposal is itself an act of bankruptcy, and nothing stops a petition until the agreement is made.** s
   40(1)(ha) makes giving a proposal an act of bankruptcy, and (hb)-(hd) make acceptance, breach and termination under s
   185P, 185Q or 185QA acts of bankruptcy too. s 185F, which applies while the proposal is processed, stops enforcement, not a
   creditor's petition; only s 185K(1), once the agreement is in force and on the Index, does that. So during the 35 days a
   creditor owed $10,000 or more could petition on the proposal itself. Asserted (the petition limbs only; whether the Court
   would make a sequestration order is not encoded).
4. **Wage deductions of any kind stop, on the words.** s 185F(1)(c) and s 185K(3)(b) say a person entitled under any law "to
   retain or deduct money from money that is or will be owing or payable to the debtor must not retain or deduct money",
   without limiting it to provable debts, though s 185K(2) lets maintenance liabilities be enforced. On its words that reaches
   the Registrar's deduction of child support from Jake's wages in 2039 while the agreement runs. The Child Support
   (Registration and Collection) Act 1988 was not read. Asserted.
5. **The 10-year bar runs from the end, not the start.** s 185C(4)(a) bars a debtor who "at any time in the 10 years
   immediately before the proposal time ... has been a party (as debtor) to a debt agreement" or "has been a bankrupt". A
   debtor stays a party until the agreement ends, so Jake, completing on 2042-05-31, is barred until 2052-05-31, not
   2047-06-01. (Inference from the words.) Asserted.
6. **The Index question k075 asks is answered by the Regulations, not the Act.** Regulations s 82(1): on completion the
   Official Receiver must remove the agreement "within 1 month after the later of" 5 years after it was made and the day it
   ends. On the scenario's dates that is 2042-06-01; on a lawful 3-year term it is the same day. A terminated agreement stays
   until the later of 5 years after making and 2 years after termination (s 82(2)). A failed proposal goes within a year (s
   83). The credit file is the Privacy Act's and is not here. Asserted.
7. **The threshold is not in the deposit at a usable figure.** s 185C(5) makes the threshold amount "7 times" the Social
   Security Act 1991 point 1064-B1, Table B, item 2, column 3 amount. The deposited Social Security Act compilation prints
   $6,767.80 there, which gives $47,374.60; the scenario says "about $150,000". The printed figure is presumably the
   un-indexed statutory rate (an inference; the Social Security Act's indexation provisions were not read). The threshold is
   an input, and the scenario's figure is used for Jake. The Regulations prescribe no greater amount under s 185C(4)(b)(ii).
   Asserted (the arithmetic; the figure is flagged).
8. **Two formulas are missing from the deposit.** s 185C(4)(e) (and s 185M(1E)) print "the following formula" and no formula;
   so does s 139S, which fixes a bankrupt's income contribution. The payment-to-income test is an input, and for bankruptcy
   only liability (s 139P) and the excess over the threshold are computed. Asserted as far as encoded.
9. **Completion releases provable debts, with the bankruptcy exceptions.** s 185NA(1) releases the debtor from the provable
   debts a discharge would have released, taken when acceptance for processing was recorded; s 153(2) keeps fraud debts and
   maintenance liabilities alive; co-debtors and guarantors are not released (s 185NA(3)); a void declaration undoes the
   release (s 185NA(2)). Asserted.
10. **Bankruptcy would have been 3 years, and probably no contribution.** s 149(1): discharge 3 years from the debtor's
    petition's acceptance (or the statement of affairs' filing), 5 or 8 years if the trustee objects (s 149A). Income
    contributions bite only above the actual income threshold amount, raised 18% for one dependant (s 139K); Ruby, living with
    him in 2037, is a dependant under the Regulations s 36 limit. On an illustrative base threshold Jake's assumed income is
    below it. Asserted on illustrative figures.
11. **Two arrears tests, two consequences.** A 3-month arrears default (s 185LB(3)) obliges the administrator to tell the
    creditors; a designated 6-month arrears default (s 185LC(3)) obliges it to tell the Official Receiver, who, if satisfied,
    "must declare in writing that the agreement is terminated" (s 185QA). Becoming bankrupt terminates the agreement (s 185R).
    Asserted.
