## Why this scope

This is a large Act. The plan keeps to what the separating parent meets: the divorce (s 48 and its separation rules, the children
condition, when it takes effect), parental responsibility and the best-interests considerations as far as the events need, family
dispute resolution before parenting proceedings, what a parenting plan is, and the property time limit. Parenting orders and their
contents, relocation and contravention, child maintenance, spousal maintenance, superannuation splitting, financial agreements, the
s 79(4)-(5) weighing, and enforcement are left out. Child support is a different Act (the Child Support (Assessment) Act 1989) and
is the subject of k071.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Twelve months apart is the only ground.** s 48(1) makes irretrievable breakdown the only ground; s 48(2) says it is
   established "if, and only if" the parties lived separately and apart for 12 months before filing, and s 48(3) bars the order if
   resumed cohabitation is reasonably likely. Separated on 1 June 2038, Jake may file from 1 June 2039. Counting "12 months
   immediately preceding the date of the filing" as reached on the same date a year later is an inference. Asserted.
2. **Separation can happen under one roof, and by one spouse alone.** s 49: neither living in the same residence nor one spouse
   doing household services for the other prevents a separation; one party's conduct can end cohabitation. Whether cohabitation
   ended remains a fact. Asserted.
3. **One reconciliation of up to 3 months does not restart the clock.** s 50(1) aggregates the periods before and after a single
   resumption that ends within 3 months; the time together does not count. Apart 183 days before a one-month reconciliation, Jake
   would need 182 more days, to 2 July 2039. A longer reconciliation restarts the 12 months. Asserted.
4. **The divorce takes effect one month after the order, and not before the children declaration.** s 55(1): the later of 1 month
   after the order and the s 55A order. Order 20 August 2039, so effective 20 September 2039; an appeal moves it to 1 month after
   the appeal ends (s 55(3)); a death stops it (s 55(4)). Jake may remarry from then (s 59). Asserted.
5. **Ruby must be dealt with before the divorce takes effect.** s 55A(1) needs a declaration that proper arrangements have been made
   for each child of the marriage under 18, or that there are circumstances for the divorce to take effect anyway. Ruby, born before
   the marriage, is a child of the marriage under s 60F(1)(a) and also under s 55A(3). Asserted.
6. **In Western Australia, Part VII reaches Ruby because her parents married.** s 69ZE(2) extends Part VII to WA only if WA refers
   the matters or adopts the Part, and the deposit does not show which. s 69ZH confines the parenting provisions to children of a
   marriage as an independent basis. So for Ruby, whose parents married, Part VII applies. For a child of parents who never
   married, the Act read alone leaves the question open in WA. Asserted.
7. **Separation does not touch parental responsibility.** s 61C(1)-(2): each parent of a child under 18 has it, and it "is not
   affected ... by the parents becoming separated"; only a court order takes it away, and only to the extent expressly provided or
   necessary (s 61D(2)). Asserted.
8. **A parenting plan is not enforceable, but it counts.** s 63C(1) requires writing, the parents, signatures, a date and a s 63C(2)
   matter; s 63C(1A) requires freedom from threat, duress or coercion. The note to s 63B says enforceable arrangements need court
   orders. s 65DAB makes the court have regard to the most recent plan if that is in the child's best interests. s 63D lets it be
   varied by written agreement. Asserted.
9. **No parenting application is filed without a dispute resolution certificate or an exemption.** s 60I(7): any of the five
   certificates in s 60I(8) is enough. Otherwise the court must grant an exemption on a s 60I(9) ground: consent, a response,
   abuse or family violence on reasonable grounds, a serious contravention within 12 months, urgency, or inability to participate.
   The Rules separately require pre-action procedures before a parenting or property proceeding, but not for a divorce alone
   (r 4.01(2)(a)). Asserted.
10. **Best interests: six matters, eight for an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander child, optional for consent orders.**
    s 60CA makes the child's best interests paramount; s 60CC(2) lists six matters, (3) adds two for an Aboriginal or Torres
    Strait Islander child, and (4) frees the court from them for a consent order. Asserted.
11. **Property proceedings must start within 12 months after the divorce takes effect.** s 44(3), unless both consent or the court
    gives leave, which it may give only for hardship (or, for maintenance, dependence on a benefit) under s 44(4). For Jake the last
    day is 20 September 2040, counting the 12 months to the same date (an inference). Asserted.
12. **With almost nothing to settle, an order may not be made at all.** s 79(2): no order unless "just and equitable". The
    encoding asks that as the court's judgment. That there must be some property to alter is an inference from s 79(1). Asserted.
13. **De facto property in WA is not split under this Act.** The note to s 90RA(3) says Western Australia is not a referring State,
    so Part VIIIAB orders need a link to a participating jurisdiction (s 90SK). The Family Court Act 1997 (WA) governs instead. That
    Act is named on the plan's instruction; it is not deposited and was not read. Elsewhere a de facto party has 2 years after the
    relationship ends (s 44(5)(a)(i)). Asserted.
14. **Jake need not attend an undefended divorce hearing.** On a sole application he need not attend if he asks for the hearing to
    proceed in the parties' absence and Tahlia has not asked to attend (r 15.15(2)(ba)). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The life does not give the filing date, who applied, the date of the s 55A declaration or the form of the plan. All are assumed.
- How a month is counted (the Acts Interpretation Act 1901) was not read against ss 44, 48, 50 and 55.
- Whether WA has referred or adopted Part VII, and the Family Court Act 1997 (WA) generally, were not read. Both decide the
  position of an ex-nuptial child and of de facto property in WA.
- s 50(2) (an interruption that is not substantial), ss 57-58 (rescission), s 64D and the content of parenting orders are not
  encoded. Neither are the s 79(4)-(5) considerations, which are the real substance of any property case.
- The Family Law Regulations (s 60I(8)(aa), (d), (9)(f) prescribed matters) are not deposited.
- No case law was consulted. There was no independent test pass.
