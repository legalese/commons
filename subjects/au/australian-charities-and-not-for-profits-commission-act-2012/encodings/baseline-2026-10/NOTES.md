# Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission Act 2012 — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
There is no REFUSE in it.
It was checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
**Not for public use as advice.**

Every question is a function named as the responsible person would ask it (`Is my foundation entitled to registration as a charity`, `What must my foundation lodge`, `May I be suspended or removed`, `Am I personally liable for my foundation's amount`). Each is exported with `@export`, with a `@desc` on every input, so it can be deployed to Legalese Cloud.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act; this is the first.

## Seat

**The founder of a charity and director of its trustee company: a responsible person of the registered entity.** In Daniel's life that is Daniel as a director of the Rossi Foundation's trustee company from 2051, and his children Anjali and Luca as its directors from 2076. The questions are "Must my foundation register, and what does registration give it?", "What must my foundation lodge and tell the Commissioner?" and "What can be done to me, and what can I be made to pay?"

The Act's duties fall on the registered entity, not the individual. They are asked as "my foundation" questions because the person asking runs it, and because s 180-20(1) puts each obligation of a trust on the directors of its company trustee too.

The 2026-10-18 defaults, and where this encoding departs from them:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** The Regulations that set the governance standards and could set other size thresholds are not deposited, so those are inputs, not guesses. The Crimes Act penalty unit and body corporate multiplier are brought in.
- **The actors the text names:** the registered entity, its responsible entities, the Commissioner, the trustee and its directors, auditors and reviewers (named only).
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** Every "the Commissioner reasonably believes", "is satisfied", "considers" and "may" is an input: revocation grounds, the decision to skip a show cause notice, keeping last year's size, excluding review, withholding Register details, the necessity of a suspension. So is the entity's own judgment that a breach is "significant" (s 65-5(3)).
- **The Act's own definitions are worked out from facts:**
  - responsible entity (s 205-30);
  - federally regulated entity (s 205-15);
  - small, medium and large registered entity (s 205-25);
  - basic religious charity (s 205-35, simplified at (3A) and (6));
  - the base penalty amounts (ss 175-20, 175-40).

  Borrowed terms are asked as facts: "charity" (Charities Act 2013), "not-for-profit entity" (not defined in this Act), constitutional corporation (Constitution s 51(xx)), revenue "in accordance with accounting standards", deductible gift recipient.
- **One case.** One foundation, one application, one year's reporting, one change to notify, one statement, one late lodgement, one responsible person.
- **The Act's own terms, each glossed.** Each term is quoted in the comment above the rule that uses it.
- **Departure: provisions addressed to the Commissioner.** Revocation, show cause, the Register, warnings, suspension and removal are written as what they mean for the foundation or the person: whether it can be done, from when, by when they must be told, and how long they have to answer.

## Why this scope

The Act is about 240,000 characters. This baseline keeps to what a responsible person of a registered charity meets:

- registration, its refusal and revocation, and objecting;
- the Register's details about responsible persons;
- the governance standards as far as the Act states them;
- records, size, reporting and the duty to notify;
- warnings, suspension and removal of responsible entities;
- the administrative penalties;
- what falls on directors of a trust's company trustee.

**Left out:**

- **The standards themselves:** the governance standards and external conduct standards (Regulations; Division 50).
- **Reporting detail:** financial report contents (s 60-15), auditors and their reports (ss 60-30 to 60-55), additional reporting (ss 60-75, 60-80), collective and joint reporting (ss 60-95 to 60-105).
- **Commissioner's other powers:** information gathering and monitoring (Divisions 70, 75), directions, undertakings and injunctions (Divisions 85, 90, 95), acting responsible entities and vesting orders (Subdivisions 100-C, 100-D).
- **Other entities:** unincorporated associations and bodies corporate (Subdivisions 180-B, 180-D).
- **Penalty detail:** the s 175-20(3) reduction for following the Commissioner's advice, remission (s 175-60), the general interest charge (s 175-65).
- **Review beyond the objection:** objection decisions, the ART and the courts (ss 160-15 to 170-30).
- **Machinery:** the ACNC, the Commissioner and the Advisory Board, secrecy, forms and service.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The Act does not require a charity to register.** Section 30-10(1): an entity "may apply". Registration is "a prerequisite for an entity to access certain Commonwealth tax concessions" (s 20-5(2)); whether a private ancillary fund's DGR endorsement needs it is a matter for the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997, not this Act. Asserted (registration status, entitlement).
2. **There is one type of registration, "Charity", and fourteen subtypes**, twelve keyed to the paragraphs of the Charities Act 2013's definition of charitable purpose (s 25-5(5)). A subtype needs registration as a charity first. A not-for-profit that is not a charity cannot register at all. Asserted.
3. **Entitlement turns on the governance standards, which the Act does not contain.** Compliance with them is a condition of entitlement (s 25-5(3)(b)) and of keeping it (ss 45-10(1), 35-10(1)(c)(ii)). Their content is in the Regulations, not deposited; the Act says only what form they may take (s 45-10(2)) and two limits: they cannot bind a basic religious charity (s 45-10(5)), and they cannot forbid lawful advocacy that furthers the entity's purpose (s 45-10(6)). Asserted.
4. **The applicant can force the question after 60 days.** If no outcome is notified by the end of the 60th day after applying (or 28 days after the last information the Commissioner asked for, if later), the applicant may treat the application as refused and object (s 30-15). Asserted.
5. **Size goes by revenue, on figures the Regulations can change.** Small is revenue "less than $250,000", medium "less than $1,000,000", otherwise large, "or any other amount prescribed by the regulations" (s 205-25). Exactly $250,000 is medium. The encoder understands, without having read it in any deposit, that the Regulations now prescribe $500,000 and $3,000,000; on those, the Foundation's first year would be medium, not large. Asserted (both sets, as inputs).
6. **Reporting is proportional.** Every registered entity lodges an information statement by 31 December after the year (s 60-5); medium entities add a reviewed or audited financial report, large ones an audited one (ss 60-10, 60-20, 60-25). A substituted accounting period moves the deadline to six months after it ends (s 60-85(2)(b)). A basic religious charity lodges no financial report (s 60-60) — and a DGR is not one (s 205-35(3)), save the narrow (3A) exception. Asserted.
7. **A change of directors must be notified: 60 days for a small entity, 28 otherwise** (s 65-5(4)). A significant breach that costs the entity its entitlement must be notified within 28 days even by a small entity. Asserted (d091).
8. **A private ancillary fund is always a federally regulated entity.** A PAF's trustees must all be constitutional corporations (TAA s 426-105(1)(a), as in the TAA baseline), and a trust all of whose trustees are constitutional corporations is federally regulated (s 205-15(b)). That matters because warnings, suspension and removal for breach of the Act or a governance standard reach only federally regulated entities (ss 80-5, 100-5); a WA trust with individual trustees could be reached only for an external conduct standard. Asserted.
9. **A removed director stops being a director, and must stop managing.** A director of a trust's company trustee who is suspended or removed "ceases to be a director of the company" (s 100-20(3)); a removed one can never again be a responsible entity of the entity (s 100-20(2)); managing it regardless, or passing instructions the remaining directors are accustomed to follow, is an offence: 1 year or 50 penalty units, or both (s 100-25). The suspension or removal goes on the Register, not before 14 days have passed unless the public interest requires it (s 40-5(2)). Asserted (except the Register timing).
10. **Directors of a company trustee share the trust's obligations but not, ordinarily, its debts or its offences.** Each obligation of the trust is imposed on each director at the time it arose (s 180-20(1)(b)). An amount payable by the trust is payable by a director only if it is payable "because of a deliberate act or omission of the director involving dishonesty, gross negligence or recklessness" (s 180-20(3)). The trust's offences are deemed the trustees' only where no trustee is a body corporate (s 180-25), so a PAF's offences are not its directors'. Asserted.
11. **Late lodgement costs 1 penalty unit per 28 days or part, up to 5, doubled for medium and multiplied by five for large entities** (s 175-40) — the Act's own example (31 days, 2 penalty units) is reproduced. False or misleading statements cost 60, 40 or 20 penalty units by culpability, plus 20% for obstruction or repetition, nil if disclosed before an examination is notified (ss 175-20 to 175-30). Asserted.
12. **The records offence is 20 penalty units, strict liability** (s 55-5(6), (7)). For a company trustee, Crimes Act s 4B(3) allows five times that (INFERENCE that the trustee company is the offender, from s 205-5(3)). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- **The Regulations.** Deposit the Australian Charities and Not-for-profits Commission Regulations 2022 and encode the governance standards (the duties of responsible persons in particular), the external conduct standards and the prescribed size thresholds. Without them d091's question about what is expected of the new directors is answered only in outline.
- **The PAF Guidelines.** The responsible person requirement the scenario names belongs to the Taxation Administration (Private Ancillary Fund) Guidelines 2019 (see the TAA baseline), not deposited.
- **The ITAA 1997.** Whether a PAF's DGR endorsement requires ACNC registration is in Division 30 of that Act; check it against finding 1.
- **The size thresholds.** Verify the encoder's understanding in finding 5 against the deposited Regulations once they exist.
- **Earlier and later law.** d072 (2051) and d091 (2076) are read on the 2025 compilation.
- **Wider sources.** No ACNC guidance, ruling or case law was read.
