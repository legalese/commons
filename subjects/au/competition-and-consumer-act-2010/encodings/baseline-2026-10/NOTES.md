# Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE. Checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`.
It encodes everything that one encoded: ss 131, 131A; Sch 2 ss 3, 18, 23, 24, 26, 27, 50, 54, 64, 64A, 259–263; and the Part IVA notification test.
It reuses that encoding's hand-checked readings (the s 54(6) two-limb test, who chooses the remedy, the s 131A reach of s 50, the Part IVA control and Chapter 6 exceptions).
It changes one answer: the naive encoding left the "greater amount" for s 3(1)(a) as an input and tested business purchases against $40,000. The Regulations are now deposited, and reg 77A prescribes $100,000.
It adds:

- the reg 77A threshold, dated from 1 July 2021;
- which guarantees the supplier answers for (s 259(1)(b) leaves out ss 58 and 59(1));
- a single "what can I do" answer to the major and minor failure questions;
- what the supplier must give on rejection, whether the consumer must return the goods, and the s 263(5) bar on a store credit;
- unsolicited consumer agreements: what one is, permitted hours, the dealer leaving on request, the termination period, payment and supply during it, and compensation after a late cancellation (ss 69, 73, 75, 82, 85, 86; regs 81, 95).

## Seat

**The consumer**: the person who buys goods or services, or who is door-knocked or cold-called.
For Daniel it is also the small business: his company Jarrah, as customer and as trader on standard terms, and as the acquirer under Part IVA.
The question is "What guarantees and protections do I have, and what may not be done to me?".
Schedule 2 is mostly written as duties on suppliers, dealers and "a person"; the consumer is the one it protects and the one who must act to use it.
Every exported question is the consumer's, for example:

- "Did I acquire it as a consumer?" and "Do I have the guarantee of acceptable quality?"
- "Is it a major failure?" and "What can I do about my goods?"
- "What must the supplier give me on rejection?" and "Must I return the goods myself?"
- "Is the term void under s 64?" and "Is the term void as unfair?"
- "May the dealer call on me now?" and "May I still cancel?"
- "What does s 50 say about the collector?"
- "Must I notify the ACCC?"

The supplier's and dealer's duties are encoded as what may be done to the consumer and what the consumer must be given: s 73 becomes "May the dealer call on me now", s 86 becomes "May the supplier take payment yet", and s 263(4) becomes "What must the supplier give me on rejection".

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The Regulations are deposited and used for the consumer threshold (reg 77A) and for the unsolicited agreement exclusions and the $500 supply exception (regs 81, 95). The Part IVA thresholds instrument is not deposited and is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: consumer, supplier, dealer, manufacturer (named only to show s 58 and s 59(1) are not the supplier's), the party proposing a term, the collector (as "a person" in s 50), the acquirer, the Minister and the ACCC.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. Acceptable quality, major failure, the rejection period, "not fair or reasonable", unfairness, "likely to mislead", "undue harassment", and whether the Minister's threshold is met are all inputs.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out: "acquired as a consumer", "acceptable quality" (from its limbs), "major failure", "small business contract", "consumer contract", "unfair", "unsolicited consumer agreement", "Chapter 6 entity" and "required to be notified". Asked: "corporation", "financial services", "financial products", "control" (Corporations Act s 50AA), "voting power", "listed company" and "business day" counts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one supply of goods, one contract term, one agreement, one acquisition.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in the comments and the `@desc` lines.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. The ACCC's constitution and powers, the regulation-making powers, enforcement and penalties are out.

## Why this scope

The Act fills four volumes.
The plan entry names Schedule 2 ss 18, 23, 50, 54, 64 and 259, and Part IVA.
The brief adds who is a consumer with the current threshold, the other guarantees, major and minor failures, and unsolicited consumer agreements "as far as the events need".
That is encoded with what each answer needs:

- the definition of a consumer (s 3), with the regulation that sets its number;
- the guarantees for goods and which of them the supplier answers for (ss 54–59, 259(1));
- the remedies against the supplier (ss 259–263);
- terms that try to exclude the guarantees (ss 64, 64A);
- unfair terms (ss 23–27);
- the door-to-door and telephone sales rules a consumer meets most (ss 69, 73, 75, 82, 85, 86);
- s 18 and s 50;
- the Schedule's reach as Commonwealth law (ss 131, 131A);
- the Part IVA test for d065.

Left out:

- Part IV and the rest of Part IVA;
- the services guarantees and remedies (ss 60–63, 267–270);
- manufacturers' liability (ss 271–274);
- the title guarantees (ss 51–53) beyond their name;
- s 3(4)–(9), so the price is an input;
- the s 25 examples and ss 28, 28A;
- the disclosure and document rules for unsolicited agreements (ss 74, 76, 78–81), which appear only as "was it contravened" inputs;
- the rest of Parts 2-1 and 3-1;
- product safety, enforcement, penalties and damages (except s 259);
- the ACCC/ASIC debt collection guideline, which k064 names but which is not law;
- the ACL as a law of Western Australia (Fair Trading Act 2010 (WA), not deposited).

None of the lives' events is a door-to-door sale, so the unsolicited consumer agreement section is a short one.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The consumer threshold is $100,000, but only through the Regulations.** Sch 2 s 3(1)(a) still says "$40,000", or "if a greater amount is prescribed ... that greater amount". Reg 77A prescribes $100,000, and the endnotes date it from 1 July 2021. Reg 100 applies it to acquisitions on or after that day, so an acquisition on 30 June 2021 is still tested at $40,000. Asserted.
2. **A business customer paying up to $100,000 for Jarrah's software or services is a consumer, whatever the software's kind.** "Goods" includes "computer software" (s 2). The price test is alone enough (s 3(1)(a), (3)(a)). The naive encoding's answer for a $60,000 business purchase (not a consumer) is reversed by reg 77A. Asserted.
3. **The retailer chooses the remedy for a fixable minor failure; the consumer chooses on rejection.** Under s 261 the supplier "may comply ... by repairing ..., replacing ... or refunding". Under s 263(4) the supplier must refund or replace "in accordance with an election made by the consumer". So the repair in c070 is the retailer's lawful choice unless the fault is major or cannot be remedied (s 259(3)), or the retailer fails to remedy within a reasonable time (s 259(2)(b)). Asserted.
4. **Rejection is lost after the rejection period, but compensation for the drop in value is not.** s 262(1)(a) limits only rejection; s 259(3)(b) stands on its own. Asserted.
5. **The supplier does not answer for spare parts or the manufacturer's warranty.** s 259(1)(b) excludes "sections 58 and 59(1)". Those guarantees are enforced against the manufacturer (s 271), which is not encoded. Asserted.
6. **A store credit is not a refund.** s 263(5): "The supplier cannot satisfy subsection (4)(a) by permitting the consumer to acquire goods from the supplier." Asserted (as the value returned).
7. **The Act is silent when the consumer elects a replacement that is not available.** s 263(4)(b) requires replacement only "if such goods are reasonably available to the supplier", and says nothing about what follows if they are not. The encoding returns that gap rather than guessing. Asserted.
8. **s 54(6) needs both limbs.** Goods do not fail to be of acceptable quality if the consumer caused the problem or failed to take reasonable steps "and" they are damaged by abnormal use. Neglect alone does not take the goods out of the guarantee. Asserted.
9. **Every contract Jarrah makes for a supply is a small business contract.** s 23(4)(b) needs only "at least one party" employing fewer than 100 persons or with turnover under $10,000,000, and sets no limit on the contract's value. Limb (b) of "unfair", that the term is not reasonably necessary, is presumed (s 24(4)). Proposing an unfair term is itself a contravention, one per term (s 23(2A), (2B)). Asserted.
10. **A cap at the fee is void against a consumer customer, whatever the unfair-terms answer.** s 64 voids a term that restricts liability for the guarantees. s 64A saves only limits to repair, replacement or resupply (or their cost) for non-household supplies, and even those fail if reliance is "not fair or reasonable". A cap at a sum of money is none of those forms. That is an inference from the words; the life does not give the cap's wording. Asserted.
11. **The cooling-off period is 10 business days, stretched to 3 months or 6 months by the dealer's own breaches.** s 82(3) takes "whichever ... is the longest". A breach of the hours, identity or leave rules (ss 73–75) gives 3 months. A breach of the information, document or no-supply rules (ss 76, 78–81, 86) gives 6. Asserted.
12. **Goods of $500 or less may be delivered during the cooling-off period; payment may not be taken.** Reg 95 lifts only s 86(1)(a), and only for goods. Asserted.
13. **Jake's collectors may fall outside s 50 as Commonwealth law.** s 131A(1) takes financial services and financial products out of the Division that applies Schedule 2 as Commonwealth law. If the $41,000 is credit card debt (he took two cards, k048), s 50 does not reach it here. That the cards are financial products is an inference; the ASIC Act is not deposited. Asserted.
14. **The Act does not say whether Formly is notifiable; the Minister's determination does.** s 51ABO makes notice turn on "circumstances determined under subsection 51ABP(1)", which must use "a specified threshold" (s 51ABP(2)), and no figure is in the Act. Neither the control exception (s 51ABS(1)) nor the Chapter 6 exception (s 51ABT) helps Jarrah. If notice is required and not given, the acquisition "is stayed" (s 51ABE(2)). Asserted, with the threshold as an input.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Deposit the Competition and Consumer (Notification of Acquisitions) Determination 2025 and encode its thresholds. Until then d065's "not notifiable at this size" cannot be checked.
- Deposit the ASIC Act (s 12DJ and its unfair contract terms provisions) for Jake's credit card debts, and the Fair Trading Act 2010 (WA), which applies the ACL as a law of Western Australia to unincorporated traders.
- Encode the services guarantees and remedies (ss 60–63, 267–270): Jarrah's software subscription may be a supply of services rather than goods, and its customers' remedies then differ.
- Encode manufacturers' liability (ss 271–274), where ss 58 and 59(1) are enforced.
- Every evaluative test (acceptable quality, major failure, the rejection period, unfair, misleading, undue harassment) is an input. No case law was read.
- The price under s 3(4)–(9) (mixed supplies, credit) and business-day counts are inputs.
