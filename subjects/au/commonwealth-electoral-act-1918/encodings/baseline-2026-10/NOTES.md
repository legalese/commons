# Commonwealth Electoral Act 1918 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`: where the
encoding does not answer, a `MAYBE` result is `NOTHING`, with a comment citing
why. Every question is published with `@export` and every input carries a
`@desc`, ready for Legalese Cloud. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** Compilation No. 81, compilation date 1 July 2026, includes
amendments up to Act No. 16, 2025 (register id C2026C00260, in two volumes), as
deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/C1918A00027.txt`. No regulation is
used. The Commonwealth Electoral Regulation 2016 is not deposited, and none of
the encoded sections takes a number or date from it. The "prescribed time" in
s 245 is, by s 245(18), the time stated in the notice. The value of a penalty
unit (Crimes Act 1914 s 4AA) and the s 321 indexed election-funding figures are
inputs.

**Checks:** two modules (`cea-elector.l4`, `cea-candidate.l4`) and two case
files (`cea-cases-elector.l4`, 44 assertions; `cea-cases-candidate.l4`, 26):
70 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings, with the stable `l4` from VS Code
extension 1.6.98. The case files are generated by
`gen_commonwealth-electoral-act-1918_baseline.py` (scratchpad). The expected
values in it were written by hand from the text.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers
everything that one covered (ss 93, 99, 100, 101, 163, 164, 166, 170, 172, 173,
245, 287(9), 294, 310A). It reuses those hand-checked values that still hold:
Jake's 246 days, the 4% boundary at 400 of 10,000, the $5 x 41,000 funding
capped at $150,000, and the return due 25 February 2041. It adds:

- dates in place of ages;
- transfer and change of address (s 101(1), (5), (6A));
- the 3-month deadline for a penalty notice and the second notice (s 245(3), (6));
- the false-response offence (s 245(15C)), with the offences as a table;
- double nominations (s 165) and the 48-hour bulk deadline (ss 170(2)(a), 175);
- the candidate as his own agent (s 289(2));
- the s 310A civil penalty;
- section 44 of the Constitution as a borrowed fact.

## Seat

There are two seats, both of the person the law is applied to.

- **The elector.** This is the person who must enrol and vote, and who receives
  the penalty notice. The Act puts the duties on that person: s 101(1) "shall
  forthwith fill in and sign a claim"; s 245(1) "the duty of every elector to
  vote". The Commissioner and the Divisional Returning Officer (DRO) appear only
  as what they must do to the elector, and by when. For example: `Must the DRO
  send me a penalty notice`, `By when must a penalty notice be sent to me`.
- **The candidate.** This is the elector who stands, as Daniel does in 2040. The
  decisions are `May I stand`, `Is my nomination valid`, `Must the Commissioner
  reject my nomination`, `Do I get my deposit back`, `Must a candidate return be
  provided for me` and `Must I provide the candidate return myself`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.**
  Followed. The deposit ($2,000), the $20 and the 4% are in the Act. The
  regulation is not deposited and supplies nothing these sections need. Two sets
  of numbers come from elsewhere and are inputs: the value of a penalty unit and
  the CPI-indexed s 294 figures (s 321).
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the elector, the candidate and the
  candidate's agent, the Electoral Commissioner, the DRO, and the registered
  officer of a party.
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally.** Followed. The
  DRO's satisfaction under s 245(4) and (8) is an input ("must send a notice
  unless the DRO is satisfied ..."). So is the Electoral Commission's acceptance
  of expenditure under s 298C, as an amount.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed.
  "Elector" (s 4(1), with s 93(4) and s 245(17)), entitlement to enrolment
  (s 93) and the day of entitlement (s 99) are worked out. Other terms are asked
  as facts:
  - temporary visa and unlawful non-citizen (Migration Act 1958);
  - section 44 of the Constitution;
  - the disclosure threshold having been crossed;
  - Form DB's mandatory questions answered.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one person, one election,
  one nomination, one calendar year for the return.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed. The terms are "age 16
  enrolment", "penalty notice", "valid and sufficient reason", "hour of
  nomination" and "qualification checklist", with the statutory words quoted
  above each rule.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out: the Commission,
  the Rolls' upkeep, redistribution, polling and scrutiny procedure, and
  registration of parties.

One departure in kind: every qualification other than age and residence is
taken to have held throughout. The day a person became entitled is worked out
from the 18th birthday and the month at the address only. A person who became a
citizen after both would state a later date than the encoding computes.

## Why this scope

The Act has more than 390 sections. This encoding keeps to what the two seats
meet:

- the elector: enrolment and its duty and offence, voting and its duty, the
  penalty notice and the offence;
- the candidate: standing, the nomination, the deposit, funding for an
  unendorsed candidate, and the annual return.

Left out:

- overseas, itinerant and Antarctic electors beyond their exclusion from s 245;
- citizenship applicants' provisional enrolment (ss 99A, 99B);
- direct enrolment (ss 103A, 103B) beyond its bar on proceedings;
- silent enrolment, objections and review;
- how to vote, and scrutiny;
- Senate group requests beyond the deposit;
- sitting independents' single signature (s 166(1C));
- formal-defect leniency (s 172(2));
- withdrawal and death of candidates;
- party registration;
- funding for groups and parties, and its claims;
- gift caps and expenditure caps (Divisions 3A, 3AB);
- expedited disclosure and federal accounts;
- the contents of the return;
- the Court of Disputed Returns.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **No penalty notice can reach a person who never enrolled.** Jake's life
   (k034) expects "a penalty notice for failing to enrol". The penalty notice
   and the $20 belong to s 245. Section 245 binds an "elector", and s 4(1)
   defines an elector as a person "whose name appears on a Roll". What Jake
   commits is the s 101(4) offence: still off the Roll after 21 days from
   becoming entitled. It is an offence of absolute liability (s 101(6AB)),
   punishable on conviction by a fine of up to 1 penalty unit (s 101(6)), and
   has no penalty-notice route. Asserted.
2. **Sending a claim ends the exposure.** No proceedings for a s 101(1) or (4)
   offence committed before a claim is sent (s 101(7)), or before the
   Commissioner enrols the person directly (s 101(8)). Asserted.
3. **Enrolling at 16 or 17 is optional, and the Act does not call it
   provisional.** The Act calls it "age 16 enrolment" (s 100). Its "provisional"
   enrolment is for applicants for citizenship (s 99A). Section 101(1) excepts a
   person entitled "by virtue of section ... 100". A person enrolled at 17 has
   nothing to do at 18 (c025). Asserted.
4. **Enrolment at 17 does not give a vote before 18.** A person enrolled at 17
   is not entitled to vote at, and is not taken to be enrolled for, an election
   polled before the 18th birthday (s 93(3), (4)). The d021 event sits on
   Daniel's 18th birthday and has him vote in the 2014 Senate re-run. The
   re-run's polling day is not in the deposit. If it fell before 9 April 2014,
   he could not vote at it. The rule is asserted; the date is not verified here.
5. **The 21 days are counted from the day after entitlement.** This is the
   encoding's reading of "upon the expiration of 21 days from the date upon
   which the person became so entitled". For Jake, the last day off the Roll
   without an offence is 20 September 2024, and the offence runs from
   21 September. The boundary is asserted as the encoding's reading.
6. **A long-term prisoner keeps the enrolment but loses the vote.** Section
   93(8AA) removes the vote from a person serving 3 years or longer. Section
   93(1) and (8) leave the enrolment. That such a person has no duty to vote is
   an inference from s 245(4)(c) ("ineligible to vote"), not an express rule.
   Without the DRO's satisfaction, the notice must still be sent. Asserted.
7. **The $20 is written into the Act and is not indexed. A religious belief is
   a reason.** The elector "may ... pay to the DRO a penalty of $20" to keep the
   matter out of court (s 245(5)(c)(iii), (9)); the offence itself carries
   1 penalty unit (s 245(15)). A belief that it is one's religious duty to
   abstain "constitutes a valid and sufficient reason" (s 245(14)). A notice
   must be sent within 3 months after polling day (s 245(3)). A second notice
   follows if there is no response in time (s 245(6)). Asserted.
8. **The Commissioner cannot reject an unqualified candidate.** A nomination is
   rejected "if, and only if" ss 166, 167, 170 or 171 are not complied with
   (s 172(1)). The Commissioner has "no power to determine whether" a person is
   qualified (s 170A(3)). A wrong checklist answer does not affect validity
   (s 170(1A)). An unanswered mandatory question, a late deposit, or a claimed
   renunciation without supporting documents does. Daniel must lodge his
   renunciation documents with the nomination (s 170(1B)). Once a nomination
   is accepted, a missing checklist answer or missing documents no longer
   matter: the nomination is "taken for all purposes to be valid"
   (s 170A(4)). This is not encoded. The rest is asserted.
9. **Section 44 is not in this Act.** The Act's own tests are age 18,
   citizenship, and being or being qualified to become an elector (s 163),
   together with not sitting in a State or Territory legislature (s 164). The
   nomination only declares qualification "under the Constitution" (s 170(1)(b)).
   The encoding asks s 44 as a borrowed fact. Asserted.
10. **A pre-1984 British subject may vote but may not stand.** Section
    93(1)(b)(ii) keeps the franchise; s 163(1)(b) requires an Australian
    citizen. Asserted.
11. **The deposit is a flat $2,000 and comes back at 4%.** Section 321 does not
    index it. It is returned if the candidate is elected or has at least 4% of
    first preferences; a Senate candidate in a group may rely on the group's
    total (s 173). Daniel's 41% gets it back (d063). Asserted.
12. **Funding for an unendorsed candidate is indexed.** The same 4% opens it:
    $5 a vote, capped at the accepted expenditure once above $10,000 (s 294).
    Both figures are indexed under s 321, so they are inputs. Asserted with the
    unindexed figures as illustrative inputs.
13. **The candidate return is annual, and with no agent it is the candidate's
    own duty.** It is due "within 8 weeks of the end of the calendar year"
    (s 310A(2)): for a 2040 candidacy, 25 February 2041 (56 days after
    31 December). It is not required if an endorsing party's return covers it,
    or if there were no gifts, no electoral expenditure and no Commonwealth
    discretionary benefit. A candidate who appoints no agent is his own agent
    (s 289(2)). The civil penalty is the higher of 120 penalty units and 3 times
    the amount not disclosed. Candidacy ends 7 days after polling day
    (s 287(9)(b)). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Section 44 of the Constitution decides Daniel's candidacy. It is a separate
  subject, and nothing here checks it.
- The value of a penalty unit and the s 321 indexed figures have to be supplied
  from outside the Act.
- Several counts are the encoding's reading:
  - the 21 days in s 101(4) and s 101(5);
  - "one month" in s 99 as a calendar month;
  - attaining an age on the anniversary;
  - the 8 weeks in s 310A as 56 days.

  None was checked against case law or AEC practice.
- Citizenship acquired after 18 and after the month at the address is not
  modelled.
- Not encoded: Form DB itself (alterable by regulations), the gift and
  expenditure caps that now bind candidates, Senate group funding, and the
  disclosure threshold that can start a candidacy (it is asked as a day).
