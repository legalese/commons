# Aged Care Act 2024 (Cth) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE. Checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act; this is the first.

## Edition

- **The Act:** Compilation No. 2, compilation date 1 July 2026 (includes amendments up to Act No. 45, 2025), register id C2026C00301, deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/C2024A00104.txt`.
- **Not deposited:** the Aged Care Rules 2025. They set almost every number the older person meets: the individual contribution rates for each means testing category (s 314(1)), the lifetime cap (s 7), the base efficient and unit prices (s 8(1)(g)(iii)), the eight income and asset thresholds (s 319(4)), the maximum accommodation and hotelling supplement amounts (s 235(3), (4)), the maximum non-clinical care contribution (s 279(3)), the basic daily fee (s 277(2)(a)), the eligibility requirements for each service group (s 65(3)(b)), and the prescribed periods. Every one of them is an **input** here, and no figure from them is stated as fact.
- **Borrowed from the Social Security Act 1991:** the basic age pension amount (point 1064-B1) and the parts of the income free area (s 319(2)) — inputs.

## Seat

**The older person seeking care** — "Am I eligible for Support at Home, and what do I contribute?".
The Act is the one an older Australian meets when they ask for help at home or a place in a residential care home, so it is written from their side.
Almost every operative provision is addressed to the System Governor (the Secretary) or to a registered provider; each is turned into what it means for the person: whether they can be found eligible, what can be approved for them, what they are still waiting for, what notice they must be given and by when, and the most they can be charged.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers:** followed. The instrument that sets the numbers, the Aged Care Rules 2025, is not deposited, so its numbers are inputs, as the default directs.
- **The actors the text names:** followed — the System Governor, the approved needs assessor, the registered provider, the Complaints Commissioner.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is asked conditionally:** followed — "the System Governor decides I require access" (s 65(1)), "the System Governor is satisfied an assessment is not reasonably possible" (s 61(2)(b)), "is satisfied a s 71(4) circumstance applies", the urgency rating (s 86(5)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked:** followed — "care needs", "home or community setting", the daily means tested amount and the minimum permissible asset value are worked out; "Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person", "homeless", "sickness", "impairment" and the basic age pension amount are asked.
- **One case is the natural unit:** one person and one service, or one day in a home.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss:** followed ("Support at Home" is glossed as the program name for the service group home support; the Act never uses it).
- **Operative sections in; machinery out:** followed, narrowed further by the size limit; see "Why this scope".

## The lives

- **Chloe, c097 (June 2085, aged 82): a Support at Home package after an aged care assessment.** The event asks who assesses, what is subsidised and what she contributes. Answered by: `Can I be found eligible for an aged care needs assessment` (she is over 65, so s 58(c) does not reach her); `Which kind of care can be delivered where I am` (her own apartment: home support); `Can home support services of this type be approved for me` and `Will my approval be limited to particular service types`; `Must I be given a priority category`; `What am I still waiting for` and `Can I get funded home support services now`, on fixtures for each stage of her path; `What is my individual contribution for the service`, on hypothetical rates and prices; and the Statement of Rights. "What is subsidised" — the Commonwealth's subsidy to the provider (Chapter 4 Part 2) — is not encoded; her side of it, the contribution, is.

## Why this scope

The Act runs to more than 600 sections. The brief keeps to the older person seeking care: entry (Chapter 2 Parts 2, 4 and 5 as far as they decide whether and when the person gets a service), the contributions the person can be charged (Chapter 4 Part 5 Divisions 1 and 2, Part 6 as far as the room price is chosen and the deposit capped, Part 7 Division 1 and s 319), and the Statement of Rights (ss 23, 24).
Left out: the supporter regime (Chapter 1 Part 4); classification assessments and levels (Chapter 2 Part 3), beyond whether a classification decision is in effect; reassessment (s 64) beyond its notice; the number and method of places (ss 91, 93) and provider places (ss 94-102); specialist aged care programs; assistive technology and home modifications beyond the priority-category question and the s 273 Step 1 rate; how the contribution rate is fixed (s 314(1), rules); total assessable income and assets (ss 322-335); the compensation payment fee and fee reduction supplement (inputs); means not disclosed status at home beyond its notice; respite (s 283), higher everyday living fees (s 284) and prescribed classes; accommodation agreements, deposit balances, deductions and refunds (ss 289-295, 303-313) beyond s 305; the subsidies; and Chapters 3 and 5-8 (providers, regulation, complaints process, review, information, penalties).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. The age gate for an aged care needs assessment is 65, or 50 for an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person — and also 50 for a person who is homeless or at risk of homelessness (s 58(a)). Under 65, the person must also have elected in the approved form to receive services early and been told of other services (s 58(c)). Asserted.
2. s 58 is a bar, not an entitlement: the System Governor "must not make an eligibility determination ... unless" its limbs hold. The encoding says when the person *can* be found eligible, not that they will be. Asserted.
3. Eligibility for the assessment does not require proving care needs; s 58(b) asks only that the information the rules prescribe about them "has been provided". Asserted (an 82-year-old without that information cannot be found eligible).
4. s 9(2)(c) makes undergoing an aged care needs assessment a condition of accessing any funded service, yet s 65(4) lets the System Governor approve access "despite an individual not having undergone an aged care needs assessment" in exceptional circumstances. The encoding follows s 9(2) literally; the two are not reconciled in the text read.
5. Home support can be approved through s 66 (Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person), or s 67 (a long-term impairment with a s 67(1)(b) purpose, or a medical service needed because of sickness). Residential care needs s 66 or s 68 — a continuing need, by reason of sickness, for care including nursing; s 67 does not reach residential care. Asserted.
6. For anyone who is not an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person, a home support approval names particular service types (s 65(2)(b)); for an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander person it covers the whole group. Asserted.
7. Ongoing home support needs a priority category and an allocated place unless the rules say otherwise (ss 84(2), 92(1A)). For permanent residential care, a person in a prescribed area, or who is Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander or homeless, gets priority category 1 whatever their urgency rating (s 86(5)). Asserted.
8. A home support place takes effect only on a start day named in a provider's start notification, and lapses if that start day falls outside the period the rules allow (s 92A). Asserted.
9. An approval can reach back to the first service day only if the person applied within 5 days of it (unless the rules or the System Governor allow longer) and the System Governor is satisfied of urgency or a s 71(4) delay (s 71(2)-(4)). Asserted.
10. The Support at Home contribution is the person's contribution rate times the hours, priced at the lesser of the base price and the provider's price, less any fee reduction supplement, never below zero (s 273(2)). It cannot be demanded before the service is delivered (s 273(3)), and it falls to zero once, on an earlier day, the person's counted contributions have *exceeded* the lifetime cap (s 273(5)). Asserted.
11. The Act sets no contribution rate. Every percentage, and the lifetime cap, is in the rules (ss 7, 314(1)); without them, no contribution can be worked out.
12. The daily means tested amount is fully stated in the Act except its thresholds: 50% of income in two bands, 17.5% and then 7.8% of assets, each divided by 364, not 365 (s 319(1)). Asserted.
13. The basic daily fee is 85% of the basic age pension amount, worked out per day and rounded down to the cent, only if no rules are in force (s 277(2)); the Act does not say how the annual amount is turned into a daily one. Asserted.
14. In a residential care home, the hotelling contribution starts where the daily means tested amount passes the maximum accommodation supplement amount, and the non-clinical care contribution where it passes that plus the maximum hotelling supplement amount; a person with means not disclosed status pays both maxima (ss 278, 279). Asserted.
15. Whether a resident can be charged the full room price (an accommodation payment) or only a means-tested accommodation contribution turns on the daily means tested amount at the start day; a resident whose means have not yet been determined, and who does not have means not disclosed status, can be charged an accommodation payment (s 296(a)(iii)). Asserted.
16. s 298(b) says a daily accommodation contribution "must not exceed (i) the maximum accommodation supplement amount ... or (ii) the amount assessed ... based on the individual's daily means tested amount". The "or" is read as "neither", giving the lesser. INFERENCE. Asserted.
17. The minimum permissible asset value (2.25 times the basic age pension amount, rounded to the nearest $500) protects only a deposit paid within 28 days after the start day by a person who gave enough information for the means test (s 305). Asserted.
18. The Statement of Rights belongs to anyone "accessing, or seeking to access" funded aged care (s 24(1)), but it "creates [no] rights or duties that are enforceable by proceedings in a court or tribunal" (s 24(3)); the route is a complaint to the Complaints Commissioner (s 358(a)(ii)). The right to choose is about services "the individual has been approved to access" (s 23(1)(a)(i)). Asserted.
19. Nearly every decision the person waits for must be notified within 14 days after it is made (ss 59, 64(3), 70, 73(2), 79, 88, 314(2), 314A(5), 320(5)); a decision on treating the residential contributions as zero for hardship has 28 days from the application (s 280(6)). The decisions themselves must be made "within the period prescribed by the rules" (ss 57(2), 65(5), 78(4), 86(3)). Asserted.
20. A person can ask for their eligibility determination to be revoked only while under 65; an access approval can be given up at any age (s 72). Asserted.
