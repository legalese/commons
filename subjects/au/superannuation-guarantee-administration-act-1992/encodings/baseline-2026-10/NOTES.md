## Seat

**The employee, and the worker who may be one under s 12.** The plan entry puts the seat as "Must super be paid for me, and how much?".
The Act addresses the employer ("Superannuation guarantee charge ... is payable by the employer", s 16A) and the Commissioner.
Each exported question is phrased as the worker would ask it: "Am I an employee for super", "Must super be paid for me on this pay", "How much super must my employer pay on this pay", "By when must my super reach my fund", "Which fund must my super go to", "Must the Commissioner tell me about my shortfall component".
The employer's charge appears as what the employer can be made to pay for the worker's super.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed. The Regulations 2018 are deposited and are used for the excluded kinds of employee (reg 11) and payment (reg 12), the reductions of the uplift (regs 13B-13D), the stapled fund (reg 17A) and the employee's notice and nomination (regs 18, 21(3)).
  The maximum contributions base (s 10A(5)) and the notional earnings component (s 19A) are inputs: their formulas were images and are not in the deposit.
  Departure: the instrument that matters most for four of the six events is the earlier compilation of the Act itself. It is not deposited, so those events get an explicit "not answered" value (see "The two regimes" below).
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the employee, the employer, the Commissioner, the fund.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. Asked as facts: whether a contract is "wholly or principally" for the worker's labour (s 12(3)); the Commissioner's notifications about a stapled fund (s 32C(1A), (2)(aa)) and the Commissioner's satisfaction under reg 17A(3)(d); whether an exemption certificate is in force (s 17B).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out: employee (s 12), part-time employee, ordinary time earnings, qualifying earnings, business day, usual period, extended usual period, the choice loading limit, the stapled fund. Asked: the ordinary meaning of employee, complying fund, MySuper product, temporary visa, eligible contribution.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one payment of qualifying earnings on one payday (the "QE day") to one employee by one employer.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in the comments; record fields are in the worker's own words.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Assessments, collection, penalties, administration, records and the regulation-making power are out.

### The two regimes

The deposited compilation is the Payday Super text, in force from 1 July 2026.
The endnotes record ss 11, 23, 27 and 28 as "rep No 57, 2025" and s 19 as "rs No 57, 2025".
The application items of Act No. 57, 2025 are listed in the endnotes but not reproduced.
INFERENCE: a payday before 1 July 2026 is governed by the earlier compilation, which is not deposited.

So, for a payday before 1 July 2026:

- `What percentage of my pay must go to super` returns `not answered: the earlier compilation is not deposited`;
- `Must super be paid for me on this pay`, `By when must my super reach my fund` and `Did my super arrive in time for this payday` return `not answered: the earlier compilation governs this payday and is not deposited`;
- `How much super must my employer pay on this pay` takes "the percentage in the earlier compilation" as an input the asker must supply, labelled as not in the deposit; for a payday from 1 July 2026 that input is ignored and 12 is used.

`Am I an employee for super` is answered for any date. The endnotes show s 12 last amended in 2006 (No 101, 2006), so INFERENCE: it read the same in 2018 and 2023.

## Why this scope

The seat's questions are: am I covered, on what earnings, at what percentage, by when, into which fund, and what if it is not paid. The scope answers each:

- **Am I covered:** s 12; reg 11 with s 6(1) "part-time employee"; s 17B.
- **On what earnings:** s 10A(1)-(3), s 6(1) "ordinary time earnings", reg 12(1)(a), (b), (c), (f); the cap in s 10A(6).
- **At what percentage:** s 17A(2).
- **By when:** s 6(1) "business day", "usual period", "extended usual period"; s 18C(1)(c), (2) item 1.
- **Into which fund:** ss 32C(1)(a), (1A), (1B), (2), (2A), 32CA, 32Q with reg 17A, 32F(2), 32N(2), 32NA(1), (5), (11).
- **If it is not paid:** ss 16A, 16B, 18C, 18D, 19B with regs 13B-13D, 20A, 20C, 20D; regs 18 and 21(3).

Not encoded: defined benefit schemes; the complying-fund presumption (ss 18A(2), 18B); issuing exemption certificates; out-of-cycle earnings and exceptional circumstances (s 18C(2) items 2-4, reg 13); the Commonwealth as employer; international agreements; reg 12(1)(d), (e), (g)-(j), (2), (3); s 30; the rest of Part 3A; assessments, collection, the s 59C penalty, the rest of Part 8; records.
The charge is imposed by the Superannuation Guarantee Charge Act 1992, which was not read.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **For four of the six events the governing text is not in the deposit.** The compilation is the Payday Super text from 1 July 2026; ss 11, 23, 27 and 28 are repealed and s 19 is replaced. c020 (2018), c032 (2023) and c041 (2024) fall under the earlier compilation (INFERENCE: the application items are not reproduced), and so does any part of a life before 1 July 2026. Those paydays return an explicit "not answered" value. Asserted.
2. **The under-18 rule did not go with old s 28: it is now in the Regulations.** Reg 11(f) excludes from qualifying earnings "a part-time employee who is under 18", and s 6(1) defines a part-time employee as one "employed to work not more than 30 hours per week". So, under today's text, Chloe at 15 on a 12-hour contract earns no super (asserted), and a 17-year-old employed for 32 hours a week does (asserted). The test is the hours the young person is *employed* to work, not the hours worked in a particular week. The naive encoding said that any exclusion "has to be in regulations that were not deposited"; the deposited Regulations supply it. Asserted.
3. **The charge percentage is a flat 12, with no table of dates.** s 17A(2): "charge percentage means 12". On the inferred formula a $5,833.33 month paid after 1 July 2026 gives $699.9996, and the text has no rounding rule. The life's $641.67 at 11% depends on the old s 19, which is not deposited: with 11 supplied as an input the encoding gives $641.6663. Asserted.
4. **"Wholly *or principally*" for labour.** s 12(3) makes a worker an employee if the contract is "wholly or principally for the labour of the person". The Jake narrative says the platform pays no super because the contract is not *wholly* for his labour. The text asks for less than that. Both answers are asserted, turning on that judgement. This Act never mentions minimum standards orders, so k041's second question is not answered here. Asserted.
5. **A paid intern is caught by s 12(3) even if the ordinary meaning is doubtful.** An unpaid placement with no contract for labour is not. Asserted.
6. **Super is due within seven business days of each payday; a holiday in any one State counts everywhere.** "business day" excludes a public holiday "for the whole of ... any State", the ACT or the NT. So a holiday in one eastern State alone also lengthens a Perth employer's period. A first contribution to a new fund gets 20 business days (s 18C(2) item 1). Asserted. A contribution received in the 12 months before the payday also counts (s 18C(1)(c)(ii)); the encoding reads that as 365 days, and it is not asserted.
7. **Overtime is not qualifying earnings; a shift loading is.** Ordinary time earnings are earnings for ordinary hours plus "over-award payments, shift-loading or commission". Reg 12(1) also excludes parental leave pay (asserted) and fringe benefits. Asserted.
8. **There is no small-employer threshold and no monthly minimum.** Nothing in ss 10A, 12 or 17A asks the employer's size or the amount paid. Jarrah owes from its first payday. Asserted.
9. **The domestic-worker rule is now stated twice.** Section 12(11) says such a worker paid for no more than 30 hours a week "is not regarded as an employee". Reg 12(1)(j) also excludes "payments ... under a contract for the employment of the person, for not more than 30 hours per week, for work that is wholly or principally of a domestic or private nature". A nanny paid for exactly 30 hours a week is outside; at 38 hours she is in. Asserted (s 12(11)).
10. **Owning up early can cut the 60% uplift to nothing.** Reg 13C cuts 20 points if there was no Commissioner-initiated assessment or estimate in the past 24 months, counted from 1 July 2026 at the earliest. Reg 13D cuts 40, 35, 30 or 15 points for a voluntary disclosure lodged within 30, 60 or 120 days of the payday, or later, provided it comes before any assessment. Both together, early, reach 0% (the regulation's Example 1); 13D item 2 alone gives 25% (Example 2). Asserted.
11. **A late standard choice form stops the stapled-fund and default-fund routes.** Under s 32C(1B) and (2A), while the employer is late giving the form, a contribution complies only if it goes to a chosen fund. Without a chosen fund, no route under s 32C(1A) or (2) opens until the Commissioner has told the employer whether there is a stapled fund. INFERENCE from the two subsections: no other route in s 32C was read. The employer must give the form within 28 days of the employee starting (s 32N(2)), but not to a temporary visa holder (s 32NA(11)). Asserted.
12. **The stapled fund is picked by the Commissioner's last notice, then the latest contribution, then the largest balance.** Reg 17A(3) gives that order, and each step applies only if no eligible fund is caught by the one before. So a fund that received the latest contribution is not the stapled fund if a notice in the selection period named another fund. Asserted.
13. **The employee is told if more than $20 is collected for them.** Reg 18(1). If the notice names a fund and the employee nominates no other within 28 days, the employee is taken to have nominated it (reg 21(3)). Asserted.
14. **Five formulas did not survive conversion.** The deposit has "equal to:" and then "where:" in ss 10A(5), 17A(2), 18C(1), 18D(2) and 19A(2). The forms used for 17A(2), 18C(1) and 18D(2) are inferences from the words around them. The maximum contributions base and the notional earnings component are inputs.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- **The earlier law.** Deposit the compilation of the Act in force immediately before 1 July 2026, the application items of Act No. 57, 2025 (sch 1 items 181, 182, 184-189), and the earlier compilation of the Regulations. Only then can c020, c032 and c041 be answered for their own dates, including the life's $641.67 at 11%.
- **The formulas.** Re-fetch the compilation in a format that keeps the formulas, and check the three inferred ones. Encode s 10A(5) once the basic concessional contributions cap (ITAA 1997) is in hand.
- **Not encoded.** Notional earnings (s 19A) and the general interest charge; defined benefit schemes; the eligible contribution rules; out-of-cycle earnings; the rest of Part 3A; assessments, collection and the s 59C penalty; the Superannuation Guarantee Charge Act 1992.
- **Testing.** No ATO guidance, ruling or case law was consulted. The "wholly or principally" test for platform work is the open question, and it is left as an input. "12-month period" is read as 365 days.
