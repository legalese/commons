# Life Insurance Act 1995 — baseline encoding

## Method

Baseline encoding, written straight from the deposited text with the writing-l4-rules skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

One module, `lia-policies.l4`, and one case file, `lia-cases-policies.l4`, generated by the scratchpad script `gen_life-insurance-act-1995_baseline.py`.
Every expected value in the cases was written by hand from the text.
No REFUSE, `#ASSERT REFUSED` or BECAUSE: where the Act gives no answer it returns a value that says so (for example `the Act gives me no capacity; the general law decides`, `the Bankruptcy Act 1966 decides`).

There was no first-pass (naive) encoding of this Act to replace.

## Edition

Life Insurance Act 1995 (Cth), Compilation No. 69, compilation date 21 February 2025 (register id C2025C00135), as deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/C2004A04860.txt`.

No regulations or prudential standards are deposited.
So the following are inputs, and the cases use the Act's own figures:

- the amount the regulations may prescribe instead of $100,000 (ss 211, 212);
- the amount the regulations may prescribe instead of $50,000 (s 213);
- the prudential-standards minimum surrender value (s 207(4)).

## Seat

**The insured person, or the owner of the policy.**
For Chloe that is the fund member whose life her fund's group cover insures; she is not the owner (the trustee is), and the encoding says so.
The Act is almost wholly addressed to life companies, APRA and ASIC.
What reaches the individual is in Part 10 (policies) and in the concepts that decide whether a contract is a "life policy" at all.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. None is deposited, so the prescribed amounts and the surrender floor are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the owner, the person whose life is insured, the life company, APRA (s 208 suspension), the personal representative and the family (ss 211-213), the court (s 221).
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed: "satisfies the company" (ss 211(1)(d), (e), 213(2)), APRA's suspension (s 208) and APRA's s 12A declaration are asked as facts.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts; borrowed terms asked as facts.** Followed for "life policy" (s 9), "continuous disability policy" (s 9A), "owner" (s 10(2)), "superannuation policy", "child's advancement policy" and "vesting age" (s 218). "Spouse", "de facto partner", "consumer credit insurance" and "health insurance" are asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one cover, one policy, one unpaid premium, one deceased person's policies with one insurer (ss 211, 212 add them up).
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in the comments.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed: registration, statutory funds, financial management, monitoring, external administration, transfers and prudential standards are out.

## The lives

| Event | What the life asks | Decisions |
|---|---|---|
| c04a, Chloe at 23, 15 September 2026: income protection and life cover inside her super fund | What may her fund's insurer offer; who owns the cover? | `What kind of policy is my cover` (her yearly group life cover: a life policy under s 9(1)(a); her yearly income protection whose premiums reset: not a life policy; income protection to 65 with premiums changed only for everyone at once: a continuous disability policy); `May a life insurer offer me this cover` (yes for the life cover; no for the yearly income protection unless APRA has declared it life business under s 12A, or the insurer already did general insurance in 1995); `Am I the owner of my policy` (no: the trustee holds it); `Is my cover a superannuation policy` (yes) |

Whether her fund may give her any cover at 23 is the SIS Act, not this Act.
The SIS Act baseline (`subjects/au/superannuation-industry-supervision-act-1993/encodings/baseline-2026-10/`) answers it: no cover under 25 unless she elects in writing (s 68AAC), and default death and permanent incapacity cover for a MySuper member at 25 (s 68AA).
That baseline also notes that s 68AA does not require income protection.
This encoding does not contradict either point; it answers only what kind of contract the cover is and who owns it.

## Why this scope

The brief asked for:

- the cover a fund's insurer, or one's own life insurer, may offer;
- who may be insured (own life, spouse, children, up to the Act's age limits);
- nominations and payment on death;
- the policy owner's rights.

The Act turns out to cover only some of these.

- **What may be offered** is ss 9, 9A and 234. A life company may carry on only life insurance business, so the question becomes whether the cover is a "life policy".
- **Age limits** are s 199 (capacity from 10 with a parent's written consent, and an owner's full rights from 16) and Division 6 (children's advancement policies, full age 16, vesting age at most 25).
- **Who may be insured** — insurable interest in one's own, a spouse's or a child's life — is not in this Act. It is the Insurance Contracts Act 1984, Part III.
- **Nominating beneficiaries** is not in this Act either. Inside super it is the SIS Act s 59(1A) and reg 6.17A; outside super it is the policy's terms.
- **Payment on death** in this Act is:
  - the protection of policy money from the deceased's debts (s 205);
  - payment to family without probate up to $100,000 (ss 211, 212);
  - transfer of a deceased owner's policy up to $50,000 (s 213).
- **The owner's rights** are:
  - surrender (s 207);
  - paid-up (s 209);
  - no forfeiture without 28 days' notice, and none at all while the surrender value covers the debt after 3 years (s 210);
  - no set-off of other policies without consent (s 217);
  - a replacement document, or a court order after 6 months (s 221).

The baseline is 45 assertions, as the brief asked (25-45).
It therefore tests the c04a questions fully and each other section once or twice.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Whether income protection is a "life policy" depends on whether the insurer can reprice it.** A contract paying on injury, disability or a stated condition is a life policy only as a "continuous disability policy" (s 9(1)(e)). That needs:
   - a term of more than 3 years, or a shorter term only because of the owner's age at entry (s 9A(1)(a), (2));
   - no power in the insurer to alter benefits or premiums, apart from improvements the owner accepts or premium changes made "on a simultaneous and consistent basis" across all such contracts (s 9A(3)-(5)).

   Chloe's group income protection, renewed yearly with premiums reset, is not one. Income protection to 65 with only across-the-board repricing is. Asserted.
2. **A death policy for a year or less that pays only on accidental death, or death from a specified sickness, is not a life policy** (s 9(2)). The same cover for five years is one (s 9(1)(a)). Asserted.
3. **A life insurer may offer only life insurance business** (s 234(1)). It may also offer business APRA has declared life business under s 12A, and an existing company may keep the general insurance it carried on before the Act commenced (s 234(2)). Cover that is not a life policy, offered by a life company, therefore needs one of these. INFERENCE: such cover is otherwise general insurance, under the Insurance Act 1973. Asserted (the decision, not the inference).
4. **Inside super the member is not the owner.** The owner is the person to whom the policy is issued (s 10(2)). A life policy maintained for a super scheme and owned by its trustee is a "superannuation policy" (Dictionary). So the owner's Part 10 rights — surrender, paid-up, forfeiture notice, replacement document — belong to the trustee, not to Chloe. Asserted.
5. **The Act sets no insurable-interest rule and no beneficiary-nomination rule.** s 199(1) assumes a policy may be on one's "own life or on another life", but says nothing on whose. Not asserted (nothing to assert).
6. **A child of 10 to 15 may take out a policy, including on another's life, with a parent's written consent. From 16 an owner exercises the owner's rights as an adult** (s 199). The Act gives no capacity under 10, and says nothing about entering a policy at 16 or 17. INFERENCE: that is left to the general law of minors' contracts. Asserted.
7. **A child's advancement policy vests in the child at the age the policy names, if 10 or more, or at 25, whichever is earlier** (s 218(1)). Until then it belongs to the person who took it out (s 219(3)). If that person dies or becomes bankrupt first, their representative holds it in trust for the child (s 220(2)). Asserted.
8. **The Act protects a policy on one's own life, or on a spouse's or de facto partner's life, from court process for one's debts, whoever owns it** (s 204(1), (2)(b)). It also protects death money paid to the estate unless the deceased had:
   - contracted for it to pay the debt; or
   - charged it with the debt; or
   - expressly directed it in a will. A mere direction to pay debts does not count (s 205(1)).

   Both are "subject to the Bankruptcy Act 1966", which the encoding hands on rather than guesses. Asserted.
9. **An owner may surrender after 3 years in force, or at once for a policy with no premiums due after the first year.** The request must be in writing. The insurer must pay the surrender value less debts, unless APRA has suspended the obligation (ss 207, 208). The surrender value is never less than the prudential-standards floor or, for a pre-1995 policy, its adjusted 1 July 1995 value. Asserted.
10. **After 3 years' premiums the owner may ask in writing for a paid-up policy** (s 209). Asserted (2.5 years: no).
11. **No forfeiture for an unpaid premium while 3 years' premiums are paid and the surrender value (on the day before the due date) exceeds the premium and other debts** (s 210(1), (2)). Otherwise there is still no forfeiture until written notice is given and 28 days have passed from the later of the notice and the due date (s 210(5)). For a premium due 15 March 2027 with notice on 1 March, the first day is 12 April 2027. Asserted.
12. **Up to $100,000 (net of debts to the insurer) across one insurer's policies may be paid to the spouse, de facto partner, parent, child, sibling, niece or nephew of the deceased without probate** (ss 211, 212). Anyone else needs the insurer to be satisfied that they take under the will or intestacy, or could get probate. The insurer "may" pay; it need not. Asserted.
13. **When an owner dies before the life insured, the policy may be endorsed to the person entitled without probate only if its adjusted surrender value is less than $50,000** (s 213). Exactly $50,000 is not less. Asserted.
14. **A policy may exclude suicide only expressly** (s 228). A war-service limit is void unless the person to whom the policy was issued signed an acknowledgment on the document (s 229). The company's failure to comply with the Act does not invalidate a policy (s 230). Asserted for s 228; s 229 is encoded, not asserted; s 230 is not encoded.
15. **If the insurer does not replace a lost policy document within 6 months of the owner's written request, the owner may apply to a court of summary jurisdiction** (s 221(5)). INFERENCE: the owner may apply from the day after the 6 months end. Asserted.
