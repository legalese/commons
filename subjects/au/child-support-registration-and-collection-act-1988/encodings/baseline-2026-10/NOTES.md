# Child Support (Registration and Collection) Act 1988 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the
individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage
table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** Child Support (Registration and Collection) Act 1988, Compilation No. 77, compilation date 1 July 2026 (includes
amendments up to Act No. 17, 2025), Federal Register of Legislation register id C2026C00287, deposited at
`../../registers/source-bundle/C2004A03596.txt`. The Child Support (Registration and Collection) Regulations 2018 are not
deposited. The protected earnings rate (s 4(1)), the prescribed periodic deduction from a social security payment (s 72AA), the
kinds of payment credited under s 71C and the "satisfactory payment record" of s 38B are therefore inputs or left out. The general
interest charge rate that s 67 borrows from the Taxation Administration Act 1953 s 8AAD(1) is an input. Penalties are stated in
penalty units. Their dollar value is set by the Crimes Act 1914 s 4AA, which was not read.

**Checks:** one module (`csrc-collection.l4`) and one case file (`csrc-cases-collection.l4`, 70 assertions): 70 assertions
satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings, with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. A mutated copy of the case file, with two
expected values changed, fails as it should. Every decision a paying parent would ask is marked `@export`, with `@desc` on each
input. The two internal helpers and the per-period share of the annual rate are not exported. The case file is generated by the
scratchpad script `gen_child-support-registration-and-collection-act-1988_baseline.py`, and its expected values were written by
hand.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act to replace.

## Seat

**The paying parent** ("payer" in the Act): "How can child support be collected from me?" Most of Parts IV and V speak to the
Registrar or to employers. The person the Act is applied to is the payer, though, so every decision is phrased as the payer
would ask it: `How is my child support collected`, `Must the Registrar collect from my pay`, `How much can my employer deduct
from this pay`, `What late payment penalty for one period`, `Can a departure prohibition order be made against me`, `Can the
payee sue me`. Where a section only directs the Registrar or an employer, the encoding says what that means for the payer: what
may be taken, from where, by when, and what the payer must be told.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed, as far as the deposit allows. The Regulations that set
  the protected earnings rate and the prescribed deductions are not deposited, so those figures are inputs. The case file uses
  an illustrative $500 a week, not the prescribed figure. The general interest charge rate belongs to another Act, so it is an
  input too.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the payer, the payee, the Registrar, the employer, the Secretary (Centrelink), the
  Commissioner of Taxation, the court. The Bankruptcy Act's debt agreement comes in only as an input (see finding 8).
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed throughout. The Registrar's states of mind (s 39(5)
  "satisfied", s 44(1) "likely to make timely payments", s 68 "fair and reasonable", s 72D "persistently and without reasonable
  grounds", s 72L) are inputs named after the judgment, never guessed.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. "Registrable maintenance liability" (s 17), "child
  support liability" (s 72E), "payment period" (s 4(1)) and "protected earnings amount" (s 46(4)) are worked out. "Employee"
  rests on the Taxation Administration Act's withholding payments, so it is asked as a fact ("pay subject to PAYG withholding").
  "Social security pension or benefit" belongs to the Social Security Act 1991 and is also asked as a fact.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one liability of one payer, one pay, one payment period.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a gloss.** Followed. The comments quote the section, and the `@desc` gives the plain meaning.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. The Register's particulars, the Account, objections and review, secrecy and
  the employer's own record-keeping and penalties are out, except where they reach the payer (ss 49, 50, 57).

## The lives

- **Jake, k072 (2039-01-15): "Three missed payments; the Registrar collects by deduction from his wages."** The event asks "How
  is child support enforced?" The case file answers it on the reading the facts most naturally bear. Tahlia applied for the
  assessment (k071), so it was registered at once (s 24A). Jake had elected to pay the Registrar himself and the Registrar had
  accepted (s 44(1)). Three missed payments then oblige the Registrar to vary the Register so that employer withholding applies
  (s 44(5)), and to collect from his pay at Sentinel (s 43(2)). That gives $360 a fortnight on an assumed $180 a week, with the
  protected earnings floor shown. Sentinel must pay it over by the 7th of the next month (s 47(1)), and Jake is discharged as
  each amount is deducted (s 49). The case file also covers what else the missed payments open up: the late payment penalty on
  the $2,340 of arrears, a tax refund intercept, a notice to his bank, and a departure prohibition order. It also takes the other
  reading, private collection after a s 38A election. On that reading Tahlia's s 39 application must be granted, because Jake has
  not been complying. Finally, k072 is checked against the debt agreement in force from k065 to k075.

## Why this scope

The plan names Part IV (collection from salary or wages) and Part V (payment and recovery of child support debts) for k072. The
seat's question ("how can it be collected from me?") also needs the step before them: whether the Registrar collects at all.
That brings in registration (ss 17, 24, 24A, 25) and ending and restarting Registrar collection (ss 38-39). It also needs the
enforcement tools in Part VA (departure prohibition orders) and the payee's own right to sue (ss 30(3), 113A). The amount of
child support belongs to the Child Support (Assessment) Act 1989 and is not here. Overseas liabilities (s 18A), transferred State
liabilities (ss 20-22) and lump sums and carer debts (ss 69A, 69B) are uncommon for an ordinary payer and are left out.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Who applies for the assessment decides whether the Registrar collects.** Under s 24A(2)(c) an assessment is not registered
   automatically if "the application giving rise to the child support assessment was made by the parent by whom child support is
   payable". So a payer who applies himself starts in private collection. The payee can switch to the Registrar at any time
   under s 25, and the Registrar then has 28 days. Asserted.
2. **The payer cannot end Registrar collection alone.** The s 38A election belongs to "the payee ... or the payee and payer
   jointly". All the payer can do alone is opt out of employer withholding under s 44, which still means paying the Registrar.
   Asserted.
3. **Missing payments brings employer withholding back as a duty, not a discretion.** Under s 44(5) the Registrar "must vary"
   the Register once the payer "does not make timely payments". The only escape is the Registrar being satisfied that withholding
   is inefficient or that timely payment will soon resume (s 44(5A)). After that the payer cannot opt out again for 6 months
   (s 44(7B)). Asserted.
4. **Part IV reaches contractors.** Section 65AA applies Part IV to "a person ... engaged under a contract for services" as if
   that person were an employee. Jake's years as a delivery contractor would have been within its reach. Asserted.
5. **The protected earnings rule has a literal gap.** Section 46(4) caps the deduction where pay "exceeds" the deduction by less
   than the protected earnings amount. Where pay is less than the full deduction, it does not "exceed" it, and s 46(2) on its own
   would take the whole rate. The encoding takes the protected earnings floor in every case, and takes nothing where pay is below
   it, reading s 46(4) with s 46(4B). (Inference from the words.) Asserted.
6. **The payer is discharged as soon as the amount is deducted.** Under s 49 the payer is "to the extent of the amount
   deducted, discharged". An employer who pays late or goes bankrupt does not revive the payer's debt. Under s 50 the employer's
   trustee must pay the Registrar, ahead of other debts. Asserted.
7. **Low-income non-enforcement is not open to a payer under an assessment.** Section 37B(2) covers only a liability "covered
   by subsection 17(1)", that is, one under a court order or agreement, or an overseas order. Jake's assessment-based liability is
   a s 17(2) liability. Section 72AA(1)'s ongoing Centrelink deduction, by contrast, applies only to s 17(2) assessment
   liabilities. Asserted (the s 37B limb; the s 72AA(1) limb is encoded, not asserted).
8. **This Act says nothing about debt agreements or bankruptcy of the payer.** The deposit mentions bankruptcy once, in s 50(3),
   and that is about a bankrupt employer. Sections 43(2) and 46(1) are unqualified. The Bankruptcy Act baseline's finding 4 reads
   s 185F(1)(c) and s 185K(3)(b) literally as forbidding any person entitled under a Commonwealth law to deduct from money payable
   to the debtor, and s 185K(2) saves only the "enforcing [of] a remedy" for maintenance. Neither text settles whether that reaches
   an employer's s 46 deduction. The encoding keeps the two consistent by making the Bankruptcy Act reading an input. On the
   literal reading, Jake's January 2039 deductions are required by this Act and forbidden by that one. Asserted.
9. **When Registrar collection ends, arrears survive unless the payee says otherwise.** Only a s 38A election that expressly
   includes the unpaid amounts makes them "cease to be debts due by the payer to the Commonwealth" (s 38A(3)(b)). A s 38B
   decision does not, and a wage notice stays in force for remaining child support debts (s 45(4)(c)). The Act does not say
   whether amounts dropped under s 38A(3)(b) become owed to the payee, except for overseas liabilities (s 38C). Asserted (the
   Commonwealth side).
10. **The late payment penalty is the general interest charge rate, split by payment period.** Section 67 charges 1/52, 1/26,
    4/52 or 1/12 of "the general interest charge rate under subsection 8AAD(1) of the Taxation Administration Act 1953" on the
    whole balance outstanding, each period. Remission is the Registrar's, on three alternative grounds (s 68). A refusal must
    come with notice of the payer's right to object. Asserted (the penalty and remission grounds).
11. **A departure prohibition order is the Registrar's, not a court's, and lifting it is a duty.** Section 72D lets the
    Registrar make the order. Section 72I(1) says the Registrar "must revoke" it once the liability is gone, arranged for, or
    irrecoverable. Under s 72L a travel certificate must issue on the conditions stated, including humanitarian grounds. Leaving
    without one carries up to 60 penalty units or 12 months (s 72F). Asserted.
12. **The payee may sue only through s 113A once the liability is registered.** Under s 30(3) the payee "may not enforce
    payment ... other than by instituting a proceeding under section 113A". That requires 14 days' written notice to the
    Registrar, or a shorter period the court allows in exceptional circumstances. Asserted.
