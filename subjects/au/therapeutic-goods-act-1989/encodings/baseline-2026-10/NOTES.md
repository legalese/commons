## Seat

**The minor found with a vape (Jake, at 16).**
Chapter 4A is applied to that person in three ways: whether holding the vape is an offence, whether the person who sold it to them offended, and what could lawfully be supplied to them.
Every exported question is phrased as that person would ask it: "Is my possession of vaping goods an offence?", "Was the supply of vaping goods to me an offence by the supplier?", "May a pharmacist lawfully supply me vaping goods?".
The seller's penalties are encoded because the seat asks whether "only the sale" is the offence; they are what the seller faces, stated from the buyer's side.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed as far as the deposit allows. The Regulations that set the commercial and permitted quantities are not deposited, so those numbers are inputs. The Minister's s 41R determinations and s 41RA indications are not deposited either; whether goods are covered, or a person named, is asked.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the person in possession, the supplier (pharmacist, medical practitioner, nurse practitioner, wholesaler, retailer), the importer, the manufacturer, the Minister and the Secretary.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. Asked as facts:
  - whether the goods are covered by a Minister's determination (s 41R);
  - whether the Secretary has consented (s 41RC);
  - whether the State law authorises the supplier (Schedule 3 authority; s 41QB(11)(c)(ii));
  - whether the Customs Act 1901 prohibits an importation (s 41Q(5)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. These are worked out from facts:
  - "vaping goods" (s 41P), from the kind of goods;
  - the quantity bands, from the quantity held and the commercial quantity;
  - "final dosage form" (s 41QB(12)), asked as the one fact it turns on.

  "Retailer" (s 41QD(10)) is the Act's own term but is asked as one fact (am I a retailer holding them at the retail premises). Its four limbs are in the comment. This is a departure for size.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one holding of one kind of vaping goods (`My vaping goods`), and one supply (`The supply to me`).
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments and in the enumeration labels.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out: the consent procedure (ss 41RB, 41RC) beyond whether a consent is held, the breach of consent conditions (s 41RD), advertising (Part 5-1A), executive officer liability (ss 54B, 54BA), the Secretary's evidentiary certificates, and the rest of the Act.

## Why this scope

The Act is large: therapeutic goods registration, medical devices, manufacturing licences, advertising and recalls.
The seat and the one life event need only Chapter 4A's offences about vaping goods.
Those offences cover import, manufacture, supply, commercial-quantity possession and retail possession, with the exceptions that decide whether a pharmacy supply, or a person's own holding, is lawful.
Import and manufacture are included briefly because the seat asks about "the vaping goods provisions" as a whole, and a teenager may order online or mix liquid.
The size is kept to 43 assertions.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A person who is not a retailer commits no possession offence below the commercial quantity.** Section 41N says possession "is prohibited, subject to some exceptions". The possession offences, though, reach only a holding of at least the commercial quantity (s 41QC) or a retailer holding goods at retail premises (s 41QD(1)(a), (b)). A customer holding one vape commits no offence under this Act, whether or not it was lawfully supplied. Asserted.
2. **The Act names no age.** Nothing in Chapter 4A, or in the Act's definitions, turns on whether the person is under 18. The same holding gives the same answer at 16 and at 30. Any age rule for the sale lives in State law (here the Tobacco and Other Products Control Act 2006 (WA) and the WA medicines law), which this Act reaches only through the supplier's "authority ... under a law of the State" (s 41QB(11)(c)(ii)). Asserted.
3. **On k031's date the Chapter did not exist.** Chapter 4A commenced on 1 July 2024 (endnotes, No. 50, 2024, sch 1). On 2 June 2023 neither Jake's possession nor the shop's sale was an offence under Chapter 4A. The scenario's citation of ss 41QD-41QE for a 2023 event is anachronistic. Whatever offence the shop committed in 2023 was under other law, which is not encoded. That law would be the WA Act and, by inference (not read here), the Customs and poisons controls that applied before Chapter 4A. Asserted.
4. **After 1 July 2024 the shop's sale is an offence, and so is its stock.** A supply in Australia by a shop that is not a pharmacist, or an authorised doctor or nurse practitioner, falls outside both the wholesale and the retail exceptions (s 41QB(5)-(11)). The penalty is 7 years or 5,000 penalty units; the strict liability offence carries 200 penalty units; the civil penalty is 7,000 penalty units for an individual, or 70,000 for a body corporate, for each unit supplied (s 41QB(4)). The shop's stock below the commercial quantity is a separate s 41QD offence: 12 months or 500 penalty units, strict liability 60, civil 1,000 per unit. Asserted.
5. **The personal-use exceptions differ between the two possession offences.** Under s 41QD(9) a retailer's own vape at work is excused if it is for personal use and within the "permitted quantity". The goods need not have been lawfully supplied. Under s 41QC(11A) a holding of at least the commercial quantity is excused only if it was "lawfully supplied", is for personal use, and is "less than 5 times the commercial quantity". The s 41QC exception does not reach the 100-times and 1,000-times offences. Asserted.
6. **The pharmacy route is narrow, but it has no age bar in the Act.** A supply is lawful under s 41QB(9)-(11) only if all of these hold:
   - the goods are on the Register, are notified exempt goods, or are covered by a s 41R determination;
   - the supplier is a pharmacist, or a medical practitioner or nurse practitioner with State Schedule 3 authority;
   - the supply is for smoking cessation, the management of nicotine dependence, or a s 41RA indication;
   - any vaping substance is in final dosage form;
   - the supply is consistent with the supplier's State authority.

   Recreational supply is never lawful. Asserted.
7. **A nicotine-free liquid is still vaping goods.** "Vaping substance" includes "any liquid or other substance for use in, or with, a vaping device" (s 41P(1)), whatever its nicotine content. Asserted (a liquid is vaping goods).
8. **Mixing one's own liquid is "manufacture".** The s 3(1) definition reaches any "part of the process of producing the goods". Section 41QA's exception requires therapeutic goods and a licence, conformity assessment document or consent. Asserted.
9. **Inference, not asserted:** "lawfully supplied" in s 41QC(11A)(a) is read as a supply that was not an offence. The Act does not define it, and State law may also bear on it. It is asked as a fact.
