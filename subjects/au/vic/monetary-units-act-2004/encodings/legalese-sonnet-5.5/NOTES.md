## Seat

The seat is a person or agency that sets, indexes or applies a Victorian fee or penalty.
It asks what a fee unit or a penalty unit is worth in a financial year, how an amount stated in units converts to dollars and is rounded, and when the values must be notified.
The seat is chosen because the Act is read by those who put a number of units into an instrument, by those who turn that number into dollars for a person who has to pay, and by those who keep the values up to date.
The Act mostly speaks to the Treasurer, the Minister and the Governor in Council.
There is no seat for an official: what the Treasurer or the Minister must do is encoded in the layer-1 modules for what it decides, and in the seat layer only for what it means to the person asking (the day by which a value must be notified, whether the Minister must give notice of the dollar amounts).
The seat layer is `vmu-seat.l4` (25 questions, each with `@export` and `@desc`, in the order of the text) and `vmu-cases-seat.l4` (worked cases, at least one for every question and a case the text does not answer where the question can have one).
Every question takes plain inputs, and the layer calls the layer-1 rules and restates none of them.
Financial years are named by the calendar year in which they start on 1 July, so the financial year starting on 1 July 2025 is 2025 (F4).
Amounts are in dollars as the text writes "$".

## What is encoded and what is not

Encoded, each with `@ref` to its provision and a test with the expected value written beside it:
- the meanings of "fee" and "penalty" in section 3, and the four meanings that section borrows from other Acts, as inputs (F14);
- the meaning of a number of fee units (section 4) and the calculation of a fee or a penalty from a number of units, with the roundings the text permits (section 7);
- the value of a fee unit and of a penalty unit, and the annual rate, for a financial year (sections 5 and 11), and the limits of section 5(6) and (7);
- every date and period the text states, in the time module `vmu-time.l4` (sections 2, 5(4), 6, 9(2) and 11), and every amount (sections 4, 5, 7, 9 and 11);
- the construction rules of section 8, the regulation-making power and the certificates of section 9, the notification duty of section 10 and the exclusion of the Subordinate Legislation Act 1994 in section 12;
- the provision index and the cross-reference rows (`vmu-provisions.l4`, `vmu-xrefs.l4`, `vmu-tests-xrefs.l4`), generated by `vmu-gen-index.py` from the text file.

Not encoded:
- the Subordinate Legislation Act 1994, the Sentencing Act 1991 and the Petroleum (Submerged Lands) Act 1982, which the text cites and which are not among the sources: the four meanings borrowed from them in section 3 are asked as inputs;
- section 1 as rules: it states purposes and creates none, so it is held as twelve lines of data and asserted (inert, reason 1);
- the day of the Royal Assent, which sections 1 to 12 do not state: it is an input to the commencement functions (F7);
- the amounts that the Treasurer fixes for years other than 2012, which the text does not state: they are inputs;
- any conversion between the forms the annual rate takes in different provisions (F1);
- the front matter and the endnotes (out of scope).

`vmu-time.l4` was written from the text and the `daydate` library alone; no other time module was used.
The time module takes the last day before 1 March and before 1 June as the day before the 1st, by day arithmetic (`Date (Day (Date 1 3 year) MINUS 1)`), which gives 29 February in a leap year; the tests assert 28 February 2023, 29 February 2024 and 29 February 2000.

## What the stable binary does not accept

The brief's rules for writing L4 that the stable binary accepts were followed: no `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` or `BECAUSE`; every `NOT` parenthesised; each `#ASSERT` on one line; arithmetic across a line break bracketed; `IMPORT` only of modules in this folder; `Date day month year`; `WAITUNTIL`; no `TYPICALLY` on a field; `@export` and `@desc` on every question.
The encoding was not re-run against the rejected forms, so this section does not say what the build does with them.
Met while writing, on `1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`:
- `head` is not in the prelude: reading the first element of a list is `at list 0`, or a `CONSIDER` over the list.
- A type ascription such as `(EMPTY : LIST OF NUMBER)` is a parse error.
- `(length list) EQUALS 0` did not typecheck; `count list EQUALS 0` did, and the tests use `count`.
