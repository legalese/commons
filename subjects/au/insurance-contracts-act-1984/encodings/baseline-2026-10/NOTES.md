## Seat

**The insured.** The plan entry puts it as the insured asking "What must I disclose, and must the insurer act in good faith?".
For the one life event, Chloe's cover inside her super fund, she is not the insured: the trustee is, and she is a life insured under a group life contract.
The Act treats that person separately (ss 25, 26(3), 27AA(2), 32), so the seat covers both, and the contract record says which one the person is.
Almost every remedy provision is addressed to the insurer ("the insurer may avoid", "the insurer may not refuse"); each is encoded as what the insured can expect: "What may the insurer do because of my relevant failure", "May the insurer refuse my claim because of something done after the contract".

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed. The Regulations are deposited and brought in for the 11% refund rate (reg 10), the standard cover for sickness and accident policies (regs 24, 26) and the interest rate on claims (reg 38). Reg 38's formula is not in the deposit, so that rate is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the insured, the life insured, the insurer, the third party beneficiary, the court (as the holder of judgments asked as facts), ASIC (only through the civil penalty).
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. Asked as facts: whether I took reasonable care (s 20B(2)-(4)); what the insurer would have done without the failure (ss 28(1), 29(1), (3)); whether reliance on a term would be a failure of utmost good faith (s 14); the prejudice to the insurer (s 54(1)); whether a court has relieved a fraudulent claim (s 56(2)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out: consumer insurance contract (s 11AB), the duty of disclosure (s 21), misrepresentation for each kind of contract (ss 20B(5), 26, 27), relevant failure (s 27AA), when a s 22 reminder was needed. Asked: "life policy" (Life Insurance Act 1995), fraud, the kind of contract under s 9.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one contract, one matter, one statement, one term, one act after the contract. A life contract with several kinds of cover is one case per kind (s 27A says so), so Chloe's death cover and her income protection are separate fixtures.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments; the record fields use the insured's own words.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. ASIC's powers, administration, penalties and infringement notices are out, apart from the civil penalty on an insurer that breaches utmost good faith (s 13(2A), s 75D(2)), which says what can be done to the other party.

## Why this scope

The seat's questions map onto five blocks of the Act, and the encoding takes each whole enough to answer them:

- **What must I tell the insurer:** ss 11AB, 12, 20A, 20B, 20E, 21, 22, 68(2).
- **Was what I said a misrepresentation, and does it count:** ss 23A, 25, 26, 27, 27AA.
- **What can the insurer do about it:** ss 28, 29, 30, 32, with reg 10.
- **Utmost good faith, both ways:** ss 13, 14, and the terms the Act will not let an insurer rely on (ss 37, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 53).
- **When I claim:** ss 40(3), 54, 56, 57, with reg 38; renewal and cancellation notice (ss 58, 59); standard sickness and accident cover (s 35, regs 24, 26).

Not encoded: ASIC and administration (Part IA, ss 14A, 55A, Part IXA apart from s 75D(2)); insurable interest (Part III); Key Facts Sheets; flood; interim and instalment contracts; liability insurance beyond s 40(3); third party beneficiaries' claims (ss 48-48A); ss 49, 50; subrogation; cancellation grounds (ss 59A, 60, 61); s 27A as a computation (each part is one case); s 29(5), (7), (8); s 30(3), (3A); the court's relief for fraud (ss 31, 56(2), asked as a fact only); late or broker-given notices (ss 69, 71); reasons (s 75); the other standard covers (regs 15-23, 27-33) and the reg 25(2) exclusions; protected genetic information.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Chloe has no duty of disclosure.** Cover inside a super fund is a group life contract, and the insured is the trustee (s 11(1), (4)). The duty of disclosure is the insured's (s 21(1)). A life insured is reached only through misrepresentation: s 25 for contracts that are not consumer contracts, and s 27AA(2), which holds her to the standard of the duty to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation "whether or not the contract is a consumer insurance contract". Her silence is not a relevant failure; a careless untrue answer is (INFERENCE from the words of ss 21, 25 and 27AA(2)). Asserted.
2. **A super fund's group cover is not a consumer insurance contract** on the s 11AB(1) test, which looks to "the personal, domestic or household purposes of the insured", and the insured is the trustee (INFERENCE: the trustee's purposes are the fund's). It does not matter much for Chloe, because s 27AA(2) applies the reasonable-care standard to her anyway. Asserted.
3. **An honest answer is safe, and so is a careful one.** For a contract that is not a consumer contract, a statement made on a belief a reasonable person would have held is not a misrepresentation (s 26(1)), and s 26(3) carries that to a person joining a super scheme. A life insured's untrue answer given with reasonable care is a misrepresentation but not a relevant failure (s 27AA(2)), while the same answer from the insured under a business policy is (s 27AA(1)(b)(ii)). Asserted.
4. **A blank answer is never a misrepresentation, and a blank question is a waiver.** s 20B(5) and s 27 say failing to answer is not a misrepresentation; s 21(3) deems the insurer to have waived disclosure of the matter the question asked about. Asserted for s 21(3).
5. **On death cover, the insurer's non-fraud remedies end three years after Chloe became a life insured.** s 29(3) lets it avoid only "within 3 years" and only if it would not have insured her on any terms; s 29(10) lets it reduce death cover's sum insured only "before the expiration of 3 years" and switches off s 29(6). The three years run from the day she became a life insured, not from when the trustee's policy began (s 32(1)(b)). On 16 September 2029 a careless misstatement about her health leaves the insurer with no remedy on her death cover; on her income protection, which has no surrender value and is not death cover, it can still vary the contract. Fraud lets it avoid at any time (s 29(2)). Asserted.
6. **The formulas in ss 29(4), 30(1), 44(3), 58(6) and reg 38 are not in the deposited text.** The source has the definitions of the letters but the formula itself, an image in the printed Act, did not survive conversion. The encoding takes s 29(4) and s 30(1) as S x P / Q and s 44(3) as A x S / P, which is what the definitions imply (INFERENCE, to be checked against the printed Act), and takes the reg 38 rate as an input. Asserted on the inferred formulas.
7. **The utmost good faith runs both ways and reaches a third party beneficiary only after the contract.** s 13(1) implies the duty on "each party"; (3) includes a third party beneficiary; (4) only "after the contract is entered into". An insurer that breaches it faces a civil penalty of 5,000 penalty units, which for a body corporate s 75D(2) multiplies by 10, or raises to three times the benefit, or to 10% of turnover capped at 2.5 million penalty units, whichever is greatest. Asserted.
8. **A pre-existing condition exclusion fails for a condition Chloe could not have known of.** s 47(2): the insurer "may not rely on" it where the insured "was not aware of, and a reasonable person in the circumstances could not be expected to have been aware of, the sickness or disability". Asserted.
9. **Something done after the contract, including a late claim, rarely defeats the claim outright.** s 54(1) turns a right to refuse into a reduction for prejudice unless the act could cause or contribute to a covered loss (2), and even then not where the insured proves no part of the loss was caused by it (3), or for the part not caused (4), or where the act was needed for safety or to preserve property (5). An omission counts (6). The Act sets no general time limit for making a claim: a policy's time limit works through s 54 (INFERENCE), and for claims-made liability cover, notice of facts given before the cover ends keeps a later claim covered (s 40(3)). Asserted.
10. **The Regulations refer to a section of the Act the deposit does not have.** Reg 13A (protected genetic information, added by the Insurance Contracts Amendment (Genetic Testing Protections in Life Insurance) Regulations 2026) is made "for the purposes of subsection 33F(5) of the Act"; the deposited Act, compiled at 1 March 2024, has no s 33F. Whatever limits the Act now places on an insurer's use of genetic test results in life insurance are not in the deposit and are not encoded. That matters to Chloe's disclosure at 23. Not asserted.
11. **Standard cover does not protect income protection held inside a life policy.** Reg 24(2) takes out "a life policy" and "a continuous disability insurance contract incorporated within a life policy", so the reg 26 minimum amounts (for example $25,000 on death where no amount is stated, and a daily amount after the first 14 days of total disablement) apply only to stand-alone sickness and accident policies. Asserted.
12. **A misstated date of birth is not a relevant failure remedy.** s 29(1)(b) sends it to s 30, where an under-insured life gets a refund of overpaid premium with interest at 11% a year (reg 10) or a higher sum insured. Asserted for s 30(2); the s 29(1)(b) hand-off itself is not asserted.
