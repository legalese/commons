## Seat

**The parent (the claimant)**: "What family payments and child care subsidy can I get for my child?"
The Act's benefits are claimed by and paid to an individual for an FTB child; that individual is who the Act is applied to, not the Secretary who decides or Services Australia that pays.
Every exported question is the parent's: "Does my child meet the immunisation requirements?", "Will my FTB child rate be reduced?", "Am I eligible for family tax benefit?", "How much FTB Part A do I get a year?", "Is newborn supplement added for the day?", "Am I entitled to the newborn upfront payment?", "What is my CCS percentage?", "How many hours are subsidised each fortnight?".

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The Minister's Rules and the vaccination schedules are not deposited, so their effect is asked as a fact; the indexed rates are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the individual, the partner, the FTB child, the Secretary, the approved child care service.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is asked conditionally.** Followed. The Secretary's determination that a child meets the requirements (s 6(6)), the choice of which member of a couple is eligible (s 26(2)), the date of the s 61B(3) notice, and the higher-rate-child fee-reduction determinations (Sch 2 cl 3B) are all inputs.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed. "FTB child", "meets the immunisation requirements", "higher income free area", "income excess", "subsidised hours" and "higher rate child" are worked out. "member of a couple" and "Australian resident" (Social Security Act 1991), "parental leave pay" and "flexible PPL day" (Paid Parental Leave Act 2010), "adjusted taxable income" (Schedule 3, beyond cl 3) and "senior secondary school child" are asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Departed from in part. The FTB rate is the family's (the counted FTB children and the two incomes); the newborn supplement is one child; the CCS questions are one session or one fortnight. These are the units the provisions themselves use.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in the comments and `@desc` lines.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Commencement, the Secretary's powers and Schedule 4's indexation mechanics are out.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names s 6, Part 3 (FTB eligibility), s 58AA, Part 4A (CCS) and Schedule 1 (the newborn supplement). The brief adds FTB Parts A and B rate calculation. That is what is encoded, with the definitions each answer needs: the FTB child limbs in s 22, the income tests in Schedule 1 and the couple income rules in Schedule 3 cl 3, and the CCS percentage, cap and hours in Schedule 2.

Left out because no life asks for them and each is a self-contained scheme: the maintenance income test, rent assistance, the closed energy supplements, FTB advances, the multiple birth allowance, the return-to-work Part B method, shared care and blended families, ACCS, the stillborn baby payment, the health check reduction, and the Administration Act's claims and reconciliation (a separate subject).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Parental leave pay cancels the newborn supplement, and with it the $500.** Sch 1 cl 35A(8): the clause "does not apply, and is taken never to have applied … if parental leave pay is payable to the individual for that child"; cl 35A(9) does the same where the partner is paid for a flexible PPL day. s 58AA pays the upfront amount only where newborn supplement "is added". The lives give the newborn payments alongside Paid Parental Leave (c064, d040, k054); on the text they do not go together. Asserted.
2. **Parental leave pay also zeroes FTB Part B for the day.** Sch 1 cl 28C: the Part B rate "is nil if the day is a day for which parental leave pay is payable to the individual, or the individual's partner". Asserted.
3. **Immunisation reduces FTB but removes CCS entirely.** s 61B reduces the FTB child rate by "$737.30 for each day" in the reduction period (Sch 1 cl 7(2)); s 61C keeps the entitlement alive at nil. For CCS, meeting s 6 is a condition of eligibility for each session (s 85BA(1)(a)(iii)). Asserted.
4. **Missing the grace period backdates the reduction to the notice date.** s 61B(4): the grace period "ends 63 days after" the notice date; s 61B(2): the reduction period begins "on the day specified as the date of the notice". A child immunised on day 100 costs 100 days of reduction. Counting day 63 as inside the grace period is an inference. Asserted.
5. **s 6 has no conscientious-objection ground.** The seven grounds are the schedule, certified contraindication or natural immunity, a vaccine study, a certified shortage, certified overseas vaccination, and a Secretary's determination. Asserted.
6. **The FTB Part B income test is applied to two different incomes.** Sch 3 cl 3(2): for the $100,000 limit (cl 28B) the couple's income is the higher earner's; for the cl 32 income test it is the lower earner's. FTB Part A, by contrast, adds the two (cl 3(1)). Asserted.
7. **A single parent's Part B has no income test below the limit.** Sch 1 cl 29(1) adds the standard rate and the supplement with no reduction step; only a member of a couple has one (cl 29(2)). Asserted.
8. **Part B stops at 13 for couples, not for single parents.** Sch 1 cl 28D: nil for a member of a couple whose youngest FTB child "has turned 13", unless a grandparent or great-grandparent. Asserted.
9. **The FTB Part A supplement's $80,000 limit is not indexed.** Sch 1 cl 38A(1A) sets it; the Schedule 4 cl 2 table lists the $600 supplement (item 8A) but not this limit. Its item 18 $80,000 is the CCS threshold in Sch 2 cl 3(4). The encoding holds it fixed. Asserted (via the $90,000 and $100,000 cases).
10. **A second child under 6 in care gets a higher CCS percentage.** Sch 2 cll 3A, 3B: a younger child under 6 is a "higher rate child" while an older child under 6 is in CCS; its percentage is 95%, 80% or 50% in fixed bands, with two formula bands between, never lower than the basic cl 3 percentage (cl 3A(2)), and not for in home care. Ella (c071) qualifies while Oliver is under 6: at $90,000 she gets 95%, Oliver 88%. Asserted.
11. **The formulas the text deposit lost are recovered, and they are images in the authorised Word text.** The newborn supplement (Sch 1 cl 35B), the basic CCS taper (Sch 2 cl 3(2)) and the higher-rate tapers (cl 3A(4), (5)) each read "using the following formula:" and stop in the `.txt`. The authorised Word rendering sets each as an equation image; they are transcribed in `C2004A00490.formulas.md` and now encoded. Asserted.
12. **The activity test matters only above three days.** Sch 2 cl 11(1A): the 3 day guarantee "is 72"; cl 12 lifts it to 100 for "more than 48 hours" of recognised activity; a couple takes the lower result. Asserted.
13. **Marriage plays no part.** Neither s 22 nor cl 35A asks whether the parents are married. Asserted.
14. **Two events cannot be answered from this compilation.** d002 (1996) predates the Act; k002 asks about "the maternity payment of the day", which is not in the 2026 text. Asserted for d002; k002 is answered only for its FTB half. (k002's maternity payment is now answered by the 2006 arm: finding 18.)
15. **The basic CCS percentage falls one point for every $5,000 above the lower threshold.** Sch 2 cl 3(2): 90 − (ATI − $80,000) / 5,000, to 2 decimal places, which reaches 0 exactly at the upper threshold ($80,000 + $450,000). $82,345 gives 89.53; $200,000 gives 66. Rounding a half upwards is an inference: the clause says only "rounding the result to 2 decimal places". Asserted.
16. **The higher-rate tapers fall one point for every $3,000.** cl 3A(4): 115 − (ATI − lower (other rate) threshold) / 3,000, capped at 95%, so the cap binds until $60,000 above the lower threshold ($132,466 at printed rates); $150,000 gives 89.16. cl 3A(5): 80 − (ATI − third threshold) / 3,000, falling from 80% at $256,756 to 50% at $346,756, which joins the fixed bands. $300,000 gives 65.59. Asserted.
17. **The newborn supplement is a yearly figure for a 13-week entitlement.** Sch 1 cl 35B: $1,501.50 × 365 / 91 for a first child born alive, for each child of a multiple birth, and for the first child entrusted or adopted; $500.50 × 365 / 91 otherwise. The dollar amounts are indexed (Sch 4 item 7A). Multiplying by 365/91 turns the 91-day amount into a yearly rate that s 58(3) turns back into a daily rate. Asserted (k054, and a second child).

## The 2006 arm (REQ-0062)

`fa-2006-maternity.l4` encodes, from the compilation in force on 30 August 2006 (C2006C00402):

- the maternity payment: its four cases (s 36(2) a parent, (3) a non-parent given care, (4) a stillborn child, (5) an adoption), only one individual eligible (s 37), the amount (s 66) and its indexation days (Schedule 4 item 17A);
- what a new FTB child under 13 adds to the Method 1 maximum rate of Part A (Sch 1 cll 3, 7, 38A), to the higher income free area (cl 2) and to the Part B standard rate (cl 30).

The seat and defaults are those of this encoding.

- **Inputs.** Who is eligible for FTB within 13 weeks and the Secretary's s 37 determinations are asked as facts.
- **Rates.** The FTB rates are inputs (`Rates in 2006`), and `the rates as printed in C2006C00402` holds the printed figures as labelled examples.
- **Not encoded.** The maternity immunisation allowance, s 38 (death), the income tests, Method 2, rent assistance, the large family supplement, the multiple birth allowance and sharing. A third child or a multiple birth answers `not covered`.

18. **Jake's maternity payment was $4,000 (k002).** s 66(1): "The amount of maternity payment in respect of a child is $4,000."

    - **Why $4,000 on 30 August 2006.** The figure took effect on 1 July 2006 (Act No. 59, 2004, Schedule 2 Part 3; the compilation incorporates it). Schedule 4 indexes it as "MAT" (item 17A) on 20 March and 20 September. No indexation day fell between 1 July and 19 September 2006, so the "current figure" (s 3(1)) on Jake's birth was the printed $4,000. From 20 September 2006 it is an indexed figure no deposited text holds.
    - **A reading.** The amount is the one in force on the day of the birth. The Act ties it to "the child", not to the claim, and the life dates the event 1 October 2006.
    - **Who gets it.** Both Graham and Sue are parents eligible for FTB for Jake within 13 weeks, so both "would otherwise be eligible", and the Secretary determines which one is (s 37(2)). Never both in a couple (s 37(4)).

    The life gives no figure for the payment. Asserted.
19. **Lump sum or instalments is not in this Act.** The maternity payment is claimed and paid under the Family Assistance Administration Act. This Act's s 38 speaks of amounts "paid to the individual under subsection 47(2) of that Act", so payment in more than one amount was possible in some case. Whether Sue was paid a lump sum is a question for the Administration Act as in force in 2006. That Act is not deposited as at 2006; the question is raised as REQ-0064.
20. **The 2006 FTB rates are not printed in the 2006 compilation.** Schedule 4 indexes the FTB child rates, the supplement amounts, the free areas and the Part B standard rates every 1 July from CPI index numbers (cl 3 table; cl 5 factor, transcribed in `C2004A00490.2006-07-01.formulas.md`). Clause 7 may reset the under-13 and 13-15 child rates from the pension: CPC rate × 16.6% and × 21.6%. The compilation prints $2,920.00 (under 13), $600 (Part A supplement), $2,569.60 and $1,781.20 (Part B), but those are not the 2006-07 rates. The indexed figures are set by that arithmetic, not by an instrument, and no deposited text holds them. They are taken as inputs and raised as REQ-0063. On the printed figures Jake adds $3,520 a year to the maximum rate of Part A and nothing to Part B, because Chloe, the youngest before him, was 3. Asserted.
21. **The 2006 compilation as served carries a later definition.** In C2006C00402, s 3(7) "CPC rate" has a "pension supplement" limb (Module BA of Pension Rate Calculator A). That limb is from after 2006. The Register flags the compilation as "affected by a retrospective amendment" (Act No. 49 of 2012), which may explain it. s 3(7) feeds only the cl 7 adjustment, which is not encoded. Recorded, not asserted.
22. **The workbench fetcher drops table cells from an older compilation's EPUB.** `epub_text()` in `harvest/fetch/fetch-source.py` matches a `<td>` that holds paragraphs, skips it as a container, and resumes after it, so the paragraphs inside are lost. Here that is every rate table (Sch 1 cll 2, 7, 14, 30; Sch 4 cll 2, 3). The 2006 deposit was converted with that function plus one change that keeps those paragraphs. Its output is a line-for-line superset of the fetcher's (`.meta.json` "converter"). The workbench is not changed here.
