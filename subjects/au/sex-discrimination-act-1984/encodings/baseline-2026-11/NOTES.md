# Sex Discrimination Act 1984 — baseline encoding: harassment at work and the positive duty

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the
individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no
coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

The focus is the positive duty that Part IIA (s 47C) has put on employers and businesses since 13 December 2022. Around
it are the provisions that a person who has been harassed, or made to work in a hostile workplace, meets on the way to a
claim: what counts as harassment, when it is unlawful at work, who else is liable, victimisation, and the complaint,
time-limit and costs steps in the Australian Human Rights Commission Act 1986. This Act had no earlier encoding.

Every question is exported for Legalese Cloud with `@export`, and every input has a `@desc`.

## Seat

**The employee, worker or job seeker on the receiving end of the conduct.** This is the person who was harassed, made to
work in a hostile workplace, discriminated against at work, or victimised for speaking up. The questions are the ones
that person asks:

- Was I sexually harassed? Is it unlawful?
- Does my employer have to do something about it?
- Is my employer liable for what my colleague did?
- Can I complain, and by when?
- Could I end up paying costs?

Part IIA speaks only to the duty holder ("must take reasonable and proportionate measures"). The encoding turns it into
what it means for the person: whether the duty reaches the conduct they suffered, and which paragraphs of s 47C reach
it. No other seat is written. The natural second seat would be the employer's: what the duty requires of me, and the
deadlines in a compliance notice under AHRC Act ss 35F-35H.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed, with the same addition the Age Discrimination
  Act baseline makes. The Act sends complaints to the AHRC Act (s 48 note; AHRC Act s 3(1)(c)), so ss 3(1), 46P,
  46PH(1)(b), 46PO(2) and 46PSA of that Act are brought in. No instrument sets a number.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed. They are the duty holder (an employer or a person conducting a business or
  undertaking); its employees, workers and agents; "any person" (s 47C(4)); the person harassed; the Commission; the
  President; and the court.
- **Judgments handed to someone else are answered conditionally.** Followed. These are inputs:
  - the reasonable-person tests in ss 28A(1), 28AA(1)(b) and 28M(2)(c), with their listed circumstances in the
    input's description;
  - whether the organisation has established that it took all reasonable steps (s 106(2));
  - whether an allegation has been proved false and not made in good faith (s 47A(3));
  - whether a complaint is reasonably arguable (AHRC Act s 46P(1A));
  - the court's satisfaction under AHRC Act s 46PSA(4) and (6).

  Whether the positive duty was complied with (s 47C(1), weighed by the (6) factors) is the Commission's judgment, and
  the encoding does not ask it. The encoding answers whether the duty reaches the conduct, not whether it was met.
- **The Act's own definitions are worked out; borrowed terms are asked.** Followed, with one departure.
  - Worked out: sexual harassment (s 28A), harassment on the ground of sex (ss 28AA, 8), hostile workplace environment
    (ss 28M, 8A), the s 28B situations, victimisation (s 47A), and the s 47C(2)-(5) lists.
  - Asked as facts: "worker", "person conducting a business or undertaking" and "workplace". These have their Work
    Health and Safety Act 2011 meanings (s 4).
  - **Departure:** sex discrimination in work (ss 5, 14(1), (2), 15, 16, 17) is the Act's own term, but it is asked as
    one input where s 47C(2)(a) and s 106(1)(a) need it. Encoding Division 1 is outside this focus.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one piece of conduct towards one person, judged against one
  organisation (the employer, or the business the questions are about).
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out: the Commission's functions (Part III), the Sex
  Discrimination Commissioner (Part VI), exemptions granted by the Commission, and commencement. The exception is the
  commencement of Act No. 85, 2022, which is used as a date because s 47C and s 28M did not exist before it (finding 1).

## Scope

**In:** ss 8, 8A, 28A, 28AA, 28AB, 28B(1)-(8), 28M, 47A (with (3)), 47B, 47C(1)-(5), 94 (penalties), 105, 106, 110;
AHRC Act ss 3(1)(c), 46P(1), (1A), (2), (3), 46PH(1)(b), 46PO(2), 46PSA(2), (4), (5), (6).

**Out, and why:**

- **Sex discrimination itself and the exemptions** (ss 5-7D, 14-27; Part II Division 4): a different focus, and a large
  one. One input stands in for it.
- **Harassment in areas other than work** (ss 28C-28L: qualifying bodies, registered organisations, employment
  agencies, education, goods and services, accommodation, land, clubs, Commonwealth programs). Where harassment falls
  outside every s 28B situation, the answer is NOTHING, not no.
- **s 47C(6)-(8)** (the matters taken into account, and other duties not affected): compliance is not decided.
- **AHRC Act Division 4A** (inquiries, compliance notices, reconsideration, review, enforcement, undertakings): these
  are powers used against the duty holder, and none of them is a step the person takes.
- **The rest of the complaint process:** the other grounds for terminating a complaint (AHRC Act s 46PH(1)(a), (c)-(h),
  (1B)), conciliation, remedies, any further time a court allows (s 46PO(2)), and representative complaints beyond
  s 46P(3).
- **s 9, the Act's constitutional reach:** assumed to reach the organisation.
- **The value of a penalty unit under s 94:** not deposited.

## Regulative rules

None is written. s 47C imposes a continuing duty with no period ("must take reasonable and proportionate measures to
eliminate, as far as possible"), and whether it is met is a weighed judgment (s 47C(6)). A `PARTY … MUST … WITHIN` rule
would therefore carry no deadline, and no breach the person could state. The deadlines in this area belong to the duty
holder (AHRC Act ss 35F(2)(d), 35G(2)(c), 35H(1A): 21 days each) and so to an employer seat. The person's own periods
(24 months, 60 days) are plain date questions.

## Fork register

These rulings are recorded word for word from `docs/ENCODING-GUIDE.md` §5, because this encoding's `JUST FALSE` and
`NOTHING` answers rest on them.

> **Periods, "where" and scope limits** (a question raised on 2026-11-05 while checking an encoding of the Retirement and Re-employment Act 1993, not a bench): (a) a period the text fixes in its own words ("within one month", "begins from ... until the employee attains the age", "on the last day of employment"): outside the period the answer is `JUST FALSE`; (b) a "where ..." condition: when it fails the answer is `JUST FALSE`; (c) a scope limit with no "only" (for example "below" the minimum age): outside the scope the answer is `NOTHING`. Meng's words, verbatim apart from the trigger word that ended the message: "i agree with your lean: no for a and b; nothing for c." Typed 2026-11-05T04:30:00.322Z, ruled 2026-11-05.

> **Questions with no convention: decide case by case (Meng, typed 2026-11-05T10:25:24Z).** Six questions the rulings above leave open are decided site by site, at encoding time, on the text's own words and the encoder's best judgment: an "unless" or "except" once it is met; a bare "must" not done; "listed in Schedule N"; a duty whose "if" or "where" is unmet; "(other than X)"; a missing event in the state ledger. Each such site gets one fork line (the provision, the function, the answer taken, the other reading) for the human reviewer to overrule, and the rulings above still govern whatever they cover.

Fork lines (provision; function; answer taken; the other reading):

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The positive duty and the hostile-workplace prohibition start on 13 December 2022.** Act No. 85, 2022 added Part
   IIA, s 8A and s 28M (endnote 4). Endnote 3 gives commencement as "Sch 1, Sch 2 (items 1–13), Sch 7 (item 16) and
   Sch 8 (items 2, 3): 13 Dec 2022". The endnote does not say which item inserted which section. That all three came in
   on that day is read from it, not stated. Conduct on 12 December 2022 is outside both. Asserted.
2. **A person cannot enforce the positive duty.** s 110 gives no right of action for an act unlawful under Part IIA. The
   AHRC Act's "unlawful discrimination", which is what a complaint must allege (s 46P), reaches "Part II of the Sex
   Discrimination Act 1984" only (s 3(1)(c)). Enforcement is the Commission's: it may inquire when it "reasonably
   suspects" non-compliance, give compliance notices, and accept undertakings (AHRC Act ss 35B, 35F, 35K). What the
   person can complain of is the conduct itself (harassment, a hostile workplace, victimisation), which is in Part II.
   Asserted (s 110; a complaint alleging only a Part IIA failure).
3. **The duty reaches customers and clients, but only through s 28B(6) or (8).** Conduct by "any person" towards the
   duty holder's employees or workers is covered only if it is unlawful under s 28B(6) or (8): that is, if the
   harassment occurs "in connection with" the person being a worker or an employee. A customer's advance at work is
   covered. The same customer's advance outside any work connection is not. Asserted.
4. **The duty also protects people who are not yet staff.** Under s 47C(1)(a), the duty reaches harassment *by* the duty
   holder, its employees, workers and agents, whoever the target is. That includes a job applicant (s 28B(1)(b), (3)(b)).
   An agent's discrimination in hiring is covered through (2)(a) and (3)(d). Asserted.
5. **Workers who are not employees fall in a gap between the duty and vicarious liability.** s 47C protects "workers in
   the business or undertaking" ((3)(c), (5)(b)), such as contractors, labour-hire workers and volunteers. s 106 makes an
   organisation liable only for its "employee or agent". A contractor who creates a hostile workplace is within the
   duty, but outside s 106. Asserted (s 106 answers NOTHING; s 47C answers yes).
6. **s 47C(2)(d) and (4)(c) cover victimisation "within the meaning of section 47A", not victimisation "unlawful under"
   it.** Paragraphs (2)(a)-(c) say "unlawful under". The s 47A(3) defence (an allegation proved false and not made in
   good faith) operates only "in a proceeding for a contravention of subsection (1)". Read literally, an act of
   victimisation for which the defence is proved is still conduct the duty holder must work to eliminate. This may be
   deliberate, or a drafting asymmetry. Asserted on the literal reading.
7. **Vicarious liability does not reach victimisation, but accessory liability does.** s 106(1) covers Divisions 1, 2
   and 3 of Part II, but not Division 5 (s 47A). s 105 covers "Division 1, 2, 3 or 5". So an employer is not
   vicariously liable under s 106 for a supervisor's victimisation, unless it caused, instructed, induced, aided or
   permitted it (s 105). Asserted.
8. **Vicarious liability does reach a hostile workplace.** s 28M is in Division 3, so s 106(1)(b) applies to an
   employee's hostile-workplace conduct, subject to the all-reasonable-steps defence in s 106(2). Asserted.
9. **Discrimination on grounds other than sex is outside the positive duty.** s 47C(2)(a) names "discrimination on the
   ground of a person's sex". Discrimination because of pregnancy, breastfeeding, family responsibilities, marital or
   relationship status, sexual orientation, gender identity or intersex status is unlawful under the same ss 14-17, but
   it is not in the list. Not asserted: the discrimination input asks about sex only.
10. **s 28B(5)-(8) swallow most of (1)-(4).** Any harassment "in connection with" either person's being a worker,
    employee, employer or business is unlawful under (5)-(8). The specific subsections still matter, because s 47C(4)(a)
    names only (6) and (8). Asserted (the subsection lists).
11. **The 24 months is a discretion, not a bar, and its end date is not fixed by the text.** The President "may
    terminate" a complaint lodged "more than 24 months after" the conduct (AHRC Act s 46PH(1)(b)). Lodging exactly 24
    months later is not "more than". For conduct on 29 February 2024, the encoding takes 24 months as ending on 28
    February 2026 (daydate's `add months` clamps the day), so 1 March 2026 is late. A court could read it as 1 March.
    Asserted.
12. **60 days to go to court after termination** (AHRC Act s 46PO(2)), counted from "the date of issue of the notice",
    with "such further time as the court concerned allows". Asserted.
13. **Costs protection for the person bringing the claim.** If the applicant loses, they "must not be ordered" to pay
    the other side's costs, unless (a) the proceedings were vexatious or without reasonable cause, (b) the applicant's
    unreasonable act or omission caused those costs, or (c) the respondent won and has neither a significant power
    advantage nor significant resources relative to the applicant (AHRC Act s 46PSA(5), (6)). If the applicant wins on
    one or more grounds, the court "must" order the respondent to pay the applicant's costs. The exception is costs the
    applicant's own unreasonable act or omission caused, which the court "is not required" to order (s 46PSA(2), (4)).
    Asserted.
14. **Victimisation is both unlawful and an offence.** It is unlawful under s 47A. Under s 94 it is an offence: 25
    penalty units or 3 months' imprisonment, or both, for an individual; 100 penalty units for a body corporate.
    Asserted.
