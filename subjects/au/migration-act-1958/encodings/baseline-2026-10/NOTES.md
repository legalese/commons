# Migration Act 1958 — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent
test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

One module, `mig-visas.l4`, holds every rule. Each question is a function named as the
visa applicant or holder would ask it, and is published with `@export`, with a `@desc` on
every input: 50 exported questions. One case file, `mig-cases-visas.l4`, is generated by a
script (scratchpad `gen_migration-act-1958_baseline.py`). Its expected values were written
by hand from the text.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers everything that
encoding covered:

- ss 13, 14, 5F, 5CB, 29, 30, 65, 72, 73;
- s 82(2), (3), (5), (6), (8), and ss 79, 81(2);
- ss 245AB, 245AC, 245AG;
- ss 140E, 140GB, 140H, 140Q, 486R(5).

It reuses that encoding's hand-checked values where they are still right: the 1,200 and 300
penalty units under s 140Q, the s 5F/5CB couples, and the s 245AB reasonable-steps
exception. It also adds the Migration Regulations 1994, which were not deposited when that
encoding was written. Every answer it left as an input "in the Regulations" is now worked
out where the lives need it.

## Seat

**The visa applicant and visa holder.** In the life this means two people:

- **Priya Nair**, an Indian national on a student visa since 2019. She marries Daniel, an
  Australian citizen, on 30 November 2024. She then applies in Australia for the partner
  visas, waits on a Bridging A, holds the temporary visa (820) and then the permanent visa
  (801), and leaves for London days after the 801 is granted.
- **The machine-learning engineer from Chennai** whom Jarrah sponsors on a Skills in
  Demand visa (482).

The plan entry put the seat this way: "What visa do I need, may I leave and return, may I
work?" The Act and the Regulations are mostly addressed to the Minister and to sponsors.
So the encoding asks what each provision means for the person who holds the visa:

- what they must show;
- what they may and may not do;
- what can take the visa away;
- what a sponsor must give them.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed. The
  Regulations are deposited, so they set the numbers:
  - the two-year wait for the 801;
  - the five-year travel period;
  - 35 days on a Bridging A;
  - 40 or 48 hours of work a fortnight;
  - the 90, 180 and 365 days of condition 8607;
  - the $73,150 and $135,000 thresholds;
  - the $1,475 and $4,015 charges.

  The ministerial instruments that the Regulations point to are not deposited, so they are
  inputs (see Edition).
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the Minister, the sponsoring partner, the
  approved work sponsor, the employer and the ART. The ART appears only in the Bridging A
  end dates.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is asked conditionally.** Followed. Each of these is
  asked as an input:
  - "if the Minister is satisfied" for s 65;
  - the public interest criteria;
  - the "substantial ties" of 155.212(3A);
  - the skills the Minister "considers necessary";
  - "reasonable to disregard";
  - the s 116 grounds.
- **The Act's own definitions are worked out; borrowed terms are asked.** Followed. These
  are worked out from facts:
  - lawful and unlawful non-citizen;
  - spouse and de facto partner;
  - long-term partner relationship;
  - Australian permanent resident;
  - approved work sponsor;
  - "allows to work".

  The validity of a marriage (Marriage Act 1961 Part VA, through s 12) and ANZSCO codes
  are asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one application, one visa, one trip,
  one nomination.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed: every input's `@desc` glosses
  it.
- **Operative provisions in, machinery out.** Followed. Visa classes, criteria, conditions,
  cessation and cancellation grounds are in. These are out:
  - the Department's and the ART's procedures, beyond notice before cancellation;
  - regulation-making powers;
  - detention and removal.
- **Departure.** The plan's seat names Priya and "the sponsor's worker". The sponsor's own
  duties (ss 140E, 140GB, 140H, 140Q; regs 2.79, 2.80, 2.87) are encoded only as far as
  they reach the worker:
  - is my employer a sponsor;
  - must my nomination be approved;
  - what must my sponsor pay me;
  - what may it not charge me;
  - what does it risk.

## Why this scope

The Act runs to about 1.5 million characters and the Regulations to about 3 million.
Following the brief, the encoding keeps strictly to the two visa holders in the life:

- who needs a visa;
- the criteria for the four subclasses the events name (820, 801, 155, 482), plus the
  Bridging A that sits between application and grant;
- the conditions the holder must keep;
- the cancellation grounds as they reach the holder;
- what a sponsor owes the person it sponsors.

**Left out:**

- **Visa subclasses:** every other subclass, including the student visa's own criteria,
  Bridging B to F, and other partner and skilled visas.
- **Within subclasses 482, 820 and 801:**
  - the 482 Labour Agreement stream;
  - the routes for prospective-marriage visa holders (820.211(5)-(9));
  - secondary applicants.
- **Criteria asked as one fact:** the public interest criteria (Schedule 4), the special
  return criteria (Schedule 5) and Schedule 3.
- **Application charges:** the partner visa charges. Item 1124B's ten tables are keyed on
  transitional cases.
- **Sponsors:**
  - the criteria for approving a sponsor;
  - labour market testing;
  - the other sponsorship obligations (records, notifications, inspectors);
  - sanctions (ss 140K-140M).
- **Cancellation:** character cancellation (s 501) and every cancellation power other than
  s 116.
- **Other:** merits and judicial review; detention and removal; the Minister's personal
  powers.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The resident return visa in d035 was unnecessary, and gave less than the visa Priya
   already held.**
   - **What the 801 gives.** A subclass 801 is a "Permanent visa permitting the holder to
     travel to and enter Australia for 5 years from date of grant" (cl 801.511). Leaving
     days after the grant and returning about 14 months later is inside that period
     (ss 79, 81(2)).
   - **What the 155 could give.** Days after her grant, Priya could not meet 155.212(2):
     2 years in the last 5 as a permanent visa holder or citizen. Her only route is
     155.212(3A), substantial ties as the Minister is satisfied. That gives "a period of
     one year from the date of the grant" or less (155.511(c)), for a $1,475 charge
     (item 1128).

   Asserted.
2. **The two-year wait for the 801 does not fit the dates unless the couple had been
   together three years.**
   - **The rule.** 801.221(2)(d) requires that "at least 2 years have passed since the
     application was made". The only exception that fits is (6A): a long-term partner
     relationship at application, meaning 3 years, or 2 with a dependent child of both
     (reg 1.03). Subclause (7) lets the Minister grant early only on a partner's death
     or family violence.
   - **The dates.** If Priya applied on 10 January 2025 (the d033 date; INFERRED), a grant
     "days before" 9 January 2027 is under two years. They married on 30 November 2024
     and had no child until June 2027, so the grant needs three years together before
     January 2025. The scenario does not say.

   Asserted on both assumptions.
3. **The bridging visa sits only between the application and the 820, and it carries the
   student visa's work limit at 40 hours, not 48.**
   - **No gap between the two partner visas.** The 820 lasts "until the holder is notified
     that his or her application for a Subclass 801 (Partner) visa has been decided"
     (820.511). The Bridging A itself ends at the grant, or 35 days after a refusal
     (010.511).
   - **The work limit.** The Bridging A takes "whichever of conditions 8101, ... 8105, ...
     applies to the visa held by the holder at the time of application" (010.611(4)). A
     student visa carries 8105 (cl 500.611). The 48 hours of 8105(2A) apply only to a
     student visa "or a bridging visa granted on the basis of a valid application for a
     Subclass 500 (Student) visa". Priya's bridging visa was granted on a partner
     application, so the limit is 40 hours a fortnight in session.
   - **The exception.** None of this applies if she is in a class the Minister has
     specified for 010.611(1)(c). That instrument is not deposited.
   - **Travel.** Her Bridging A is permission only to remain, so leaving ends it under
     s 82(8) (INFERENCE).

   Asserted.
4. **Neither partner visa carries any condition.** Clauses 820.6 and 801.6: "Conditions:
   Nil". Once the 820 is granted, Priya may work without limit. Asserted.
5. **A permanent visa holder in Australia is safe from s 116(1), but not from s 116(1AB)
   or (1AC).** Section 117(2) bars cancelling a permanent visa "under subsection 116(1)" if
   the holder is in the migration zone and was immigration cleared on last entering.
   - **What it does not reach.** The bar does not mention subsections (1AA) (identity),
     (1AB) (incorrect information relied on) or (1AC) (a benefit for sponsorship). On
     Priya's return those remain open.
   - **Notice.** Before a s 116 cancellation the holder must be told the grounds and
     invited to answer (s 119), unless the holder is outside Australia and the Minister
     proceeds under s 128.

   Asserted.
6. **Section 235 is gone; the Act now penalises the employer, and the worker's own risk is
   paying for a sponsorship.**
   - **Section 235.** The endnotes record "s 235 ... rep No 1, 2024".
   - **The employer.** For d047, an employer that allows an unlawful non-citizen to work,
     or a lawful one to work in breach of a work-related condition, contravenes s 245AB
     or s 245AC. That is 240 penalty units as a civil penalty, and 2 years or 360 penalty
     units as an offence. It escapes only if it took "reasonable steps at reasonable times
     to verify".
   - **The worker.** He loses his status (s 14) or his visa (s 116(1)(b)).
   - **Paying for sponsorship.** If he offers or gives a benefit for a nomination or a job,
     he contravenes s 245AS himself (240 penalty units). That is a ground for cancelling
     his visa (s 116(1AC)). It also bars a 482 for 3 years unless the Minister disregards
     it (482.213).

   Asserted.
7. **"What salary?" has two floors, and the stream matters.**
   - **The two floors.** Under reg 2.72(15) the worker's annual earnings must be at least
     the market salary rate. They must also be at least the threshold for the stream:
     "$135,000" for Specialist Skills and "$73,150" for Core Skills (reg 1.03). The
     exception is earnings the Minister is satisfied reach an exemption amount set by an
     instrument that is not deposited.
   - **Indexing.** The thresholds are indexed each 1 July, but only "if the indexation
     factor ... is greater than 1" (reg 5.42A). So the text's figures are floors
     (INFERENCE); the published figure is an input.
   - **The streams.** Specialist Skills is open only to ANZSCO Major Groups 1, 2, 4, 5
     and 6 (reg 2.72(8)(a)). On the HYPOTHETICAL figures, $150,000 against a market rate
     of $145,000 passes in either stream. $130,000 passes only Core Skills.

   Asserted.
8. **Condition 8607 ties the worker to the occupation and the sponsor, with a buffer.**
   - **Who he may work for.** He must work only in the nominated occupation, in the
     sponsor's business or an associated entity's. The exceptions are an occupation in
     the reg 2.72(13) instrument and working out a statutory notice period.
   - **Starting.** He must start within 90 days.
   - **Gaps.** He may be without such work for no more than "180 consecutive days" and
     no more than 365 days in all over the visa period (8607(1)-(5)).
   - **Health insurance.** Condition 8501 (health insurance) is mandatory (482.612).

   Asserted.
9. **What the sponsor owes the worker.**
   - **Pay.** Earnings no lower than the nominated salary and than an Australian's for
     equivalent work, unless the worker's earnings reach an instrument amount (reg 2.79).
   - **The fare home.** Paid on a written request made while he holds the visa
     (reg 2.80).
   - **No passing on its costs.** It may not make him pay the costs of being a sponsor,
     the nomination fees and charge, or the costs of recruiting him (reg 2.87).
   - **What reg 2.87 does not cover.** His own visa application charge ($4,015,
     item 1240) is not in reg 2.87's list (INFERENCE that reg 2.87 does not forbid passing
     it on).
   - **Penalties.** A sponsor that breaks an obligation faces up to 240 penalty units.
     The figure is 60 for a former sponsor, and five times either for a company: 1,200
     and 300 (ss 140Q(1), 486R(5)). Section 140H binds a person who "is or was" a sponsor.

   Asserted.
10. **Section 82(5), read literally, could end a permanent visa with a travel period.** A
    visa to travel to and enter Australia "during a particular period" ceases at its end
    unless the holder "has entered Australia in that period" and "is in Australia at the
    end of that period".
    - **Priya.** Her return from London inside the 801's five years satisfies (a). Being
      in Australia when the period ends satisfies (b).
    - **Open question.** The text read here does not answer whether the rule reaches a
      holder granted the visa in Australia who never leaves. The rule applies the words
      as written.

    Asserted for Priya.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- **Deposit and read the instruments the Regulations point to.** Then encode the
  occupation lists and the indexed thresholds instead of asking for them. The instruments
  are:
  - the Core Skills occupation list (reg 2.72(9));
  - the reg 2.72(13) list;
  - the market salary method (2.72(17));
  - the exemption amounts (2.72(15)(b), 2.79(1A)(b));
  - the 010.611(1)(c) class;
  - the language tests;
  - the threshold published under reg 5.42A(5).
- **Encode the public interest criteria** (Schedule 4: health, character, 4020 and 4021),
  at least as they reach a partner applicant and a skilled worker.
- **Check the d035 dates with the scenario author.** Finding 2 turns on when Priya
  applied and how long the couple had been together.
- **Look at the decided cases.** Whether s 82(5) reaches an onshore permanent visa
  (finding 10), and how "substantial ties of benefit to Australia" is read.
- **Encode the 482 Labour Agreement stream and secondary applicants** if a life needs
  them.
- **Have someone other than the author test the encoding.** No case law was searched and
  there has been no independent test pass.
