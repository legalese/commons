## Seat

**The fund member.** The plan entry puts it as the member asking "When can I take my super out, what cover must I be offered, and who gets it when I die?".
Almost every provision in scope is addressed to the trustee ("each trustee ... must ensure", "the trustee must pay"); the member is the person those duties are for.
Each exported question is phrased as the member would ask it, and a trustee duty is encoded as what the member can expect: "Must my fund give me default death and incapacity cover", "Must my fund pay my death benefit as my nomination says", "May my super be paid to this person when I die".

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed. The SIS Regulations 1994 are deposited and brought in for what the Act leaves to them: preservation age and retirement (reg 6.01), permanent incapacity (reg 1.03C), terminal medical condition (reg 6.01A), the conditions of release and cashing restrictions (Schedule 1, reg 6.18), the binding nomination (reg 6.17A), cashing on death (regs 6.21, 6.22) and the s 62 age (reg 13.18).
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the member, the trustee, the employer, the member's dependants and legal personal representative, witnesses, medical practitioners, the Regulator (s 62(1)(b)(v), as an occasion).
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. Asked as facts: the trustee's reasonable satisfaction that ill-health makes work unlikely (reg 1.03C), that the member intends never to work again (reg 6.01(7)), that the member cannot meet living expenses (reg 6.01(5)(a)); the doctors' certification (reg 6.01A); whether the s 68AAE and s 68AAF exceptions apply.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out: dependant, spouse, child, interdependency relationship, legal personal representative, preservation age, retirement, severe financial hardship (both limbs, including the "preservation age plus 39 weeks" date). Asked: dependence in the ordinary sense, "gainfully employed", a registered relationship under a prescribed State law, a MySuper or defined benefit membership.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one member's circumstances on one day; one nomination with one nominee (a notice naming several is asked once per person); one account for insurance.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments; the record fields use the member's own words ("I signed and dated it in front of two witnesses").
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Licensing, covenants, investment rules, APRA's powers, penalties and Parts 8 to 32 are out.

One departure from the plan entry's provision list: it names ss 59, 68AAA, 68AAB and 68AAC. The lives c090 and k094 ask about the conditions of release, which live in the Regulations, so Schedule 1 and reg 6.01 are the larger part of this encoding.

## Why this scope

The seat's three questions map onto three blocks of law, and the encoding takes each whole enough to answer the lives:

- **When can I take my super out:** reg 6.18(1) and (3), Schedule 1 Part 1 items 101, 102, 102A, 103, 105, 106, 108, 109, 110, with the definitions they need (regs 1.03C, 6.01(2), (5), (7), 6.01A, 6.15(1)), and the purpose test in s 62 with reg 13.18.
- **What cover must I be offered:** ss 68AA, 68AAA-68AAD.
- **Who gets it when I die:** ss 10, 10A, 59(1), (1A), regs 6.17A, 6.21(1), (2A), (2C), 6.22(1)-(3).
- Kept from the naive encoding: s 29WA and s 64 (c041).

Not encoded: restricted and unrestricted non-preserved benefits (every contribution is taken to be preserved, reg 6.15(1)); temporary residents (reg 6.01B); Schedule 1 items 103A, 103B, 104, 107, 107A, 109A, 111-114 and Part 2; compassionate release (reg 6.19A); the caps in reg 6.18(2) and (4); reg 6.21(2B); the conditions of the s 68AAE and s 68AAF exceptions; successor-fund elections; penalties; licensing, covenants, investment, Parts 8 to 32.
Choice of fund, the stapled fund and the 11% rate are in the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992; death benefit tax is the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 Div 302.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **At 60 and still working, Chloe can draw only an income stream.** Her preservation age is 60 (born after 30 June 1964, reg 6.01(2)(f)). Item 110 ("Attaining preservation age") restricts cashing to a transition to retirement income stream or another non-commutable income stream. "Retirement" (item 101, Nil) needs an employment arrangement to have ended (reg 6.01(7)(b)); item 106 (65, Nil) is five years off. Asserted.
2. **But leaving any one job at 60 or over is retirement.** Reg 6.01(7)(b) needs only that "an arrangement under which the member was gainfully employed has come to an end" and that the person was 60 on or before it ended. No intention to stop work is needed, and a second job can continue. A member who left a job before turning 60 needs the trustee to be satisfied she will never work again. Asserted.
3. **Permanent incapacity has no age: Jake could draw at 56.** Item 103 is Nil, and reg 1.03C turns only on the trustee's reasonable satisfaction that ill-health makes suitable work unlikely. The life puts his access "at sixty", but on these facts it was open from the stroke (14 March 2063), if the trustee accepted the incapacity. At 60, without that acceptance and without the trustee being satisfied he will never work again, he left work at 56, before turning 60, so he has not "retired" and has only the item 110 income stream. Asserted.
4. **A binding nomination lapses three years after it was last signed, confirmed or amended, and the Regulations have no non-lapsing kind.** Reg 6.17A(7): a notice "ceases to have effect ... at the end of the period of 3 years after the day it was first signed, or last confirmed or amended" (or a shorter period the rules fix). Chloe's 14 February 2041 notice is in effect on 14 February 2044 and gone on 15 February 2044. Daniel's "non-lapsing" nomination of 9 April 2065, read as a reg 6.17A notice, lapsed on 10 April 2068, ten years before his death, unless he confirmed it. The text does not say what a fund's non-lapsing nomination is; it is not a reg 6.17A notice, and whether it binds the trustee by some other route (for example rules with trustee consent, s 59(1)(a)) is not answered by the deposit (an inference left open). Asserted for the lapse.
5. **A binding nomination is the fund's permission, not the member's right.** s 59(1A) says the rules "may" permit it, and reg 6.17A(2) lets them do so only if the trustee gives the member the information in (3). The notice must be in writing, signed and dated before two witnesses who are 18 or over and not named in it, with their declaration (reg 6.17A(6)), and each person's share must be "certain or readily ascertainable" ((4)(b)). A witness who is the nominee spoils it. Asserted.
6. **Without a binding nomination Priya still may take the super; only who may receive is limited.** Reg 6.22 confines a death benefit to the legal personal representative and dependants (or, if after reasonable enquiries the trustee finds none, any individual). Priya is a spouse; Anjali and Luca are children. All are dependants, whatever their age. A friend can be paid only as executor or if no dependant or legal personal representative is found. Asserted.
7. **"Dependant" in SIS includes every child, of any age.** s 10(1) says a dependant "includes ... any child of the person", with no age or support test. So Daniel, at 56, is his father's dependant (d074), and Ruby, at 46, is Jake's. The life's "taxed as a non-dependant" for Ruby is a tax-law test (ITAA 1997), not this one. Asserted.
8. **But an adult child who is not financially dependent takes only a lump sum.** Reg 6.21(2A): a pension or annuity goes only to a dependant who, if a child, is under 18, or under 25 and financially dependent, or has a disability within (2C). Maria and Priya may take a pension; Daniel and Anjali may not. Asserted.
9. **Under 25, no insurance by default; at 25 a MySuper member gets death and incapacity cover by default.** s 68AAC bars cover for a member "under the age of 25 years" without a written election. s 68AAB does the same below $6,000 never reached since 1 November 2019, and an election under either counts for both. s 68AA requires death and permanent incapacity cover for a MySuper member unless barred or declined. Chloe at 20 and at 23, without electing, has none; electing gets it; at 25 she gets it unless she opts out. Income protection is not required by s 68AA, which names only death and permanent incapacity benefit. Asserted.
10. **The dangerous occupation exception does not save an inactive account.** ss 68AAB(4)(e) and 68AAC(4)(e) exempt a member under s 68AAF; s 68AAA(6) has no paragraph (e). Asserted.
11. **The age in the sole purpose test is 65, not preservation age.** Reg 13.18 fixes 65 for s 62(1)(a)(ii); a benefit on ceasing work through ill-health is an ancillary purpose (s 62(1)(b)(ii)). Asserted.
12. **"Part 7" is not choice of fund** (carried from the naive encoding). The life's c041 citation "Part 7 (choice of fund; stapled fund)" points at this Act's provisions for regulated funds; choice of fund and the stapled fund are in Part 3A of the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 (as s 29R(4) of this Act and the plan entry note). s 64's 28-day rule is about amounts deducted from pay, not the employer's own guarantee contribution. Asserted for s 64.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- An independent test pass and a coverage table, especially for Schedule 1: only the items the seat and lives meet are encoded.
- Non-preserved benefits (regs 6.10-6.13, 6.19, 6.20) and the amount cap in reg 6.18(2), which a real "how much can I take" needs.
- Temporary residents (reg 6.01B) and the compassionate grounds (reg 6.19A).
- What a fund's "non-lapsing" nomination is and whether it binds; the deposit does not say, and case law was not searched.
- The ordinary (financial) sense of "dependant" comes from case law, which was not searched. The matters to be weighed, and the further cases of interdependency, that reg 1.04AAAA prescribes for s 10A(3) are not encoded; only s 10A(1) and (2) are.
- The tax on a death benefit (ITAA 1997 Div 302) and choice of fund (SG Act Part 3A) are separate subjects.
- The 29 February edge in the lapse date: a notice signed on 29 February is carried to 1 March three years later by the date library; the text does not say.
