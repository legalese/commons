# Paid Parental Leave Act 2010 (Cth) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE. Checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`.
It encodes everything that one encoded (a few of its 55 assertions, such as the pre-2025 day counts, are encoded but no longer asserted here, to keep within 70).
It adds the deposited Paid Parental Leave Rules 2021, which the naive encoding did not have: the conditions that lift the partnered limit (rules s 21), the days that do not count toward the shared-day limit (rules s 7B), the PPL rules route to eligibility (rules s 9), unpaid leave on compensation (rules s 22), activities that are not paid work (rules s 23), the special circumstances for the work test (rules s 24B) and the nominal interest multiplier (rules s 36).
It also adds who may rely on s 36A, the s 33(1) choice of the day the work test period counts back from, the s 57A(2) 100-day backdating limit, the employer's permitted deductions (ss 67–70) and s 99.
It reuses the naive encoding's hand-checked day counts, s 21 and s 101 cases (Ironbark, Jarrah), and its Tahlia and Jake fixtures, rewritten from the parent's seat.

## Seat

**The parent**: the birth mother, or her partner or the child's other parent, whether an employee or self-employed.
The question is "Am I eligible for parental leave pay, for how many days, at what rate, and who pays me?".
Parental leave pay is claimed by and paid to that person. The Secretary decides and the employer may pay, but the Act is applied to the parent.
Every exported question is the parent's, for example:

- "May I make a PPL claim?" and "Do I need the birth mother's permission?"
- "How many more days can I be paid?" and "May I be paid for a shared day?"
- "Am I eligible on the day?", "Do I satisfy the work test?" and "Do I satisfy the income test?"
- "How much do I get for a day?"
- "Will my employer pay me?", "Who pays me for the day?" and "May my employer deduct it?"
- "How much superannuation contribution is paid for me?"

The employer's duties (ss 72, 74, 80) are encoded as what the parent must be given.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The PPL rules are deposited and used wherever they fill a gap the Act leaves to them. The national minimum wage, the indexed limits and the SG charge percentage come from outside both and are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the claimant, the partner, the birth mother, the employer, the Secretary. The Commissioner of Taxation appears only as the end-point of the elapsed-days count.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. The Secretary's satisfaction under ss 36A and 36AA, the discretion to decline an employer determination (s 101(4)), and rules s 7B(5) ("any other reason") are inputs. The rules s 14, 16 and 17 circumstances, which also turn on the Secretary's satisfaction, are asked as facts.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out: "flexible PPL day", "qualifying work", "paid work", "permissible break", "continuous flexible period", "work test", "income test" and "reference income year". Asked: "partner", "Australian resident" and "income support payment" (Social Security Act), and "adjusted taxable income" (Family Assistance Act Sch 3). "Previously satisfied the work and income tests" is asked, though s 6 defines it by the absence of determinations, because the parent knows whether one is in force.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one parent, one child, one flexible PPL day (eligibility) or one claim (days, employer).
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in the comments and the `@desc` lines.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Commencement, the rule-making power, administration, review, debts and penalties are out.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names Chapter 2 (when parental leave pay is payable), Part 2-3 (eligibility), s 11D, s 21 and Part 3-5 (the employer as paymaster), and the brief adds the rate and how the days are shared.

That is encoded with what each answer needs:

- who may claim and the birth mother's permission (ss 13, 15, 54, 59A);
- the day limits (ss 31AB, 31ABA);
- the claim window (ss 57A, 60);
- the rate (s 65);
- who pays and what the paymaster must do (ss 70–74, 80, 84, 101);
- s 99;
- the superannuation contribution (ss 115B, 115C), which c061's note raises.

Left out, because no life asks and each is a scheme of its own:

- adoption;
- exceptional-circumstances and special PPL claims;
- stillbirth;
- multiple births;
- COVID-19 claimants and jobkeeper;
- instalment periods;
- funding amounts beyond s 73;
- the employer's notices and records;
- arrears and transfers;
- review, debts and penalties.

The day limits assume that neither the parent nor the partner is in exceptional circumstances (s 31AB(4)–(6)).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A child born on or after 1 July 2026 brings 130 days, not 120.** s 31ABA(1)(d): "130 flexible PPL days for the child". c061 expects 24 weeks, the 2025–26 figure (s 31ABA(1)(c)). Oliver, Luca, Ruby and Ella are all born after 1 July 2026. Asserted.
2. **The "reserved weeks" are a cap on whichever parent was partnered at the first claim.** s 31AB(3) stops a claimant who "has a partner at the time ... [of] their first effective claim" at the s 31ABA(2) number, 110. That leaves 20 days, four weeks, that neither partnered parent can take alone, which matches c061's 4. The PPL rules lift the cap where the claimant is experiencing family and domestic violence or its impacts. They also lift it where the partner is incapable of caring for at least 26 weeks, the child is stillborn or dies, the partner is deployed overseas for every day, the child is in hospital or has higher care needs for 18 weeks or more, or the birth mother relinquishes the child (rules s 21). Asserted (110, 20, and the family violence case).
3. **A partner's days come through the birth mother.** The claim is "not an effective claim" without her permission (s 59A(1)). The Secretary pays only up to the days her permission names (s 13(2)(d)), and only if she has satisfied the work test (s 13(2)(b)). She may vary or revoke the permission, but not for days already passed (s 15(4)–(7)). A search of the deposited rules found no provision made for s 59A(2) or s 13(2)(d), so no prescribed exception applies. Asserted.
4. **No marriage condition anywhere.** Neither s 54(1) nor s 31AA asks whether anyone is married. Jake claims as "a parent ... [who] is not the child's birth mother" (s 54(1)(c)), whether or not he counts as Tahlia's "partner". Partner status matters to the 110-day cap and to the family income test. Asserted.
5. **Self-employment counts, but a paid day must be a day off.** s 35(3) counts work "for the purposes of a business that is carried on for profit" by the person or an entity they control. Working as the employee of one's own company is excluded from s 35(1) by s 35(2) but caught by s 35(3). Rules s 23 excludes volunteering, mutual-obligation activities, most prison work and passive income. On a paid day the parent may do "no more than one hour of paid work" (s 31AA(2)(c)). Asserted.
6. **A partner's short block is paid by the Secretary, not the employer.** An employer determination needs at least 40 consecutive week days (s 101(1)(b)) and a continuous flexible period, which itself needs 40 consecutive week days (ss 71A(c), 101(1)(d)). A s 109 election with the parent's consent lifts paras (b) and (c) only (s 101(2)). So Liam's 20 days are paid by the Secretary (s 84(2)) whatever his employer elects. Asserted.
7. **Jarrah cannot be Chloe's paymaster for Ella in April 2034.** A continuous flexible period ends no later than "the day before the child's first birthday" (s 71A(e)(ii)), 11 April 2034 for Ella. Chloe joined Jarrah on 1 February 2034, so she cannot have 12 months' service before Ella's due date (s 101(1)(c)). Unless d04c means a later child the life does not name (an inference), the paymaster duties do not arise at Jarrah. Asserted (with and without an election).
8. **Twenty shared days, and some do not count.** For a child born on or after 1 July 2025, s 21 bars a further day paid to two people once 20 such days are counted. Rules s 7B leaves out days where it "would be unreasonable to count" them: a caesarean or birth complication, a child in hospital 14 days or more, higher care needs, a severe medical condition, a stillbirth or death, a multiple birth, or another reason the Secretary accepts. Read literally, the rule changes only the count, not the bar on the day once the count is reached. Asserted.
9. **A parent may work while the baby is in hospital.** s 31AA(4) lets the PPL rules replace "caring" and "no more than one hour of paid work". Rules s 9(3)(b)(ii) and s 14 accept a child who must stay in hospital after the birth. For the birth mother, that applies only from the 14th day after the birth (rules s 14(d), not modelled). Asserted.
10. **The waiting period is tested on the birth day, and a later cure does not reach back.** s 31AA(2)(e)(ii) requires, where the birth day is in the parent's newly arrived resident's waiting period, that s 31A(7) or (7A) applied on the birth day itself. Citizenship later (s 31A(7A)(c)) lifts the day-by-day bar in s 31AB(8) but not this condition. Priya's case turns on visa dates the life does not give. Asserted on stated facts.
11. **Premature birth can carry a partner over the work test; pregnancy complications cannot.** s 36A(1)(a)(i) covers the birth mother, a parent who is not the birth mother and the birth mother's partner. Para (ii) covers only "the child's birth mother". Both need the Secretary to be satisfied the test would otherwise have been met. Rules s 24B adds family and domestic violence, severe medical conditions, caring for a family member, and declared natural disasters. Asserted.
12. **The rate is the minimum wage, for every parent alike.** s 65(2): "7.6 times" the hourly national minimum wage, for each paid flexible PPL day. The wage is an input. Asserted on hypothetical figures.
13. **Superannuation is paid on parental leave pay, with interest.** s 115C: the base (funding amounts and Secretary-paid instalments in the income year) times the SG charge percentage, plus a nominal interest amount. Rules s 36 sets the multiplier at elapsed days ÷ 365 × 10%, to four places; its own example gives 0.0836 for 305 days. Asserted for the formula and the example.
14. **Unpaid leave stays unpaid leave.** s 99: unpaid leave is not paid leave "just because a person receives instalments of parental leave pay". The employer may deduct only what the parent authorised in writing for their own benefit, PAYG withholding, or child support (ss 67(1), 68, 69(1), 70). Asserted.
15. **This Act does not mention the newborn supplement or the newborn upfront payment.** The words appear nowhere in the deposited text. The interaction found by the Family Assistance baseline lives in that Act (Sch 1 cl 35A(8), (9); s 58AA): when parental leave pay is payable for the child, the supplement, and so the upfront payment, are not. Nothing in this Act states it from this side. Not asserted here.
16. **The 600-day COVID-19 work test period is spent.** s 33A gives COVID-19 affected claimants 600 days. s 19B bars a payability determination for such a claimant "if the child is born after 31 March 2021". Not encoded.
