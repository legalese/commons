# Corporations Act 2001 — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent
test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

One module, `ca-founder.l4`, holds every rule. Each question is a function named as the
founder would ask it, published with `@export` and with `@desc` on every input (44
exported questions). The s 257B buy-back table is encoded as data: one row of seven steps
per kind of buy-back. One case file, `ca-cases-founder.l4`, is generated by a script
(scratchpad `gen_corporations-act-2001_baseline.py`). Its expected values were written by
hand from the text.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers everything that
one covered. It reuses that encoding's hand-checked values where they are still right:
the $500,000 placement, the lent-money variant, the buy-back vote split, the certificate
ages, the 50% subsidiary edge, and the 51-member takeover variant. It adds:

- the small proprietary company test (s 45A(2));
- s 112(1);
- the business judgment rule's s 180(3) gate and its "rational belief" proviso;
- s 182 and the s 184(2), (3) offences;
- the senior manager exemption (s 708(12));
- the whole s 257B table, deriving the kind of buy-back from facts, with ss 257F to 257H.

## Edition

- **Act:** Corporations Act 2001, Compilation No. 149, compilation date 19 September 2026,
  includes amendments to Act No. 87, 2026 (register id C2026C00432), as deposited at
  `../../registers/source-bundle/C2004A00818.txt`. The compilation is in seven volumes; the
  deposit joins them.
- **Regulations:** the Corporations Regulations 2001 are **not deposited**. These are
  therefore inputs:
  - the s 708(8)(c) net assets and gross income amounts. The case file uses $2,500,000
    and $250,000, labelled hypothetical;
  - any figures prescribed in place of the s 45A(2) revenue, gross assets and employee
    numbers. The Act's own figures are $25 million, $12.5 million and 50.

## Seat

**The founder-director and shareholder** of a proprietary company: Daniel, of Jarrah Legal
Technologies Pty Ltd. The plan puts his question as "What must I do as a director, and how
may I raise and sell shares?" All seven events that cite this Act are Daniel's. Chloe's
and Jake's lives do not cite it.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The Regulations
  are not deposited, so the amounts they set are inputs and are said to be so.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed:
  - the director, officer, employee and member;
  - the company and its related bodies corporate;
  - ASIC and the Registrar;
  - the listed disclosing entity and the market operator;
  - the insider.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** Followed:
  - "if a court finds I used a reasonable director's care and diligence" (s 180(1));
  - ASIC's discretion to register (s 118(1) "may"). The registration question is
    phrased "if ASIC decides to register it".
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Worked out from facts:
  - director (s 9AC) and eligible officer (s 1272B);
  - small proprietary company (s 45A(2));
  - personal offer (s 708(2));
  - selective, equal access, on-market, minimum holding and employee share buy-back;
  - the 10/12 limit;
  - subsidiary (s 46) and related body corporate (s 50);
  - generally available (s 1042C) and inside information (ss 1042A, 1042D).

  Asked as facts:
  - "material personal interest";
  - "improperly";
  - "reckless" and "dishonest";
  - the s 9 professional investor categories;
  - "controls";
  - voting power and relevant interest (ss 608 to 610);
  - whether the listing rules require notification.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one application, one director, one
  decision, one offer, one buy-back, one acquisition.
- **The Act's own terms, glossed.** Followed in every `@desc`.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. ASIC's powers, the Registrar,
  regulation-making powers and penalties are out.

**Departures.** Three, all small:

- **Questions addressed to the company are put to the director.** Several provisions bind
  the company, not Daniel: s 113(3), s 254T, Part 2J.1 and s 674(2). They are asked as
  "Must my company ..." or "May my company ...", because as director he is the one who
  makes it comply. Continuous disclosure is the listed entity's duty. For Daniel it
  becomes s 674A(3): is he personally liable as a person involved?
- **d081 puts Daniel on the other side of s 708.** He is the investor being offered
  shares, not the issuer. The offer questions are phrased from the issuer's side ("the
  person I am offering shares to"), and the same tests answer his own position.
- **d073 is a listed company.** The proprietary-company rules give way there to s 191's
  general rule, continuous disclosure and insider trading.

## Why this scope

The Act runs to seven volumes. The brief keeps this encoding to what Daniel meets as
founder, director and shareholder:

- registering a proprietary company and its limits;
- the director's duties, as questions he would ask;
- the s 708 exemptions he raises money under, with the s 708 thresholds;
- share buy-backs;
- as far as the events need, groups, the takeovers threshold, continuous disclosure and
  insider trading.

Left out:

- the rest of s 708 ((7), (10), (13) to (21)), s 708AA and s 708A;
- prospectus content, s 727 and Part 6D.3 liability;
- Chapter 7 licensing;
- capital reductions (Division 1 of Part 2J.1) and redemption;
- ASIC's s 257D(4) exemption, s 254Y and s 259A beyond the table;
- insolvent trading (s 588G) and creditor-defeating dispositions;
- Chapter 2E related party benefits;
- ss 47 to 49;
- relevant interest and voting power (ss 608 to 610) and the s 611 exceptions (an input);
- Note 3 to s 606(1);
- the CSF rules;
- the insider trading exceptions (one input) and the s 1043M defences;
- s 1042G;
- every penalty;
- the ASX Listing Rules, which are not law in this repository.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The $500,000 placement is a sophisticated-investor offer, not a small-scale one.**
   d043 cites both exemptions. At exactly $500,000 payable on acceptance, s 708(8)(a) is
   met. s 708(5)(a) then disregards that issue in the small-scale count, so the small-scale
   question answers no for it. Asserted.
2. **Money Daniel lends the investor does not count.** s 708(9) disregards amounts paid
   "out of money lent by the person offering the securities or an associate". $500,000 of
   which $450,000 was lent counts as $50,000. That is not a sophisticated-investor offer,
   though a personal offer within 20 investors and $2 million is still exempt. Asserted.
3. **Priya is not saved by s 708(12) through Daniel.** s 708(12)(a) exempts an offer to a
   senior manager "or their spouse, parent, child, brother or sister". s 9 defines a senior
   manager as "a person (other than a director or secretary of the corporation)". An offer
   to a director's partner is not within it. Priya's 10% rests on the small-scale
   exemption (a personal offer, the second issue in 12 months). Whether she is Daniel's
   spouse is the encoder's reading of the life; it does not change the answer. Asserted.
4. **A proprietary company that needs a prospectus is breaking s 113(3).** The offence is
   of strict liability (s 113(3A)), but the transaction stays valid (s 113(4)). A $499,999
   offer to a stranger that is neither personal nor sophisticated needs a disclosure
   document, and so breaches s 113(3). The only exceptions are offers to existing
   shareholders and employees, and CSF offers. Asserted.
5. **The buy-back's "solvency test" is not s 254T.** d064 cites s 254T, which is the
   dividend rule: net assets enough to pay, fair and reasonable, no material prejudice to
   creditors. A buy-back's own condition is s 257A(a): it must "not materially prejudice
   the company's ability to pay its creditors". There is no net-assets test for a
   buy-back. Asserted, separately.
6. **A selective buy-back has one column in the s 257B table.** The 10/12 limit does not
   matter to it. The steps are:
   - a s 257D special or unanimous resolution;
   - offer documents lodged (s 257E);
   - 14 days' notice (s 257F);
   - a statement of all material information with the offer (s 257G);
   - cancellation (s 257H).

   The investor's assumed 15% exceeds the 10/12 limit, and that changes nothing. Asserted.
7. **The seller may vote against but not for.** s 257D(1)(a) bars votes "in favour" by the
   seller or associates; s 250MA(b) needs 75% of the votes cast. On the encoder's split
   (Daniel 525, Priya 75, the fund 250, the investor 150), the resolution passes 850 to
   150 with the investor against. It fails 600 to 250 (70.6%) if the fund votes against
   and the investor abstains. If the investor votes for, only the unanimous route in (b)
   remains. Asserted.
8. **The small proprietary company figures may not be the Act's.** s 45A(2) sets $25
   million revenue, $12.5 million gross assets and 50 employees, "or any other amount
   prescribed by the regulations". The encoder understands, from outside the deposit and
   unverified, that higher figures have been prescribed. The thresholds are therefore
   inputs. With three tests and "at least 2", a proprietary company is always exactly one
   of small or large. Asserted with the Act's figures and with hypothetical prescribed
   ones.
9. **A de facto director owes the duties but needs no director ID.** s 9AC(1)(b) makes an
   investor whose wishes the board follows a director. s 1272C binds only an "eligible
   officer", and s 1272B(1)(a) limits that to a director who "is appointed". A pending,
   timely application excuses an appointed director (s 1272C(2)). Asserted.
10. **The business judgment rule deems s 180 met, and only s 180.** It applies only to a
    "business judgment" (s 180(3)). It fails where the director has a material personal
    interest. Even a director a court would fault is "taken to meet" s 180(1) if all four
    limbs hold. The Note confines it to s 180 and its general-law equivalents. Asserted.
11. **The duty about information survives the sale.** s 183(1) binds a person who obtained
    information because they "are, or have been" a director, and Note 1 says the duty
    continues. After d071, Daniel still may not misuse Jarrah's information. Asserted.
12. **s 184(1) needs recklessness or dishonesty and a failure of good faith or proper
    purpose.** Dishonesty alone, in a decision taken in good faith for a proper purpose,
    is not the s 184(1) offence. s 184(2A) and (4) also rule out a defence that the
    advantage went to the company. Asserted.
13. **A sole director of a proprietary company never discloses interests under s 191.**
    s 191(5) says so. On a listed board (d073) notice is required unless s 191(2) applies,
    for example a still-effective standing notice under s 192. Asserted.
14. **Trading windows are not in the Act.** s 1043A binds "a person" who possesses inside
    information and knows or ought reasonably to know it is such. No trading-window fact
    is asked, because none changes the answer. Asserted.
15. **Continuous disclosure is the company's duty; the director's exposure is s 674A(3).**
    s 674(2) binds the listed entity. A director contravenes s 674A(3) only by being
    "involved" in the entity's fault-based contravention of s 674A(2). s 674A(4) excuses
    one who took all reasonable steps and then believed on reasonable grounds that it was
    complying. Asserted.
16. **For d081 the certificate's age matters, and $10 million ends the question.** The
    s 708(8)(c) certificate must be given "no more than 6 months before the offer".
    Exactly six months will do; seven will not. s 708(11)(b) separately exempts any offer
    to a person who has or controls gross assets of at least $10 million, whatever the
    certificate says. What Daniel is "not protected from" is investing without a
    prospectus. Asserted.
17. **Selling Jarrah is no takeover; Formly becomes a subsidiary.** s 606(1) reaches only a
    listed company or one with more than 50 members. Jarrah, unlisted with an assumed 4,
    can be bought outright; with 51 members the purchase would be caught. Once Jarrah holds
    more than half of Formly's votes or capital, Formly is its subsidiary (s 46). Exactly
    50% is not "more than one-half". Asserted (s 50 encoded, not asserted).
18. **Registration is a lodgment, and ASIC "may" register.** s 118(1) gives a discretion,
    not a duty. An application lodged without the written consents fails s 117(5).
    Asserted.

A drafting note, not a finding about the law: in this `l4`, `NOT a AND b` parses as
`NOT (a AND b)`, so every negation in the module is bracketed.
