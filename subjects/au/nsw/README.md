# new-south-wales/

New South Wales Acts, one subject-id per body of law, following the subject-sidecar shape described in [`subjects/README.md`](../README.md).

The subjects here were laid out on 2026-09-22 for the Acts that govern twelve everyday topics -- payroll, leave, termination, pensions, government benefits, personal tax, insurance claims, utility billing, passenger compensation, consumer and tenancy, customs and duties, and work permits. The mapping and the check of each Act against the official register are in `tools/topics/`. Until 2026-10-20 no statute text had been deposited; nothing is encoded yet.

## Social security and benefits, and personal income tax (Priority 3 sample, 2026-10-20)

> **Warning.** Every NSW text deposited for this sample is **taken from the Open Australian Legal Corpus, collected on 2025-03-10; it is not the current official text and may be out of date — check legislation.nsw.gov.au before relying on it.** The official register could not be fetched (see "Terms and access" below).

### How the NSW set connects to the Commonwealth

In Australia personal income tax and the main social security payments are Commonwealth law, and their texts are already sourced beside this folder: [`../income-tax-assessment-act-1997/`](../income-tax-assessment-act-1997/), [`../income-tax-rates-act-1986/`](../income-tax-rates-act-1986/), [`../medicare-levy-act-1986/`](../medicare-levy-act-1986/), [`../social-security-act-1991/`](../social-security-act-1991/), [`../social-security-administration-act-1999/`](../social-security-administration-act-1999/) and [`../a-new-tax-system-family-assistance-act-1999/`](../a-new-tax-system-family-assistance-act-1999/). NSW levies no income tax. What a NSW resident meets on top of the Commonwealth layer is (a) state payments and concessions, some of them switched on by a Commonwealth status, and (b) state taxes that fall on individuals rather than businesses, chiefly on the home.

The joins. Claims confirmed by a deposited text cite the provision; the rest are marked **unverified (from general knowledge, not from the texts)**.

- **Commonwealth pension status opens the council-rates concession.** Local Government Act 1993, Chapter 15, Part 8, Division 1, s 575, reduces the rates and domestic waste charges of an "eligible pensioner" by one-half, capped at $250 a year for ordinary rates and domestic waste charges and $87.50 each for water and sewerage special rates (s 575(3)), as the text stood at 2025-01-01. The Local Government (General) Regulation 2021, cl 134(a), prescribes as eligible pensioners people who receive a pension, benefit or allowance under Chapter 2 of the Social Security Act 1991 (Cth), or a service pension under Part III of the Veterans' Entitlements Act 1986 (Cth), and are entitled to a Commonwealth pensioner concession card; cl 134(b)-(d) add certain DVA and military compensation recipients.
- **Workers compensation weekly payments stop by reference to the Commonwealth age pension age.** Workers Compensation Act 1987, s 52, defines "retiring age" as the age at which the person would be eligible for the age pension under the Social Security Act 1991 (Cth), and ends weekly payments one year after it (or 12 months after the first incapacity, for an injury received after it).
- **State payments and Commonwealth income tests.** That workers compensation weekly payments are assessable income under the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997 and are "compensation" that can reduce or preclude a Centrelink payment (Social Security Act 1991, Part 3.14), and that victims support recognition payments are not counted as income for either, is **unverified (from general knowledge, not from the texts)**.
- **The home ties the state tax side together.** The land tax principal place of residence exemption (Land Tax Management Act 1956, s 10, paragraph (r), and Schedule 1A), the First Home Buyers Assistance Scheme in transfer duty (Duties Act 1997, Part 8, Division 1, including ss 78A and 78AA) and the first home owner grant (First Home Owner Grant and Shared Equity Act 2000, Part 2: s 9, citizenship or permanent residency; s 12, the residence requirement) each turn on owning and occupying a home as a principal place of residence. That the Commonwealth main-residence capital gains exemption (ITAA 1997, Subdivision 118-B) asks a similar but not identical question is **unverified (from general knowledge, not from the texts)**.

### Currency and gaps

- **Currency.** The corpus records each document's latest version as of its scrape (2025-03-10): version dates run from 2023-09-04 (First Home Owner Grant Act) to 2025-02-01 (victims support). Everything enacted or indexed since -- land tax thresholds for 2026, any change to the duty concession or grant, the pensioner rebate cap, victims support amounts -- is missing. Check the official register before encoding any number.
- **Tables.** The corpus had already flattened tables into space-aligned lines (for example the counselling rates in the victims support regulation); they were not re-formed into `| a | b |` rows.
- **Numbers set outside these instruments**: the indexed workers compensation weekly maximum and other amounts (SIRA notices in the Government Gazette); the land tax thresholds for each year (worked out under the Management Act, Part 7, Division 4, and published by Revenue NSW). The Land Tax Management Regulation 2024 (sl-2024-0196) is in the corpus but was not taken.
- **Out of scope**: the Taxation Administration Act 1996 (objections, interest and penalties for state taxes -- the next instrument to add for the tax side); concessions run as programmes without their own statute (Seniors Card, transport concessions, most energy rebates).
- **To replace**: when legislation.nsw.gov.au can be fetched (for example after asking the NSW Parliamentary Counsel's Office for a permitted route), re-read its copyright page and replace all eleven texts with current official ones.

## Encodings (2026-10-24)

Six of the eleven texts were encoded on 2026-10-24, each at `encodings/baseline-2026-10/` in its subject's folder. The other five are not encoded on their own: each is read into the encoding of the Act it serves, as the first column says.
Each encoding is a baseline from the seat of the individual: a seat-neutral core in the Act's own actors, with a regulative rule wherever the text states a duty or a deadline, and a seat layer of questions that restates no rule.

Every text encoded is the Open Australian Legal Corpus copy collected on 10 March 2025. **It is not the current official text and may be out of date** (see the warning above), and each `NOTES.md` says so.
A figure that changes each year is an input unless the deposited text states it; where the text states it, it is encoded as stated and flagged for checking.

Each was checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98 (`JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` unset): 0 errors, 0 warnings, every assertion satisfied.
The workers compensation and victims support encodings were independently verified on 2026-10-24. Every issue the verifier raised (four and five) held and was fixed the same day, and no asserted value was wrong (their `NOTES.md` findings 16-19 and 24-28).
The other four have not been independently verified. For this README the stable `l4` was run again on every file of those four on 2026-10-24, with the result their `encoding.json` records: 0 errors, 0 warnings, and 274, 186, 162 and 82 assertions satisfied.
None had a pipeline, coverage table or human gate. They are drafts and not for public use as advice.
Each `NOTES.md` gives the method, edition, seats, scope, every finding, and what would be needed before relying on it.

### Still inputs, because they are not deposited

Shared by several encodings:

- **The Interpretation Act 1987 (NSW)**, which says how days, months and years are counted and defines "de facto partner" (s 21C). Each encoding states its reading of the periods in `NOTES.md`. For example, "within 3 months after" 30 November 2025 ends on 28 February 2026 (duties, finding 15), and "within 2 years after" a day ends on the same day 2 years later (victims support, finding 16). The first home owner grant borrows s 21C, so a de facto partner is asked as a fact (finding 8).
- **The Taxation Administration Act 1996 (NSW)**: objections, interest, penalty tax and tax defaults for duty and land tax. The land tax family election runs to the end of the s 89 objection period, so its deadline is an input (land tax finding 11).
- **The value of a penalty unit.** The offences are stated in penalty units.

By encoding:

- **Duties Act.** The deposited s 32 table holds the amounts for 2019-20. For any later year, the five threshold amounts and the premium threshold that the Chief Commissioner publishes under s 33AF are inputs. The base amounts are worked out from them under s 33AD, and amounts that cannot be s 33AC results answer `not covered` (finding 2). The corpus dropped the formulas in s 78A(2), (2A) and s 78B(3), so the first home concessional band ($800,000 to $1,000,000 for a home, $350,000 to $450,000 for a vacant block) and the shared equity concession answer `not covered` until the formulas are transcribed from an authorised rendering (finding 1). The s 104C residential apportionment factor is an input. The commencement of the 9% surcharge rate is not in the deposit, so a surcharge liability before 1 January 2025 answers `not covered` (finding 10).
- **First Home Owner Grant Act.** Any cap prescribed by regulation under s 13A(4): no regulation is deposited, so the cap is an input that defaults to the s 13A(2) figures. The start of the 2012 transitional period (s 18(5)) is asked as a fact (finding 17).
- **Land Tax Management Act.** The s 62P and s 62S thresholds ($1,075,000 and $6,571,000) are stated in the deposited text for every land tax year after 2023, so they are encoded as stated, not as inputs; for 2025 and later they must be checked against the current register (finding 1). Inputs: land values and any land value adjustment, and the Treasurer's s 40 discount rate. The Land Tax Management Regulation 2024 is in the corpus but not deposited. So a lessee the regulations make an owner, and a retirement visa holder's home exemption, answer `not covered`, and surcharge assumes no prescribed modification (finding 8).
- **Local Government Act.** The deposited Regulation prescribes the form of an application but not its time, so the time is the one fixed by council resolution under s 579(2), an input. Whether the council has verified eligibility "as prescribed" (s 579(3)) is an input too (finding 7). The $250 and $87.50 caps are in s 575(3) itself; a regulation under s 580 varying them answers `not covered` (finding 10). The Commonwealth statuses of cl 134 are facts the pensioner gives (see below).
- **Workers Compensation Act.** None of the SIRA indexation orders is deposited, so every indexed amount is an input that defaults to the figure the deposited text states: for example the maximum weekly compensation amount of $1,838.70, the $788.32 floor for a worker with highest needs and the $750,000 death benefit. The cases use those figures, which are not 2026 amounts (findings 2, 3). The corpus dropped the s 66(2)(a) and (b) formulas, so the lump sum for permanent impairment of 11% to 50% answers `not covered` (finding 1). The Workers Compensation Guidelines (reasonable excuse, claim forms, work capacity) and the worker's age pension age are inputs.
- **Victims Rights and Support Act.** The weekly rate for actual loss of earnings (Regulation cl 9) is the rate under the pre-2012 Workers Compensation Act 1987 s 37, indexed. Neither is deposited, so cl 9 answers `not covered`, and the weekly rate is an input, capped at $20,000 by cl 10(3)(a) and cl 11(2)(a) (finding 9). The Regulation's other amounts (cl 5, 8, 10-14) are fixed in the deposit and encoded as stated.

**Not encoded at all:** the Motor Accident Injuries Act 2017 (statutory weekly benefits after a road injury) and the Energy and Utilities Administration Act 1987, which have no text here.

Within NSW, the encodings meet in three places. Victims support excludes financial assistance for loss already paid for, workers compensation included (s 30(2)), and borrows the pre-2012 workers compensation s 37 weekly rate (cl 9). Land tax takes "foreign person", "residential land" and "retirement visa holder" from Duties Act Chapter 2A, as facts. And the Duties Act scheme and the grant each set a residence requirement that starts with moving in within 12 months of completion, each with its own definition of a first home owner.
