# National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 (Cth) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE. Checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

## Seat

**The person with disability**, here Jake Harding after his stroke at 56.
The question is "Am I eligible to become a participant, and what happens to my participation when I turn 65 or go into aged care?".
The access request is made by or for that person and participation is theirs. The CEO decides, but the Act is applied to the person.
Every exported question is that person's, for example:

- "Do I meet the age requirements?", "Do I meet the residence requirements?" and "Am I a protected SCV holder?"
- "Do I meet the disability requirements?", "Do I meet the early intervention requirements?" and "Do I have developmental delay?"
- "Do I meet the access criteria?", "Which requirements will my notice say I meet?" and "On what day do I become a participant?"
- "What has happened to my access request?" (when the CEO is silent), "Am I in time to ask for a review?" and "May I make another access request?"
- "Have I ceased to be a participant?", "Does aged care end my participation?" and "May my participant status be revoked?"

The CEO's duties (ss 20, 26, 28(2), 30) are encoded as what the person can expect: when silence counts as a refusal or a withdrawal, what the notice must say, and when participation may be taken away.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed, except that the one instrument named, the Becoming a Participant Rules, is not deposited. What it prescribes is an input, as the brief directs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the person (prospective participant, participant) and the CEO.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are all inputs:
  - the CEO's satisfaction that the person resides in Australia, weighing the s 23(2) matters;
  - whether episodic or fluctuating impairments "may be taken to be permanent" (ss 24(3), 25(1A));
  - the three s 25(1)(b)–(d) satisfactions;
  - whether non-compliance was reasonable (ss 26(3), 30(6)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out: "protected SCV holder", "developmental delay", "child" (as the under-6 limb), and the s 24 and s 25 requirements from their limbs. Asked:
  - "Australian citizen", "permanent visa" and "special category visa" (Migration Act meanings);
  - "ongoing funded aged care service" and "specialist aged care program" (Aged Care Act 2024 meanings);
  - "NDIS support" (s 10, defined by the rules), as part of the s 24(1)(e) and s 25(1)(d) facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one person, one access request; one participant's life for s 29.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in the comments and the `@desc` lines.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Plans, supports, nominees, providers, the Agency, the Commission and the rule-making power are out (see "Why this scope").

## Why this scope

The plan entry names ss 21–25 and the brief adds what happens at 65 and on entering aged care.
That is encoded, with what each answer needs:

- the request itself (s 18);
- what the notice must say (s 28);
- how long the CEO has, and what silence means (ss 20, 21(3), 26);
- review time and asking again (ss 19(2), 100(2), (5));
- how participation ends (ss 29, 29A, 30).

Left out:

- plans, supports and funding (Chapter 3, Parts 2 and 3);
- nominees and child representatives (Chapter 4, Part 1);
- s 30A, which turns entirely on circumstances the rules prescribe;
- further review under s 103;
- information, compensation, recoverable amounts, providers, the Commission and penalties.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. The age requirement is fixed at the request: a person meets it if "aged under 65 when the access request ... was made" (s 22), while residence and disability are tested "at the time of considering the request" (s 21(1)(b), (c)). Asked the day before his 65th birthday, Jake qualifies, even if the CEO decides after it. Asserted.
2. Turning 65 does not end participation. Section 29(1) lists death, aged care, revocation and withdrawal, and age is none of them. Section 30(1) allows revocation only for residence or the disability and early intervention requirements, not age. Asserted.
3. Aged care ends participation only if it is ongoing funded aged care on a permanent basis, not under a specialist aged care program, and "this first occurs only after the person turns 65" (s 29(1)(b)). Permanent aged care that began before 65 does not end participation later. INFERENCE: "after the person turns 65" is read as from the 65th birthday itself. Asserted.
4. Early intervention (s 25) has no lifetime-need limb and no reduced-functional-capacity limb for adults. A permanent impairment plus the CEO's three satisfactions is enough, at any age under 65. Where the rules prescribe the impairment, s 25(2) supplies the first two satisfactions, but the third (the supports would be NDIS supports) is still needed. Asserted.
5. A New Zealander on a special category visa meets the residence requirements only as a protected SCV holder. That means in Australia on 26 February 2001 holding the visa, or 12 months in the 2 years before that day and a return after it (s 9). One who first came in 2010 cannot qualify on that visa. Asserted.
6. Silence is a decision. If the CEO neither decides nor asks for more within 90 days (or a lesser number the rules prescribe), the person is taken to be refused, and the deemed refusal is reviewed automatically (ss 20, 21(3), 100(5)). If information is asked for and arrives in time, the CEO has 14 days. If it does not arrive within 90 days (or the longer period allowed), the request is taken to be withdrawn, unless the CEO finds the delay reasonable (s 26(3)). Asserted.
7. A person may make a fresh access request "at any time", except while a review of the refusal is under way (s 19(2)). Asserted.
8. A request for review must be made "within 3 months after receiving the notice" (s 100(2)). INFERENCE: from a notice received on 30 November the period ends on the last day of February. Asserted.
9. A withdrawing participant must be given a cooling-off period of at least 90 days, and is taken to withdraw only at its end, if they have not cancelled (s 29A(3), (5)). A shorter period stated in a notice is read up to 90 (INFERENCE). Asserted.
10. Nothing in ss 21–25 reads the disability support pension. Jake qualifies for the DSP on 20 points in the Social Security Act baseline, and that neither helps nor hinders his NDIS access (INFERENCE). Both baselines agree that a 65th birthday ends neither benefit: the DSP gives way only when another pension becomes payable (s 103(2)), and NDIS participation ends only on the s 29 events.
