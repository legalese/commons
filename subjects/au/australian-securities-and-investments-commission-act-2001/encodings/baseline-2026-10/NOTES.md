# Australian Securities and Investments Commission Act 2001 (Cth) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE. Checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.
The Act is large and mostly machinery (ASIC itself, the Takeovers Panel, the Financial Reporting Council, the accounting and auditing standards boards, confidentiality, fees).
This baseline keeps to Part 2: where ASIC's role comes from (ss 11, 12A), and Division 2, the consumer protection provisions for financial services.
It adds the part of Part 3 that a founder meets when ASIC investigates: the examination notice, the notice to produce books, the penalty for ignoring them, and the self-incrimination rule.

## Seat

**The consumer of financial services, and the company founder as far as the registration event needs.**
The plan seats this Act with the company founder ("What does ASIC do with my registration?").
The Act, though, does not register companies: s 11(1) gives ASIC the functions "conferred on it by or under the corporations legislation", and registration is Corporations Act s 117.
The part of this Act that reaches individuals directly is Part 2 Division 2, so the seat is widened to the person it protects: the borrower, the cardholder, the person chased for a debt, and the small business on a bank's standard terms.
The founder appears in four places: issuing shares to an investor, the company's own standard-form banking, the company constitution, and an ASIC examination.

Every exported question is that person's, for example:

- "Where does ASIC's role in what I am doing come from?"
- "Is it a financial service?" and "Did I acquire the financial services as a consumer?"
- "Is the term of my contract void as unfair?"
- "May a collector or supplier use this conduct on me?"
- "May the issuer send me this card?" and "Must I pay for the services?"
- "Do I have the warranty of due care and skill?"
- "By when must I sue for damages?"
- "What is the most I can be ordered to pay as an individual?"
- "Must I comply with ASIC's notice?" and "Can what I said at the ASIC examination be used against me?"

The provisions written as duties on "a person" or on ASIC are encoded as what may be done to the individual.
For example, s 12DJ becomes "May a collector or supplier use this conduct on me", s 12DL becomes "May the issuer send me the card", and s 19 becomes "Must I comply with ASIC's notice".

## Why this scope

The plan names "Part 2" as the provisions the founder's event turns on.
Part 2 Division 1 is ASIC's constitution, with one operative point for the founder: ASIC's powers over his company come from other Acts (ss 11, 12A).
Division 2 is where this Act governs individuals directly, and it is the federal home of consumer protection in financial services.
The ACL carves financial services out (Competition and Consumer Act s 131A), and they land here.
So the baseline covers the Division 2 core that an individual meets: misleading conduct, unconscionable conduct, harassment, unsolicited cards and services, the implied warranties, unfair contract terms, damages and penalties.
Part 3 is in because a founder is the person an ASIC investigation most often reaches.

Left out, beyond the machinery:

- Subdivision DA (add-on insurance);
- ss 12DB-12DI, 12DK, 12DMB, 12DMC (specific false representations, bait advertising, pyramid selling, add-on commission caps);
- the remedies beyond damages (injunctions, non-party redress, orders on unfair terms, infringement and substantiation notices, proportionate liability);
- most of Part 3 (records, inspection, orders, admissibility beyond s 68).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **ASIC's role in registering a company is not in this Act.** s 11(1) gives ASIC whatever the "corporations legislation" confers. Registration is Corporations Act s 117, and business names come from s 12A(1)(k). The plan's question "What does ASIC do with my registration?" is answered here only by pointing elsewhere. Asserted.
2. **A company issuing its own shares provides a financial service.** Issuing a financial product is dealing (s 12BAB(7)(b)). The own-behalf exception in s 12BAB(9) does not apply to an issuer dealing in its own products. So Division 2, including s 12DA, reaches what Daniel tells the investor about Jarrah. That shares are a financial product follows the s 12BAA(4) Note 1 example and is asked as a fact. Asserted.
3. **A forecast without reasonable grounds is misleading, and the onus runs against its maker.** s 12BB(1) deems it misleading; under s 12BB(2) the maker is taken to lack grounds "unless evidence is adduced to the contrary". Asserted.
4. **The s 708 placement is not carved out of s 12DA.** s 12DA(1A) excludes conduct that contravenes Corporations Act s 728 or concerns a s 953A or s 1022A disclosure document. An exempt offer to a sophisticated investor has no disclosure document, so s 12DA applies to the pitch. This is an inference from the Corporations Act structure, which is not encoded here. Asserted on the stated input.
5. **Misleading conduct (s 12DA) is neither an offence nor a civil penalty provision.** s 12GB(1) excludes s 12DA from the offence, and s 12GBA(6)(b) excludes it from the civil penalty provisions. Its remedies are damages and the other orders. Every other Subdivision D provision is both. Asserted.
6. **The company constitution is outside the unfair terms regime.** Jarrah's constitution falls under s 12BL(1), whatever its terms. Its standard-form bank terms, by contrast, are a small business contract: upfront price up to $5,000,000, and fewer than 100 employees or turnover under $10,000,000 (s 12BF(4)). Asserted.
7. **The price term is shielded.** A term that sets the upfront price is outside s 12BF, however unfair it is on the s 12BG limbs (s 12BI(1)(b)). Asserted.
8. **The legitimate-interest limb is presumed against the supplier.** Under s 12BG(4), a term is unfair on all three limbs unless the advantaged party proves it reasonably necessary. Asserted.
9. **Credit card debt collectors are bound by s 12DJ.** Physical force, undue harassment or coercion is forbidden in connection with "the payment for financial services by a consumer". Jake's card is of a household kind, so he is a consumer whatever the $41,000 is (s 12BC(1)(b)), and s 12BC(4) presumes it anyway. Contravening s 12DJ is an offence (s 12GB(1)) and a civil penalty provision. Whether the $41,000 is credit card debt is an inference from k048; the life does not say. Asserted.
10. **A card may be sent only on a written request, or as a renewal or replacement.** A card renewing one the person asked for in writing or has used may be sent (s 12DL(2)). An unrequested first card may not. Asserted.
11. **No payment is owed for unsolicited financial services.** Under s 12DMA, the recipient owes nothing for the services and is not liable for loss or damage from their supply. Asserted.
12. **Insurance is outside the implied warranties.** s 12ED(3) takes services under a contract of insurance out of s 12ED. For non-household services, a term limiting liability to re-supply stands unless the customer shows reliance on it is not fair or reasonable (s 12EC). Asserted.
13. **There are six years to sue, and carelessness reduces damages only for s 12DA.** The limit is s 12GF(2). s 12GF(1B) reduces damages for the claimant's own carelessness only in a s 12DA claim for economic loss or property damage, and only where the defendant neither intended the loss nor caused it fraudulently. Asserted.
14. **Civil penalties are set in penalty units.** For an individual, the maximum is the greater of 5,000 penalty units and three times the benefit (s 12GBCA(1)). For a company, it is the greatest of 50,000 units, three times the benefit, and 10% of turnover capped at 2.5 million units (s 12GBCA(2)). The penalty unit value is not in this Act; the cases use an assumed 330. Asserted.
15. **Self-incrimination is no excuse in an ASIC examination, but it buys a use immunity.** s 68(1) says that refusing on that ground is not a "reasonable excuse" for the s 63 offence, which carries 2 years' imprisonment. Under s 68(3), if the person claims the privilege before answering, the answer cannot be used against them in a criminal or penalty proceeding, except one about its falsity. Asserted.
16. **s 68 says nothing about other civil proceedings.** It does not keep an answer out of a civil proceeding that seeks no penalty; ss 76-77, not encoded, deal with that. The encoding answers "can be used" there, as far as s 68 goes. Not asserted.
