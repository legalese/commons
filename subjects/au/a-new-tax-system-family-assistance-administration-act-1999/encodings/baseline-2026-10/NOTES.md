## Seat

**The parent (the claimant)**: "How do I claim, what is reconciled at year end, and what if I was overpaid?"
The Act's duties fall on the Secretary: to determine claims, pay instalments, vary determinations, raise and recover debts and review decisions. The person those duties are applied to is the claimant, so each provision is encoded as what it means for her: whether her claim counts, what her rate is worked out on, when she must lodge, what she gets back or owes, when she must pay, and how long she has to object.
Every exported question is the parent's. Examples: "Must I make a claim to be paid this?", "Is my claim for FTB by instalment effective?", "By the end of which income year must I act?", "Have I satisfied the FTB reconciliation conditions?", "What is the result of reconciling my FTB?", "Must I pay interest charge?", "How long do I have to ask for internal review?".

## Why this scope

The plan entry names Part 3 (determinations and reconciliation) and Part 4 (overpayments and debt recovery). The prompt adds claims and the time limit for lump-sum claims, instalments on an estimate, under- and over-estimates, and review rights. That is what is encoded, from the parent's side:

- how to make an effective claim of each kind;
- what the instalment rate is worked out on, and what a revised estimate does during the year;
- when the reconciliation conditions are met and what the year-end review then gives or takes;
- the two penalties for not lodging a return or for over-estimating twice;
- the debt, its interest, recovery and waiver;
- the time limits for review.

Left out because no life asks for them and each is a scheme of its own:

- FTB advances;
- bereavement claims;
- the TFN, bank account, address and departure variations after the claim stage;
- restoring FTB after a late return (ss 28(3)–(6)) and the prohibited periods (ss 32AA–32AE);
- maintenance income;
- the couple-separation reconciliation times (ss 32E, 32F) and foreign income (s 32M);
- when an indexation notice may be given and its start day (ss 20A(1)–(4), 20B(1), (3)) and couples (s 20C);
- the child care subsidy machinery;
- recovery by garnishee and set-off;
- the other waivers (ss 98, 100(1)–(3), 101, 102) and write-off;
- ART procedure.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The newborn upfront payment needs no claim.** s 5(2): "A claim is not required for an amount of family tax benefit under section 58AA". Every other payment of family tax benefit needs one (s 5(1)). Asserted.
2. **A parent can claim up to 97 days before the birth, but not earlier.** s 7(3) and the s 3 "early claim day" ("97 days before" the expected day). s 15B then holds the claim until the child is born, or 28 days after the due date if that is earlier. Asserted.
3. **A lump-sum claim for a past period must be made by the end of the next income year.** s 10(2). The Secretary can allow more time for special circumstances, but never past the end of the second income year (s 10(2A)). The same deadline pattern governs lodging the tax return (ss 14A, 32C). Asserted.
4. **A lump-sum claim fails if the return was late.** s 14A: a claim for one of the two previous income years is "taken never to have been made" if the claimant's required return was not lodged by the end of the next income year. Asserted.
5. **An estimate the Secretary does not accept means no FTB at all, not a default rate.** s 20(2): "the eligibility or rate cannot be determined", and s 19 applies. The parent cannot be paid on her own figure until she gives one the Secretary accepts, or until an indexation notice's start day passes (s 20(2A)). Asserted.
6. **The supplements are never paid with the instalments.** s 32A(2): the FTB Part A supplement and FTB Part B supplement are disregarded "unless and until" the FTB reconciliation conditions are satisfied. s 105A then forces a review that adds them. So even a perfect estimate ends in a top-up: $1,200 for Chloe's two children at printed rates when family income is at or under $80,000. A parent whose final income is over the Part A supplement limit gets no Part A supplement at all. Asserted.
7. **For a couple, reconciliation waits for both partners' tax assessments.** s 32C (the claimant's return lodged by the end of the next income year and assessed) and s 32D (the partner's, likewise). s 32B takes the later of the two times. Asserted.
8. **Not having to lodge does not excuse a parent from reporting income.** A single parent on income support throughout the year falls under s 32P (reconciled at the end of the year). If she is not required to lodge, s 32J also applies ("the first individual ... is not required to lodge"), and its time is when she notifies her income or the Secretary can work it out. s 32B takes the later time. Reading both sections as applying at once is the encoding's reading of s 32B. Asserted.
9. **Under-estimating income makes a debt; over-estimating makes a top-up.** The year-end review compares what was paid with the correct amount on final income, and s 71(2) makes the excess "a debt due to the Commonwealth". On the c07a figures, a $70,000 estimate against a $100,000 final income is a debt of $303.60. The reverse is a top-up of $1,503.60, or $303.60 if the reconciliation conditions have not yet been met. Asserted.
10. **A revised estimate never reaches back into instalments already paid, and the overpayment so far is clawed back over the rest of the year.** s 31A(2)(b)(iii): a decrease runs from no earlier than the day after the last instalment period. s 31E: the excess paid so far, divided by the days left in the year, comes off each remaining day's rate, but not below nil. Asserted.
11. **Never lodging a required return costs the whole year's FTB, and the upfront payment.** s 28(2): two income years on, with no return and no assessment, the parent "is not, and never was, entitled" for those days, and everything paid becomes a debt (s 71(1)). s 28AA takes the newborn upfront payment with it. Asserted.
12. **Two consecutive years of over-estimating down to a nil rate ends payment on estimates.** s 32AF: from the next 1 July, Part A (or Part B) cannot be paid "on an estimated income basis", unless one of the exceptions in ss 32AG–32AL applies. The parent can still claim after the year ends. Asserted (the exceptions as one fact).
13. **A debt is due 28 days after the notice. Interest does not run while the parent is still receiving FTB instalments.** s 77(2). s 78D(1)(a) exempts a person receiving FTB instalments or a social security payment on the day before interest would start. s 78C sets the rate at the bank bill rate plus 7 points, per day. Asserted.
14. **There is no time limit on recovering a debt.** s 93B: recovery "may be commenced or taken at any time". Asserted.
15. **Small debts and late administrative-error debts must be waived.**
    - s 99: a debt under the Social Security Act small-debt amount ($250 just before 1 July 2026) must be waived. Chloe's $303.60 is not.
    - s 97: the part of a debt that is solely the Commonwealth's error, received in good faith, must be waived in either of two cases: on severe hardship, or if the debt is raised after both the end of the next income year and 13 weeks from the payment.

    Asserted.
16. **A decision based on an income estimate cannot be reviewed until reconciliation is possible.** s 108(3): it can be reviewed only after the income year ends, and once every relevant person has been assessed on a return lodged in time, or nobody had to lodge. Asserted.
17. **Decisions about instalments and about debts have no review time limit, either at the Secretary or at the ART.** s 109D(6) and s 111A(5) make them "excepted". Other FTB decisions have 52 weeks for internal review and 90 days for the ART. A lump-sum decision for this or last year's period can be reviewed even after 52 weeks (s 109D(3)). Asserted.
18. **But a late review of an instalment decision pays arrears only from the previous income year.** s 109E(2AA): if the application comes more than 52 weeks after notice, the increase cannot be paid for days before the start of the income year before the one in which the parent applies. This is lifted where the reconciliation conditions were met for the period (s 109E(2A)). Asserted.
19. **Reconciliation is not a named procedure.** The year-end comparison is a review under s 105, forced by s 105A once the reconciliation conditions are met. s 32AF(1)(b) describes it in passing, as a review "on the basis of the individual's adjusted taxable income as finally determined". There is no section headed "reconciliation" that sets out the comparison. Not asserted (a reading).
20. **The formulas the text deposit lost are recovered.** The indexation factor in ss 20A(5) and 20B(4) and the present-value formula in s 100(6) read "as follows" and stop in the `.txt`; the authorised Word rendering sets them as equation images. They are transcribed in `C2004A00491.formulas.md` and now encoded. Asserted.
21. **An indexed estimate grows with wages, never shrinks.** ss 20A(5), 20B(4): the factor is AWE in the most recent November over AWE in the highest previous November, to 3 places with a round-up at the fourth, and never less than 1. Because the denominator is the highest November since 2004, a fall in wages leaves the estimate where it was. A $70,000 estimate with AWE up from 1900 to 2000 becomes $73,710 (factor 1.053). Reading "worked out to 3 decimal places" as cutting off, so that the 0.001 increase completes a round-half-up, is an inference. Asserted (hypothetical AWE figures).
22. **An indexed actual income is used only if it raises the figure.** s 20B(2)(b): a notice may be given only if the indexed actual income "is greater than" the current ATI number. Asserted.
23. **A debt can be settled for less only at or above its present value.** s 100(5), (6): the agreed amount must be at least (annual repayment / interest) × [1 − 1 / (1 + interest)^rp]; s 100(4) then waives the rest. At $1,200 a year over 3 years at 5% the floor is $3,267.90 (to the cent), so $3,300 on $3,600 waives $300 and $3,000 cannot be agreed. The interest rate is a Minister's instrument that is not deposited; it is an input. Asserted (hypothetical figures).
