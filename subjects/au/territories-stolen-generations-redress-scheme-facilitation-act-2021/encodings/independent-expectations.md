# Independent expectations: Territories Stolen Generations Redress Scheme (Facilitation) Act 2021 (Cth)

Written from `BRIEF.md` and `../registers/source-bundle/C2021A00126.txt` only, before reading any `.l4` file.

## The Act's words relied on

- s 4(1): the payment "does not affect the person's eligibility for, or entitlement to, any pension, benefit, payment or service (however described) under a law of the Commonwealth."
- s 4(2): "Without limiting subsection (1), a payment ... is not compensation or damages for the purposes of: (a) the Social Security Act 1991 or the Veterans' Entitlements Act 1986; or (b) any other law of the Commonwealth; or (c) a law of a State or a Territory prescribed by the regulations for the purposes of this paragraph."
- s 4(3): "However, subsections (1) and (2) do not apply for the purposes of determining the value of a person's assets under: (a) [SSA/VEA]; (b) any other law of the Commonwealth; (c) a [prescribed] law of a State or a Territory".
- s 4(4): "A payment ... is absolutely inalienable, whether by way of, or in consequence of, sale, assignment, charge, execution, bankruptcy or otherwise."
- s 4(5): "Subsections (1) to (4) have effect despite anything in a law of the Commonwealth, a State or a Territory ... unless, in the case of a law of the Commonwealth, the law expressly provides otherwise."
- s 4(6): "The regulations may prescribe laws of the Commonwealth to which one or more of subsections (1), (2) and (3) do not apply."

## Reading

- Protection under (1) and (2) is the "yes, the Act protects you" answer.
- (1) speaks only of Commonwealth pensions and benefits. For a State or Territory law, the Act gives no answer on eligibility, so the result is NOTHING.
- (2)(c) reaches only *prescribed* State or Territory laws. For an unprescribed one, the Act gives no answer on compensation, so the result is NOTHING.
- (3) removes the (1)/(2) protection for the assets question under the SSA, the VEA, any other Commonwealth law and prescribed State laws. The Act does not say the payment *must* be counted, only that the protection does not apply. So I expect "not protected", meaning it may be counted. For an unprescribed State law no protection ever existed and (3) does not speak, so the result is NOTHING.
- (4) covers every kind of law, State laws included (s 4(5): "despite anything in a law of ... a State or a Territory"). It yields only to a Commonwealth law that expressly provides otherwise.
- (5): the express-provision escape is for Commonwealth laws only. A State law that "expressly provides otherwise" still yields to (1)–(4).
- (6): a regulation exclusion can switch off (1), (2) or (3) only, **not (4)**. A payment is still inalienable under a Commonwealth law that the regulations exclude.

## Where I read the Act differently from, or more precisely than, the brief

- The brief's input is one flag for "whether the regulations exclude it (s 4(6))". The Act lets the regulations exclude a law from "one or more of" (1), (2), (3). One flag cannot say which. I read the flag as "excluded from the subsection relevant to the question asked". For the assets question that creates an oddity: excluding a law from (3) would bring the (1)/(2) protection *back*. I do not assert any scenario for assets plus regs exclusion, and I list it as a gap.
- The brief lists s 4(6) beside s 4(5) as though both defeat every protection. On my reading, (6) cannot reach (4) (scenario 23).
- The brief frames the assets question as "is it counted in an assets test". The Act only removes the protection; whether the payment is counted is for the law asking. "Not protected" is my answer, not "counted".
- Commencement (1 March 2022, column 3, which s 2(2) says is not part of the Act) is not an input in the brief and is not tested.
