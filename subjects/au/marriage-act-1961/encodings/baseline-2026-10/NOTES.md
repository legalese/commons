# Marriage Act 1961 (Cth) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual
to whom the Act is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. Checked with the stable `l4` of VS Code
extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

It replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers everything that encoding covered and reuses its hand-checked dates
(the notice windows, the 14 days, s 89 on Jake's facts). It adds what the naive encoding left as inputs because the Regulations were
not then deposited: the Schedule 3 circumstances for shortening the month (reg 72), the registering authority (reg 6), the
one-certificate offices (reg 75, Schedule 4) and the age declaration (reg 71). It also works out the prohibited relationships from the
relationship instead of asking them, and adds Schedule 1 (whose consent), ss 19 and 21, s 42(2) (who may watch the notice signed),
s 42(1)(b) (evidence of birth), s 42(10), ss 47 and 47A, Part VA (marriages overseas), ss 94, 95, 103 and 104 (the couple's own
offences), s 111A and s 113(2).

## Seat

**The person marrying.** The plan asks: "May I marry, by whom, with what notice, and is my marriage valid?" Part II and Part III
address the parties directly (marriageable age, consent, voidness). Most of Part IV Division 2 is addressed to the celebrant
(notice, meeting, witnesses, explanation, certificates), so it is encoded as what the person marrying must produce and is owed:
the earliest and latest days they may marry, who may watch them sign, what the celebrant must say and send, and by when.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act with the instruments that set its numbers:** followed. The Act sets its own periods (1 and 18 months, 3 months,
  6 months, 14 days) and penalties. The Regulations set the late-notice circumstances, the registering authorities, the
  one-certificate offices and the age declaration; they are deposited and used.
- **The actors the text names:** followed. They are the parties, the authorised celebrants of each kind, the Judge or
  magistrate, the prescribed authority, the Minister, the persons in Schedule 1, the registering authority and the officiant of a
  religious ceremony.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is an input:** followed. The Judge's or magistrate's satisfaction (s 12(2)), the
  decision-maker's satisfaction of a substantial change (s 19), the prescribed authority's satisfaction (s 42(5)) and the
  Minister's exemption (s 46(2)) are asked.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked:** followed. Worked out: minor, authorised celebrant,
  marriageable age, prohibited relationship (from the relationship, counting adoptions), real consent (from the kind of defect).
  Asked: domicile, lawful marriage to another, duress or fraud as facts, the local law of a foreign country, the common law rules
  of private international law.
- **One case is the text's natural unit:** followed. Each decision takes one intended or solemnised marriage.
- **The Act's own terms with a plain gloss:** followed, in the comments and in each `@desc`.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out:** followed. Out: Part IA, the registers and celebrant administration (ss 25-39M),
  s 120. Part IV Division 3 and Part V are out because the lives do not reach them; this is a scope choice, not machinery.

## Why this scope

The lives ask four things of this Act. When may I marry? Before whom? What notice, and how much warning? Is the marriage valid,
and does a religious ceremony afterwards add anything? A person marrying also meets the under-18 path (the court order, consents),
the prohibited relationships, the evidence the celebrant will ask for, the certificates, recognition of a marriage made abroad, the
offences a party can commit, and the abolished action for breach of promise. Those are in. Celebrant registration and
discipline, diplomatic and Defence Force marriages, and the State registration Acts are out.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **At 18 Chloe may marry, without anyone's consent.** s 11: of marriageable age at 18. Below 18, only a 16- or 17-year-old with
   a court order made in circumstances "so exceptional and unusual as to justify" it, to marry one particular person, and the
   order lapses if the marriage is not within 3 months (s 12). On 13 March 2021 she was not of marriageable age; on 14 March she
   was. Asserted.
2. **A minor also needs written consent, and the Act says whose.** s 13(1) requires consent dated not earlier than 3 months
   before the wedding, from the persons in Schedule 1: ordinarily each parent, and a prescribed authority where no other item
   applies. Failure to produce it does not invalidate the marriage (s 48(2)(f)); marriage below marriageable age makes it void
   (s 23B(1)(e)). Which item governs when a parenting order and a guardianship order both exist is not said; the encoding takes
   the parenting order first (an inference). Asserted.
3. **The notice window is 1 to 18 months.** s 42(1)(a). Chloe's notice of 10 April 2029 allows a wedding from 10 May 2029 and
   supports one until 10 October 2030. Daniel's of 28 October 2024 allows 28 November 2024. Jake's of 10 August 2033 allows
   10 September 2033. All three weddings were in time. Months are counted as calendar months keeping the day of the month; that
   is an inference, as the Acts Interpretation Act is not deposited. Asserted.
4. **The month can be shortened only for five listed reasons, and only by a prescribed authority's satisfaction.** reg 72 and
   Schedule 3: travel or employment commitments, binding wedding arrangements or a religious consideration, a serious medical
   condition, legal proceedings, and a celebrant's error. Asserted.
5. **Almost every procedural failure leaves the marriage valid, but not all.** s 48(2) excuses failures under ss 42, 44, 46 and 13.
   It does not excuse an unauthorised or absent celebrant (s 41), unless a party believed the person authorised (s 48(3)). Read
   literally, it does not excuse **s 42B** either: the separate in-person meeting with each party is a "preceding provision" and is
   not in the s 48(2) list. The encoding follows the words, so a marriage without those meetings is not valid. That is a reading,
   not a decided point. Asserted.
6. **The prohibited relationships are only two.** s 23B(2): ancestor or descendant, and siblings of the whole or half blood. By
   s 23B(3) adoptive relationships count as birth relationships, even after the adoption ends (s 23B(5)). First cousins, aunts and
   uncles, step-relations and in-laws may marry. Asserted.
7. **Who may marry Jake and Tahlia "at the registry".** A WA registry officer who registers marriages may solemnise them in WA
   under s 39(1), and such an officer holds a Schedule 4 office and prepares only one official certificate (reg 75). A registered
   marriage celebrant may solemnise anywhere in Australia (s 39F). Which one married them is not stated; both are asserted valid.
8. **The celebrant sends the certificate; the State registers.** s 50(4) gives the celebrant 14 days to forward the official
   certificate to the registering authority, which reg 6 makes the WA Registrar of Births, Deaths and Marriages (also for Christmas
   Island and the Cocos Islands). For 18 May 2029 that is 1 June 2029. Asserted.
9. **Daniel and Priya's Hindu ceremony has no legal effect, and is regulated.** s 113(5) permits a religious ceremony between
   persons already married to each other only if they produce their marriage certificate and give the officiant a signed and
   witnessed statement; s 113(6) disapplies ss 42, 44, 50 and 51 and forbids any certificate under the Act. Without that, an
   unauthorised officiant is exposed under s 101 (s 113(7) protects only one who performs a complying ceremony), and an
   authorised one who knows they are married under s 99(6). s 113(1)(a) forbids the couple to go through it, but states no
   penalty for them, and s 94(5) says marrying one's own spouse is not bigamy. Asserted, except the absence of a penalty, which is
   noted.
10. **Ruby is legitimated by the marriage, on the face of the Act.** s 89(1): a child whose parents later marry is "for all
    purposes the legitimate child of his or her parents as from his or her birth", if the marriage is in Australia or a parent is
    domiciled here (s 89(3)). Jake's life says nothing changes for Ruby. Whether the status does anything is a question for State
    law, which is not deposited; that it has no practical effect is an inference. Asserted.
11. **A marriage abroad is recognised if valid where made, with four exceptions.** s 88D: not if a party was already married, if
    a party domiciled in Australia was under marriageable age, if the parties are within a prohibited relationship, or if consent
    was not real; and not while a party domiciled elsewhere is under 16, or while the marriage is voidable locally. s 88E keeps
    the common-law rules as a fallback, except for an under-age Australian domiciliary. A 17-year-old who married abroad, neither
    party domiciled here, is recognised. Asserted.
12. **There is no action for breaking an engagement.** s 111A(1), though gifts made in contemplation of marriage can still be
    recovered (s 111A(2)). Encoded, not asserted.
