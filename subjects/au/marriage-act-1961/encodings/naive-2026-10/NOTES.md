The encoding is scoped to those provisions, plus the definitions, conditions and

"Prohibited relationship", "real consent", domicile and a party's belief are inputs.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The marriage does change something for Ruby, on the face of the Act

Jake's life says nothing changes for Ruby and that "the law has no word for
illegitimacy". The Act still has one. s 89(1): a child whose parents "were not
married to each other at the time of his or her birth but have subsequently married
each other ... is, by virtue of the marriage, for all purposes the legitimate child of
his or her parents as from his or her birth". s 89(3) needs a parent domiciled in
Australia, or a marriage in Australia; both hold for Jake and Tahlia. Whether that
status matters to anything is a question for the State status-of-children law, which
is not deposited here: that it has no practical effect is an inference, not something
this Act says. Asserted (legitimated from birth on k056's facts; not where the
marriage is abroad and neither parent is domiciled here).

### 2. Daniel and Priya's Hindu ceremony is regulated, not merely without effect

The life's answer is "nothing legally". As to effect, that is right: under s 113(6)
ss 42, 44, 50 and 51 do not apply, and no certificate under the Act may be issued.
But s 113(1) forbids persons already married to each other to go through another
ceremony "except in accordance with this section", and s 113(5) permits a religious
ceremony only where they have produced a certificate of their existing marriage and
given the officiant a signed statement, witnessed by the officiant. Without that, an
unauthorised officiant is exposed under s 101 (s 113(7) protects only one who
performs a complying ceremony), and an authorised one who knows they are married
under s 99(6): 6 months or 5 penalty units either way. Asserted. Treated as if it
were a marriage, the ceremony before an unauthorised priest whom neither party
believed authorised is not valid (s 48) and so void (s 23B(1)(c)). Asserted.

### 3. Almost every procedural failure leaves the marriage valid, but not all

s 48(1): a marriage solemnised "otherwise than in accordance with the preceding
provisions of this Division is not a valid marriage". s 48(2) then excuses failures
under s 42 (notice, declarations, birth evidence, "any other requirement"), ss 44 and
46 (witnesses, the explanation) and s 13 (parental consent). A notice received two
weeks before and a ceremony with one witness both leave the marriage valid, while the
celebrant commits an offence (s 99(1)). Asserted. What is not excused: an
unauthorised or absent celebrant (s 41), unless a party believed the person
authorised (s 48(3)); the form of ceremony (s 45); and, read literally, **s 42B**, the
separate in-person meeting with each party, which is a "preceding provision" and is
not in the s 48(2) list. The encoding follows the words and makes a marriage without
those meetings invalid. That is a reading, not a decided point. Asserted.

### 4. The notice window: at least one month, at most eighteen

s 42(1)(a): the notice must be received "not earlier than 18 months before the date of
the marriage and not later than 1 month before". Chloe's notice of 10 April 2029
allows a wedding from 10 May 2029 and supports one until 10 October 2030; Jake's of
10 August 2033 allows 10 September 2033; Daniel's of 28 October 2024 allows
28 November 2024. All three weddings (18 May 2029, 16 September 2033, and Daniel's
30 November 2024 from the cast list) were in time. A prescribed authority may shorten
the month on prescribed circumstances (s 42(5)), an input. Months are counted as
calendar months keeping the day of the month, which is an inference: the Act does not
say how, and the Acts Interpretation Act is not deposited. Asserted.

### 5. At 18 Chloe may marry without anyone's consent

s 11: of marriageable age at 18. Under 18, only a person of 16 or 17 with a court
order made in circumstances "so exceptional and unusual as to justify" it, to marry a
particular person, and the order lapses if the marriage is not within 3 months
(s 12(2), (3), (5)). A minor who has not been married before also needs written
consent, dated not earlier than 3 months before the wedding (s 13(1)); failure to
produce it does not invalidate the marriage (s 48(2)(f)) but marriage below
marriageable age makes it void (s 23B(1)(e)). Asserted.

### 6. The Act does not register the marriage; it makes the celebrant send the paper

c054 asks what the marriage registers. Under s 50, the celebrant prepares a
certificate for the parties and two official certificates, signed immediately by the
celebrant, both parties and two adult witnesses, and forwards one "within 14 days
after the solemnisation" to the State or Territory registering authority. For a
wedding on 18 May 2029 that is by 1 June 2029. Registration itself is State law. The
certificate is conclusive evidence of the form of ceremony (s 45(3)), not of the fact
of the ceremony or a party's identity (s 45(4)). Asserted.
