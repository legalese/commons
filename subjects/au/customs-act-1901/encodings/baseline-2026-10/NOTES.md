# Customs Act 1901 — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill. It is written from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied,
with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage
table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

Every decision is exported for Legalese Cloud with `@export`, and each input has a
`@desc`. There was no earlier encoding of this Act to replace.

The Act runs to five volumes. This encoding keeps to the incoming traveller, as
the brief asked: the passenger card, the passenger concession, prohibited imports
as the traveller meets them, and the penalties for a false card or for smuggling.

Files: `ca-traveller.l4` (the module) and `ca-cases-traveller.l4` (the cases,
generated by the scratchpad script `gen_customs-act-1901_baseline.py`).

## Seat

**The incoming traveller**: a passenger arriving in Australia on an international
flight or voyage, with baggage. That is the person the Act's passenger provisions
are applied to. The two life events both put the question from that seat: "What
may I bring in duty-free, and what must I declare?"

Most of the provisions speak to someone else: the owner of goods (s 68), a
Collector (s 71AAAB; reg 27), an officer (s 195), the Crown (s 229) or a court
(ss 233AB, 234). Each is turned into what it means for the traveller: must I fill
in the card, what must I tick, what can be taken from me, what is the most I can be
fined, and by when must I pay a notice.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The
  Regulation, the Tariff item and the Crimes Act penalty unit are brought in, as
  deposited. The By-laws and the Prohibited Imports Regulations are not deposited,
  so what they say is asked as an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the traveller, a Collector, an officer,
  the court.
- **A judgment the law hands to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed.
  Whether the Collector "reasonably suspects" the Travel Declaration, whether the
  court "can determine the amount of the duty", and whether the quantity exceeds
  what a passenger "could reasonably be expected" to need are all inputs.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts; borrowed terms asked as
  facts.** Followed. "Smuggling" (s 4(1)), "prohibited imports" (s 51) and the
  s 68(4) exclusion are worked out. "Personal or household effects", "recklessness"
  and the customs value are asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One arrival: the traveller's goods and
  one statement (the card).
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in `@desc`.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed: no Collectors' powers,
  warehouses, depots, prosecutions procedure or regulation-making powers.

## The lives

- **Chloe c014 (2011, age 8): home from Bali.** The passenger card, the duty-free
  concession, and nothing to declare. Decisions asked:
  - `Must I fill in the incoming passenger card`: yes, a Collector asked;
  - `Do I have anything to declare for customs`: no;
  - `Are my other goods within the concession`: yes;
  - `Must I enter my goods`: no, they are her personal effects.
  
  Her parents' variants show the $900 card threshold. The biosecurity part of the
  event belongs to the Biosecurity Act 2015 and is not here.
- **Jake k046 (2031, age 24): back from Bali with three cartons of cigarettes and a
  false passenger card.** Decisions asked:
  - `Must I declare my tobacco`: yes;
  - `How many of my cigarettes are duty-free`: 25, on the plan's By-law figure;
  - `How many cigarettes must I pay duty on`: 575 (600 assumed);
  - `Could my statement be an offence under s 234(1)(d)`: yes;
  - `Could my statement be an offence under s 243T`: yes;
  - `Could my statement be an offence under s 243U`: no;
  - the maximum fines, with an illustrative duty and penalty unit;
  - the infringement-notice ceiling and payment period;
  - forfeiture of the undeclared cigarettes (s 229(1)(q));
  - the smuggling and tobacco-fraud penalties, if intent to defraud the revenue is
    proved.

## Why this scope

The Act is mostly about cargo, ships, warehouses, licensed brokers, exports,
drawbacks and the officers' powers. None of that reaches a person walking through
the arrivals hall. What does reach them comes from five places:

- the exclusion of passengers' effects from entry (s 68);
- the card (s 71AAAB and reg 27);
- the questions (s 195);
- what may be forfeited (s 229);
- the offences for smuggling, prohibited imports and false statements (ss 233, 234,
  243T, 243U, 233BABAD), with the infringement-notice alternative (s 243X).

The passenger concession itself is in the Customs Tariff Act and the By-laws, not
in this Act. It is brought in as far as the deposit allows.

Left out:

- tier 1 and tier 2 goods (ss 233BAA, 233BAB), narcotic goods and restricted goods
  (s 233BABAE). These matter to a traveller with weapons or drugs, but the lives
  do not reach them;
- search and detention powers;
- the prosecution procedure;
- the outgoing traveller.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A passenger's bags are not "entered".** Section 68 does not apply to "goods
   that are accompanied or unaccompanied personal or household effects of a
   passenger". Goods lose that character only if they are more than the
   passenger's own use needs, or are known or believed to be for sale or trade
   (s 68(4)). Even then, reg 25 keeps them free of entry up to $1 000 (reg 26).
   Tobacco that must be entered cannot be warehoused (s 68(3)). Asserted.
2. **The card is due when a Collector asks, not on arrival.** Section 71AAAB puts
   the duty on "the owner of Subdivision AA goods", that is, passengers' personal
   effects. The circumstance reg 27(3) specifies is "that a Collector has requested
   the person to give the information". A traveller who has shown an Australia
   Travel Declaration message can be asked for the card only if no decision has
   been made about the goods and the Collector reasonably suspects the Declaration
   (reg 27(6)). Crew give the crew declaration instead. Asserted.
3. **The card's thresholds are in the deposited Regulation; the concession's are
   not.** Reg 27(2) asks whether the traveller has:
   - "more than 2,250 millilitres of alcoholic beverages";
   - "more than 25 cigarettes or more than 25 grams of tobacco products";
   - goods bought overseas or duty-free worth "more than $900" ($450 for crew);
   - prohibited or restricted goods;
   - commercial samples.

   How much actually comes in free is for the By-laws under Customs Tariff
   Schedule 4 item 15, which are not deposited. The plan's figures (25 cigarettes,
   $900) are used as inputs. Asserted.
4. **The plan's citation of the Tariff is wrong.** In compilation C2026C00399,
   Schedule 4 item 21 is goods imported for repair and re-export. The passenger head
   of power is item 15. (Not asserted; recorded.)
5. **Jake's false card is an offence under s 234(1)(d) and s 243T, but never
   s 243U.** Section 243U(5)(b) takes out of "statement" anything a passenger says
   "in relation to his or her accompanied personal or household effects". So a
   passenger's false card that costs no duty is caught only by s 234(1)(d), which
   needs intention and recklessness. Section 243T has no such exclusion. A false
   card that costs duty is a strict-liability offence, with a fine up to the
   greater of 60 penalty units and the duty shortfall. Asserted.
6. **Owning up can avoid s 243T.** A voluntary written error notice avoids s 243T,
   but only if the duty is also paid before an infringement notice or proceedings
   (s 243T(4)). The notice is not voluntary once an officer moves to verify the
   statement (s 243T(4A)). A statement that flags its own uncertainty is also
   outside s 243T (s 243T(5), (6)). Asserted.
7. **The s 234(1)(d) maximum is 250 penalty units, or 100 penalty units plus twice
   the duty for a statement about the amount of duty (s 234(3)).** This encoding
   reads "subject to subsection (3)" as (3) replacing the 250-unit cap in its case.
   That is a reading, labelled in the module. Asserted on that reading.
8. **An infringement notice can be a quarter of the court maximum, uncapped, when
   the penalty runs on duty or value.** Section 243X(2) caps a notice at a quarter
   of the court maximum and, for an individual, 15 penalty units. Section 243X(3)
   lifts the 15-unit cap where the penalty is set by reference to duty or value and
   that can be determined. For Jake's s 243T offence, the quarter is the only cap.
   Section 234(1)(d) is not in Schedule 8 and is not strict liability, so no notice
   is available for it. Asserted (the ceilings).
9. **A notice for importing prohibited imports, given in the arrivals area, must be
   paid before you leave it.** Reg 133 item 1 says so. A s 243T notice gives
   28 days (item 2), and the duty obligation continues (reg 138(2)). Asserted.
10. **Denying you have dutiable goods forfeits them.** Section 229(1)(q) forfeits
    "all dutiable goods found in the possession or in the baggage" of a person who
    has denied having dutiable goods, or not fully disclosed them when questioned.
    Prohibited imports are forfeited anyway (s 229(1)(b)). Asserted.
11. **You must answer the officer.** Section 195(2) makes it a strict-liability
    offence (30 penalty units) not to answer questions about dutiable, excisable or
    prohibited goods on you, in your baggage, or with a child or other person
    accompanying you. Asserted.
12. **Smuggling needs intent to defraud the revenue (s 4(1)).** The fine is up to
    5 times the duty, or 1,000 penalty units if the duty cannot be determined
    (s 233AB(1)). For tobacco, s 233BABAD sets up to 15 years and 30 times the duty
    for intent, and 10 years and 20 times the duty for recklessness. Importing
    prohibited imports is strict liability (s 233(1AB)): the greater of 3 times the
    value and 1,000 penalty units (s 233AB(2)). Asserted.
13. **The Biosecurity declaration is a different duty.** The incoming passenger
    card also carries biosecurity questions, which the Biosecurity Act 2015 requires.
    The Customs Regulation's own text shows the two are separate: a duty-free outlet
    notice in it tells travellers that approved confectionery "does not need to be
    declared on your incoming passenger card", a quarantine matter. Nothing here
    answers the biosecurity questions. (Not asserted.)

  carries them.
