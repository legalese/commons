## Seat

**The patient (the Medicare-eligible person)**: "Am I covered by Medicare, and what will I pay?"
The Act pays benefits for a person's medical expenses, and it is the patient who is or is not an eligible person, who incurs the expenses, who assigns the benefit and who reaches a safety net. The Chief Executive Medicare, the Minister and the practitioner act on that person.
Every exported question is the patient's: "Am I an eligible person?", "Is a Medicare benefit payable for my service?", "Is my service prescribed for 100%?", "How much Medicare benefit do I get?", "How much will I be out of pocket?", "Is my bulk-billing agreement valid?", "Who is my benefit paid to?", "Have I reached the Medicare safety net?", "By how much does the extended safety net increase my benefit?", "Does s 128C forbid charging me?".
Section 128C addresses the practitioner. It is encoded as what it means for the patient: whether that person may charge them.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The deposited 2018 Regulations are used where they fix a number or a circumstance the patient meets (ss 28, 65B, 65C, 90). The Schedule fees and the s 6A regulations are not deposited and are inputs. The indexed amounts are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the eligible person (assignor), the professional, the medical practitioner, participating midwife and nurse practitioner, the recognised hospital, the Minister and the Chief Executive Medicare.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is asked conditionally.** Followed. Ministerial orders (s 6) and directions (s 19(2), (3), (5)) are facts. The Chief Executive Medicare's being "satisfied" (ss 10AC-10ADA) is read as the facts it is satisfied of. Whether a service is "clinically relevant", and so a professional service, is a fact.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed. "Australian resident", "eligible person", "patient contribution", "relevant service", "out-of-pocket expenses" and "public hospital service" are worked out. "Resides", the visa terms (Migration Act 1958), "concessional beneficiary" (National Health Act 1953), the FTB Part A instalment (Family Assistance Administration Act), "recognised hospital" (Private Health Insurance Act 2007) and "public patient" are asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one person for eligibility, one service for the benefit, one claim for the safety nets. The safety nets add the year's earlier claims as a total the patient states.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in the comments and `@desc` lines.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Family registration procedure (s 10AA), indexation mechanics (s 10A), claims (s 20B), billing agents and Parts IIA onwards (except s 128C) are out.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names s 3 (eligible person) and Part II (Medicare benefits). The brief adds the 85/75/100% cases, the safety nets "as far as the Act states", bulk billing and what the patient may be charged, and public-patient treatment as far as the Act states. That is what is encoded, with the definitions each answer needs.

Left out because no life asks for them and each is a self-contained scheme: the multiple-operation, anaesthesia, pathology, diagnostic imaging and radiation oncology rules (ss 15-16G); the practitioner-side bars and the My Health Record rules (ss 19AA-19AI); simplified billing for privately insured in-hospital services (s 20A(2), s 20AAA); claims (s 20B); the s 10B caps on extended safety-net increases; Professional Services Review, the registers and the offences in Parts IIA onwards, except s 128C.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Standard GP attendances are paid at 100%, not 85%.** Section 10(2)(aa) pays 100% of the Schedule fee for a service outside hospital "prescribed by the regulations". Regulations s 28(1) table item 1 prescribes Group A1 items "3, 4, 23, 24, 36, 37, 44, 47, 123, 124", which on the deposit include the usual GP consultation items (an inference from the item numbers; the Table itself is not deposited). The 85% rate is for everything else outside hospital. Asserted.
2. **The 85% benefit is never more than $50.00 below the Schedule fee, and is rounded up.** Section 10(3) and (5): if the fee exceeds 85% by more than the "greatest permissible gap" of $50.00, the benefit is the fee less $50.00. Section 10(4) rounds "up to the nearest multiple of 5 cents". Section 14(1): the benefit "shall not exceed the medical expenses incurred". Asserted.
3. **Bulk billing is an agreement the professional may offer, not a right, and it must be in writing.** Section 20A(1) lets the eligible person assign the benefit to a professional who "accepts the assignment in full payment". Regulations s 65B requires an episodic agreement meeting s 65C or an enduring one meeting s 65CB. Section 65C(6)(c) requires "a written agreement that is signed and dated by the assignor". The Act puts no duty on a practitioner to offer one. That the patient then owes nothing is a reading of "full payment". Asserted.
4. **Unpaid, the benefit is not paid to the patient at all.** Section 20(2): a person who "has not paid the medical expenses ... shall not be paid the medicare benefit", but on request gets a cheque made out to the practitioner. Asserted.
5. **The original safety net pays the whole patient contribution once the year's contributions reach $246 (indexed).** Section 10AD(3) and s 10AC(2) (family): from the threshold claim on, the benefit "is increased by the amount of the patient contribution". The patient contribution is the gap between the Schedule fee (or lower expenses) and the benefit (s 8(1A)), so above the threshold Medicare pays the Schedule fee in full. Services given during hospital treatment do not count (s 10AC(1)). Asserted.
6. **The extended safety net pays 80% of out-of-pocket costs, counting what the doctor charges above the Schedule fee.** Sections 10ACA(2) and 10ADA(3): "increased by 80% of the out-of-pocket expenses", which are the medical expenses less all other benefits (s 10ADA(4)). Its thresholds are $500 for a concessional person or an FTB(A) family and $2,000 otherwise (s 8(1A), indexed). It applies only if the patient "has paid at least 20% of the out-of-pocket expenses ... directly" to the professional (s 10ADA(5)(c)). Asserted.
7. **The claim that crosses the extended threshold gets 80% of only the part above it.** Sections 10ACA(7) and 10ADA(8) give the increase as 80% × [out-of-pocket expenses for the current claim − balance of safety-net], the balance being what the earlier claims fall short of the applicable amount by. The formula was an image the text deposit dropped; it is transcribed in the sidecar `C2004A00101.formulas.md` and now worked out, not asked. A concessional claim of $100 with $450 earlier against $500 gets $40; an FTB(A) family claim of $300 with $350 earlier gets $120. Asserted.
8. **"Patient contribution" has lost its paragraph (a) in s 3(1) of the deposit.** The s 3(1) definition begins at "(b)" (nursing-home type patients). The Part II definition in s 8(1A) is complete and is the one used. Not asserted (an observation on the deposit).
9. **The public-patient bar covers childbirth and nothing else.** Section 128C(1) forbids a medical practitioner, participating midwife, participating nurse practitioner, or anyone acting for one, charging a known public patient for a public hospital service "in circumstances set out in the regulations" (50 penalty units). Regulations s 90 sets those circumstances: a service "for attendance at, or associated with, the delivery of a baby", or requested "in connection with the delivery of a baby". The hospital itself is not in the list. Chloe's birth (c060) is protected; Jake's stroke (k090) and wrist (k044) are not, under this Act. Asserted.
10. **Medicare benefits do not pay for public-hospital care.** Section 17(1)(a): no benefit if the expenses "have been paid, or are payable, to a recognized hospital"; s 19(2): none, "unless the Minister otherwise directs", for a service rendered by or for a State. So the k090 "nothing to pay" is not something this Act gives the patient. It would come from the State's hospital law and the funding arrangements behind it, which are not deposited here (an inference). Asserted.
11. **Eligibility turns on residence and visa status, not age or enrolment.** "Eligible person means an Australian resident or an eligible overseas representative"; an Australian resident "resides in Australia" and is a citizen, permanent visa holder, NZ citizen lawfully present, or within the other paragraphs (s 3(1)). A newborn citizen in Perth is eligible from birth; a citizen living in London is not. A temporary visa holder with a pending permanent visa and a citizen spouse is a resident under paragraph (f), unless a parent visa application is also pending. Asserted.
12. **Recovering benefits out of damages (k044) is not in this Act.** The deposit has no such rule. The recovery regime is, by inference, in the Health and Other Services (Compensation) Act 1995, indexed in this repository. Not asserted.
