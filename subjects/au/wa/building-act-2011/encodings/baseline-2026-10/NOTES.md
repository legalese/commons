# Building Act 2011 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

One module, `ba-house.l4`, and one case file, `ba-cases-house.l4`. The case file is
generated by a script (scratchpad `gen_wa_building-act-2011_baseline.py`) from a table
of facts. The expected values were written by hand from the text. Every question is
exported with `@export` and `@desc` for Legalese Cloud.

## Edition

- **Act:** compilation of the Building Act 2011 (Act No. 24 of 2011) from
  legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 49441, retrieved 7 September 2026), as deposited at
  `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49441.txt`. The latest amendment noted in the
  sections read is No. 14 of 2025 (s 19).
- **Regulations:** the Building Regulations 2012 are **not deposited**. Everything
  they set is an input:
  - which classifications may use an uncertified application (s 14(2));
  - the period the permit authority has to decide (s 23);
  - the buildings and work that need no building permit or no occupancy permit
    (s 66(1), (5));
  - the "prescribed interest" that makes a person an owner (s 5).

## Seat

**The home owner building a house** — an "owner" of the land under s 5(1), usually
the registered proprietor. The Act is applied to that person when they apply for a
building permit, when the house is built, and when they move in. Most of its
operative provisions address someone else:

- the permit authority (ss 17, 20, 23, 24, 58);
- the building surveyor (ss 4, 19);
- the builder (ss 29, 33, 34, 37(1)).

Each is encoded as what it means for the owner: what they must sign, what they must
be given and by when, whether the permit must be granted, and what they can be
prosecuted for. An owner who is also the named builder (an owner-builder) carries the
builder's duties too; that is a field of the person.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed as far as
  possible. The Building Regulations 2012 are not deposited, so their numbers and
  classes are inputs (see Edition).
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the owner, the occupier, the person named
  as the builder, the building surveyor, the permit authority and the State
  Administrative Tribunal.
- **Judgments handed to others answered conditionally.** Followed. What the permit
  authority is satisfied of under s 20(1) is a set of inputs, the last marked
  "(permit authority)". The ss 11 and 12 defences are inputs for the accused to make
  out.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.**
  - Worked out: "building work" (s 3), "owner" (s 5), "independent building
    surveyor" (s 4), "occupier" (s 40), the s 69 temporary-building exemption.
  - Asked as facts: registration and owner-builder approval under the Building
    Services (Registration) Act 2011, home indemnity insurance under the Home Building
    Contracts Act 1991, the building services levy.
- **One case is the natural unit.** One piece of work, one application, one building.
- **The Act's own terms with a gloss.** Followed in the comments.
- **Operative sections in scope; machinery out.** Followed. Permit authorities,
  authorised persons and entry, records, evidence and transitional provisions are
  out.

## Why this scope

The seat is the owner having a house built, from the permit to moving in. The covered
provisions are:

- the building permit, from who signs to how long it lasts: ss 9, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20,
  23, 24, 28, 32, 119;
- the offence of building without a permit and its defences: ss 9, 11, 12;
- completion: ss 33, 34(3), 37;
- the occupancy permit and the certificate that goes with the application: ss 40,
  41, 46, 47, 51, 54, 63, 66(5), 69;
- the time limits for prosecution: s 133(3), (4).

The following are left out:

- demolition permits;
- staged permits;
- inspections;
- Building Commissioner declarations;
- duties under an occupancy permit already in effect (ss 42-45);
- modification, replacement and extension of occupancy permits;
- the industrial exemptions (ss 70-74);
- work affecting neighbouring land (Part 6), existing buildings (Part 7) and
  enforcement (Part 8). The one exception: work done under a building order needs no
  permit.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The Act itself does not exempt a house from the occupancy permit.** Section 41
   applies to every "completed building", and nothing in the Act names Class 1 or a
   house. An exemption can come only from:
   - Part 5 (the one-month temporary building in s 69; ss 70-74 do not reach a
     house);
   - regulations under s 66(5) ("an occupancy permit is not required for a building
     of a kind specified by the regulations");
   - a Ministerial order under s 67.

   Whether a Class 1a house is exempt is therefore a question for the Building
   Regulations 2012, which are not deposited. It is an input here. (It is widely
   understood that the regulations exempt houses, but that was not read here and is
   not asserted.) Asserted both ways.
2. **Building without a permit is the same offence whoever does it.** Section 9 says
   "A person must not do building work". The penalty is $50 000, then $75 000, then
   $100 000 and 12 months' imprisonment. The same scale applies to occupying without
   an occupancy permit (s 41) and to a building not meeting the standards (s 37).
   Asserted.
3. **Without a permit, the building standards become the owner's problem.** With a
   permit, s 37(1) puts compliance at completion on the named builder. Where work is
   done without a permit, s 37(2) puts it on "each owner", with the same penalty
   scale. Asserted.
4. **Every owner signs, and so does the builder.** Section 16(b), (c): each owner of
   the land and the proposed builder must name themselves in and sign the application.
   The permit document goes to each owner (s 28(1)). The occupancy permit application
   is signed by each owner, and the permit is given to each owner (ss 54(1)(b), 63).
   Asserted.
5. **The certificate of design compliance comes from the owner or from the authority,
   depending on the route.**
   - Certified application: the owner attaches a certificate signed by a building
     surveyor (s 16(e)).
   - Uncertified application: the permit authority refers the plans to a building
     surveyor, who decides whether to sign (s 17). This route is open only to
     classifications the regulations prescribe (s 14(2)).

   The surveyor must be independent of both the owner and the builder (s 4). A
   surveyor employed by the builder cannot support the permit (s 20(1)(d)(ii)).
   Asserted.
6. **Silence is a refusal, and the fee comes back.** If the permit authority does not
   decide within the prescribed period, it "is to be taken to have refused" (s 23(3)).
   It must refund the fee, which is recoverable as a debt (s 23(4)). This does not
   apply if the applicant failed to supply information asked for under s 18, or if
   Heritage Council advice is outstanding (s 23(5)). The deemed refusal is asserted;
   the refund is encoded but not asserted.
7. **Notice of completion is the builder's duty, not the owner's.** Section 33 puts it
   on the "responsible person", the named builder, within 7 days, with a fine of up to
   $10 000. If the builder stops acting, the owner must get a copy of the notice of
   cessation within 7 days (s 34(3)). Asserted for the notice of completion.
8. **A permit with no stated period lasts 2 years** from grant (s 32(2)) and ends when
   the notice of completion is received (s 32(4)). Asserted.
9. **The time limits differ.** A prosecution for building without a permit (s 9) or
   for failing the standards (s 37) may be brought within 6 years. Occupying without
   an occupancy permit (s 41) is not on that list, so its limit is 3 years (s 133(3),
   (4)). Asserted.
