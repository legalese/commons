# Defamation Act 2005 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Seat

**The person defamed.** That person asks:

- "May I sue, or may my company?"
- "What must I do before suing? Is my letter a concerns notice, and what does it do?"
- "Could the publisher still offer to make amends? If I accept, can I still sue?
  If I refuse, does the publisher get a defence?"
- "Which defences can the publisher raise?"
- "What is the most I can get for non-economic loss?"
- "By when must I sue?"

Part 3 speaks to the publisher ("The publisher may make an offer"), and the
defences and s 35 speak to the court. Each is encoded as what it means for the
person defamed: when an offer can still reach them, what it must give them, what
accepting it costs them, and what they can be awarded.

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed, with one
  addition: the Limitation Act 2005 ss 15 and 40, because the seat's "by when" has
  no answer in this Act. No instrument fixing the cap is deposited, so it is an
  input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the aggrieved person, the publisher,
  the corporation, the court, the Minister (only as declarer of the cap).
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Followed. Whether matter is
  defamatory (the general law, s 6); whether imputations are substantially true;
  whether matter was opinion, of public interest, on proper material, honestly
  held; whether harm was unlikely; whether an offer was made as soon as
  practicable, with readiness, and was reasonable (s 18(1)); whether a renewed
  offer is a genuine attempt (s 16(5)); whether aggravated damages are warranted
  (s 35(2)); whether the court extends time (Limitation Act s 40(2)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed for "excluded
  corporation" (s 9(2), (3), (6)), "concerns notice" (s 14(2)-(5)), an offer to
  make amends (ss 14, 15) and the maximum damages amount (s 35(4), (7)). Borrowed
  terms asked as facts: "related" corporations (Corporations Act s 50), and
  "defamatory".
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One publication, about one person.
- **The Act's terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments.
- **Operative sections in, machinery out.** Followed. Out: ss 1-5, 45-48,
  Schedule 4 (which amends The Criminal Code).

## The lives

- **Daniel, d062 (30 June 2040, aged 44): a false post about his company during his
  campaign; a concerns notice; an offer of amends accepted.** The life asks "Is it
  defamatory; what does the concerns notice do; what is an offer of amends?" The
  questions:
  - `Is my company an excluded corporation`: Jarrah Legal Technologies Pty Ltd is
    for profit and, on an illustrative 40 full-time equivalents, not small, so no;
  - `May I sue for defamation`: Jarrah may not (s 9(1)); Daniel may, over what the
    post says of him personally (s 9(5)), if it says anything defamatory of him;
  - `Is my notice a concerns notice` and `What does my notice do`: his written
    notice naming the imputations is one; it starts the publisher's 28 days to
    offer amends, and in this edition is not a precondition to suing;
  - `Was the offer to make amends made in time`, `Does the offer meet the
    requirements of s 15`: an illustrative offer 10 days after the notice,
    containing a correction, steps to tell others, and expenses, is a valid
    offer;
  - `What happens if I accept the offer`: once the publisher carries it out, he
    can no longer sue over the matter (s 17(1));
  - `Which defence defeats my claim`: for a false statement of fact, none of
    truth, contextual truth, honest opinion or triviality;
  - `Am I in time to sue`: within one year of 30 June 2040.

  "Is it defamatory?" is the general law's question, so it is asked as a fact.
  The life's citations of s 10A and ss 12A-12B point to provisions that WA has
  not enacted (checked on legislation.wa.gov.au, 21 October 2026). They are in
  the other States' Acts, not in WA's.

## Why this scope

The life needs ss 9, 14, 15 and 17. The seat also meets: whether anyone can sue
at all (ss 7, 10, 23); the time limit; the defence a refused offer gives (s 18);
the defences the brief named (truth, honest opinion), with contextual truth and
triviality, which turn on the same findings; and the cap on damages (ss 35, 37).

Left out, each for a reason:

- s 11 (choice of law): a matter published wholly in WA needs none;
- ss 13(2)-(4), 15(1)(g)-(4), 16(1)-(3), 17(2)-(3), 19, 20: the detail of offers,
  withdrawals, expenses, admissibility and apologies, which do not change the
  seat's answers here;
- ss 21, 22 (juries), ss 34, 36, 38, 39 (how damages are assessed), s 40 (costs):
  for the court;
- ss 27-30 (privilege, public documents, fair report), ss 31(2)-(3), 32: defences
  the life's events do not raise; a fuller encoding should add them;
- Part 5 and Schedule 4: machinery and criminal defamation.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The deposited edition is the 2005 Act unamended.** No serious harm element,
   no precondition notice, no public interest defence. The plan flagged s 10A and
   ss 12A-12B as not found. They are not there, and they are not law in WA: as at
   21 October 2026 legislation.wa.gov.au shows no amending Act and no later
   version (see Edition).
2. **A for-profit company can sue only if it has fewer than 10 full-time-equivalent
   employees and is unrelated to another company, and is not a public body.** Ten
   is not fewer than 10: eight full-timers and four half-timers make 10, so no.
   Asserted.
3. **A not-for-profit can sue whatever its size, unless it is a public body.**
   Asserted.
4. **Daniel's company cannot sue, but Daniel can, over what the post says of him.**
   s 9(5) keeps the individual's claim "even if the publication of the same matter
   also defames the corporation". Asserted.
5. **No one can sue about a person who has died, nor against a publisher who has
   died.** s 10, including a personal representative. Asserted.
6. **Defamation is actionable without proof of special damage** (s 7(2)): in this
   edition the person defamed need show no loss and no serious harm. Asserted
   (in the outcome of `May I sue for defamation`).
7. **In this edition a concerns notice is not required before suing.** It only
   starts the 28 days after which the publisher can no longer offer amends
   (s 14(1)(a)). It must be in writing and name the imputations. Asserted.
8. **If the publisher asks for further particulars, I have 14 days, or longer if
   agreed; miss them and my notice is taken never to have been given** (s 14(5)).
   Asserted.
9. **An offer made on the 28th day after the notice is too late; a renewed offer
   in new terms escapes the 28 days if it is genuine and within 14 days of the
   withdrawal** (s 16(5)). Asserted.
10. **An offer must include a correction and the aggrieved person's reasonable
    expenses; a limited offer must say so.** An apology and compensation are
    optional (s 15(1)(g)). Asserted.
11. **Accepting the offer bars the claim only once the publisher has carried out
    its terms** (s 17(1)), even if it was limited to some imputations. Asserted.
12. **Refusing an offer gives the publisher a defence only if the court finds it
    prompt, backed by readiness, and reasonable** (s 18(1)). Asserted.
13. **Honest opinion fails if the person defamed proves the opinion was not
    honestly held** (s 31(4)(a)), and only then. Asserted.
14. **The cap on non-economic loss started at $250 000 and is re-declared each
    year by average weekly earnings, rounded to the nearest $500.** It does not
    apply where the court finds aggravated damages warranted (s 35(2)). Exemplary
    damages cannot be awarded at all (s 37). Asserted. Whether a half ($250) rounds
    up is the encoder's reading of "nearest".
15. **One year from publication, extendable to three only if suing within the year
    was not reasonable** (Limitation Act ss 15, 40). The minors and mental
    disability suspensions and the fraud extension do not apply to defamation
    (ss 34, 37, 38(4), 41(4)). Asserted.

  not. These are serious harm, the concerns notice precondition, the public
