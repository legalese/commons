# Young Offenders Act 1994 (WA) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE, no BECAUSE.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** the compilation published on legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 49140, retrieved 7 September 2026), deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49140.txt`.
The deposit gives no compilation number.
Its compilation table ends with the Community Protection (Offender Reporting) Amendment Act 2024 Pt 3 Div 2 Subdiv 4 (in force 31 December 2025). The last reprint it lists is Reprint 7 as at 1 June 2018.
The uncommenced provisions table lists, among others, the Young Offenders and Prisons Legislation Amendment Act 2024 Pt 3 (to be proclaimed). It is not shown in the deposit and not encoded.
No regulations set a number used here. The numbers are the Act's own: the ages 17 and 18, the $2 000 fine (s 71), the one-year good behaviour term (ss 69, 70), the 2 years in s 189, and the $10 000 / 12 months in s 40.

**Checks:** one module (`yoa-diversion.l4`, 27 exported questions) and one case file (`yoa-cases-diversion.l4`, generated by the scratchpad script `gen_wa_young-offenders-act-1994_baseline.py`).
70 assertions written, 70 satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings, on the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
Expected values were written by hand from the text.

This baseline replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`.
It covers everything that one covered (ss 3, 4, 14, 22A-23, 25, 27, 29, 30-33, 41, 55) and reuses its hand-checked results where they still hold.
It adds:

- s 23A (the caution certificate);
- s 22(4) and s 25(4) (what an admission can be used for);
- s 26 (release when referred);
- s 32(5), (6), s 37A, s 38, s 40 and s 62 (the team);
- the sentence kinds that s 55 turns on (ss 60, 66, 67, 69-71);
- ss 189 and 190, which say when a youth conviction stops counting. The first pass missed these, and k036 and k082 turn on them.

Every decision is recast as a question from the seat, and dates replace the first pass's ages.

## Seat

**The young person dealt with by police (Jake, at 15).**
The Act is applied to the young person: what police must consider before charging, whether a caution or a team is open, what the team can ask of them, what completing it does, and whether anything is left on a record.
Every exported question is phrased as that person would ask it ("May police caution me instead of charging me?", "Is there a conviction from my matter to spend or disclose?").
Provisions addressed to police, the team, the chief executive officer or the court are encoded as what they mean for the young person. That covers what may be done to them, what they must be given (s 23A), and whether they must be released (s 26).

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. No regulation sets a number used here. The regulations under s 14(3) on destroying records were not retrieved; that is noted, not encoded.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the young person, the police officer ("member of the Police Force"), the responsible adult, the victim, the juvenile justice team and its Coordinator, the chief executive officer, the court.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are asked as facts:
  - the officer's judgment that previous offences make a caution alone inappropriate (s 23);
  - that an infringement notice is inappropriate (s 25(2));
  - the team's view that the young person lives independently (s 30(3));
  - the court's "exceptional reasons" (s 55);
  - its "special circumstances" (s 189(3)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out from facts:
  - "young person" (s 3 with s 4), from dates of birth and offence;
  - "independent young person" (age 17 plus one fact).

  "Schedule 1 offence" and "Schedule 2 offence" are the Act's own terms, but working them out means encoding about 300 lines of Schedule. They are asked as a fact, with the entries the lives touch noted (a departure, for size).
  Whether an infringement notice can be given is set by other written laws, so it is asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one alleged offence (`My matter`), one team meeting, one conviction.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments. Field names use the young person's words ("I accept responsibility for what I did"), each tied to its subsection.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out:
  - Part 3 beyond s 14;
  - the custodial officer removal machinery;
  - s 36 and s 37 (appointing Coordinators and teams);
  - Parts 8 and 9 (the Supervised Release Review Board and detention centres);
  - regulation-making.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names s 7, ss 22-23, ss 24-32 and s 33. Its seat asks what may be done instead of charging, and whether that leaves a record.
So the encoding covers the cautioning and team Divisions of Part 5, s 41's list of police options, and s 14 (the record).
It also covers the two places where the Act decides whether there is a conviction at all: s 55 on a finding of guilt, and ss 189-190, which make a youth conviction stop counting.
For s 55 it needs the sentence kinds, so ss 60, 66, 67 and 69-71 are encoded only as far as s 55 turns on them.
s 7 is a set of principles that guide discretion and decide nothing alone. s 7(g) is quoted as a comment.

Not encoded:

- the contents of Schedules 1 and 2;
- s 28 (referral by a court);
- Part 4 (custody and bail);
- ss 42-45 (notices to attend court and court procedure);
- Part 7 beyond the provisions listed: the conditions of each order, Division 9 special orders, detention;
- Parts 8 and 9;
- the Spent Convictions Act 1988, which s 189(9) borrows and which k036 and k082 also cite.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The sections the scenario cites for the outcome are the wrong ones.** The scenario cites "ss 29-30 (effect of completing the action plan)". In this compilation s 29 is the first-offender preference and s 30 is the role of the responsible adult. The effect of completing the team's terms is s 33. Asserted (s 33).
2. **A completed plan does not end the matter; it makes a later charge fail.** s 33(1): referral "does not prevent a charge from being subsequently laid". s 33(2): if he complied, a court "must dismiss the charge without determining it". s 33(3): partial compliance is taken into account. The scenario's "no charge" is right in practice, but the protection works at the court door. Asserted.
3. **"Nothing recorded" is not what the Act says; "no conviction" is.** s 14(1) requires the chief executive officer to keep records of every young person "dealt with under this Act for an offence". That includes how a team's terms were complied with (s 14(2)(d)(i)). What a team never produces is a finding of guilt or a conviction. So there is a departmental record but nothing that s 55 or s 189 treats as a conviction. Reading a caution as "dealt with under this Act" is an inference. Asserted (s 14; the spend question).
4. **The caution test looks backwards, and the scenario's reason is not the Act's.** k022 says "a caution is not enough for a second offence of the night". s 23(1) displaces the preference for a caution only "because of the number of previous offences with which the person has been charged or for which the person has been dealt with under this Part". A second offence the same night has been neither (an inference from the words). On Jake's facts the Act still prefers a caution to a charge. But s 23 ranks a caution only above "laying a charge", and a referral is not a charge. So s 23 does not stop police preferring a team, and s 29(1) requires the s 27 discretion to favour a team for someone who has not previously offended. Asserted.
5. **Diversion does not use up the first-offender preference.** s 29(2): a young person is not "taken to have previously offended" merely because he was cautioned, given an infringement notice, accepted responsibility, or agreed to or complied with a team's terms. Asserted.
6. **The Schedules and the Weapons Act cut differently.** A Schedule 1 or 2 offence bars both a caution (s 22(3)(a)) and a team (s 25(1)). A Weapons Act 1999 s 6(1), 6A or 6B offence bars only a caution (s 22(3)(b); s 22 was last amended by No. 33 of 2024 s 24). Plain stealing is in neither Schedule; Schedule 2 lists only stealing a motor vehicle aggravated by reckless or dangerous driving (s 378(2)) and robbery (s 392). Asserted.
7. **The police must consider no action or a caution even when a caution is barred.** s 22B's duty attaches "before starting a proceeding against a young person for an offence", with no exception for Schedule offences. For those, only "no action" is left to consider. Asserted (the robbery case).
8. **The team needs a responsible adult, a stand-in, or a 17-year-old living independently.** s 30(1) requires a responsible adult present and agreeing. s 30(4) allows an appointed substitute. s 30(3) lets the team dispense with the adult only for an "independent young person", who must be 17 (s 3). A victim who is present has a veto (s 31(2)). A split team sends the matter back (s 38). Asserted.
9. **The team cannot order restitution.** s 32(5): it "cannot make an order for restitution or compensation" but records an undertaking. Jake's restitution is something he agreed to do. No lawyer may represent anyone before the team (s 37A(2)), and identifying him in the media is an offence ($10 000 or 12 months, s 40). Asserted.
10. **What he said cannot be used against him.** An admission at the time of a caution is inadmissible "in civil or other proceedings" (s 22(4)). Accepting responsibility to go to a team is not an admission or a plea if the matter goes back (s 25(4)). Asserted.
11. **Once a young person, always a young person for that offence.** s 4: the test is the age at the offence. At 40 Jake is still a young person for the 2021 matter. Asserted.
12. **In court, a conviction is the exception for ordinary offences.** s 55(2): outside the Schedules and without a custodial sentence, no conviction "unless ... exceptional reasons". s 55(5) forbids one when the court refrains from punishment under s 66 or s 67. s 55(5) does not name ss 69 and 70, which also "refrain from imposing any punishment", so those fall under s 55(1) or (2). Even unrecorded, s 55(4) deems him convicted for consequential orders, licence disqualification and appeals, "but not for any other purpose". Reading only a Division 8 sentence as "custodial" is an inference. Asserted.
13. **A youth conviction stops counting after 2 years, and then is treated as spent.** s 189(2): 2 years after every sentence is discharged (or after the conviction, if there was nothing to discharge), the conviction "is not to be regarded as a conviction for any purpose". A youth community based order does this at once unless he is dealt with again (s 189(5)). It never applies to murder, attempted murder or manslaughter (s 189(1)). s 189(9) applies Part 3 of the Spent Convictions Act 1988 "as if it were a spent conviction". Anyone else who discloses it commits an offence unless the disclosure is for this Act or a court (s 190(2)). This is the provision k036 and k082 needed, and the scenario does not cite it. On Jake's facts there is no conviction for it to act on. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Schedules 1 and 2 encoded as data, so that "is this a Schedule offence?" is decided rather than asked.
- Whether an infringement notice can be given for an offence depends on other written laws and regulations not retrieved.
- The regulations under s 14(3) on destroying records were not retrieved; whether the 2021 team record still exists in 2047 depends on them.
- The Spent Convictions Act 1988 (k036, k082) and the Children's Court of Western Australia Act 1988 (k022's "which court") need their own encodings.
- The 29 February edge case in the s 189 two-year count is not handled.
- No WA Police cautioning guidance and no case law was searched. Nothing here has been checked by a lawyer.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added 6 November 2026, following the graffiti encoding's design (D2, D4, D8).
The layer is `yoa-qa.l4`, and its cases are in `yoa-qa-cases.l4`.
It imports `prelude`, `daydate` and `yoa-diversion`, and changes none of the baseline's rules or exports.
Every exported question takes `my role` first ("Which of these describes you").
For a role a question is not about, the answer is `does not arise for you`, and no other input is read.

**Roles.** The Act puts duties on, or gives rights to, four kinds of person:

- `I am, or was, a young person dealt with by police for an offence`: the baseline's seat (Jake).
- `I am a parent or other responsible adult of a young person dealt with for an offence`. A team must give a responsible adult notice. It can go ahead only with a responsible adult present and agreeing (s 30(1)). If one is present, it can dispose of the matter only in a way that adult agrees to (s 30(2)). It must tell them its determination (s 32(1a)(a)).
- `I am the victim of an offence by a young person`. The team must give the victim a chance to take part (s 31(1)). A victim who is present can veto how the team disposes of the matter (s 31(2)). The team must tell the victim its determination (s 32(1a)(b)).
- `I would publish or pass on information about someone else's matter`. s 40(1) and s 190(2) bind "a person".

Police, the team, the Coordinator, the chief executive officer and the court are never seats.

**Situation inputs.** Each comes straight after `my role`, and is identical in name, type and `@desc` wherever it is taken.

| Name | Type | Gives does not arise | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `the matter was referred to a juvenile justice team` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | 21 questions. Young person: s 26, s 30, ss 30-32/38, s 32 terms, s 37A, s 40 fine, s 62, s 33(1), s 33. Parent: s 30 without me, s 30(2), s 32(1a), s 32(2) withdrawal, s 37A. Victim: s 31(1), s 31(2), s 32(1a), s 32(5) order, s 32(5) undertaking, s 37A, s 33 |
| `I was cautioned` | BOOLEAN (the baseline's own input) | FALSE | The caution certificate, s 23A (young person) |

For s 189 and s 190(2): every value but `a finding of guilt with a conviction recorded` | 3 young person questions. It is also an ordinary input, read however the matter ended, of the s 14 record question (young person and parent) and of "Is there a conviction from my matter to spend" |

**Decisions.**

- **D-QA1. The baseline's records are taken apart.** The records (`My matter`, `My team meeting`, `My conviction`) are built from plain inputs. Each question asks only for the fields its baseline function reads. The other fields get a fixed value and are never asked: "May police caution me", for example, asks no team or infringement facts. `I have been cautioned or been to a team before` is read nowhere in the baseline (s 29(2)), so it is always FALSE.
- **D-QA2. Some inputs are fixed by the question's own terms.**
  - In s 26, `my matter was referred to a team` is TRUE, because the referral is the situation.
  - In the parent's "Can the team deal with my child without me", the responsible adult's presence is FALSE.
  - In the parent's "If I withdraw my agreement", the young person attends and agrees, the team is unanimous, and the adult does not agree. The baseline's team function then answers that the matter goes back (s 32(2)). With the adult taking part, s 30 lets the team go ahead whatever the young person's age, so the two dates are fixed values and not asked (fix of 7 November 2026).
  - The victim's s 31(2) question also uses the baseline's team function, with everyone agreeing except the victim who is present.
- **D-QA3. Questions with no situation.** "Will a conviction be recorded if I am found guilty" (s 55) takes no situation, because it is asked before the finding as well as after it. s 71's maximum fine and the publisher's penalty are fixed for their role, so they take none either.
- **D-QA4. New helpers, worked from the text.**
  - s 30(2): when the adult is present, their agreement is needed.
  - s 31(1): the victim gets a chance to take part, unless that is impracticable.
  - s 32(1a): the team gives notice of its determination to the adult who had s 30 notice, and to the victim.
  - s 34: civil liability is not affected. So "Does diversion take away my right to sue" answers no. The question arises however the matter went, so it takes no situation.
  - s 40(1): may I publish this.
  - s 40(2)(b): up to 12 months' imprisonment.
- **D-QA5. Each role keeps its own wording.** The parent's facts about the child are worded of the child, for example `my child's date of birth` and `there is enough evidence to charge my child`. They are not shared with the young person's first-person inputs. A name that is shared has one `@desc` everywhere, worded for every role that takes it, for example "the young person's matter (yours, if you are the young person)".

- **D-QA6. Fix after verification (7 November 2026).** The site's form drops a question for a role it is not about only when it can try every combination of the question's open yes/no and choice inputs, at most 1,024 (the form engine's PROBE_CAP). "What happens to my matter at the juvenile justice team" had eleven yes/no inputs after the role (2,048 combinations), so the parent, the victim and the publisher were asked it. The L4 was right; the shape was not. s 38 (a decision that is not unanimous goes back) and s 32(6) (the team thinks a caution or a court is the right course) are now asked as one yes/no, `the team members all agree to deal with the matter themselves`, passed to the baseline as the team's unanimity with "a caution or a court is the right course" FALSE. The baseline reads the two only as (not unanimous) or (caution or court), so its answer is unchanged for every pair of facts. The question now has 1,024 combinations. In the same fix, the parent's "If I withdraw my agreement" no longer asks the young person's date of birth or the meeting date (D-QA2), and the layer's fixed date is built with the prelude's `DATE_FROM_DMY` rather than daydate's `Date`.

**Not covered for the new roles.**

- The parent: who may be appointed in the parent's place (s 30(4)-(5)); the bail and custody duties in Part 4; court orders against a responsible adult (Part 7); and the parent's own liability.
- The victim: compensation orders made by a court; restitution under other Acts; and the victim's place in court proceedings.
- The publisher: punishment as a contempt under s 40(2)(a), which is named only in the question text; ss 11CR and 15A, which are outside the baseline's scope; and the Spent Convictions Act.
