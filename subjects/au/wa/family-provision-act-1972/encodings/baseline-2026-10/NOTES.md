# Family Provision Act 1972 (WA) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE, no BECAUSE.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** the compilation published on legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 49104, retrieved 7 September 2026), deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49104.txt`.
The deposit gives no compilation number.
Its compilation table ends with the Statutes (Minor Amendments) Act 2017 s 10 (in force 13 September 2017), and the last reprint it lists is Reprint 2 as at 22 February 2013.
Two amendments are uncommenced and not shown: the Evidence Act 2025 s 492(1) and the Assisted Reproductive Technology and Surrogacy Act 2025 Pt 17 Div 7.
No regulation is deposited. The only prescribed number, the amount for s 7(1)(eb) that s 21B lets the Governor set, is an input; the cases use a HYPOTHETICAL $500,000.
The Act's own numbers are the 6 months to apply (ss 6(7), 7(2)), the 10 months for a child born after the death (s 7(1)(c), (d)(iii)), and the 3 and 12 months of s 20(5). Two dates come from the compilation table and its notes.

**Checks:** one module (`fp-claims.l4`, 11 exported questions) and one case file (`fp-cases-claims.l4`, generated by the scratchpad script `gen_wa_family-provision-act-1972_baseline.py`).
44 assertions written, 44 satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings, on the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
A deliberately wrong expected date was caught by the runner (43 of 44), so the cases do bite.
Expected values were written by hand from the text.

There was no first-pass encoding of this Act to replace.

## Seat

**The family member left out of a will, or left too little by it or by the intestacy rules**: a spouse or de facto partner, a former spouse or partner the deceased was maintaining, a child, a grandchild, a stepchild or a parent.
The Act is applied to that person: whether they may apply, by when, and what the Supreme Court may then do for them.
Its other addressees, the Court and the administrator, appear only through what they mean for the claimant: the Court's opinion and leave are asked as facts, and the administrator's protection appears only where it limits the claimant (s 20(4), (5)).
Every exported question is phrased as the claimant would ask it.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The one instrument, the s 21B regulation, is not deposited, so its amount is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the deceased, the applicant in each s 7(1) class, the administrator (as s 4(1) defines it, including the Public Trustee on an election), the Court.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. Whether the will or intestacy fails to make "adequate provision ... for the proper maintenance, support, education or advancement in life" of the claimant is the Court's opinion (s 6(1)); so are leave to file out of time (ss 6(7)(b), 7(2)(b)), refusal for character or conduct (s 6(3)), the necessity of an interim order (s 7A) and whether undisclosed property "would have materially affected" the provision (s 6(5)(b)). Each is a boolean input; none is guessed.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts; borrowed terms asked as facts.** Followed. "Stepchild" (s 4(1)) is computed from whether the claimant was alive when the deceased married or began a de facto relationship with the claimant's parent. "Child" and "grandchild" include an illegitimate child (s 4(1)), so no legitimacy fact is asked; the s 4(4) parentage rule is computed. "Married", "de facto partner" and "maintenance" are not defined here and are asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one claimant against one estate.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in the comments and `@desc` lines.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Transitional provisions, the Crown, regulations and rules of court are out, and so are the post-order machinery (ss 13, 15-18) and the evidence rules (s 21A), which an ordinary claimant meets only through a lawyer.

## The lives

| Event | What happens | Question asked of this Act | Decisions |
|---|---|---|---|
| d085 (2065) | Daniel makes a will with a testamentary trust and a third to the Foundation | Can a will leave a third to the Foundation; what claims can it not shut out? | `May I claim against the estate` for Priya (wife) and Luca (son): yes; Sofia (sister): no. `Can the will or the intestacy shut out my claim`: no. `Can the Court order provision for me` with no application made: no order. `How much of my provision falls on that share`: a third falls on the Foundation's gift (HYPOTHETICAL figures) |
| k084 (2052) | Graham dies; his will gives everything to Sue | Does Jake take anything? | `May I claim against the estate` for Jake: yes (a child living at the death); for Ruby, Graham's granddaughter whose father is alive and whom Graham did not maintain: no. `What is the last day I can apply without leave` and `Is my application in time`, on a HYPOTHETICAL grant of 14 October 2052. `Can the Court order provision for me`: only if the Court is of the opinion the will made inadequate provision for him |
| k098 (2079), not in the plan entry | Jake dies intestate, divorced from Tahlia since 2039 | Can his former wife claim? | `May I claim against the estate` for Tahlia, not maintained by Jake: no; a former wife receiving maintenance: yes. `Can the will or the intestacy shut out my claim`: no, for an intestacy too (s 6(2)) |

The Wills Act baseline answered k084 with "the gift to Sue takes effect" and "this Act gives Jake nothing", and pointed here; the Administration Act baseline gives Jake nothing from the intestacy rules because the will disposes of everything. This encoding supplies the remaining answer: Jake may apply, within 6 months of the grant, and gets something only if the Court forms the opinion s 6(1) requires.

The lives do not date the grant of probate of Graham's will, nor say how large Daniel's estate is. Those figures are HYPOTHETICAL and say so in the case file.

## Why this scope

The plan named s 6 (the Court's power) and s 7 (who may claim). A claimant asking "may I claim?" also needs to know by when (s 7(2), (3)), what parentage counts (s 4(4), (4a)), whether a stepchild's claim depends on the date of death (s 7(2A)), whether the Court can help before the hearing (s 7A), whether a second claim is possible if assets were hidden (s 6(5), (7)), and what giving notice or consent to the administrator does (s 20(4), (5)). Those are in. The burden rule (s 14(2)) is in because d085's question is about the Foundation's third: it shows who pays for an order. s 19 is one line and protects the claimant from borrowing against provision not yet ordered.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Any child of the deceased may apply, at any age and whether or not dependent.** s 7(1)(c) asks only that the child was living at the death or born within 10 months after it. So Jake, 45, may apply against his father's will. Asserted.

2. **A grandchild may apply only if maintained by the deceased or if the grandchild's parent died first.** s 7(1)(d). Ruby, whose father Jake is alive, may not claim against Graham's estate. Asserted.

3. **A former spouse or partner may apply only if receiving or entitled to maintenance from the deceased at the death.** s 7(1)(b). Tahlia, divorced in 2039 with no maintenance, may not claim against Jake's estate. Asserted.

4. **A stepchild may claim only for deaths on or after 16 January 2013, and only if maintained (or entitled to be) or if the deceased took more than the prescribed amount from the stepchild's parent's estate.** s 7(1)(ea), (eb), (2A), with the compilation table's commencement of the 2011 amendments. A child of a partner born after the relationship began is not a "stepchild" at all (s 4(1)). The commencement of s 9(1)(b) with the rest of Part 2 is an inference from the table. Asserted.

5. **Parentage counts only if admitted or established in the parent's lifetime, unless it arises under the Artificial Conception Act 1985.** s 4(4), (4a); a parent's own claim has its own version of the rule (s 7(1)(e)). Applying s 4(4) to a stepchild's tie through its own parent is an inference. Asserted.

6. **The clock runs from the grant, not from the death.** s 7(2)(a): 6 months from the date the administrator "becomes entitled to administer the estate ... in Western Australia", which by s 4(1) is the grant of probate or administration (or the Public Trustee's election). After that, the claim is heard only with leave, which may be sought at any time (s 7(3)). The encoding reads "6 months from" as ending on the same day of the month six months later (an inference; the Interpretation Act 1984 is not deposited). Asserted.

7. **No will and no intestacy can shut a claim out.** s 6(1) measures the will, the intestacy or both against adequate provision, and s 6(2) forbids assuming the intestacy rules are adequate. Nothing in the Act gives effect to a clause excluding a claim. Asserted.

8. **The Court acts only on an application.** s 6(1): "on application made by or on behalf of any such person". If no one applies, Daniel's gift of a third to the Foundation stands, as the life says. Asserted.

9. **Adequacy is the Court's opinion, and the order its discretion.** The Act gives no formula and no list of factors; it may attach conditions or refuse for character or conduct or "any other ground which the Court thinks sufficient" (s 6(3)), and may order a lump sum or periodic payment (s 6(4)). The encoding answers only whether the Court *may* order, on the Court's opinion given as a fact. Asserted.

10. **Unless the Court orders otherwise, every beneficiary bears the provision in proportion to the value of their interest.** s 14(2). On HYPOTHETICAL figures, $300,000 for Luca from a $3,000,000 estate falls $100,000 on the Foundation's third. Asserted.

11. **A notice of intention to claim protects the claimant for at most 3 months, and never past 12 months from the grant, unless the claim is filed.** s 20(5). The claim can still be made later, but the administrator may by then have distributed. Asserted.

12. **Consenting in writing to a distribution bars an action against the administrator, not, on its words, the claim.** s 20(4). Asserted for the action only.

13. **A de facto claim on a death before 21 September 2002 is not covered.** The 2002 amendments do not apply to such deaths (Other notes, note 2), and the text they replaced is not in the deposit. The encoding says so rather than guessing. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Deposit the Family Provision Regulations (the s 21B prescribed amount) and the Interpretation Act 1984 (the reading of "6 months from").
- Say what "estate" includes. The Act does not define it; whether a jointly owned home or a superannuation death benefit can bear an order is outside the deposit (c087, d085 turn on it).
- Decide whether the Court's adequacy judgment should be supported with the case-law factors, as an annotated input rather than a computed answer.
- Encode ss 8, 9 and 11 (claims after distribution, Trustees Act 1962 s 65) and s 12(2) (one application for all) if a life turns on a late claim.
- An independent test pass, a coverage table and the human gates.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added on 6 November 2026, following the design of the Graffiti Vandalism Act 2016 layer (its D2, D4 and D8). The
layer is `fp-qa.l4`, which imports `prelude` and `fp-claims.l4`; its cases are `fp-qa-cases.l4`, which also import
`daydate` to write dates, as `fp-cases-claims.l4` does. Nothing in `fp-claims.l4` or `fp-cases-claims.l4` was changed.
The layer builds the baseline's `My position` from plain answers and calls the baseline's functions. Every exported
question takes `my role` first (`@desc Which of these describes you`) and answers `does not arise for you` for a role
it is not about, without reading any other input. No exported question takes a DEONTIC or EVENT input.

**Roles.**

- `I am a family member who may have been left out of the estate, or left too little`: the baseline's seat (11
  questions, one for each of the baseline's 11 exports).
- `I take a share of the estate under the will or on intestacy`: the beneficiary, whose share bears provision ordered
  for a claimant (s 14(2)) (3 questions).
- `I am the executor or administrator of the estate`: s 20 protects them, and s 7(2) and s 20(5) time what they may do
  (4 questions).

The Supreme Court is not a seat. The administrator may be the Public Trustee (s 4(1)); the questions read the same for
it. The administrator is a seat because the executor is usually a private person, often family, whom s 20 addresses.

**Situation inputs.** Each is a BOOLEAN. FALSE gives `does not arise for you` without reading anything else.

| Name | Roles | Questions that take it |
|---|---|---|
| `probate or administration has been granted in Western Australia` | all three; claimant | the last day to claim without leave (s 7(2)(a)); whether my application is in time (s 7(2), (3)) |
| `I have applied to the Court and my claim is undecided` | claimant | s 7A interim order |
| `estate property the evidence on my claim did not reveal has come to light` | claimant | s 6(5), (7) further claim |
| `the Court has ordered provision for me` | claimant | s 14(2) |
| `I have given a mortgage, charge or assignment over my provision` | claimant | s 19 |
| `the administrator has been given notice of an intended claim` | claimant, administrator | s 20(5) lapse date |
| `I advised the administrator in writing that I consent, or that I will not apply` | claimant | s 20(4) |
| `the Court has ordered provision for a claimant` | beneficiary | s 14(2) |
| `I have distributed, or propose to distribute, part of the estate` | administrator | s 20(1), (3), (4), (5) |

Each situation is what the provision ties the rule to: the grant ("the date on which the administrator becomes
entitled to administer the estate", s 7(2)(a)), the pending application (s 7A "before making an order"), the property
the evidence "did not reveal" (s 6(5)(a)), the provision ordered (s 14(2)), the dealing (s 19), the notice (s 20(5)),
the written advice (s 20(4)) and the distribution (s 20(1), (3)). Who may claim, whether the will can shut a claim out,
and whether the Court can order provision arise for every claimant and take none.

**Decisions.**

- **QA1. Where a situation is the baseline's own input, it is passed TRUE within the situation.** This covers s 7A
  (`an application under section 6(1) is before the Court`), s 6(5) (`the evidence did not reveal the property`) and
  s 20(4) (`I advised the administrator in writing ...`). Within the situation the answer equals the baseline's with
  that input TRUE; outside it the question does not arise, where the baseline answered no.
- **QA2. The Court-order question keeps the baseline's Standing and Timing inputs.** The baseline's `Can the Court order
  provision for me` takes the answers to "May I claim" and "Will my application be heard" as inputs of those types, and
  so does this layer's question, with `@desc` saying so. Computing them inside from the facts would repeat 17 inputs
  and both rules.
- **QA3. Two questions serve more than one role from one rule.** The last day to claim without leave serves all three
  roles; the s 20(5) lapse date serves the claimant and the administrator. Their inputs are worded for any of them.
- **QA4. The administrator's protection is a helper here,** `s 20(1), (3), (4) -- no action lies against the
  administrator for the distribution`. It holds where there was no notice of an application or an unlapsed intended
  application, and the distribution was properly made (s 20(1)) or the claimant's tie is not through a legal marriage or
  adoption (s 20(3), which omits "properly"); or where the claimant consented in writing with full capacity (s 20(4),
  the baseline's rule). A lapsed notice of intention counts as no notice, since s 20(5) lets the administrator "act as
  if he had not received the notice" (INFERENCE). When none applies, the answer is "section 20 does not bar it: whether
  the action lies is for the general law", not "yes": s 20 only bars actions, it does not create one.
- **QA5. The beneficiary's two s 6 and s 14(2) questions are mirrors of the claimant's.** They call the same rules, and
  a case asserts each agrees with the claimant's answer on the same facts.

**Not covered for the new roles.** For the beneficiary: whether a gift is "settled property" charged against corpus
(s 14(3)), the Court's power to order the burden otherwise (s 14(2) "unless the Court otherwise orders"), and claims
after distribution (ss 8, 9). For the administrator: urgent distributions (s 11), service of the application (s 12),
the trustee's protection beyond s 20, and the costs order (s 14(6)).

**Checks.** `fp-qa.l4` runs and `l4 check` succeeds, with 0 errors and 0 warnings. `fp-claims.l4` and
`fp-cases-claims.l4` still give 0 errors and 0 warnings, and 44 of 44 assertions. `fp-qa-cases.l4` has **103
assertions, 103 satisfied**:

- 63 worked by hand (48 claimant, 5 beneficiary, 10 administrator). Of these, 11 assert the claimant's answer equal to
  the baseline export's on the same facts, one for each of the claimant's 11 questions; 3 mirror cases assert the
  beneficiary's or administrator's answer equal to the claimant's.
- 27 ask each question for each role it is not about, with every other input ASSUMEd.
- 13 ask each situation question, for each role it is about, with the situation FALSE and the rest ASSUMEd.

The parameter script (`qa_params.py`, in the session scratchpad) lists all 15 exported questions with their
parameters. It checks that `my role` comes first in each, that each situation input is identical in name, type and
`@desc` wherever it appears, and that no input is DEONTIC or EVENT. It also generated the 40 role and situation cases.
The baseline reads `Relationship`, `When I was born`, `Standing` and `Timing` through CONSIDERs with OTHERWISE, so an
ASSUMEd value of them would not fail if read (graffiti finding 31). They are read only after the role, which the
script checks.
