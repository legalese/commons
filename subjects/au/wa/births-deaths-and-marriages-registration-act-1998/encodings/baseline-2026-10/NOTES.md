## Seat

**The parent registering a child's birth; the family (and the executor) of a person who has died; and, for Part 6, the couple
marrying.** The plan asks: "Who must register, by when, and what goes on the record?" These are the people the Act reaches in the
three lives. Most of Part 3 is addressed to the parents directly (ss 15, 16, 22, 23). Most of Part 7 is addressed to officials
(the funeral director, the doctor, the Registrar), so those sections are encoded as what the family is owed and by when: who
must notify the death, which doctor must certify, within how many hours or days, and who registers.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act with the instruments that set its numbers:** followed. The Act sets its own periods (one month, 60 days, 48 hours,
  14, 7, 28 and 30 days) and penalties. The Regulations set the fees (r 4) and are deposited and used.
- **The actors the text names:** followed. They are the parents, the foundling's carer, the hospital's chief executive, the
  doctor or midwife, the funeral director, the institution, the coroner, the Registrar, the State court and the Family Court.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is an input:** followed. The Registrar's satisfaction (ss 15(1), 18(1)(c), 22(4)(b)), the
  Registrar's opinion that a name is prohibited (s 4), whether the Registrar considers it appropriate (ss 13(3), 18(1)(e)), a
  refusal under s 40(6), an adequate reason under s 55 and a remission under s 70 are all asked.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked:** followed. Worked out: disposal (s 4), and neonatal death and
  stillborn child, which are reduced to the facts the parent states. Asked: public or private hospital (Health Services Act 2016;
  Private Hospitals and Health Services Act 1927), and whether a death is reportable (Coroners Act 1996 s 17(3)).
- **One case is the text's natural unit:** followed. Each decision takes one birth, one marriage or one death.
- **The Act's own terms with a plain gloss:** followed, in the comments and in each `@desc`.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out:** followed. Out: Part 2, regulation-making (s 69) and the rest of Part 9 except s 70.
  Parts 4, 5 and 5A are out because the lives do not reach them; this is a scope choice, not machinery.

## Why this scope

The lives ask four things of this Act. Who must register a birth, and by when? Will both unmarried parents be on the record, and
what of the child's name? What registers a marriage? Who notifies and certifies a death, and by when? A parent also meets the
hospital's notice, the one-year chance to change given names, and the cost of a certificate. A family also meets disposal, the
30-day notice and the certificate an executor needs. Those are in. Parentage changes, change of name, change of registration of
sex or gender, and the Register's internal machinery are out, because none of the twelve events reaches them.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Daniel's 1996 birth is not governed by this Act.** The compilation table gives "Act other than s. 1 and 2: 14 Apr 1999".
   Daniel was born on 9 April 1996, three years before. The law then in force is not deposited. Chloe's registration in 2003 is
   within it. Asserted.
2. **Both parents register jointly, and marriage plays no part.** s 15(1) makes "the parents" jointly responsible, and both must
   sign. s 18(1)(a) puts both identities on the Register on their joint application. Neither section mentions marriage, so
   Ruby's unmarried parents (k051) stand exactly as married parents would. Asserted.
3. **Sixty days, but late is still accepted.** s 16(1) gives 60 days, with a penalty of $1 000. s 16(2): "The Registrar must
   accept a birth registration statement even though it is lodged after the end of the 60 day period." Days 15, 19 and 30 are in
   time; day 61 is late but still accepted. Asserted.
4. **The hospital has a separate duty: one month.** s 12 requires the hospital's chief executive (or, outside a hospital, the
   doctor, midwife or attendant) to notify the Registrar within one month. "One month" is read as a calendar month, which is an
   inference. That KEMH is a public hospital is assumed, not read. Asserted.
5. **One parent alone needs the Registrar's satisfaction and a reason the Act lists.** The reasons are death, disappearance, ill
   health, unavailability, or unwarranted distress (s 15(1)). Asserted.
6. **The name is a matter of choice.** s 22(3) does not require the surname to be a parent's. The Registrar may assign a name
   only if the name is prohibited or both parents cannot agree (s 22(4)), and must follow the Family Court (s 22(5)). A
   stillborn child, or one who dies within 28 days, need not be named (s 22(2)). Asserted.
7. **Given names can be changed once, within 12 months, by the right people.** s 23(2): by both parents if both signed, by the
   surviving parent if one has died. The Regulations charge $60 for this (r 4 item 9). Asserted (except the fee, which is
   encoded but not asserted).
8. **A marriage solemnised in WA must be registered, and Part 6 puts no duty or time limit on the couple.** s 37 requires
   registration. s 38 says how: by lodging the Marriage Act 1961 certificate. Its note places the lodging duty on the celebrant
   under s 50(4) of the Commonwealth Act. Registration is asserted; the absence of a time limit is noted, not asserted.
9. **The funeral director notifies a death; the Registrar registers it.** s 42 gives the funeral director (or the institution
   where the body is placed for research) 14 days to lodge the statement and the doctor's certificate. s 48(1): "The Registrar is
   to register a death". c086's title ("the funeral director registers the death") blurs the two. Asserted.
10. **The doctor has 48 hours, and certifies to the funeral director, not the Registrar** (s 44(1)(d)). There is no certificate
    where the death must be reported to a coroner (s 44(5)). Asserted.
11. **Organ donation does not change who notifies.** Donating kidneys (k097) is not placing the remains at an institution "for
    the purpose of medical education or research" (s 42(b)). This is an inference, and the funeral director remains the
    notifier. Asserted.
12. **Cremated remains leaving the State are not a "disposal".** s 4 "disposal" (d) excludes removal of remains that "have been
    cremated". So s 46 applies only to uncremated remains. Asserted.
13. **Registering costs nothing; the certificate costs $60.** The Regulations' fee Table lists no fee for lodging a birth,
    marriage or death registration. That registration is free is an inference from that absence. A certified copy is $60
    (item 1), and priority processing adds $44 (item 11). The Registrar may remit any fee (s 70). Asserted.
14. **The scenarios cite the wrong provisions.** The plan already corrects this, and the deposit confirms it. Marriages are
    Part 6 (Part 5 is change of name), and deaths are Part 7. The parents' 60-day duty is in ss 15-16, not ss 12-13. Names are in
    s 22, not s 19. Not an assertion.
