# Age of Majority Act 1972 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Edition

- **The Act:** the reprint compiled as at 22 August 2003, with amendments to
  No. 14 of 1996, as deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/act-1972-46.txt`
  (retrieved 7 September 2026).
- **No regulations.** The Act has no numbers set by an instrument.
- **The commencing day is not in the deposit.** It is the day fixed by
  proclamation under s 2. The reprint omits both the compilation table's rows and
  the proclamation, so the commencing day is an input to every question that needs
  it. The cases use 1 January 1973 as an **assumed placeholder**, only to exercise
  s 4(2) and s 5(1)(b). Any commencing day before 2021 gives Chloe the same answers.

## Seat

**The young person turning 18**, who asks: am I an adult in law, from what
moment, and how are "full age", "infant" or "minor" read when another law uses
them? The Act speaks of "a person", and that person is the one it applies to. It
names no official.

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. There
  are no such instruments. The proclaimed commencing day is not deposited, so it
  is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed. There are none besides the person.
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Two such judgments, both
  asked as facts:
  - whether another law has "a definition or ... an indication of a contrary
    intention" (s 5(2));
  - whether a word is a "similar expression".
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed:
  - "commencing day" is a date input;
  - "enactment", "instrument" and "laws of the State" (s 3) shape the list of
    places a word can appear.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one person, and their date
  of birth.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed: "full age", "sui juris",
  "nonage", and "commencement of the relevant anniversary" (the first moment of
  the birthday).
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Partly departed from. Ss 4 and 5
  are in scope. Some of s 5 is transitional, and three parts of it are left out:
  - s 5(6): money orders made before the commencing day;
  - s 5(8): property vested before it;
  - s 5(10), (11): limitation for causes of action that arose before it.

  None of these can reach anyone born after the commencing day. s 5(9) (an
  enactment incorporated in an instrument) is also out.

## The lives

- **Chloe, c025 (14 March 2021): turns 18.** The scenario asks: "What can she do
  today that she could not yesterday?" This Act answers the part that is its own:
  - she reaches 18 at the very start of 14 March 2021 (s 4(1));
  - she comes of full age and full capacity then (s 5(1)(a));
  - on 13 March 2021 she is not of full age; on 14 March she is.

  What follows from that under other laws (voting, marriage, wills, liquor,
  juries, enduring powers of attorney) is for those Acts' encodings. Many of them
  state "18 years" outright, and s 5(3) leaves such references alone.

## Why this scope

The Act is five sections long. Only s 4 and s 5 are operative, and the seat meets
both. The transitional subsections left out speak only to people and documents
from before the proclaimed day.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **You are 18 from the first moment of your birthday, not the day before.** s 4(1)
   fixes the time as "the commencement of the relevant anniversary of the date of
   his birth". This applies only to anniversaries on or after the commencing day
   (s 4(2)). Asserted.
2. **Anyone born on 29 February has no 18th anniversary, and the Act does not say
   when they turn 18.** A leap year plus 18 is never a leap year, so the 18th year
   of a 29 February birth never has a 29 February. The Act says nothing about the
   case. The encoding returns "not decided by this Act" rather than choosing
   28 February or 1 March. Twentieth birthdays, by contrast, do fall in leap years.
   Asserted.
3. **Full age and full capacity come together at 18, "for all the purposes of the
   laws of the State".** That covers WA law only (s 3). Under s 5(1)(b), people who
   were already 18 to 20 on the commencing day came of full age on that day.
   People who were already 21 are in neither limb: the Act does not say when they
   came of full age. Asserted.
4. **"Minor", "infant", "adult", "full age", "sui juris" and like words mean the
   18 line, in every WA enactment whenever made, and in instruments made on or
   after the commencing day.** There are two exceptions to the rule:
   - the law in question defines the word, or shows a contrary intention;
   - the instrument was made before the commencing day. A will made before then
     is not brought in merely by a later codicil (s 5(5)).

   Asserted.
5. **An age written in years is left alone.** "21 years of age" stays 21 (s 5(3)).
   So a law that says "18 years" is not applying this Act at all. Asserted.
6. **Employment is carved out.** s 5 does not touch:
   - industrial awards, orders, determinations or agreements;
   - apprenticeship instruments made under a wages-and-conditions enactment;
   - provisions of enactments on conditions of employment (s 5(7)).

   An award's use of "adult" or "junior" is read without this Act. Asserted.
7. **Turning 18 cures only incapacity due to age.** Incapacity "attributable to
   insanity, or mental infirmity, or any other factor distinct from age" remains
   (s 5(4)). Asserted.
8. **The Act does not say how age is reckoned before the commencing day.** s 4
   does not reach anniversaries before then (s 4(2)). For s 5(1)(b) ("of or over
   the age of 18 years but under the age of 21 years" on the commencing day), the
   encoding reckons by the anniversary as s 4 does. This is the encoder's reading,
   and the deposit does not settle it.

  only the Gazette holds.
- Read the omitted s 6 and Schedule: the consequential amendments, and the repeal
  of the Housing Advances (Contracts with Infants) Act 1968. The reprint drops

- Check the treatment of 29 February birthdays against the Interpretation Act 1984
  (WA) and any decided case.
- Link each "what follows at 18" question in Chloe's c025 to the encodings of the
  other Acts it cites.
