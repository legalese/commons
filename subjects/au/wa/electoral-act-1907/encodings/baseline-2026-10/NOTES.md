No REFUSE, no BECAUSE.

Not for public use as advice.

It adds the seat's other questions: compulsory enrolment and its bars (s 45), the arrangements that join the State register to the Commonwealth roll (ss 31, 31A, 35A), s 18(1)(ca)-(cd), the silent-elector status at 16 and 17 (s 17AA(2)(c)), another elector responding for an absent one (s 156(14a)), and real dates in place of ages (via `daydate`, as the Commonwealth baseline does).

## Seat

**The State elector**: the person who may enrol, must be enrolled and must vote, and who is sent the penalty notice.
The Act lays the duties on that person ("An elector must vote", s 156(1)) and the offences too (s 45(1), (2); s 156(16), (16A)).
The Electoral Commissioner appears only as what may be done to the elector, and by when: `Must I be sent a penalty notice`, `May I be sent an infringement notice`, `Must my name be removed from the register`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed. The caps ($50, $75), the fines ($50, $200, $1 000), the 21-day periods and the commencement of s 17AA (1 July 2024, from the compilation table) are in the deposit. The regulations are not deposited and nothing encoded takes a number from them.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the elector, the Electoral Commissioner, the enrolment officer (folded into "enrolled"), the Australian Electoral Officer for the State, and another elector who responds for an absent one.
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally.** Followed. The Commissioner's satisfaction under s 156(5), (11) and (12), and under s 156(15)(b), is an input. Whether a single-enrolment Gazette order under s 31(3), or a s 31A arrangement, is in operation is an input: the deposit cannot say.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. "Elector" (s 4 with s 17AA(2)), entitlement to be enrolled (s 17(1), s 18(2)) and the day of entitlement are worked out. Temporary visa and unlawful noncitizen (Migration Act 1958), the Commonwealth s 93(8)(a) ground, a lack of capacity notice (s 206A, not encoded), sentences under other Acts, and "valid and sufficient reason" beyond s 156(1A) are asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one person and one election.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed: elector, enrolled, district, whole of State electorate, penalty notice, infringement notice, modified penalty, response date, silent elector, Australian Electoral Officer.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed, with one departure: ss 31, 31A and 35A are machinery (arrangements between Governors), but the seat asks how the State roll relates to the Commonwealth roll, so they are encoded as far as they change what the elector must do.

## The lives

- **Chloe c024 (13 March 2021)**, "The State election, the day before her eighteenth birthday: too young by a day". `Am I entitled to be enrolled` and `Am I entitled to vote` on 13 March 2021: no; entitled from 14 March 2021. `Can I enrol on polling day and vote provisionally`: no, s 17(2)(a) needs age 18.
- **Chloe c046 (8 March 2025)**, "First State election" ("Compulsory?"). `Must I vote`: yes, unless she was prevented by absence from the State or an impairment; `Have I committed the offence of failing to vote`: not if she voted.
- **Jake k045 (8 March 2029)**, "The State election: enrolled by then, does not vote; a fine". `Must I vote`, `Have I committed the offence of failing to vote` and `Must I be sent a penalty notice`: yes. `What is the most the modified penalty can be`: $50, because his 2025 federal $20 is not "under this section". `Can I be prosecuted for failing to vote`: only if he neither answers the penalty notice nor pays an infringement notice; then the fine is up to $200.

Also asked of Jake (not a State-election event, but his 18th birthday on 30 August 2024 falls under the current s 45): `Must I enrol`, `What is the last day by which I must be enrolled` (19 September 2024), and `Can I be prosecuted for not being enrolled` (not once he has lodged a Commonwealth claim).

The plan entry names s 17 and s 156 and the seat asks about entitlement, compulsory enrolment and voting, the penalty and valid reasons, and the Commonwealth roll.
Those run through ss 4, 17, 17AA, 18, 31, 31A, 35A, 45, 155AB and 156.
The register's claim and objection machinery (ss 19, 34, 35, 38-44A, 46 onwards), eligible overseas and itinerant electors (ss 17A, 17B), members and spouses (s 17(4)), silent electors (s 51B), how votes are cast, and everything after s 156 are out.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Chloe is too young by a day, and polling-day enrolment does not help.** On 13 March 2021 she has not attained 18, so she is not entitled to be enrolled (s 17(1)(b)); had she been enrolled at 17 she would still not be an elector and could not vote (s 17AA(2)); and the polling-day route in s 17(2), (3) also needs s 17(1)(b). That an age is attained at the start of the birthday is an inference. Answered on the current text, not the 2021 text. Asserted.
2. **State enrolment at 16 and 17 began on 1 July 2024.** Section 17AA was inserted by No. 30 of 2023, whose Part 2 came into operation on 1 July 2024. Chloe's Commonwealth age-16 enrolment at 17 (14 March 2020) has no State counterpart in the deposited text; Jake, 17 on 1 July 2024, could have enrolled for the State. Until 18 such a person is taken to be a silent elector (s 17AA(2)(c)). Asserted.
3. **There is no express duty to enrol, only an offence.** Section 45(1) makes it an offence to be entitled and not enrolled "at the end of the period of 21 days beginning on the day they become entitled" ($50); s 38(2)(a) lets regulations require a claim. The decision `Must I enrol` is drawn from the offence. Asserted.
4. **The State's 21 days end a day earlier than the Commonwealth's.** "Beginning on the day they become entitled" counts the day of entitlement as day 1, so Jake (entitled 30 August 2024) had to be enrolled by the end of 19 September 2024. The Commonwealth baseline puts his s 101(4) day at 20 September 2024. Asserted.
5. **A Commonwealth claim answers the State offence.** Proceedings for the s 45(1) offence are barred once the person "lodges a claim for enrolment under the Commonwealth Electoral Act 1918" or is enrolled (s 45(7)); the same goes for the new-address offence (s 45(8)). The scenario says only that Jake is enrolled by 2029; if he got there by a Commonwealth claim (an inference), that claim ends his State exposure for 2024-25. Asserted.
6. **The State register and the Commonwealth roll are joined only by arrangement.** The Act keeps its own register (s 19). A Commonwealth claim is also a State claim only where a single enrolment procedure is declared by Gazette order (s 31(3)), and the Commissioner "may" enrol a person directly enrolled by the AEC only under a s 31A arrangement (s 35A(2)). Neither order nor arrangement is in the deposit, so both are inputs. Two links are in the Act itself: a person off the Commonwealth roll on the s 93(8)(a) ground is barred from State enrolment (s 18(1)(aa), (2)), and a Commonwealth claim made at a polling place lets an unenrolled person vote provisionally (s 17(3)(b)). Asserted.
7. **A new address within the district must be notified within 21 days of the move.** Section 45(2) runs from "the change"; the Commonwealth s 101(5) runs 21 days from a month at the new address. Asserted.
8. **The prisoner line is a year, not three.** A person serving or yet to serve detention or imprisonment of 1 year or longer may not vote (s 18(1)(c)) but stays entitled to enrolment (s 18(2) omits (c)-(cd)); the Commonwealth bar is 3 years. Asserted.
9. **The duty to vote is laid on an elector, not on a person entitled to vote.** Read literally, s 156(1) binds an enrolled prisoner. The encoding infers, as the Commonwealth baseline does, that a person barred from voting has no duty, and keeps him on the non-voters' list: the Commissioner need not send him a notice only if satisfied that he "was ineligible to vote" (s 156(5)(c)). Asserted.
10. **Absence excuses the duty; religion excuses the offence.** Section 156(1) excuses an elector "prevented ... by absence from the State" or by impairment on the voting days; the offence in s 156(16) needs no "valid and sufficient reason", which "includes an honest belief ... that abstaining from voting is part of their religious duty" (s 156(1A)). The two lists differ; reading the offence as needing the duty is an inference. The State test is absence from the State, the Commonwealth's absence from Australia. Asserted.
11. **Jake's "fine" is first a penalty notice capped at $50.** The modified penalty may not exceed $50 for a first matter under s 156, $75 otherwise (s 156(8)); his federal $20 does not count. The response date is at least 21 days after sending (s 156(7)). If he ignores it, the Commissioner "may" send an infringement notice with the same caps (s 156(12), (13), (13A)); only after that is a court fine of up to $200 possible (s 156(16)). The Commonwealth figure is a flat $20. Asserted.
12. **Section 156(14) cites a paragraph that does not exist.** Paying an infringement notice bars proceedings "for a contravention of subsection (16)(a)", but s 156(16) has no paragraphs in this compilation. Read as (16). Asserted.
13. **Not answering can cost the enrolment.** If the Commissioner is satisfied that a person who did not respond no longer lives at the roll address, the name must be removed, and a new claim is needed (s 156(15), (15A)). Asserted.
14. **Someone else may answer for an absent elector.** Another elector with personal knowledge may respond to the notice for an elector who is away from their place of living or physically incapacitated (s 156(14a)). Asserted.
15. **Leaving the district ends the vote under s 17(1)(e).** The entitlement to vote lasts "while continuing to live in that district". Whether another provision preserves the vote of an elector who has moved but not transferred is not encoded; the answer given is the s 17(1)(e) answer. Asserted, flagged.
16. **The scenario's "at 22" for c046 is wrong.** On 8 March 2025 Chloe, born 14 March 2003, is 21, as the event's own ages say. Not asserted (the encoding takes dates, not ages).
