# Strata Titles Act 1985 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act to replace.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands. One duty with a deadline, the strata company's duty to give the owner a
s 110 certificate within 14 days, is also written as a regulative rule.
That rule, `the strata company's duty to give me the certificate`, is the one exception to the line above: its `@export` is commented out ahead of legalese/l4-ide#779 (not landed), which refuses an `@export` whose result is a DEONTIC.

## Seat

**The owner of a lot in a strata titles scheme, and, before settlement, the buyer
who is about to become one.** The Act is applied to the owner. The owner pays the
levies, is bound by the by-laws and votes on resolutions (s 14(8): the strata
company "is comprised of the owners for the time being of the lots"). The
protections in Part 10 are the buyer's, and every owner in the three lives
reaches the lot through them.

The strata company, its council, the developer, the seller and the Tribunal are
the other side. Where a provision addresses only them, it is encoded as what it
means for the owner:

- s 100(2) becomes `Must my strata company keep a reserve fund`;
- s 102(6) becomes `Can the strata company spend outside the budget`;
- s 110 becomes the certificate's fee, its 14-day deadline and its effect on
  what a buyer can be made to pay;
- s 156(4) becomes `Must the developer give me its extra disclosures`;
- s 210 becomes `Can I seek internal review`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, instruments brought in.** Followed. The deposited Strata
  Titles (General) Regulations 2019 supply every number: 11%, $5 000 000, $500 a
  lot, $2 000, $140.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the owner, co-owners, the first
  mortgagee, the guardian, the strata company and its council, the scheme
  developer, the seller, the buyer, the legal practitioner, real estate agent or
  settlement agent, the Tribunal and its President.
- **A judgment handed to someone else, answered conditionally.** Followed. These
  are inputs, never guessed:
  - whether a by-law is "unfairly prejudicial ... oppressive or unreasonable"
    (s 46(j));
  - whether an interference is unreasonable (s 83);
  - whether the buyer is "materially prejudiced" (ss 159, 160, 162), which is
    the buyer's to prove;
  - whether the Tribunal gives leave for internal review (s 210(2)(a)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked as facts.**
  Followed. These are worked out from facts:
  - "designated strata company" (s 100(7), reg 79);
  - "special resolution" (s 123(4)-(6));
  - "type 1" and "type 2 notifiable variation" (s 3(1));
  - the date and period under s 163(4).

  These are asked as facts: "registered mortgage", "legally qualified member" and
  "assistance animal".
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one lot, one levy, one
  resolution, one by-law, one contract.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in comments beside
  each type and field.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Followed. Machinery out: ss
  1-2, 4-5, 218-228 and the transitional Schedules.

## Why this scope

The lives ask what each owner owes the strata company. That is s 100, with the
unit entitlement (s 37) and the regulations' interest rate. The seat brief adds
three more things the owner meets.

- **What the by-laws can and cannot do to me.** This covers:
  - the resolution each kind of by-law needs (s 44(2));
  - the special resolution itself (s 123);
  - what makes a by-law invalid (s 46);
  - enforcement and its $2 000 cap (s 47);
  - the owner's own duty in s 83.
- **The disclosure I get when buying.** This covers:
  - ss 156-164 and the notifiable variations of s 3(1);
  - the s 110 certificate, with its fee and its effect on the previous owner's
    arrears (s 100(6)).
- **Challenging a decision, as far as the events need.** This covers:
  - who casts the lot's vote (ss 120(2), 126);
  - spending outside the budget (s 102);
  - which disputes go to the Tribunal (s 197);
  - internal review of its order (s 210).

Left out, with the reason:

- **Operative, but not met in these lives:** structural alterations (Part 7
  Div 2), insurance (ss 84, 97-99), meetings and councils (ss 127-142), strata
  managers (Part 9), leasehold schemes and survey-strata specifics, the default
  by-laws' content (Schedules 1 and 2), Part 11 variation, Part 12 termination, the
  Tribunal's orders in detail (ss 199-209), ordinary resolutions and resolutions
  without dissent as counted (s 123(1)-(3), (7)), and the ten-year plan's content.
- **Not the owner's:** planning approval, scheme documents and registration
  (Parts 2-5) and the developer's own duties (Part 6), except as s 156(4) carries
  them to the buyer.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Contributions are in Part 8, not Part 7.** All three events cite "Part 7
   (contributions)". Part 7 is "Lot owners and occupiers" (ss 82-90). The levy is
   s 100, in Part 8 Division 1 Subdivision 3, "Financial management". Asserted.

2. **The by-laws can change how the administrative fund is shared, but not the
   reserve fund.** s 100(1)(c) levies the administrative fund by unit entitlement
   "or ... if the scheme bylaws provide for a different basis ... in accordance
   with that basis". s 100(2)(c) levies the reserve fund "in proportion to the unit
   entitlements" and offers no such alternative. Asserted.

3. **Every one of the three apartments must have a reserve fund.** A strata
   company is "designated" at 10 or more lots (s 100(7)), or under reg 79 if the
   building costs more than $5 000 000 to replace. A designated company must keep a
   reserve fund and a 10-year plan revised every 5 years (s 100(2), (2A)). The lot
   counts are the encoder's, but any apartment block of 10 lots qualifies.
   Asserted.

4. **Late levies bear 11% simple interest a year, unless the company sets less.**
   s 100(4)(b) and reg 78. The company may set "no interest or interest at a lesser
   rate", generally or in a particular case. Counting the interest by days over a
   365-day year is the encoder's reading. Asserted.

5. **A buyer inherits the seller's unpaid levies, and the s 110 certificate is
   the buyer's shield.** s 100(6) makes a new owner jointly and severally liable
   for contributions and interest unpaid when they became owner. s 110(3) makes the
   certificate "conclusive evidence ... in favour of a person taking an estate or
   interest in a lot for valuable consideration". The encoder reads the two
   together: what the certificate stated caps the arrears up to its date, and later
   arrears fall outside it. Asserted.

6. **An owner behind on levies loses the vote, but not on every resolution.**
   s 120(2) bars the vote on ordinary and special resolutions. It does not bar it
   on resolutions without dissent, unanimous resolutions, termination resolutions,
   or resolutions postponing a leasehold expiry day. Asserted.

7. **Joint owners have one voice, and a mortgagee can speak first.** s 126(b)(iii):
   co-owners "may only cast the vote through jointly appointing a single proxy".
   s 126(a): the first mortgagee of a lot under a registered mortgage may cast the
   vote, and the owner may cast it only if the mortgagee does not. Daniel and
   Priya, and Jake, all meet this. Asserted.

8. **What counts as a special resolution depends on the size of the scheme.** In
   a 2-lot scheme it must be unanimous. In a 3-, 4- or 5-lot scheme it needs all
   but one lot and half the unit entitlement in favour, with no limit on votes
   against. Otherwise it needs half the lots and half the unit entitlement in
   favour, and less than a quarter against by each count. Asserted.

9. **What a by-law cannot do.** A conduct by-law needs a special resolution and a
   governance by-law needs a resolution without dissent (s 44(2)). Under s 46 a
   by-law is invalid to the extent that it:
   - restricts a sale, lease, mortgage or devolution of a lot;
   - restricts an assistance animal;
   - limits a member's vote;
   - modifies an easement;
   - conflicts with any written law;
   - is found unfairly prejudicial, oppressive or unreasonable.

   Asserted.

10. **The Tribunal can fine a by-law breach at most $2 000, and never by the
    day.** s 47 lets the strata company or another owner apply only after one of
    three things: serious harm to someone else, three breaches of the same by-law,
    or breach of a written notice. The penalty is capped by reg 58. A daily penalty
    needs a regulation authorising it (s 47(6)(d)), and the deposited regulations
    contain none. A penalty is collected like an unpaid levy, with interest
    (s 47(8)). Asserted.

11. **Jake's off-the-plan purchase is the Act's hardest-working protection.** The
    seller was the developer before registration, so s 156(4) required the extra
    disclosures: the 12-month budget estimate, the developer's s 79 benefits, and
    the services contracts. A unit entitlement registered at 32 against an estimate
    of 30 moves his share by 6.7%. That makes it a type 1 notifiable variation
    (s 3(1), read by the encoder as 5% of the notified proportion). If the developer
    did not notify it in time, Jake could avoid the contract before settlement
    without proving prejudice (s 161). Asserted.

12. **Three years from contract to settlement needs an agreed period, or Jake
    could have walked away.** s 163 requires the deposit to be held on trust by a
    named practitioner or agent. It lets the buyer avoid the contract if the scheme
    is not registered within the agreed period or, failing agreement, within 6
    months. Jake signed in 2042 and settled in 2045. Unless the contract agreed a
    longer period, he could have avoided it from month 6 until registration.
    Asserted.

13. **A quarrel about the size of a levy debt is not a scheme dispute.**
    s 197(3)(f) excludes "a dispute about an amount owed as a debt". The strata
    company recovers levies in court (s 100(4)(c)). The owner's route to the
    Tribunal is to challenge the resolution or decision behind the levy
    (s 197(1)(a)(iv)), or the validity of a by-law. Asserted.

14. **Internal review is narrow.** It is open only for an order made by a member
    who is not legally qualified (reg 162), with the Tribunal's leave, within 28
    days unless the President extends the time (s 210). Asserted.

15. **The certificate costs at most $140, or $154 with GST, and must come within
    14 days.** reg 86 and s 110. A strata company that is late commits an offence
    with a $3 000 fine. Asserted.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added 6 November 2026 (batch qa13), on the pattern of the Graffiti Vandalism Act 2016 encoding's `gva-seat.l4` (its
design D2, D4 and D8). The layer is `sta-qa.l4`, with cases in `sta-qa-cases.l4`. It imports `prelude` and
`sta-strata`; `sta-strata.l4` and `sta-cases-strata.l4` are unchanged. Every exported question takes `my role` first
(`@desc Which of these describes you`) and answers `does not arise for you`, reading nothing else, for a role it is not
about. Every answer type carries that value: `Yes or no`, `Amount`, `Days`, `Working days`, `Vote answer` (wrapping
the baseline's `Who casts my lot's vote`), `Variation answer` (wrapping `Notifiable variation`) and `Notice deadline`.
No exported question takes a DEONTIC or EVENT input. The baseline's s 110 duty is a DEONTIC, and its `@export` was
already commented out; it is not used here.

### Roles

The baseline's seat is "the owner of a lot ... and, before settlement, the buyer who is about to become one". The Act
gives those two different questions, so the layer makes them two roles, and adds two more people the Act puts duties on.

- `I own a lot in a strata titles scheme`: 20 questions. They are the baseline's owner questions: levies, the reserve
  fund, interest, the budget, the vote, special resolutions, by-laws, s 83, s 47, the certificate, s 197 and s 210.
  There is also one new question, s 45(4) (the owner's visitors).
- `I am buying a lot in a strata titles scheme, or have just bought one`: 11 questions. They are the baseline's
  Part 10 questions, s 100(6) arrears, and the certificate fee and its 14 days (s 107(2)(b) lets a buyer apply).
- `I am selling a lot in a strata titles scheme`: 10 questions. Must I give the s 156 information (s 156(1), (5)); the
  developer's extra disclosures (s 156(3), (4), the baseline's own rule); the onus of proof (s 156(6), s 157(6)); must I
  notify this change (ss 3(1), 157(1), (4)) and by when (s 157(3)); may the buyer postpone (s 158) and by how much; may
  the buyer avoid (s 159; ss 160-162, the baseline's rules from the other side); and what I must repay myself
  (s 164(2)(a)).
- `I rent or occupy a lot in a strata titles scheme, and do not own it`: 7 questions. Does a by-law bind me (s 45(1)(c),
  (3), with the baseline's test), s 83, s 45(4), and the four s 47 questions.
- Not seats: the strata company, its council, the scheme developer as such, the Tribunal and its President. A first
  mortgagee may vote (s 126) and apply under s 47(3), but is not given a seat here.

The 41 exports count each question once; 7 of them serve two roles (s 45(4) and the four s 47 questions for the owner
and the occupier; the two certificate questions for the owner and the buyer).

### Situation inputs

Each is a BOOLEAN, placed second, straight after `my role`. FALSE gives `does not arise for you`, and nothing else is
read. Each has one name, type and `@desc` across all its questions and roles (checked by script).

| Input | Roles | Questions that take it |
|---|---|---|
| `the strata company says I have broken a by-law` | owner, occupier | 4: is the breach notice valid, can I be taken to the Tribunal, the most I can be ordered to pay, a daily penalty (s 47) |
| `I paid a contribution late` | owner | 1: interest (s 100(4)(b)) |
| `the Tribunal has made an order in my strata dispute` | owner | 1: internal review (s 210) |
| `the contract of sale has been signed` | buyer, seller | buyer 6: the kind of variation, may I postpone, by how many working days, s 159, ss 160-162, s 163; seller 6: must I notify, by when, may the buyer postpone, by how many working days, s 159, ss 160-162 |
| `the buyer has avoided the contract` | buyer, seller | buyer 1: how much I get back; seller 1: how much I must repay (s 164(2)) |

A question that arises in every situation of its role takes none. That covers the owner's levy, reserve fund, budget,
vote, by-law, s 83, s 45(4), certificate and s 197 questions; the buyer's s 100(6), s 156(4) and certificate
questions; the seller's three s 156 questions; and the occupier's by-law, s 83 and s 45(4) questions.

### Decisions

- **Q1. The owner and the buyer are separate roles.** The baseline puts them in one seat, but a buyer before
  settlement does not pay levies or vote, and an owner of some years does not ask Part 10's questions. The certificate
  questions serve both.
- **Q2. The contract, not the variation, is the situation for Part 10 after signing.** ss 157-163 all run "after a buyer
  signs a contract". Whether a notifiable variation occurred is what the questions answer, so it is not a situation.
- **Q3. The s 100(6) arrears question takes no situation.** It is asked "having bought my lot". The baseline's s 159
  and s 160 questions already ask `settlement has happened` as an ordinary input; making it a situation of the arrears
  question would have given one name two places in the form.
- **Q4. The seller's notice duty (s 157(1), (4)) is a helper in the layer.** It is built on the baseline's s 3(1)
  classification, and lists every value of `Notifiable variation` with no OTHERWISE. "By when" adds s 157(3)'s two
  times. s 157(5)'s time for a notice of completion is asked as a fact.
- **Q5. What the seller repays (s 164(2)(a))** is the baseline's sum with no held deposit and no rent: the money paid to
  the seller. The deposit held on trust, less rent, comes back from its holder (s 164(2)(b)), as the baseline reads it.
- **Q6. Shared inputs were made identical** so that a merging form cannot mix them. `the scheme is registered` has one
  text for s 156(4) and s 163, and `the by-law` and `the sum of the unit entitlements` have one text each. The seller's
  inputs are in the seller's words and have their own names, such as `I gave everything s 156 requires before the
  buyer signed`.

### Not covered for the new roles

- The seller: the content of the s 156(1) items one by one (asked as one fact), the approved form (s 156(5)), s 157(2)

- The occupier: exclusive use by-laws (s 45(1)(e)), a lessee's covenant as a contract claim (s 45(3) beyond binding),

- The buyer and the seller: a dispute between them under s 197(1)(h) (the baseline's s 197 question is the owner's).

### After the independent check (6 Nov 2026)

- **Division by zero on a sum of unit entitlements of 0 (fixed).** The levy share (s 100), the buyer's notifiable
  variation (s 3(1)) and the seller's two s 157 questions call baseline functions that divide by a sum of unit
  entitlements. s 37(1) makes that sum the total of the whole numbers allocated to the lots, so a real scheme's sum is
  never 0; but the form's engine tries 0 for open numbers, and a failed evaluation stops it settling a question. Two
  guards in the layer give the baseline's answer whenever every sum is above 0, and decide only the zero case:
  - `my contribution, with no unit entitlements guarded`: with a sum of 0 every lot's entitlement is 0, so the share by
    unit entitlement is 0; a by-law basis for the administrative fund (s 100(1)(c)) still applies, since it divides by
    nothing.
  - `the kind of notifiable variation, with no unit entitlements guarded`: with a sum of 0 the proportion limb of
    type 1 cannot be measured. The baseline is asked with that limb neutral (every entitlement and sum 1, the other
    facts as given), and its type 2 limb on a changed schedule is applied to the facts as given, in the baseline's own
    order. 13 cases added: the zero cases of all four questions, and one that the guard equals the baseline above 0.
- **The two "Does this by-law bind me" questions stay open for the other roles (not changed).** The layer gates them
  correctly: each returns `does not arise for you` from `my role` alone, and every case and completion agrees. The form
  keeps them because of its engine: to drop a question it tries every combination of its open yes/no and choice
  inputs, up to 1 024 (`PROBE_CAP` in `interact-engine.ts`), and these take `the kind` (2), two yes/no facts and
  `What the by-law does` (8 yes/no facts): 2 048. Once `the kind` is answered they drop (the verifier's run shows it).
  Narrowing the inputs would mean asking s 46's validity facts under a second set of names, and the baseline needs
  all of them. The fix belongs in the engine, as noted for the Civil Liability Act: when the combinations are over the
  cap, evaluate one completion with a trace, and settle when only answered inputs were read.
