# Wills Act 1970 (WA) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE, no BECAUSE.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** the compilation published on legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 49116), deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49116.txt`.
The deposit gives no compilation number.
Its compilation table ends with the Legal Profession Uniform Law Application Act 2022 s 424 (in force 1 July 2022), and the last reprint it lists is Reprint 5 as at 10 July 2015.
One amendment is uncommenced and not shown: the Assisted Reproductive Technology and Surrogacy Act 2025 Pt 17 Div 15.
The Act has no regulations that set numbers. Its numbers are the age of 18 (ss 7, 40(2)(b)), the 2 witnesses (s 8(c)), and two commencement dates read from the compilation table.

**Checks:** one module (`wills-making.l4`, 20 exported questions) and one case file (`wills-cases-making.l4`, generated by the scratchpad script `gen_wa_wills-act-1970_baseline.py`).
68 assertions written, 68 satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings, on the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
Expected values were written by hand from the text.

This baseline replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers everything that one covered and reuses its hand-checked results where they still hold. It adds s 16(1) revival and recasts every decision as a question from the seat.

## Seat

**The will-maker (testator) and the beneficiary.**
The Act is applied to the person making the will: whether they are old enough, whether the signing is valid, and whether a marriage, a divorce or an act of theirs has revoked the will.
It is applied to the beneficiary when the will takes effect. Does a witness lose a gift? What becomes of a gift when the beneficiary dies first? Does a child left out take anything?
Both seats are named in the plan entry. Every exported question is phrased as one of them would ask it.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. There are none; the two commencement dates come from the compilation table.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the testator, the witnesses, the person signing at the testator's direction, the beneficiary and their children, the Supreme Court, the Principal Registrar.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are asked as facts:
  - the Supreme Court's satisfaction under s 32 (informal documents) and s 42(1) (statutory wills);
  - "other evidence establishing" contemplation of marriage (s 14(3)(b)) or a contrary intention (s 14A(2)(b));
  - compliance with a foreign law (s 20).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out from facts:
  - when a marriage "ends" (s 14A(3));
  - "apparent" (s 10(1)), as one fact;
  - "disposition" (s 4).

  "Marriage" (Marriage Act 1961), a "divorce order" (Family Law Act 1975) and recognition of a foreign dissolution are asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one will, with its signing, or one gift in it.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments. Field names use the will-maker's plain words ("I signed it", "the witnesses signed in my presence"), each tied to its paragraph.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out:
  - s 1, s 2 and s 5;
  - the procedural sections of Part XI (ss 41, 43-47);
  - s 48 (recognition of other places' statutory wills);
  - the international-will Annex beyond one fact.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names ss 7, 8, 11 and 13. The seat it gives covers capacity, formalities, revocation (by marriage, divorce and destruction), gifts to witnesses, and informal wills as far as the Act states.
So the encoding covers:

- s 6 (what a will may do: d085);
- s 7, s 8 and s 11;
- s 10 (later alterations);
- the deleted s 13 and its transitional note;
- ss 14, 14A and 15 (revocation);
- s 16(1) (revival);
- the other routes to formal validity: s 17, s 20, Part XA, Part X ss 32-33, and Part XI ss 39(2), 40 and 42(1);
- ss 26(1)(b), (g) and 27, the beneficiary's question when someone dies first (c098, k084).

Not encoded:

- s 9;
- s 10(4);
- s 16(2) and (3);
- s 20(2)(a), (c) and (d), and s 21;
- the other construction rules (ss 24, 25, 26(1)(a), (c)-(f), 28 and 28A);
- Part IX;
- ss 41 and 43-48;
- Part XII s 50 (rectification).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **There is no s 13 in the text, and nothing in the Act takes a gift from a witness.**
   The plan was right that s 13 is not a heading. The deposit shows only "s. 13 deleted: No. 28 of 2003 s. 211(1)", inside the bracket that also records s 12's deletion. Part IV has no surviving sections.
   The deposit does not give the deleted text, so this encoding does not say what s 13 provided.
   Note 1 sets out s 211(2) of the Acts Amendment (Equality of Status) Act 2003: the repeal applies to the will of anyone dying on or after its commencement, which the compilation table gives as 1 July 2003.
   What is left is s 8, which counts witnesses, and s 11, which disqualifies only a person "unable to see and attest that a testator has signed". Neither takes anything from a witness who is a beneficiary, or a beneficiary's spouse.
   So a will witnessed by a beneficiary satisfies s 8, and the gift stands for any death on or after 1 July 2003. That includes every death in the lives.
   For a death before that date, the encoding returns an explicit "not covered" value. Asserted.
2. **Under 18, no will is valid, and the Supreme Court cannot cure it.**
   s 7 is subject only to s 17 (privileged wills made under the repealed s 18). There is no exception for a married minor and no leave of the court.
   s 7 is not made subject to Part X. So on the text, a document the Court would accept under s 32 is still invalid if its maker was 17.
   Chloe cannot make a valid will on 13 March 2021 and can on 14 March 2021. Asserted.
3. **Two witnesses present together, each able to see.**
   With s 11 applied to s 8(c), a will with two witnesses, one unable to see, fails, and the same will with three witnesses passes.
   Witnesses who sign out of the testator's presence fail s 8(d).
   No form of attestation is needed. Asserted.
4. **s 20 decides form, not who inherits.**
   A will conforming only to the law where the land is counts as properly executed so far as it disposes of that land (s 20(2)(b)), and not otherwise.
   Whether Italian forced heirship divides Giuseppe's house is choice of law and essential validity, on which the Act is silent. Asserted as to form only.
5. **A child left out takes nothing under this Act.**
   The Act gives no share that the will does not give. s 27 saves a gift only to "a child or other issue of the testator" who dies first leaving children.
   A whole-estate gift to a spouse who dies first is "not disposed of by the will". What happens to it then is intestacy, under the Administration Act 1903.
   Any claim against the estate is the Family Provision Act 1972. Asserted.
6. **Equal shares to children: substitution first, then accrual.**
   A half share to a child who dies first leaving a child passes to that child's surviving children (s 27).
   A half share to a childless child who dies first accrues to the other half (s 26(1)(g)).
   A gift to a child as a joint tenant is outside s 27 (s 27(3)), and a contrary intention in the will displaces both rules. Asserted.
7. **Marriage revokes; divorce revokes; a spouse's death does not.**
   s 14(1): marriage revokes a will unless it was made in contemplation of the marriage, shown on its face or by other evidence (s 14(3)).
   s 14A(3) lists only three endings: a divorce order taking effect, a decree of nullity, and a foreign dissolution or annulment recognised in Australia. Separation is not on the list, and neither is a spouse's death.
   s 14A applies only to marriages ending on or after 9 February 2008. That date is an INFERENCE from the table entry "Wills Amendment Act 2007 s. 324", read as ss 3-24. Asserted.
8. **Revocation only in the s 15 ways.**
   Destruction by another person counts only in the testator's presence and at their direction. Accidental destruction does not count. Telling the family is not one of the s 15 ways either; that is encoded but not asserted.
   An unwitnessed note does not revoke unless the Court accepts it under Part X. Asserted.
9. **A trust is a disposition, but the Act never says so.**
   s 6 lets a will dispose of property the testator is entitled to at death, exercise a power of appointment, and appoint a guardian of an infant child.
   A gift to trustees is still a "disposition" (s 4), so (INFERENCE) a testamentary trust falls within s 6(a). Its terms are general law. Asserted as to the categories.
10. **The informal-will power is for the dead, and only since 9 February 2008.**
    s 32 speaks of "a deceased person". s 33(1) confines it to deaths on or after the commencement of s 23 of the 2007 amendment.
    While the will-maker lives, an informal document is not a will. After death it is one only if the Court is satisfied, and that is asked as a fact. Asserted.
11. **A statutory will needs an adult who is living.**
    s 40(2) bars an order for a person under 18 or no longer living. Under s 42(1) the Court must refuse unless it is satisfied of four matters. The encoding decides only that the Court *may* make the order. Asserted.
