# Community Titles Act 2018 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act to replace.

The encoding follows the patterns of the Strata Titles Act 1985 baseline beside it
(`../../../strata-titles-act-1985/encodings/baseline-2026-10/`). Where the two Acts
differ, the module's comments say "Unlike the Strata Titles Act 1985 ...". The two
schemes are separate: a parcel already subdivided by a strata scheme cannot be
subdivided by a community scheme (s 7(1)(c)).

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands. One duty with a deadline, the community corporation's duty to give the
owner a s 97 certificate within 14 days, is also written as a regulative rule.
That rule, `the community corporation's duty to give me the certificate`, is the one exception to the line above: its `@export` is commented out ahead of legalese/l4-ide#779 (not landed), which refuses an `@export` whose result is a DEONTIC.

- **The regulations:** the Community Titles Regulations 2021, version 00-r0-00, in
  currency from 1 July 2026, deposited at
  `../../../community-titles-regulations-2021/registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49620.txt`
  (mrdoc_49620, retrieved 20 October 2026). The regulations used:
  - reg 8: termination papers for another scheme served on the seller are a type 1
    notifiable variation;
  - reg 59 and Schedule 1: the explanation a breach notice must carry;
  - reg 60: the by-law penalty, at most $2 000;
  - regs 77 and 78: $500 a lot or tier parcel;
  - reg 79: arrears interest at most 11% a year;
  - reg 84: the certificate fee, at most $140, plus 10% if GST is payable; an
    application is not duly made until the fee is paid;
  - reg 173: which orders may be internally reviewed.

## Seat

**The owner of a lot in a community titles scheme, and, before settlement, the
buyer who is about to become one.** The Act is applied to the owner. The owner is
levied, is bound by the by-laws and votes on resolutions (s 17(6)-(8): each
community corporation "is comprised of" the owners of its lots and, for each tier
parcel, the corporation below). The protections in Part 10 are the buyer's, and
the owner in the life reaches the lot through them.

The community corporation, its council, the original subdivision owner, the seller
and the Tribunal are the other side. Where a provision addresses only them, it is
encoded as what it means for the owner:

- s 85 becomes `Must my community corporation keep a reserve fund`;
- s 87(5)-(6) becomes `Must the improvement budget pass by special resolution` and
  `Can the community corporation spend outside the budget`;
- s 88(5) becomes `Is my contribution notice valid`;
- s 97 becomes the certificate's fee, its 14-day deadline and its effect on what a
  buyer can be made to pay;
- s 130(4) becomes `Must the seller give me its extra disclosures`;
- s 175 becomes `Can I seek internal review`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, instruments brought in.** Followed. The deposited Community
  Titles Regulations 2021 supply every number: 11%, $500 a lot or tier parcel,
  $2 000, $140.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the owner, co-owners, the community
  corporation and the corporations above and below it, the original subdivision
  owner, the seller, the buyer, the legal practitioner, real estate agent or
  settlement agent, the Tribunal and its President.
- **A judgment handed to someone else, answered conditionally.** Followed. These
  are inputs, never guessed:
  - whether a by-law is "unfairly prejudicial ... oppressive or unreasonable"
    (s 47(l));
  - whether an interference is unreasonable (s 71);
  - whether the buyer is "materially prejudiced" (ss 133, 134, 136), which is the
    buyer's to prove;
  - whether the Tribunal gives leave for internal review (s 175(2)(a)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked as facts.**
  Followed. These are worked out from facts:
  - "relative unit entitlement" and the share in common property (ss 3(1), 16(7));
  - membership of each corporation (s 17(6)-(8));
  - "ordinary resolution" and "special resolution" (s 106(6), (7));
  - "type 1" and "type 2 notifiable variation" (s 3(1), reg 8);
  - the date of the contract (s 137(4)).

  These are asked as facts: "legally qualified member", "assistance animal" and
  "restricted use condition".
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one lot, one contribution,
  one resolution, one by-law, one contract.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in comments beside
  each type and field.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Followed. Machinery out: ss
  1-2, 4-5, 177-190 and the amending Part 14.

## The lives

One event in the plan, asserted with that life's facts and the encoder's figures
for the townhouse.

- **chloe c081 (2040)**: a townhouse in a community titles scheme in Baldivis,
  $1,300,000, bought with Liam. The event asks "what is a community titles scheme;
  what do they owe the community corporation?" The encoder places the townhouse as
  a tier 2 lot (80 of 1,000) in a 12-lot tier 2 scheme whose parcel carries 150 of
  1,000 in the tier 1 scheme. The answers:
  - `How do I take part in this community corporation` gives "directly, as a
    member" for the tier 2 corporation, and "through the community corporation
    below it" for the tier 1 corporation.
  - `What is my share of the tier 1 common property` gives 0.012 (80/1,000 times
    150/1,000).
  - `How much is my contribution` gives $2,880 of a $36,000 tier 2 budget.
  - `How much of the tier 1 levy reaches me` gives $2,400 of a $200,000 tier 1
    levy, passed down through the tier 2 corporation.
  - `Must my community corporation keep a reserve fund` gives yes.
  - `How much interest do I owe` on a $720 quarter paid 73 days late, at a 10%
    rate the corporation fixed, gives $14.40.
  - `Who may cast my lot's vote` gives Chloe and Liam only through one proxy they
    appoint jointly.
  - `Must the seller give me its extra disclosures` gives no, on the encoder's
    assumption of a resale from a private seller.

The plan's other questions for this event (transfer duty, the certificate of title)
belong to the Duties Act 2008 and the Transfer of Land Act 1893.

## Why this scope

The life asks what the community corporation is and what the owner owes it. That
is ss 16-17 (the corporation and the share in common property), s 85 (the funds)
and s 88 (contributions and interest), with the unit entitlement (s 41) and the
regulations' interest cap. The seat brief adds three more things the owner meets.

- **What the by-laws can and cannot do to me.** This covers:
  - the resolution a by-law needs (s 43) and an exclusive use by-law's consent
    (s 46(5));
  - the ordinary and special resolution themselves (s 106);
  - what makes a by-law invalid (s 47);
  - enforcement and its $2 000 cap (s 48);
  - the owner's own duties in ss 70-72.
- **The disclosure I get when buying.** This covers:
  - ss 130-138 and the notifiable variations of s 3(1) and reg 8;
  - the s 97 certificate, with its fee and its effect on the previous owner's
    arrears (s 88(6)).
- **Disputes.** This covers:
  - which disputes go to the Tribunal (s 162);
  - internal review of its order (s 175).

Left out, with the reason:

- **Operative, but not met in this life:** insurance (ss 73, 83-84), the
  corporation's property powers and recovery of work costs (ss 75-82), records,
  registers and inspection (ss 91-96), meetings, councils and officers
  (ss 108-116), scheme managers (Part 9), termination (Part 11), the Tribunal's
  orders in detail (ss 163-174), and how a tier parcel's vote is decided
  (s 106(9)-(10)).
- **Not the owner's:** planning and the community development statement (Part 3),
  scheme documents and registration (Parts 4-5), statutory easements, rates and
  taxes, and the original subdivision owner's own duties (Part 6), except as
  s 130(4) carries them to the buyer.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The community corporation is in Part 2, and contributions are in Part 8.**
   The life cites "Part 3 (community corporations), Part 7 (contributions)". The
   corporation is established by s 17, in Part 2. Part 3 is "Planning and
   development". Part 7 is "Lot owners and occupiers" (ss 70-74). Contributions are
   s 88, in Part 8 Division 1 Subdivision 3, "Financial management". Asserted (the
   decisions sit on ss 17 and 88).

2. **A tier 2 owner is not a member of the tier 1 corporation.** s 17(6) makes the
   tier 1 corporation's members the tier 1 lot owners and, "for each tier 2 parcel
   ..., the tier 2 corporation". The owner reaches the tier 1 corporation only
   through their own corporation: its levy (s 88(3)) and its vote (s 106(9)).
   Asserted.

3. **A tier 2 owner's share of the estate's common property is a product.**
   s 16(7)(b): the share in the tier 1 common property is the lot's relative unit
   entitlement times the tier parcel's. A tier 3 owner multiplies three. Asserted.

4. **The tier 1 levy is passed down by the same rule.** s 88(3): a contribution
   payable by a corporation as a member "must be apportioned between its members
   according to the relative unit entitlements", unless the by-laws provide
   otherwise. Asserted.

5. **Every community corporation must keep a reserve fund and a 10 year plan.**
   s 85(1)-(2), with no threshold. The Strata Titles Act 1985 requires them only
   of a designated strata company. Asserted.

6. **By-laws can change how any contribution is shared, the reserve fund
   included.** s 88(2) and s 45(3)(e): "for all or specified purposes". The Strata
   Titles Act 1985 allows a by-law basis for the administrative fund only.
   Asserted.

7. **Interest is the corporation's choice, capped at 11%.** s 88(1)(f) lets the
   corporation "fix a rate of interest", "subject to any limitations imposed by the
   regulations", and reg 79 sets 11% as "the maximum". The Act sets no default
   rate, unlike the Strata Titles Act 1985. The encoder therefore reads it that no
   interest runs unless a rate was fixed. Interest is also excluded where the
   corporation chose the inflate-and-discount method instead (s 88(1)(e)). The
   365-day count is the encoder's. Asserted.

8. **A contribution notice needs 14 days.** s 88(4)-(5): the contribution is
   payable on the date in a notice that states the amount, the date, any arrears,
   the cost of paying late and the split between the funds. The notice must be
   served "at least 14 days before the date for payment". Asserted.

9. **No objection route for spending outside the budget.** s 87(6) allows only
   $500 a lot or tier parcel a year (reg 78), or a higher amount fixed by special
   resolution, or spending a court, tribunal or written-law notice requires. The
   Strata Titles Act 1985 adds a notice-and-objection route. Asserted.

10. **A special resolution is counted against the whole scheme.** s 106(7): more
    than 3/4 of "the sum of the unit entitlements of all the lots and tier parcels
    in the community titles scheme", or more than 2/3 with only 2 or 3 members. It
    is not a share of the votes cast, and there is no limb on votes against. An
    ordinary resolution is simply more value for than against (s 106(6)).
    Asserted.

11. **The Act takes no vote away for arrears; only a by-law can, and only on
    ordinary resolutions.** s 106(13)(c), (14)(b) and s 47(g). Asserted.

12. **Co-owners have one voice, and a mortgagee has none.** s 106(8)(b): co-owners
    vote "only through the owners jointly appointing a proxy". s 107(5): a sole
    owner present at the meeting must vote in person. Nothing in the Act lets a
    mortgagee cast the lot's vote, unlike the Strata Titles Act 1985 s 126(a).
    Asserted (the mortgagee point by its absence from the decision).

13. **Every by-law is made by special resolution, and some are invalid.** There
    is no governance/conduct split (s 43(2)). Exclusive use by-laws also need each
    special lot owner's written consent (s 46(5)). Beyond the Strata Titles Act's
    grounds, s 47 adds two kinds of invalidity: inconsistency with the community
    development statement, and inconsistency with a higher tier's by-laws.
    Asserted.

14. **The Tribunal can fine a by-law breach at most $2 000, and never by the
    day.** s 48(6) and reg 60. No regulation authorises a daily penalty, though the
    Schedule 1 explanation tells the owner one may be imposed "if the regulations
    authorise it". Breaching a restricted use condition on the scheme plan is an
    offence: $10 000 plus $1 000 a day (s 70). Asserted.

15. **Termination papers for another scheme in the estate are a type 1
    variation.** s 3(1)(c) covers the buyer's own scheme. reg 8 adds termination
    papers for any other scheme served on the seller. Either lets the buyer avoid
    the contract before settlement without proving prejudice, if not notified in
    time (s 135). Asserted.

16. **An off-the-plan buyer can walk away if the lot is not created within the
    agreed period, or 6 months.** s 137(3). The test is that the lot is created,
    not that "the scheme is registered" (the Strata Titles Act's wording). Asserted.

17. **No dispute about a debt goes to the Tribunal.** s 162(3)(f) excludes "a
    dispute about an amount owed as a debt", with none of the exceptions in the
    Strata Titles Act 1985 s 197(3)(f). A dispute about an application for
    information or a certificate is a scheme dispute (s 162(1)(f)). Asserted.

18. **Internal review is narrow.** It is open only for an order made by a member
    who is not legally qualified (reg 173), with the Tribunal's leave, within 28
    days unless the President extends the time (s 175). Asserted.

19. **The certificate costs at most $140, or $154 with GST, and must come within
    14 days.** reg 84 and s 97. A community corporation that is late commits an
    offence with a $3 000 fine. Asserted.

### Decisions

- **The buyer is a role of its own.** The baseline's seat is the owner "and, before settlement, the buyer". Both are
  the baseline's seat, so both roles' answers equal the baseline's. The arrears question (ss 88(6), 97(3)) is the
  owner's, asked after buying, with `I bought my lot from a previous owner` as its situation.
- **The s 130(3)-(4) questions keep "the seller is the original subdivision owner" as an ordinary input,** not a
  situation: a buyer from someone else is better told `no` than have the question vanish (graffiti D8.1).
- **Numbers and the baseline's own enums are wrapped:** `Amount` (dollars), `Share` (a fraction), `Days`, and
  `the answer is` over `How I take part`, `Who casts my lot's vote` and `Notifiable variation`.
- **The levy question for a tier 2 owner** arises only for `tier 2`. The baseline encodes no tier 3 chain (a tier 3
  owner would apply s 88(3) twice), so the question does not arise for a tier 3 lot.
- **The s 131 time (seller).** A helper reads s 131(3) and (4)-(5). Aware less than 15 working days before settlement:
  as soon as practicable; otherwise within 10 working days. Where the contract described the change and it was
  completed as described (s 131(4)(a)-(b)), the answer names the notice of completion of s 131(4)(c) instead. A change
  that is `no notifiable variation` needs no notice: `does not arise for you`, and nothing more is read. Whether the
  s 131(4)(c) notice was itself given in time is not asked.
- **s 138(2) (seller).** Read as the baseline reads it for the buyer: the holder repays the deposit less any rent, and
  the seller owes the rest of the money paid. The seller's question calls the baseline's rule with no held deposit and
  no rent, so the two answers add up.
- **s 48(3)-(4) (enforcing against someone else).** The grounds are those of s 48(1)(b), so the baseline's rule is used
  with the other person's facts. The corporation's own breach (s 48(4)(a)'s "if a person other than the community
  corporation") is not covered.
- **Every helper that reads a situation enum lists every value with no OTHERWISE** (graffiti finding 31). The two enum
  tests (`my lot's tier`, and `the variation` in the seller's s 131 question) use EQUALS, which fails on an ASSUMEd
  value instead of taking a branch.

### Not covered for the new roles

- **Buyer:** the buyer's own s 162(1)(h) dispute with the seller is not asked as a buyer question (the owner's s 162
  question lists it).
- **Seller:** the onus of proof (ss 130(6), 131(6)), the form of the s 130 notice (s 130(5)) and the s 67 disclosures
  themselves are not asked; nor is the seller's view of the buyer's avoidance rights (ss 132-137(3)), which are asked
  of the buyer only.
- **Occupier:** the occupier's own s 162 disputes (s 162(2)), the s 44(3) lease term beyond "must I comply", exclusive
  use by-laws for occupiers of special lots (s 44(1)(f)), and a lessee of common property.
