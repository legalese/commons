# Housing Act 1980 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

One module, `ha-housing.l4`, and one case file, `ha-cases-housing.l4`, generated by a
script (scratchpad `gen_wa_housing-act-1980_baseline.py`) from a table of facts. The
expected values were written by hand from the text. Every question is exported with
`@export` and `@desc` for Legalese Cloud.

The baseline is deliberately small. The Act is constitutive: it sets up the Housing
Authority and lists what it may do. **It gives the person little to decide.**

## Edition

- **Act:** compilation from legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 37092, retrieved

  Its compilation table runs to the Public Health (Consequential Provisions) Act 2016;

- **Instruments:** none deposited, and the plan names none. The numbers the Act leaves

  - the Minister's gazetted limits on loans (s 40), unpaid purchase money (s 34) and

  - the gazetted standard rates of interest (ss 33(4), 42(1));

## Seat

**The low-income home buyer (a Keystart or shared-equity buyer) and the public housing
tenant**, asking "Can the Housing Authority help me buy or rent?". The Act is applied to
that person when they apply to the Authority for a loan, a guarantee, an interest
subsidy, a house to rent or a house to buy on terms.

Almost every operative provision is a power of the Authority: "may make a loan"
(ss 36-38), "may assist him by guarantee" (s 46), "may let or lease" (s 28), "may
credit" (s 31), "may ... sell" (s 33), "may ... extend the due date" (s 68). The Act
gives the person no entitlement to any of them. The one duty owed to an applicant is
s 15(1): the Authority "shall deal with" the application. So each decision answers
"does the Act let the Authority do this for me?". A TRUE means the power exists, not
that it will be used.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. None is
  deposited, so every gazetted or determined number is an input, and the case values
  are labelled ASSUMED.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the Authority, the Minister, the eligible
  person, approved lending institutions, lending institutions under State loan
  agreements. Keystart is not named in the Act and appears only as a lender about which
  the Act says nothing.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. "Satisfies
  the Authority" (s 38(3)), "in the opinion of the Authority" (ss 28(b), 60), "with the
  consent of the Minister" (s 28), "approved in writing" (s 47) and "cases of hardship"
  (s 68) are all asked as facts.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed, with one
  departure forced by the text: "eligible person" is defined (s 5(2)) only as whoever
  meets conditions the Authority sets, so it cannot be worked out from facts. It is
  asked as one fact. "New house" borrows a repealed Act's definition (s 46(2)(b)) and
  is asked as a fact.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One application for one kind of help.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed: Part II (the Authority), land
  development, Part V's arrangements, Part VA, funds and regulations are out.

## The lives

- **jake k074** (18 February 2042). Jake, 35 and single, contracts to buy a one-bedroom
  strata apartment off the plan in Cockburn Central for $390,000, to live in, with a
  Keystart low-deposit loan. The scenario asks "what does Keystart lend?" and cites
  "the Housing Authority's powers". The decisions asked:
  - `What does the Housing Act decide about my loan from` Keystart: nothing; the Act
    does not name Keystart and its lending policy is an input;
  - `Can the Housing Authority lend to me for this` (s 36(1)(c)): yes if he is within
    the Authority's income conditions and the apartment is his home; no otherwise;
  - `Would this loan keep me within the Minister's limit` (s 40), on an assumed
    gazetted sum;
  - `Can the Housing Authority guarantee my borrowing` (s 46), on assumed values;
  - `On what terms can the Housing Authority sell me this`: not a Division 4 sale,
    since he buys from a developer;
  - `Can the Authority pay my duty and add it to what I owe` (s 72): not for a
    Keystart loan.

  His income-condition status, the 2% deposit, the land/building split and every
  gazetted sum are ASSUMED; neither the scenario nor the deposit gives them.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names no section, only "the Housing Authority's powers", and one event.
The scope is the powers that act on an individual home buyer or tenant: lending
(Part IV Division 1), guarantees and subsidies (Part IV Division 2), letting and rent
(Part III Division 3), sale on terms (Part III Division 4), specialised housing (s 60),
hardship (s 68), and duty and fees (ss 72, 73), plus the duty to deal with
applications (s 15). Everything else in the Act is about the Authority itself, land
development or money arrangements between governments and institutions. The
assertion count (33) is in the 25-35 band asked for this constitutive Act.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. The Act decides almost nothing for the person. Every head of help is a discretion
   ("may"). The only duty owed to an applicant is to "deal with" the application
   (s 15(1)), which includes refusing it (s 15(2)(e)). Asserted.
2. "Eligible person" is a blank the Authority fills: whoever meets conditions it
   determines and the Minister approves, set "by reference to income criteria" (s 5(2),
   (3)). Income and asset limits are therefore policy, not law, and are inputs.
3. Keystart is not mentioned anywhere in the Act. Its loans rest, at most, on the
   Authority's general powers to act as a private person and to "lend money" (s 12(b),
   (d)) and its joint-venture power (s 12A). On the text, that is an inference: the
   deposit says nothing about Keystart's structure. The Act gives a Keystart borrower
   nothing to rely on. Asserted.
4. Loans to buy, build or refinance a home need the borrower to be an eligible person
   (s 36), but a loan to buy land from the Authority (s 37) or to improve a house
   (s 38) may go to "any person"; s 38 adds that the Authority must be satisfied the
   borrower's financial position justifies it. Asserted.
5. An apartment is a "house": s 5(1) includes "any single, attached or multistorey
   dwelling unit". Every loan and sale "house" must be the buyer's home (ss 32(2), 35).
   Asserted.
6. Each borrower's total Part IV debt is capped by a sum the Minister gazettes (s 40);
   the unpaid price under a contract of sale is capped likewise (s 34). Fees and duty
   the Authority pays and adds to the debt may take it over the cap (s 73(2)). Asserted.
7. A Part IV loan must be secured by a first or second mortgage to the Authority, plus
   any collateral it requires (s 41(1)), at the standard rate most recently gazetted
   (s 42(3)). Asserted.
8. The Authority may guarantee an eligible person's borrowing to build, finish or buy a
   new home only if they can pay the approved part of the cost without borrowing and
   cannot borrow the rest unaided, and the building (without the land) is worth no more
   than the gazetted sum (s 46). Asserted.
9. Interest subsidies are paid to the lender, not the borrower, and need an eligible
   person approved in writing by the Authority, a lender approved in writing by the
   Minister, a mortgage, and the Minister's approval of the payments (s 47). Asserted.
10. The Authority may rent its houses to eligible persons, or, with the Minister's
    consent, to someone it considers is providing an essential community or tradesman
    service locally and cannot find accommodation elsewhere (s 28). Rent is whatever the
    Authority determines with the Minister's approval, less any rebate the determination
    allows (s 30). Asserted.
11. A tenant who buys the house, or whose spouse or de facto partner (living or dead)
    rented it, may have the rent credited towards the price, in whole or in part, at the
    Authority's discretion (s 31). Asserted.
12. The Authority may sell a house and land on terms only to an eligible person; anyone
    else pays cash. Housing land to build on may be sold on terms to anyone (s 33(1)).
    Asserted.
13. Caps in a State loan agreement on house value and borrower income (s 54(2)(c), (d))
    bind the lending institution, not the borrower. Asserted.
14. In hardship the Authority may extend the time to pay, on terms the Minister
    approves (s 68). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Deposit the instruments that fill the blanks: the eligibility conditions approved
  under s 5(2), the Minister's gazetted limits under ss 34, 40 and 46(2)(a), the
  standard interest rates under ss 33(4) and 42(1), and the current rent
  determination under s 30. Without them every answer is "the Authority may".
- Settle how Keystart and shared-equity schemes stand to the Act (company, joint
  venture under s 12A, or s 12 general powers) and deposit their documents; a
  Keystart buyer's real questions are answered by its lending policy, not by statute.
- The tenancy itself sits in the Residential Tenancies Act 1987 (s 62G points to it for
  Part VA); join this to that encoding for public housing tenants.
- A lawyer's review of whether s 12's general powers let the Authority lend outside
  Part IV's limits; if so, a "no" under Part IV is not a "no" under the Act.
- An independent test pass and the two human gates.
