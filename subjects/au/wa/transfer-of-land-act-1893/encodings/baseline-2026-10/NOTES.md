# Transfer of Land Act 1893 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the
individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage
table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

Every question is published with `@export` and every input carries a `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese
Cloud and asked from a form.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers every section that encoding covered and reuses
its hand-checked expected values where they still hold (the paramountcy exceptions, the short lease, s 105(4), the default
periods, service, s 227). One limb is dropped: the naive encoding's s 68(1) wrong-description decision, which no life asks
about and which the 70-assertion ceiling crowded out. It is still quoted in the module's comment on s 68.

## Edition

The compilation published on legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 49493, retrieved 7 September 2026), deposited at
`../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49493.txt`.

- The deposit names no compilation number.
- Its compilation table ends with the Petroleum Legislation Amendment Act 2024 Pt 5 Div 13 (other than s 424(4)), in force
  28 May 2026.
- The last reprint listed is Reprint 11 as at 8 January 2016.
- The uncommenced provisions are not shown or encoded: Transfer of Land Amendment Act 2003 ss 43, 46, 56, 57; Community
  Titles Act 2018 s 253; Petroleum Legislation Amendment Act 2024 s 424(4); Evidence Act 2025 s 492(2).

The plan names no regulations that set this Act's numbers. The Transfer of Land Regulations (prescribed electronic service,
fees) and the approved forms are not deposited. "A way ... prescribed by the regulations" is asked as a fact.

## Seat

**The registered proprietor** — the owner, a joint tenant or tenant in common, the mortgagor — **and the buyer** about to
become one.

Why: the Act is applied to the person whose name is, or is about to be, on the certificate. The lives meet it as buyers at
settlement (c052, c081, k076), as surviving joint owners (c087, c096, d093), as an executor (c087) and as a son abroad
(d074). The lender, the caveator, the Registrar and the Commissioner appear only as what they may do to the owner.

Each question is phrased as that person would ask it: `Has the land passed to me`, `Am I bound by this interest`,
`How do I get onto the register after the death`, `May my lender sell my land`, `How much of the sale money comes to me`,
`Has the caveat lapsed on my dealing`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. No instrument sets a number used here. The
  Act's own numbers (48 hours, 5 years, 1 month, 6 months, 14 and 21 days, 6 and 30 years) are named constants.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: proprietor, transferee, mortgagee, mortgagor, caveator, Registrar,
  Commissioner, executor, administrator, devisee, the person entitled.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are all inputs:
  - whether there was "fraud" (s 68);
  - the Commissioner's satisfaction (s 227), opinion (s 142) and direction (ss 220, 122);
  - whether the Supreme Court extended a caveat (s 138B) or gave leave (s 138D).
  The amount of s 140 compensation is the judge's, so only whether the liability arises is asked.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked as facts.** Followed. A s 138A caveat is worked out from
  its kind. "Fraud", "rates" and "the person entitled" are not defined in the Act and are inputs, or are flagged as not
  answered.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One parcel and one dealing, one mortgage, one caveat, one death.
- **The Act's own terms with a plain gloss.** Followed in the comments and in each `@desc`.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. The offices, the Registrar's powers, fees, surveys, forms and the
  regulation-making power are out.
- **Departure:** the module answers the buyer's questions as well as the owner's (ss 88, 110, 148-149). The seat named
  in the plan includes the buyer, and those sections are where the Act meets them.

## Why this scope

The seat asked for five things:

- indefeasibility and its exceptions;
- joint tenancy and survivorship on death;
- mortgages, and the mortgagor's position on default as far as the Act states;
- caveats as they affect the owner;
- transmission.

The scope follows those five:

- **Indefeasibility:** ss 52(2), 53, 58, 68, 134, 199, 201, 202, 211, 214A, plus the buyer's search and stay (ss 146, 148,
  149).
- **Joint tenancy:** ss 60, 61.
- **Transmission:** ss 187, 219, 220, 227.
- **The mortgage:** from signature to discharge (ss 105, 105AA, 106, 123), the implied covenants (ss 88, 113, 115, 131),
  default, notice and sale (ss 106-108), the sale money and the buyer's title (ss 109, 110), and foreclosure (ss 121,
  122).
- **Caveats** from the owner's side: what blocks a dealing (ss 139, 142), how a caveat lapses (ss 138(3), 138B), whether
  the caveator may lodge again (s 138D), and compensation (s 140).

Left out:

- leases and easements beyond s 68(1A);
- Crown land and conditional tenure land;
- the mortgagee's possession and the abolished distress (ss 111-112A);
- the s 141A notice, powers of attorney, surveys, and title by possession;
- s 196 (cases where the State is not liable);
- the strata and community titles legislation.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Section 60 does not say "survivorship".** Four of the seven events cite it for survivorship. It says only that joint
   proprietors "shall be deemed to be entitled to the same as joint tenants". That the survivor takes the whole is the
   general-law incident of a joint tenancy, not text. Asserted as written.
2. **The survivor needs no probate; the executor has no route to jointly held land.** s 227 lets the Commissioner, on "the
   application of the person entitled" and proof of the death, register the survivor, who becomes "the transferee". So Sue
   (c087), Chloe (c096) and Priya (d093) each go onto the register by s 227. Chloe as Graham's executor (c087) is not given
   that route. Whether Graham's estate keeps any interest in the Armadale house is general law, not this Act. Asserted.
3. **The Calabria house is outside the Act (d074).** Only land "under the operation of this Act" is touched. Asserted.
4. **"No survivorship" restricts dealing; it does not say it ends survivorship.** s 61(3) forbids anyone other than the
   registered proprietors to deal without a court or Commissioner order. Asserted.
5. **The registered title is free of almost everything not on it, but not unpaid rates.** s 68(1A) still binds the land to
   seven things that need not be on the certificate. Among them are "any unpaid rates" and a prior unregistered lease of
   not more than 5 years to a tenant in actual possession. Knowing of an unregistered interest "shall not of itself be
   imputed as fraud" (s 134). Asserted.
6. **Whether strata or community levies are "rates" is not answered here (c081, k076).** The encoding returns an explicit
   "this Act does not say" rather than guess. Asserted.
7. **Nothing passes at settlement; registration passes it.** s 58 makes no instrument effectual until registered. s 53(2)
   ranks by time of registration "notwithstanding any actual or constructive notice". Asserted.
8. **The buyer's 48-hour stay beats a caveat lodged after the search.** If a properly perfected transfer is lodged within
   48 hours of the search certificate time, it has priority. It is registered "notwithstanding any caveat" lodged after
   that time (s 149). The stay needs the proprietor's written consent (s 148). Asserted.
9. **A mortgage the owner never signed does not bind the owner, though registered.** s 105(4) overrides s 58 where only the
   lender's counterpart was lodged and the owner signed none before registration. Asserted.
10. **A registered mortgage is a security, not a transfer**, and paying it off does not clear the title. Only the
    Registrar's entry of a witnessed discharge does (ss 106(1), 123). Asserted.
11. **The mortgage binds the owner to repair and to let the lender inspect, though it says neither; it does not bind the
    owner to insure.** The covenants are implied by s 113. Insurance comes only from the s 115 short form if it is used.
    Any implied covenant may be negatived (s 131). Asserted.
12. **A buyer who takes land subject to the seller's mortgage impliedly indemnifies the seller against it** (s 88), unless
    the transfer negatives that. Asserted.
13. **From default to sale is one month plus one month, and the mortgage may change both.** Service must be personal, by
    registered post, left on the land, or by a prescribed electronic way. Ordinary post is not service. A written demand
    replaces the notice where the money is payable on demand (ss 106-108). The Act puts no floor under a period the
    mortgage fixes. Asserted.
14. **The owner gets the surplus.** The sale money goes first to the sale expenses, then to the selling mortgage, then to
    later mortgages, "and the surplus (if any) shall be paid to the mortgagor" (s 109). A buyer at the sale takes free of
    the mortgage and of later encumbrances, except a lease or easement the lender consented to in writing (s 110).
    Asserted.
15. **Foreclosure needs 6 months' default and a failed auction first.** The land must have been offered at public auction
    after a s 106 notice and drawn too little or no bid (s 121). The order cannot issue less than a month after the first
    advertisement, or if a sale in the meantime raised enough (s 122). Asserted.
16. **An owner can make an ordinary caveat lapse in two ways:**
    - by applying to register a dealing, after which the caveat lapses 14 days after notice to the caveator (s 138(3));
    - by a s 138B notice, after which it lapses in 21 days unless the caveator lodges a Supreme Court extension.

    A beneficiary's caveat does not lapse under s 138(3). It yields under s 142 only if the Commissioner thinks the will
    authorises the dealing and the caveator consents or does not protest within 14 days. Asserted.
17. **A caveator whose caveat failed after a s 138B notice cannot lodge again** without the owner's endorsed consent or the
    Court's leave (s 138D). A caveator who lodges without reasonable cause is liable to compensate the owner (s 140).
    Asserted.
18. **A defrauded owner can recover from the State only in listed cases, and only within 6 years** (30 at most for a minor
    or a person of unsound mind). The listed cases are where the wrongdoer is dead, bankrupt, cannot be found, or has had
    liability capped by a bona fide sale (ss 201(3), 211). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The general law of survivorship, and the case law on "fraud" in s 68, decide what the lives actually ask. Neither is in
  the Act, and no case law was searched.
- The scenarios should say how each property is registered. This encoding assumes joint proprietorship where a life
  says "by survivorship".
- The Transfer of Land Regulations should be deposited, so that prescribed electronic service, fees and forms can be
  read. So should the Registrar's s 182A requirements.
- The Strata Titles Act 1985 and Community Titles Act 2018 encodings should answer whether levies bind a buyer (c081,
  k076).
- Leases and easements, the mortgagee's possession remedies, s 196 and the s 141A notice are untouched.
- An independent test pass and a human gate.
