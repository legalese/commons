`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

## Seat

**Two people, one policy.** The vehicle owner, who gets the policy by licensing
the vehicle, asks: "What does my compulsory third party insurance cover?" The
person injured by a vehicle asks: "Whom do I claim against, what notice must I
give, and what if the car was uninsured or never found?" The Act speaks mostly to
the Insurance Commission, the Director General and the courts. Each of their
duties is encoded as what it means for one of these two people.

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The
  Regulations are deposited and used. They set no premium, and the gazetted Amounts
  A-D and the average weekly earnings are not in the deposit, so those are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: owner, driver, insured person, the
  Commission, the Director General, the court, a police officer, a medical
  practitioner or nurse, an employer.
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Followed. The inputs are:
  - whether the harm was "a consequence of the driving" (s 3(7));
  - whether notice was given "as soon as practicable" (ss 7, 8, 29);
  - whether the court relieves a failure (s 29A);
  - whether the court orders otherwise than disqualification (s 4(4));
  - whether a refusal of examination had a reasonable excuse (s 30(5));
  - the assessed damages.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed for "motor
  vehicle", "owner", "insured person" and "uninsured motor vehicle". The borrowed
  terms are asked as facts:
  - "required to be licensed under the Vehicles Act";
  - "catastrophic injury" (MVWA(CI) Act);
  - liability under workers' compensation.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One vehicle with one policy, or one
  accident with one injured person.
- **The Act's terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments.
- **Operative sections in, machinery out.** Followed. The following are left out:
  - ss 3Q-3T (administration and premium-setting);
  - s 16 (jurisdiction);
  - s 25 (information to the Minister);
  - s 33 (regulations);
  - s 34 (transitional).

## Why this scope

The lives ask what the policy covers and who pays an injured contractor. That
means s 4 and the definitions, which the plan names. It also means ss 6, 6A, 7, 8,
10, 12, 29 and 29A: without them the injured person's question has no answer. The
damages restrictions (ss 3C, 3D, 3F, 3FB) are what an injured person meets at the
end of a claim, so they are in, with their amounts as inputs. The offences an owner
can commit (s 4(3A)-(7C), s 10(4)) and the Commission's rights of recovery
(ss 7(5), 10(6), 15) are what an owner meets when something goes wrong. The
Commission's administration, the abolished Tribunal, the visiting-motorist licence
(s 17) and the transitional provisions are out.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The licence is the policy.** Granting or renewing a vehicle licence "is to
   be taken to constitute the issue of a policy" for the licence period (s 4(8A)).
   No licence is granted without the premium (s 4(8)). The policy follows the
   vehicle to a buyer (s 20), and the Commission may not end it early (s 19).
   Asserted.
2. **The Regulations set no premium.** The plan expected the premium in the 2009
   Regulations. They hold the notice form, the conveyance formula, permits and
   plates, and the licence as evidence. The premium is "determined by the
   Commission" (s 4(8)) and approved by the Minister (s 3T). It is not encoded.
3. **Fifteen days of grace, and only if you renew.** A licence renewed within 15
   days after expiry extends the policy to the renewal. Renewed later, the vehicle
   is "an uninsured motor vehicle" for the whole gap (s 21). So on day 10 of an
   unrenewed licence, cover depends on what the owner does next. Asserted.
4. **The cover is for negligence causing death or bodily injury, by anyone
   driving, anywhere in Australia.** It covers the owner and any driver "whether
   with or without the consent of the owner" (s 6(1)(b)). It does not cover
   property damage, and does not cover the at-fault driver's own injuries, save
   the CISS payments for a catastrophic injury (s 6A). Asserted.
5. **"Caused by a vehicle" means by its driving or its running out of control
   (s 3(7)).** Jake's car door therefore turns on whether a court treats the
   opening of a door as part of "the driving". The Act does not say. Both answers
   are asserted: a claim against the insured owner or driver, or no claim under
   this Act. Asserted.
6. **An e-bike or eRideable is in only if it must be licensed.** "Motor vehicle"
   is a powered vehicle "required to be licensed ... under the Vehicles Act"
   (s 3(1)). The Act never mentions bicycles, e-bikes or eRideables. An electric
   vehicle that need not be licensed is not a motor vehicle. No policy covers the
   injuries its rider causes, unless one was issued under s 4(9). Hurt by a car,
   the rider claims like anyone else. Asserted.
7. **Five routes to the Commission.** The routes depend on the vehicle that hurt
   you:
   - insured: claim against the owner or driver, and the Commission conducts and
     pays (ss 7(1), 11);
   - insured, but the person at fault is dead or cannot be served: the Commission
     directly (s 7(2));
   - never identified: the Commission directly, after "due search and inquiry"
     (s 7(3));
   - uninsured: the judgment, if unpaid after a month, is claimed from the
     Commission (s 8(1));
   - uninsured, and the person at fault is dead or cannot be found: the
     Commission directly (s 8(5)).

   Asserted (four of the five).
8. **A Commonwealth vehicle is never "uninsured".** The definition excludes it
   (s 3(1)), so s 8 gives no claim on the Commission. A visiting vehicle insured in
   a prescribed State is not required to be insured here (s 3(4)), so it is not
   "uninsured" either. The encoder reads both as general-law claims against the
   owner or driver. Asserted for the Commonwealth.
9. **The notice of claim has no fixed deadline.** The notice of claim is due "as
   soon as practicable" in the prescribed form (s 29(1), Schedule 1), and the court
   may excuse a failure (s 29A). After 6 months without an action, the Commission
   may ask a judge to fix a time to sue. Missing that time bars the claim "forever"
   (s 29). Asserted.
10. **A breach of the policy never defeats the injured person.** A drunk or
    unlicensed driver, or a misstatement by the owner, is no defence (s 7(4)). The
    Commission recovers from the driver, and from the owner only if the owner was
    guilty of the matter (s 7(5), Schedule warranty (c)). Asserted.
11. **No waiver survives.** An advance contract giving up a claim for a driver's
    negligence is void (s 26). Asserted.
12. **The middle band and the conveyor's payment, from the recovered formulas.**
    Between Amount C and B + C the damages are the excess of the assessment over
    "Amount B − [Amount so assessed − Amount C]" (s 3C(6)): on the 2010 amounts,
    $60,000 gives $52,000 and $66,000 gives $64,000. A person (not an agreed
    ambulance service) who takes the injured to hospital is paid P = D × R, the
    kilometres times the workers' compensation rate per kilometre (r 6(b); the rate
    is an input). Both formulas were images lost from the `.txt` and are in the
    sidecars named under Edition. Asserted.
13. **The amounts are stale in the deposit.** Note 1 tables Amounts A-D only to
    1 July 2010 ($337 000, $17 000, $51 000, $6 000). They are recalculated every
    year, so they are inputs, and the cases use the 2010 figures. Asserted.
14. **The penalties are small.** Using a vehicle uninsured is $400, or $800 for a
    later offence, with 12 months' disqualification unless the court orders
    otherwise (s 4(3A), (4)). Failing to notify the Commission of an accident is
    $20 (s 10(4)). Asserted.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added 6 November 2026, after the graffiti encoding's design (Graffiti Vandalism Act 2016, `gva-seat.l4`, its NOTES D2,
D4 and D8). The layer is `mvtpi-qa.l4` (imports `prelude` and `mvtpi-ctp`), with its cases in `mvtpi-qa-cases.l4`
(imports `prelude`, `mvtpi-ctp` and `mvtpi-qa`). The baseline's files are unchanged: no rule or export of
`mvtpi-ctp.l4` was touched, and its 73 cases still pass. The layer calls the baseline's functions and restates none.

**Why.** The site's question form asks only the inputs that can still change an open question. Every exported question
of the layer takes `my role` first ("Which of these describes you"); for a role it is not about, it answers `does not
arise for you` without reading any other input. Within a role, a question that arises only in a situation takes that
situation's fact second, and answers `does not arise for you` when it does not hold, again reading nothing else.

**Roles** (`Role`):

- `I own a vehicle, or have one on hire or hire purchase`: the baseline's first seat. "Owner" in s 3(1) includes the
  hirer and the hire purchaser, so the role is worded to take them in; "Am I the owner" still answers no for one who
  lets the vehicle out on hire purchase or holds a bill of sale as security. An owner who was driving answers as the
  owner, with `I was driving or in charge of it at the time` where s 10 and s 7(5) turn on it.
- `I drive a vehicle that someone else owns`: new. The Act makes the driver an "insured person" "whether with or without
  the consent of the owner" (s 3(1), s 6(1)(b)), puts the s 10(1) notice and the s 10(5) no-admission duties on them,
  lets the Commission recover from them (ss 7(5), 10(6), 15) and makes "any person so using" an uninsured vehicle an
  offender (s 4(3A)).
- `I was hurt by a motor vehicle`: the baseline's second seat.

The Insurance Commission, the Director General, the police and the courts are not seats. Others the Act mentions (the
doctor, nurse or conveyor paid under s 12, the employer of s 22) are reached through the seats' own questions.

**Situation inputs.** All are BOOLEAN; FALSE gives `does not arise for you`; each is the second input wherever it is
taken, with the same name, type and `@desc` in every question.

| Name | Questions that take it |
|---|---|
| `someone was killed or injured by the vehicle` | owner, driver: Must I give the Insurance Commission notice of the accident; What is the most I can be fined for not giving the Commission notice; May I do this about the accident without the Commission's written consent; Can the Insurance Commission make me repay what it paid |
| `a vehicle was used on a road uninsured, and I am its owner or was using it` | owner, driver: the fine, the disqualification, the licence fee, s 4(3B), and the time to prosecute (five questions) |
| `a police officer asked me to show that my vehicle is insured` | owner: Have I complied with the police request to show my vehicle is insured; What is the most I can be fined for not showing my vehicle is insured |
| `a doctor or nurse gave me emergency treatment` | injured: Will the Insurance Commission pay for my emergency treatment; How much of the emergency treatment charge must I pay |
| `I was carried in a vehicle straight after I was injured` | injured: How much must the Insurance Commission pay the person who took me to hospital |
| `I have a judgment against the owner or driver of an uninsured vehicle` | injured: How much of my unpaid judgment against an uninsured driver or owner can I claim from the Commission |

The situations follow what the text ties each rule to: s 10 to "any accident which results in the death of or causes
bodily injury to any person and is directly caused by, or by the driving of," the vehicle; s 4(3A) to a vehicle without
a policy "used on a road"; s 4(7A) to the police officer's request; s 12(1)(a)(i) and (ii) to the treatment and the
conveyance; s 8(1) to the judgment. No question takes a situation that arises in every situation of its role.

**Decisions.**

1. **One question, several roles.** Where the Act gives two roles the same answer from the same rule (what the policy
   covers, s 6(1)(b); the s 10 duties; the s 4(3A) offence), one exported question serves both. The s 6A CISS question
   serves all three: s 6A(2) insures "any person (the injured person)", the at-fault owner or driver included.
2. **Record inputs are kept, where the form can work with them.** The baseline's questions take records (`My vehicle`,
   `The incident` and others). The layer takes the same records, so its answers are the baseline's by construction. Three
   questions take smaller records instead (decision 9).
3. **The s 10 notice for an owner who was driving** passes the baseline `the driver or person in charge of the vehicle`
   (s 10(1), forthwith); otherwise `the owner, not driving or in charge` (s 10(2)). The repay question does the same with
   `My part in the accident`.
4. **The s 10(4) fine** is the baseline's $20 for both owner and driver. s 10(4) penalises a failure under s 10(1) or
   (3), not the owner's own s 10(2) notice. The question speaks of "an accident or a claim", and s 10(3) binds every
   insured person, so it is kept for the owner; this note records the gap.
5. **The driver's own question** ("Am I insured when I drive this vehicle") is a helper in the layer (`the driver's
   cover under ss 3(1), 15`): no policy in force, no; with the owner's authority or reasonable grounds to think so, an
   insured person; without, the policy answers but the Commission recovers (s 15).
6. **The s 3C answer** is the baseline's MAYBE NUMBER unwrapped. The baseline never answers NOTHING; NOTHING would be
   read as nothing awarded.
7. **Answer types** carry `does not arise for you`: `Yes or no`, `Amount`, `Months`, `Days`, and wrappers around the
   baseline's own answer types (`Cover answer`, `Employer answer`, `Accident notice answer`, `Claim answer`).
8. **Questions with a fixed answer** (s 20, s 7(4), s 26, s 29(3), s 30(4), s 3FB) take only the role, so they settle on
   it and the form can drop them.
9. **Smaller inputs for the form** (fixed 6 November 2026, after verification). The site's form proves that a question
   does not arise for a role by trying every combination of its open yes/no and choice inputs, and gives up past 1 024.
   Three questions had more, so the form kept them open for roles they are not about, although the code answers `does
   not arise for you` from the role alone: the e-bike question for the driver and the injured person (`My vehicle` and
   the licence: 1 536 combinations), and the two claim questions for the owner and the driver (`What happened to me` and
   `My claim steps`: 7 168). The layer now asks:
   - `my e-bike or eRideable` IS A `My e-bike or eRideable`: the four facts the baseline's s 4(9)/(10) rule reads besides
     the licence (what drives it; whether it must be licensed, and complies; a s 4(9) policy). `the baseline's vehicle
     for` gives the baseline FALSE for a towed caravan or trailer (an e-bike is neither) and for the four facts the rule
     does not read (on a road; Crown, visiting, defence). 48 combinations. The owner's two other vehicle questions keep
     `the vehicle`.
   - `how I was hurt` IS A `How I was hurt`: the baseline's `What happened to me` with the s 3(7) consequence of the
     driving and the negligence as one fact, since the baseline reads them only joined by AND. Both claim questions take
     it, so the form asks it once.
   - `what I have done about my claim` IS A `What I have done about my claim`: the baseline's `My claim steps` with the
     s 7(3)(a) search and the s 7(3)(b) notice as one (read only together) and the two bars, s 30(5) and s 29(7), as one
     (read only as "neither"). 896 combinations with `how I was hurt`.
   Helpers build the baseline's records, so the answers are the baseline's for every input; cases check the folds
   against the baseline (a hit-and-run with both s 7(3) steps; a missed time a judge fixed; the door not part of the
   driving).
10. **The baseline's `Must I give notice of the accident` on the site: not fixable in the layer.** Found by verification
   (6 November 2026); the first version of these notes said `my role` was consistent everywhere, which held only inside
   the layer. The baseline's export `Must I give notice of the accident` (mvtpi-ctp.l4) takes an input named `my role`,
   of the baseline's type `My role` (the driver or person in charge, the owner not driving, the injured person, a
   passenger or witness). A deployment lists the exports of the modules the layer imports, and the site's form keeps
   every export that takes the seat's input by name, so it sends the seat's answer (a `Role` value) to that export, which
   fails for every role ("unknown enum variant"). The layer cannot rename the baseline's input: layers add, they do not
   replace. The layer's own s 10 question, "Must I give the Insurance Commission notice of the accident", is right and
   calls the baseline's function with the baseline's type. The remedy is in the form engine (keep an export for a seat
   only when its input accepts the seat's value) or a change to the baseline's input name, which Michael would have to
   approve. The Spent Convictions Act layer meets the same thing.

**Not covered for the driver:** s 14 (recovery of emergency payments from the driver of an uninsured vehicle), the
s 7(5) proviso, the s 10(3) claim notice as a question of its own, and the driver's own injuries beyond CISS (a driver
hurt by another vehicle asks as the injured person).
