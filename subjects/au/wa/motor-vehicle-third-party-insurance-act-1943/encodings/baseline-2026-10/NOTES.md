## Seat

**Two people, one policy.** The vehicle owner, who gets the policy by licensing
the vehicle, asks: "What does my compulsory third party insurance cover?" The
person injured by a vehicle asks: "Whom do I claim against, what notice must I
give, and what if the car was uninsured or never found?" The Act speaks mostly to
the Insurance Commission, the Director General and the courts. Each of their
duties is encoded as what it means for one of these two people.

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The
  Regulations are deposited and used. They set no premium, and the gazetted Amounts
  A-D and the average weekly earnings are not in the deposit, so those are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: owner, driver, insured person, the
  Commission, the Director General, the court, a police officer, a medical
  practitioner or nurse, an employer.
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Followed. The inputs are:
  - whether the harm was "a consequence of the driving" (s 3(7));
  - whether notice was given "as soon as practicable" (ss 7, 8, 29);
  - whether the court relieves a failure (s 29A);
  - whether the court orders otherwise than disqualification (s 4(4));
  - whether a refusal of examination had a reasonable excuse (s 30(5));
  - the assessed damages.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed for "motor

  terms are asked as facts:
  - "required to be licensed under the Vehicles Act";
  - "catastrophic injury" (MVWA(CI) Act);
  - liability under workers' compensation.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One vehicle with one policy, or one

- **The Act's terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments.

  - s 33 (regulations);
  - s 34 (transitional).

## Why this scope

The lives ask what the policy covers and who pays an injured contractor. That
means s 4 and the definitions, which the plan names. It also means ss 6, 6A, 7, 8,
10, 12, 29 and 29A: without them the injured person's question has no answer. The
damages restrictions (ss 3C, 3D, 3F, 3FB) are what an injured person meets at the
end of a claim, so they are in, with their amounts as inputs. The offences an owner
can commit (s 4(3A)-(7C), s 10(4)) and the Commission's rights of recovery
(ss 7(5), 10(6), 15) are what an owner meets when something goes wrong. The
Commission's administration, the abolished Tribunal, the visiting-motorist licence
(s 17) and the transitional provisions are out.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The licence is the policy.** Granting or renewing a vehicle licence "is to
   be taken to constitute the issue of a policy" for the licence period (s 4(8A)).
   No licence is granted without the premium (s 4(8)). The policy follows the
   vehicle to a buyer (s 20), and the Commission may not end it early (s 19).
   Asserted.
2. **The Regulations set no premium.** The plan expected the premium in the 2009
   Regulations. They hold the notice form, the conveyance formula, permits and
   plates, and the licence as evidence. The premium is "determined by the
   Commission" (s 4(8)) and approved by the Minister (s 3T). It is not encoded.
3. **Fifteen days of grace, and only if you renew.** A licence renewed within 15
   days after expiry extends the policy to the renewal. Renewed later, the vehicle
   is "an uninsured motor vehicle" for the whole gap (s 21). So on day 10 of an
   unrenewed licence, cover depends on what the owner does next. Asserted.
4. **The cover is for negligence causing death or bodily injury, by anyone
   driving, anywhere in Australia.** It covers the owner and any driver "whether
   with or without the consent of the owner" (s 6(1)(b)). It does not cover
   property damage, and does not cover the at-fault driver's own injuries, save
   the CISS payments for a catastrophic injury (s 6A). Asserted.
5. **"Caused by a vehicle" means by its driving or its running out of control
   (s 3(7)).** Jake's car door therefore turns on whether a court treats the
   opening of a door as part of "the driving". The Act does not say. Both answers
   are asserted: a claim against the insured owner or driver, or no claim under
   this Act. Asserted.
6. **An e-bike or eRideable is in only if it must be licensed.** "Motor vehicle"
   is a powered vehicle "required to be licensed ... under the Vehicles Act"
   (s 3(1)). The Act never mentions bicycles, e-bikes or eRideables. An electric
   vehicle that need not be licensed is not a motor vehicle. No policy covers the
   injuries its rider causes, unless one was issued under s 4(9). Hurt by a car,
   the rider claims like anyone else. Asserted.
7. **Five routes to the Commission.** The routes depend on the vehicle that hurt
   you:
   - insured: claim against the owner or driver, and the Commission conducts and
     pays (ss 7(1), 11);
   - insured, but the person at fault is dead or cannot be served: the Commission
     directly (s 7(2));
   - never identified: the Commission directly, after "due search and inquiry"
     (s 7(3));
   - uninsured: the judgment, if unpaid after a month, is claimed from the
     Commission (s 8(1));
   - uninsured, and the person at fault is dead or cannot be found: the
     Commission directly (s 8(5)).

   Asserted (four of the five).
8. **A Commonwealth vehicle is never "uninsured".** The definition excludes it
   (s 3(1)), so s 8 gives no claim on the Commission. A visiting vehicle insured in
   a prescribed State is not required to be insured here (s 3(4)), so it is not
   "uninsured" either. The encoder reads both as general-law claims against the
   owner or driver. Asserted for the Commonwealth.
9. **The notice of claim has no fixed deadline.** The notice of claim is due "as
   soon as practicable" in the prescribed form (s 29(1), Schedule 1), and the court
   may excuse a failure (s 29A). After 6 months without an action, the Commission
   may ask a judge to fix a time to sue. Missing that time bars the claim "forever"
   (s 29). Asserted.
10. **A breach of the policy never defeats the injured person.** A drunk or
    unlicensed driver, or a misstatement by the owner, is no defence (s 7(4)). The
    Commission recovers from the driver, and from the owner only if the owner was
    guilty of the matter (s 7(5), Schedule warranty (c)). Asserted.
11. **No waiver survives.** An advance contract giving up a claim for a driver's
    negligence is void (s 26). Asserted.
12. **The middle band and the conveyor's payment, from the recovered formulas.**
    Between Amount C and B + C the damages are the excess of the assessment over
    "Amount B − [Amount so assessed − Amount C]" (s 3C(6)): on the 2010 amounts,
    $60,000 gives $52,000 and $66,000 gives $64,000. A person (not an agreed
    ambulance service) who takes the injured to hospital is paid P = D × R, the
    kilometres times the workers' compensation rate per kilometre (r 6(b); the rate
    is an input). Both formulas were images lost from the `.txt` and are in the
    sidecars named under Edition. Asserted.
13. **The amounts are stale in the deposit.** Note 1 tables Amounts A-D only to
    1 July 2010 ($337 000, $17 000, $51 000, $6 000). They are recalculated every
    year, so they are inputs, and the cases use the 2010 figures. Asserted.
14. **The penalties are small.** Using a vehicle uninsured is $400, or $800 for a
    later offence, with 12 months' disqualification unless the court orders
    otherwise (s 4(3A), (4)). Failing to notify the Commission of an accident is
    $20 (s 10(4)). Asserted.

  cites are not here.

  council. None is deposited.
