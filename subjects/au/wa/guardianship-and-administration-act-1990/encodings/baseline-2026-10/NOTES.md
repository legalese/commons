# Guardianship and Administration Act 1990 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE: where the Act leaves a question open, the encoding returns a value that says so (for example, `the Act gives the Tribunal power to revoke it and is silent on revocation by me`).
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`.
It covers everything that one covered (Parts 9, 9A and 9B, s 45, ss 110ZJ and 119, the s 107 penalty).
It adds the regulations' prescribed forms, Schedule 3, s 110ZD (the person responsible), joint enduring guardians, revocation, and the guardianship and administration orders (ss 4, 43, 64) that the person may be made subject to.
Its L4 is rewritten from the seat.

## Edition

- **Act:** the compilation published on legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 49107, retrieved 7 September 2026), deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49107.txt`.
  The deposit names no compilation number.
  Its compilation table ends with the Births, Deaths and Marriages Registration Amendment (Sex or Gender Changes) Act 2024 Pt 4 Div 9, in force 30 May 2025.
  The uncommenced ss 110RA and 110ZAA-110ZAC are not encoded.
- **Regulations:** the Guardianship and Administration Regulations 2005, consolidated version 01-d0-00 (currency from 5 April 2024, retrieved 19 October 2026), deposited at `../../../guardianship-and-administration-regulations-2005/registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_46980.txt`.
  The encoding uses:
  - reg 6 and Schedule 1, the enduring power of guardianship form;
  - reg 7 and Schedule 2, the advance health directive form;
  - reg 8, which says notes are not part of a form;
  - reg 9, the 2022 transitional period.

## Checks

One module, `gaa-incapacity.l4`, and one case file, `gaa-cases-incapacity.l4`.

## Seat

**The adult planning for incapacity, and the person for whom a guardian or administrator may be appointed.**
The planning adult is:
- the donor of an enduring power of attorney (Part 9);
- the appointor of an enduring guardian (Part 9A);
- the maker of an advance health directive (Part 9B).

Parts 9 to 9B are written for the person who makes the instrument, and the three lives meet the Act in that seat.
Where the Act speaks to the Tribunal, it is encoded as what the Tribunal may do to the person:
- ss 43 and 64 (orders);
- s 106 (declaration);
- s 108 (administration and the power of attorney);
- s 110N (revocation).

Where it speaks to others, it is encoded as what they owe or may do for the person:
- s 107 (the attorney's duties);
- s 110ZD (the person responsible);
- s 110ZJ and s 119 (who decides).

Each question is phrased as that person asks it ("May I make…", "Who decides my treatment…").

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed.
  The deposited 2005 Regulations supply the prescribed forms and the transitional dates.
  The Oaths, Affidavits and Statutory Declarations Act 2005 decides who is "authorised by law to take declarations". It is not brought in, and the count of such witnesses is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the donor, donee and substitute donee; the appointor and appointees; the maker; the Tribunal; the administrator; the guardian; the person responsible.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed.
  The Tribunal's satisfaction (ss 43, 64, 106) and its opinion on less restrictive means (s 4(4)) are inputs phrased "the Tribunal is satisfied…".
  So is the s 110S(3) weighing.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed:
  - worked out: "nearest relative" for s 110ZD(4), and the meaning of not remunerated in s 110ZD(6);
  - asked as facts: "full legal capacity" and "unable to make reasonable judgments", which the Act does not define.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one instrument, one decision, or one proposed treatment.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in `@desc` and the comments.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed.
  These are out:
  - the Tribunal's procedure, reviews and appeals;
  - the Public Advocate;
  - regulation-making;
  - transitional provisions.

## Why this scope

The eight events ask four questions:
- who may make each instrument;
- who may be appointed;
- how each is made;
- when it operates and who then decides.

A person in that seat also meets:
- revocation;
- the forms the regulations prescribe;
- the person responsible, who decides when no-one has been appointed;
- the orders the Tribunal may make instead of, or over, their own arrangements.

These are in.
Medical research (Part 9E), abortion and urgent treatment (Part 9D Subdivisions 1 and 3), and sterilisation are out.
They are specialised routes the lives do not reach.
So are the recognition of other jurisdictions' instruments and the functions of limited guardians and administrators.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **At 18, all three instruments switch on together.**
   ss 104(1a), 110B and 110P use the same words: "has reached 18 years of age and has full legal capacity".
   On 2021-03-13 Chloe could make none of them; on 2021-03-14 she could make all three.
   At 17 the Tribunal may already appoint a guardian for a person, with the appointment operating from the 18th birthday (s 43(2a), (2c)).
   Asserted.
2. **An administrator can be appointed without any minimum age.**
   s 43(1)(a) requires the person to have "attained the age of 18 years"; s 64(1) states no age at all.
   s 77(4) contemplates a represented person under the incapacity of "infancy".
   The encoding reflects this by not asking for an age. There is no separate assertion.
3. **Schedule 3 is in the deposit, and Form 1 requires exactly one duration declaration.**
   The first-pass encoding said Schedule 3 was missing.
   It is there, and Form 1 paragraph 4 carries the note "One of these paragraphs must be deleted".
   An instrument that leaves both the continuing and the springing declaration in fails s 104(1)(b).
   Asserted.
4. **The donee cannot be the second witness to an enduring power of attorney, but a spouse can.**
   s 104(3) excludes only a donee or substitute donee from the non-authorised witness's place.
   Unlike ss 110E and 110Q, s 104 says nothing about the witnesses signing in each other's presence.
   Asserted.
5. **The Act never says the donor may revoke an enduring power of attorney; it assumes it.**
   s 108(2)(b) gives an administrator "the same power to vary or revoke the power as the donor would have if he were of full legal capacity".
   The encoding reads that as recognising the donor's general-law power. That is an inference.
   Asserted.
6. **The Act gives no-one but the Tribunal a power to revoke an enduring power of guardianship.**
   s 110N lets the Tribunal revoke or vary it; nothing in Part 9A speaks of revocation by the appointor.
   The encoding says so rather than guessing.
   Asserted.
7. **A directive must contain at least one treatment decision, and the advice requirement has no teeth.**
   s 110P describes a directive as "containing treatment decisions"; Schedule 2 says a form without one "is not considered a valid Advance Health Directive".
   s 110Q(1)(b) requires the maker to be "encouraged to seek legal or medical advice", but s 110Q(2) says failure "does not affect" validity.
   Asserted.
8. **The 2022 directive form had a six-month overlap.**
   Reg 9 made both the new Schedule 2 and the former form prescribed from 4 August 2022 to 3 February 2023.
   Chloe's directive in 2073 must be on the current form.
   Asserted.
9. **The directive outranks the enduring guardian, whichever is made first.**
   In all three lives the directive comes years after the enduring guardian:
   - Chloe: 2063, then 2073;
   - Jake: 2063, then 2075;
   - Daniel: 2065, then 2074.

   s 110ZJ(2) puts a covering treatment decision first.
   s 110T stops a later enduring power of guardianship from revoking a directive by itself.
   The encoding reads s 110ZJ(2) with s 110S, so a decision that does not operate does not take priority. That is an inference.
   Asserted.
10. **Ruby's Carer Allowance does not stop her being Jake's unpaid carer.**
    s 110ZD(3)(c) ranks "the primary provider of care and support … not remunerated".
    s 110ZD(6) says a carer payment or other government benefit for home care is not remuneration.
    Ruby is also Jake's nearest relative under s 110ZD(4) if he has no spouse or partner. That list (spouse, child, parent, sibling) is shorter than the s 3 definition.
    Asserted.
11. **An enduring guardian decides about the person, not the property.**
    - s 110G(1) borrows a plenary guardian's s 45 functions and limits.
    - s 45(2)(g) and (h) exclude "proceedings relating to the estate".
    - Schedule 1 tells the appointor that the guardian cannot "make decisions about your property or estate".
    - s 45(3) now also bars consent to a surrogacy parentage order and functions under the Births, Deaths and Marriages Registration Act 1998 Part 5A, beyond what the first-pass encoding listed.

    Asserted.
12. **For personal matters other than treatment, s 119 stops at the guardian.**
    If there is no enduring guardian or guardian, no-one is named.
    For treatment, s 110ZJ(5) goes on to the person responsible.
    Asserted.
13. **Jake's instruments stand or fall on his capacity two months after the stroke, which the life does not state.**
    Both answers are asserted.
    If they fall, a guardianship order is possible.
    If his enduring guardian meets his needs, s 4(4) bars one.
    Asserted.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added 6 November 2026, following the Graffiti Vandalism Act encoding's design (D2, D4, D8 and D8.1-D8.15 in `subjects/au/wa/graffiti-vandalism-act-2016/encodings/baseline-2026-10/NOTES.md`). The layer is `gaa-qa.l4` (imports `prelude`, `gaa-incapacity`); its cases are `gaa-qa-cases.l4`. The baseline's files are unchanged: the layer adds, and replaces nothing.

**Roles** (`DECLARE Role`, in the first person; every exported question takes `my role` first, `@desc Which of these describes you`, and answers `does not arise for you` for a role it is not about without reading any other input):

- `I am planning for my own incapacity, or a guardian or administrator may be appointed for me`: the baseline's seat. All 30 baseline exports are asked, each calling the baseline function (30 questions).
- `I am, or have been asked to be, someone's attorney under an enduring power of attorney`: s 104C (eligibility), ss 104B, 105, 106 (when the power is in force) and s 107 (the donee's duties: diligence, records and accounts, renunciation, bankruptcy) (8).
- `I am, or have been asked to be, someone's enduring guardian`: s 110D (eligibility), ss 110F, 110G, 45 (when and for what the guardian may act), ss 110H, 53, 54 (joint guardians) (5).
- `I am the partner, relative, carer or friend of someone who may need treatment decided for them`: s 110ZD (the person responsible, the nearest relative, the unpaid primary carer) and s 110ZJ(5) (when the person responsible decides) (4).

The State Administrative Tribunal, the Public Advocate and the Public Trustee are never a seat; what the Tribunal may do appears as what it means for the person asking. A health professional is not a seat here: the urgent treatment provisions are outside the baseline's scope. A guardian or administrator appointed by the Tribunal is not a seat: their functions and duties (Parts 4 to 6, beyond s 45) are outside the baseline's scope.

**Situation inputs** (all BOOLEAN, each straight after `my role`; FALSE gives `does not arise for you` without reading anything else; same name, type and `@desc` wherever taken):

| Input | Role | Does not arise when | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `I have made an enduring power of attorney` | the person | FALSE | the seven about my power: valid, in force, a s 106 declaration, the substitute, the attorney's fine, renunciation, an administrator (s 108) |
| `I have made an enduring power of guardianship` | the person | FALSE | the five about my power: valid, my guardian acting now, for this decision, joint guardians agreeing, survivors acting |
| `I have made an advance health directive` | the person | FALSE | the five about my directive: the form, validity, a treatment decision valid, revoked, operating |
| `I have been appointed someone's attorney under an enduring power of attorney` | the attorney | FALSE | all seven but eligibility (s 104C) |
| `I have been appointed someone's enduring guardian` | the enduring guardian | FALSE | acting now, and for this decision |
| `I am one of two or more joint enduring guardians` | the enduring guardian | FALSE | agreeing (ss 110H(b), 53(a)) and survivors acting (ss 110H(c), 54) |
| `treatment is proposed for them and they cannot make reasonable judgments about it` | the partner, relative, carer or friend | FALSE | whether I decide the treatment (s 110ZJ(1), (5)) |

The person's other 13 questions (age, presumed capacity, the guardianship and administration orders, making an instrument, whom I may appoint, revocation, the plenary guardian's functions, the nearest relative, the carer, the person responsible, and who decides under ss 110ZJ and 119) arise for every person and take none; so do the attorney's and the enduring guardian's eligibility, and the partner's three s 110ZD questions.

**Decisions.**

- **Q1. The instrument made, not incapacity, is the person's situation.** The questions about "my power" or "my directive" have nothing to answer until one is made. Incapacity is an ordinary input of the questions that turn on it (ss 110F, 110S, 110ZJ), not a gate: a capable person may ask whether a guardian could act for them later, and is told no for now.
- **Q2. The joint guardians' questions take the joint situation, not the appointment.** Being one of joint enduring guardians implies being appointed, so those two questions take only `I am one of two or more joint enduring guardians`, straight after `my role`. For the appointor, the same two questions take `I have made an enduring power of guardianship`, since the appointor asks before knowing whether a guardian has died.
- **Q3. The attorney's and the enduring guardian's questions call the baseline's functions with that seat's facts.** Eligibility is the baseline's `May I appoint this person` (ss 104C, 110D) with the asker's own age and capacity; "is the power in force", "has the substitute become the attorney", "may I renounce", "may I act now" and "may I make this decision" are the baseline's functions with inputs worded for the attorney or guardian. Each is mirrored by a case from the donor's or appointor's seat with the same answer.
- **Q4. Three s 107 duties have no baseline function and are helpers here:** the records duty (s 107(1)(b)) answers yes for any appointed attorney; liability for loss (s 107(1)(a)) needs both a lack of reasonable diligence and a loss caused by it; the bankruptcy report (s 107(1)(d)) is due once the attorney becomes bankrupt.
- **Q5. "Am I the person responsible" uses the baseline's `Who is my person responsible`.** The kinds above the asker's are set to whether someone of those kinds qualifies, the asker's to TRUE, the kinds below to FALSE; the asker is the person responsible if it names the asker's kind. The asker's own capacity, availability and willingness (s 110ZD(2)) is read first. That a nearest relative is defined by s 110ZD(4) is left to the separate question; the asker states their kind.
- **Q6. "Do I decide the treatment" uses the baseline's `Who decides my treatment`** with the patient's own judgment FALSE (the situation's terms) and the asker's answer to "Am I the person responsible" as its last input.
- **Q7. Answer types** follow gva-seat.l4: `Yes or no`, `Amount`, `Age` (`aged` HAS the completed years), and enumerations for the baseline's enumerated answers, each with `does not arise for you` and mapped from the baseline's values one by one with no OTHERWISE. The wording moves from "my" to "you" or to a neutral form, so that the partner's nearest relative answer serves the patient's question too.
- **Q8. One name, one type, one `@desc`.** The baseline gives `my age` and `I had full legal capacity` two meanings (now, and when the instrument was made). In the layer `my age` and `I have full legal capacity` mean now; the validity questions take `my age when I made it` and `I had full legal capacity when I made it`. The baseline's `my power` (three types) is `my power of attorney` and `my power of guardianship`. Inputs shared across roles (`the kind`, `a section 106 declaration is in force`, `the circumstances the power limits it to exist`, `the power limits the functions or circumstances so as to exclude it`, `the power says the survivors are not to act`, `the events or circumstances the power specifies for the substitute have occurred`, `the decision`) have one neutral `@desc`.
- **Q9. Not tested by an ASSUMEd value: three baseline functions read an enumeration through OTHERWISE** (`May I appoint this person` and `How can my instrument be revoked` on `Instrument`, `Could a plenary guardian make this decision` on `PersonalDecision`, and the in-force and s 106 functions on `AttorneyKind`). As the graffiti encoding's D8.8 says, a read of those inputs before the role or situation would not show in the cases; the parameter script and the code show that each question reads the role, then the situation, first.

**Not covered for the new roles.** The attorney: what the attorney may do with the donor's property (the instrument's terms, s 104(1a)), accountability to an administrator (s 108(2)(a)), applications to the Tribunal (s 109), and the s 107(1)(d) report's timing. The enduring guardian: the guardian's duties and the Tribunal's powers over the guardian in Part 9A beyond ss 110D-110H (not in the baseline), and the treatment decisions limited by ss 110ZR and 110ZT. The partner, relative, carer or friend: urgent treatment, medical research and the Public Advocate's role; the s 3 definition of nearest relative, which Part 9C displaces.

**Cases** (`gaa-qa-cases.l4`, 336): 117 for the person (30 of them asking the baseline export on the same facts); 18 for the attorney and 11 for the enduring guardian (mirror cases of the donor's and appointor's included); 20 for the partner, relative, carer or friend (one of them the patient's mirror); 141 asking each of the 47 questions for the three roles it is not about, every other input ASSUMEd; 29 asking each question with a situation input with that situation FALSE, every later input ASSUMEd.
