# Guardianship and Administration Act 1990 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE: where the Act leaves a question open, the encoding returns a value that says so (for example, `the Act gives the Tribunal power to revoke it and is silent on revocation by me`).
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`.
It covers everything that one covered (Parts 9, 9A and 9B, s 45, ss 110ZJ and 119, the s 107 penalty).
It adds the regulations' prescribed forms, Schedule 3, s 110ZD (the person responsible), joint enduring guardians, revocation, and the guardianship and administration orders (ss 4, 43, 64) that the person may be made subject to.
Its L4 is rewritten from the seat.

## Seat

**The adult planning for incapacity, and the person for whom a guardian or administrator may be appointed.**
The planning adult is:
- the donor of an enduring power of attorney (Part 9);
- the appointor of an enduring guardian (Part 9A);
- the maker of an advance health directive (Part 9B).

Parts 9 to 9B are written for the person who makes the instrument, and the three lives meet the Act in that seat.
Where the Act speaks to the Tribunal, it is encoded as what the Tribunal may do to the person:
- ss 43 and 64 (orders);
- s 106 (declaration);
- s 108 (administration and the power of attorney);
- s 110N (revocation).

Where it speaks to others, it is encoded as what they owe or may do for the person:
- s 107 (the attorney's duties);
- s 110ZD (the person responsible);
- s 110ZJ and s 119 (who decides).

Each question is phrased as that person asks it ("May I make…", "Who decides my treatment…").

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed.
  The deposited 2005 Regulations supply the prescribed forms and the transitional dates.
  The Oaths, Affidavits and Statutory Declarations Act 2005 decides who is "authorised by law to take declarations". It is not brought in, and the count of such witnesses is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the donor, donee and substitute donee; the appointor and appointees; the maker; the Tribunal; the administrator; the guardian; the person responsible.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed.
  The Tribunal's satisfaction (ss 43, 64, 106) and its opinion on less restrictive means (s 4(4)) are inputs phrased "the Tribunal is satisfied…".
  So is the s 110S(3) weighing.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed:
  - worked out: "nearest relative" for s 110ZD(4), and the meaning of not remunerated in s 110ZD(6);
  - asked as facts: "full legal capacity" and "unable to make reasonable judgments", which the Act does not define.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one instrument, one decision, or one proposed treatment.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in `@desc` and the comments.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed.
  These are out:
  - the Tribunal's procedure, reviews and appeals;
  - the Public Advocate;
  - regulation-making;
  - transitional provisions.

## Why this scope

The eight events ask four questions:
- who may make each instrument;
- who may be appointed;
- how each is made;
- when it operates and who then decides.

A person in that seat also meets:
- revocation;
- the forms the regulations prescribe;
- the person responsible, who decides when no-one has been appointed;
- the orders the Tribunal may make instead of, or over, their own arrangements.

These are in.
Medical research (Part 9E), abortion and urgent treatment (Part 9D Subdivisions 1 and 3), and sterilisation are out.
They are specialised routes the lives do not reach.
So are the recognition of other jurisdictions' instruments and the functions of limited guardians and administrators.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **At 18, all three instruments switch on together.**
   ss 104(1a), 110B and 110P use the same words: "has reached 18 years of age and has full legal capacity".
   On 2021-03-13 Chloe could make none of them; on 2021-03-14 she could make all three.
   At 17 the Tribunal may already appoint a guardian for a person, with the appointment operating from the 18th birthday (s 43(2a), (2c)).
   Asserted.
2. **An administrator can be appointed without any minimum age.**
   s 43(1)(a) requires the person to have "attained the age of 18 years"; s 64(1) states no age at all.
   s 77(4) contemplates a represented person under the incapacity of "infancy".
   The encoding reflects this by not asking for an age. There is no separate assertion.
3. **Schedule 3 is in the deposit, and Form 1 requires exactly one duration declaration.**
   The first-pass encoding said Schedule 3 was missing.
   It is there, and Form 1 paragraph 4 carries the note "One of these paragraphs must be deleted".
   An instrument that leaves both the continuing and the springing declaration in fails s 104(1)(b).
   Asserted.
4. **The donee cannot be the second witness to an enduring power of attorney, but a spouse can.**
   s 104(3) excludes only a donee or substitute donee from the non-authorised witness's place.
   Unlike ss 110E and 110Q, s 104 says nothing about the witnesses signing in each other's presence.
   Asserted.
5. **The Act never says the donor may revoke an enduring power of attorney; it assumes it.**
   s 108(2)(b) gives an administrator "the same power to vary or revoke the power as the donor would have if he were of full legal capacity".
   The encoding reads that as recognising the donor's general-law power. That is an inference.
   Asserted.
6. **The Act gives no-one but the Tribunal a power to revoke an enduring power of guardianship.**
   s 110N lets the Tribunal revoke or vary it; nothing in Part 9A speaks of revocation by the appointor.
   The encoding says so rather than guessing.
   Asserted.
7. **A directive must contain at least one treatment decision, and the advice requirement has no teeth.**
   s 110P describes a directive as "containing treatment decisions"; Schedule 2 says a form without one "is not considered a valid Advance Health Directive".
   s 110Q(1)(b) requires the maker to be "encouraged to seek legal or medical advice", but s 110Q(2) says failure "does not affect" validity.
   Asserted.
8. **The 2022 directive form had a six-month overlap.**
   Reg 9 made both the new Schedule 2 and the former form prescribed from 4 August 2022 to 3 February 2023.
   Chloe's directive in 2073 must be on the current form.
   Asserted.
9. **The directive outranks the enduring guardian, whichever is made first.**
   In all three lives the directive comes years after the enduring guardian:
   - Chloe: 2063, then 2073;
   - Jake: 2063, then 2075;
   - Daniel: 2065, then 2074.

   s 110ZJ(2) puts a covering treatment decision first.
   s 110T stops a later enduring power of guardianship from revoking a directive by itself.
   The encoding reads s 110ZJ(2) with s 110S, so a decision that does not operate does not take priority. That is an inference.
   Asserted.
10. **Ruby's Carer Allowance does not stop her being Jake's unpaid carer.**
    s 110ZD(3)(c) ranks "the primary provider of care and support … not remunerated".
    s 110ZD(6) says a carer payment or other government benefit for home care is not remuneration.
    Ruby is also Jake's nearest relative under s 110ZD(4) if he has no spouse or partner. That list (spouse, child, parent, sibling) is shorter than the s 3 definition.
    Asserted.
11. **An enduring guardian decides about the person, not the property.**
    - s 110G(1) borrows a plenary guardian's s 45 functions and limits.
    - s 45(2)(g) and (h) exclude "proceedings relating to the estate".
    - Schedule 1 tells the appointor that the guardian cannot "make decisions about your property or estate".
    - s 45(3) now also bars consent to a surrogacy parentage order and functions under the Births, Deaths and Marriages Registration Act 1998 Part 5A, beyond what the first-pass encoding listed.

    Asserted.
12. **For personal matters other than treatment, s 119 stops at the guardian.**
    If there is no enduring guardian or guardian, no-one is named.
    For treatment, s 110ZJ(5) goes on to the person responsible.
    Asserted.
13. **Jake's instruments stand or fall on his capacity two months after the stroke, which the life does not state.**
    Both answers are asserted.
    If they fall, a guardianship order is possible.
    If his enduring guardian meets his needs, s 4(4) bars one.
    Asserted.
