# Legal Profession Uniform Law Application Act 2022 (WA) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults
ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE: a case the encoding does not cover returns a value that says
so. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public
use as advice. This encoding replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10` of this subject.

**Edition:** four instruments, each named in every comment and every exported question.

- **Application Act** — compilation from legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 47480), deposited at
  `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_47480.txt`. No compilation or reprint number is shown.
  The compilation table lists the Act (No. 9 of 2022; Part 1 in force 14 April 2022, the rest
  1 July 2022 under SL 2022/113 cl 2), the Levy Act 2022, and the Workers Compensation and
  Injury Management Act 2023 Pt 15 Div 3 Subdiv 8 (1 July 2024).
- **Uniform Law** — the Legal Profession Uniform Law (WA), applied by Application Act s 6.
  Source: mrdoc 46913, retrieved 7 September 2026, deposited in the subject
  `wa/legal-profession-uniform-law-wa`. The deposit does not show its as-at date.
- **Admission Rules** — the Legal Profession Uniform Admission Rules 2015, applied by
  Application Act s 14(2). Source: mrdoc 49001, version 00-e0-00, current from 31 October 2025.
- **General Rules** — the Legal Profession Uniform General Rules 2015, applied the same way.
  Source: mrdoc 45732, version 00-a0-02, current from 1 July 2022.

The local regulations, which set the fees, the standard renewal period and the late fee
period, are not deposited. Those periods are inputs.

**Checks:** two modules (`lpula-practice.l4`, `lpula-costs.l4`) and two case files.
70 assertions are satisfied (46 practice, 24 costs), with 0 errors and 0 warnings. The checks
used the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98, and `runcases.py` gave written = satisfied
for each file. The case files are generated by the scratchpad script
`gen_wa_legal-profession-uniform-law-application-act-2022_baseline.py`. Every expected value
in it was written by hand from the text.

## Seat

**The law graduate and lawyer — Daniel in the Western Australian lives.** He asks how to get
admitted, how to practise in WA, and what fee arrangements he may make. The Act is applied to
him: it admits him, certifies him, supervises him and limits his fees. The Board, the Supreme
Court and the Legal Costs Committee only administer it.

The questions are phrased as he would ask them:

- `Have I attained the academic qualifications prerequisite`
- `May the Supreme Court admit me`
- `May I engage in legal practice`
- `Must I still engage in supervised legal practice only`
- `Will the Board accept my renewal`
- `Is my conditional costs agreement valid`

Where Part 4.3 puts a duty on the "law practice", the question is what his firm may agree and
recover, and whether a breach can be held against him (Uniform Law ss 181(8), 183(3)).

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers or dates — departed from, as the
  brief directs.** This Act is mostly machinery around the Uniform Law. Every question the
  lives ask is answered in the Uniform Law or the Uniform Rules, so those three deposited
  instruments are brought in. They are cited instrument by instrument. Application Act s 12
  (the Act prevails over the Law) is recorded. The local regulations are not deposited, so
  their periods are inputs.
- **The actors the text names — followed.** The Legal Practice Board, the Supreme Court, the
  Legal Services and Complaints Committee, the Legal Costs Committee, the law practice and
  the client.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally — followed.** The Board's
  satisfaction that he is fit and proper, its s 18 exemptions, its s 49(4) exemption or
  reduction, and its s 117(4) acceptance of a very late renewal are all inputs or conditional
  answers. So is the firm's reasonable belief in success (s 182(2)(a)). The Supreme Court's
  inherent jurisdiction to refuse admission (s 16(4)) is written into the answer: "may admit
  me, subject to its inherent jurisdiction to refuse".
- **The Act's own definitions are worked out; borrowed terms are asked as facts —
  followed.** Worked out: "Australian lawyer", "Australian legal practitioner", "engage in
  legal practice", "qualified entity", "litigious matter", "contentious business" and
  "supervised legal practice" (paragraph (a)). Asked as facts: "fused jurisdiction" (not
  defined in the General Rules), professional indemnity insurance, and the old Act's own
  categories.
- **One case is the text's natural unit — followed.** One application, one certificate, one
  costs agreement.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a gloss — followed.**
- **Operative sections in; machinery out — followed.** Part 3 (the Board and committees),
  levies, PII schemes, fidelity cover, registers and enforcement are out. Application Act
  s 20 (who decides) is kept as one question, because the first-pass encoding had it. The
  costs-determination review cycle (ss 137, 299) is dropped as machinery. The first-pass
  encoding asserted it.

## Why this scope

The lives ask only about admission, the practising certificate, supervision, unqualified
practice and a conditional costs agreement. The scope is what those questions need from each
instrument. It also includes all the first-pass encoding covered except the
determination-review cycle. That encoding covered ss 6, 7, 20, 113, 114, 116-118, 276, 278,
279, 289, 322 and the costs sections 3, 132-134, 137, 140, 141, 143, 149, 151 and 299. Its
hand-checked values reused here:

- the 28-day review window;
- the $12,000 / $10,000 excess;
- the 1 April 2029 commencement of a determination;
- the arbitration fork under ss 3 and 132.

Not encoded:

- registration certificates;
- foreign lawyers;
- early assessment of suitability;
- removal from the roll;
- appeals;
- conditions other than s 49 and s 47(6);
- show cause events;
- the Admission Rules' evidence, disclosure and health rules (rr 12-25);
- the competency standards (Sch 2 Part 4);
- trust money;
- PII schemes;
- disclosure (Uniform Law ss 174-178, except Application Act s 143);
- billing and costs assessment procedure.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The answers the lives ask for are not in this Act.** This Act applies the Uniform Law
   (s 6) and the Uniform Rules (s 14). It then supplies the State's machinery: who decides
   (s 20), the 3-judge bench (s 114), application formalities (ss 113, 116), renewal windows
   (s 117), deemed refusal (s 118), costs determinations (Part 6) and the transition from
   the 2008 Act (Part 16). Admission, the practising certificate, supervision, unqualified
   practice and costs agreements come from the Uniform Law and the two Rules. Each answer
   names its instrument. Asserted throughout.
2. **Daniel's 2020 admission is carried into the Law outright, not by inference.** The
   first-pass encoding read his continued standing as an inference from s 322 (the old roll
   continues). Application Act s 266(2)(a) applies Uniform Law Sch 4 clauses 11 to 18 "without
   modification". Clause 11(1) makes a pre-commencement local lawyer "an Australian lawyer as
   if he or she had been admitted ... under this Law". Clause 11(2) keeps his original day of
   admission, 24 August 2020. Clause 12(1) does the same for a practising certificate granted
   before 1 July 2022 and "expressed to be operative on or after that day". Asserted.
3. **Admission is not permission to practise.** Uniform Law s 15 Note 1: "Admission does not
   of itself entitle a person to engage in legal practice". Only an Australian legal
   practitioner, an admitted lawyer with a current certificate, is entitled (ss 6, 43). Anyone
   else who engages in legal practice faces up to "250 penalty units or imprisonment for 2
   years, or both" (s 10(1)) and cannot keep the fee (s 10(2)). Policy work is outside
   "engage in legal practice" (s 6). Asserted (d030 replayed, d038).
4. **Daniel's PLT route sets his supervised period.** A new lawyer must practise under
   supervision only until he has completed 18 months, if his PLT was "principally under the
   supervision of an Australian lawyer", or 2 years after "other practical legal training"
   (s 49(1)). That is 24 months on a PLT course. From 24 August 2020 to 1 July 2022 he had 22
   full months, under the old Act. Application Act s 289(1) counts them as "the same period"
   under the Law, so he still had to practise under supervision on commencement day. In 2029
   he no longer does. General Rules r 14 counts part-time time pro rata, and counts public
   holidays and normal leave. Asserted.
5. **A 25% uplift sits exactly at the cap, and the cap may not apply at all.** s 182(2) caps
   an uplift at 25% of legal costs (excluding disbursements) and requires a reasonable belief
   in success, but only for a "litigious matter". That is a matter involving, or likely to
   involve, proceedings "in a court or tribunal" (s 6). The Law defines neither word.
   - If an arbitral tribunal is a "tribunal", Daniel's arbitration is litigious and 25% is
     allowed (given the belief), but 30% would not be.
   - If it is not a tribunal, and no court application is reasonably likely, s 182(2) does
     not apply and nothing in s 182 caps the uplift.

   The Law separates "arbitration proceedings" from proceedings "before ... courts"
   (s 69(2)(b), (c)), which leans towards the second reading (INFERENCE). Both readings are
   asserted.
6. **An arbitration may have a conditional costs agreement.** s 181(7) excludes only
   criminal proceedings, Family Law Act proceedings and legislation specified in the Uniform
   Rules. The deposited General Rules specify none (there is no "181", "182" or "uplift" in
   them). Asserted.
7. **The cooling-off period is 5 clear business days, except between law practices**
   (s 181(4)). An agreement with 3 days fails, and is void under s 185(1). Asserted.
8. **A contingency fee loses everything; a bad uplift loses only the uplift.** If the agreement
   breaches s 183 (fees calculated on the award), the practice "is not entitled to recover any
   amount in respect of the provision of legal services" and must repay (s 185(4)). If it
   breaches s 182, it loses "the whole or any part of the uplift fee" (s 185(3)). Any other
   breach of Division 4 leaves it no more than it could recover without the agreement
   (s 185(2)). The civil penalty is 100 penalty units on the firm (ss 182(4), 183(1)). A
   breach is capable of being a conduct matter against Daniel if he was involved (ss 181(8),
   183(3)). Asserted.
9. **A WA costs determination can make an award-based fee lawful.** s 183(2) exempts an
   agreement that "adopts an applicable fixed costs legislative provision". Application Act
   s 136 makes every WA costs determination one. So a fee calculated by reference to the
   amount recovered is not a prohibited contingency fee if the calculation adopts an
   applicable determination. Encoded; the asserted case is the prohibited one.
10. **Part 4.3 governs Daniel's 2029 matter, and his valid agreement displaces any
    determination.** Sch 4 cl 18(1) applies Part 4.3 when the client first instructs on or
    after 1 July 2022. Application Act s 141(2)(a) makes a determination subject to a costs
    agreement "made in accordance with" Part 4.3 Division 4. A void agreement does not
    displace it. Asserted.
11. **Whether a WA costs determination can reach an arbitration still forks.** The
    first-pass encoding found this, and it stands. s 3 defines contentious business as work
    "before a court". s 132 says that in Division 1, court "includes an arbitrator". On the
    first reading, arbitration work is noncontentious and a determination may cover it
    (s 133(1)(a)). On the second, it is contentious work before a forum s 133(1)(b) does not
    list, so a determination reaches it only if the forum is declared under s 134(1). Both
    asserted.
12. **The word "lawyer" is in s 12's list of titles but in no item of General Rules r 9.** No
    class is authorised by s 12(2) to use it. That matters only through s 12(3)'s rebuttable
    presumption that the user claims an entitlement to practise. For an Australian legal
    practitioner the presumption is simply true. "Solicitor" is authorised unless the
    certificate is barrister-only. "Barrister" depends on whether WA is a "fused
    jurisdiction", which the General Rules do not define. Asserted (solicitor, lawyer).
13. **A qualification more than 5 years old lets the Board ask for more**, but does not bar
    admission (Admission Rules rr 5(2), 6(4)). Asserted.
14. **Renewal windows come from regulations; the 6-month grace period, the 1 July backdating
    and the 6-month deemed refusal come from the Act** (ss 117(4), (5), 118(3)). This was
    carried from the first-pass encoding and is now asserted on DATE values with hypothetical
    periods. General Rules r 17 adds that a certificate runs "until the end of the following
    30 June". It continues past 1 July if a renewal was properly made before expiry and is
    undecided. Asserted.
