# Dog Act 1976 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Edition

- **The Act:** the compilation with editorial changes as at 26 November 2024. It
  includes the amendments in force to 28 November 2024, which come from the Dog
  Amendment (Stop Puppy Farming) Act 2021. Deposited at
  `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_47983.txt` (retrieved 7 September 2026).
- **Not in this compilation:** the uncommenced provisions. The compilation's
  uncommenced provisions table lists parts of the Dog Amendment (Stop Puppy
  Farming) Act 2021 as "To be proclaimed", and s 3 already refers to an
  "approval to breed" in s 26I, which the deposit does not contain.
- **The regulations:** Dog Regulations 2013, version 01-j0-00 (currency start
  24 July 2025). Deposited at
  `subjects/au/wa/dog-regulations-2013/registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_48740.txt`
  (retrieved 20 October 2026), under that subject's own `SOURCE-LICENSE.md`
  (CC BY 4.0). The encoding uses these regulations:
  - r 4: restricted breeds;
  - r 11: RWWA;
  - r 17 and its Table: fees;
  - r 18: the 3-year period;
  - r 19: the refund on later sterilisation;
  - r 20(3): proof of sterilisation;
  - r 27: the 2-metre leash;
  - r 33, Table item 1: modified penalties.
- **The shire's local laws are not deposited.** Where a local government has
  specified a place (exercise area, rural leashing area, prohibited place), the
  owner states it as a fact.

## Seat

**The dog owner.** The Act is applied to the owner, so every question is the
owner's:

- must I register my dog, and what will it cost?
- must I microchip it?
- may it be off the leash here?
- can it be declared dangerous, and by when must I object?

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The Dog
  Regulations 2013 are deposited and are read for the fees, the 3-year period, the
  refund, proof of sterilisation, the leash length, the restricted breeds and the
  modified penalties. Local laws are not deposited, so the places they specify are
  asked as facts.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed:
  - the owner;
  - the local government and its registration officer;
  - the authorised person;
  - the veterinarian;
  - the SAT;
  - the RSPCA WA, the Dogs Refuge Home and RWWA.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** Followed. These are
  all asked as facts or answered conditionally:
  - the local government's satisfaction (destructive, unduly mischievous or
    diseased; effectively confined) under s 16(3);
  - its direction to refuse;
  - its or an authorised person's opinion under s 33E(1).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed. Worked out:
  "dangerous dog", "dangerous dog (restricted breed)" (from the r 4 list) and
  "commercial security dog", plus "attack" as far as provocation goes. Borrowed
  terms asked as facts:
  - "eligible pensioner" (Rates and Charges (Rebates and Deferments) Act 1992);
  - "metropolitan region" (Planning and Development Act 2005);
  - "assistance dog" (s 8(1): whether a prescribed trainer or the CEO approved the
    dog);
  - "townsite", which rests on Land Administration Act designations and lodged
    plans.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one dog and its owner.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed, in comments beside each
  field and rule.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Partly departed from. The
  operative sections are much wider than the life event needs, so only the parts
  the owner of an ordinary dog meets are in. Left out:
  - the duties of a dangerous dog's owner (s 33GA-33M);
  - attacks (s 33D);
  - transfers and change notices (ss 16A, 26B-26D);
  - numbers and kennels (ss 26, 27);
  - greyhounds (s 33);
  - private places (s 33A);
  - nuisance (s 38);
  - pet shops and dog supply (Part VIA).

  Machinery is out.

## The lives

- **Chloe, c075 (8 March 2035): "Mochi, a dog from the shelter: registration with
  the shire, microchip, sterilisation discount".** The scenario asks: "Must a dog
  be registered; with whom; what does sterilisation do to the fee?" It does not
  give Mochi's age, breed or papers. The cases **assume** an adult that is not a
  restricted breed, sterilised with a vet's certificate, microchipped by the
  shelter and never registered, and that Chloe is not a pensioner. With those
  facts:
  - Mochi must be registered with the local government of the district where she
    is ordinarily kept (ss 7(1), 16(1)). While the shelter held her she need not
    have been, if it was the RSPCA WA, the Dogs Refuge Home or RWWA
    (s 7(3)(b), r 11).
  - The fee with proof of sterilisation is $20 for one year, $42.50 for 3 years
    and $100 for life. Unsterilised, it would be $50, $120 and $250
    (s 15, r 17 Table).
  - A one-year registration from 8 March 2035 ends on 31 October 2035.
  - She must be microchipped (s 21(2)). The details go with the registration
    application, so no separate s 23 notice is needed.
  - On a leash of no more than 2 metres she is lawfully in a Byford street. Off
    the leash she may be lawfully at a dog exercise area only if a competent
    person carrying a leash supervises her (ss 31, 32, r 27).

## Why this scope

The plan for this Act names ss 15, 16 and 21. The owner who registers a dog meets
the following, so they are in:

- the registration duty itself (s 7);
- the grounds on which a shire may refuse (s 16(3));
- how long the registration lasts (s 15(2));
- the refund if the dog is sterilised later (r 19);
- the microchip notice (s 23);
- the leash, collar and exercise-area rules (ss 30-32), which are what an
  ordinary owner meets every day.

Dangerous dogs come in only as far as they change these answers:

- the classification;
- the one-year-only registration at $50 with no concession or discount;
- the higher microchipping penalty;
- the 7-day objection window after a declaration.

The heavy regime for keeping a dangerous dog (enclosures, muzzles, signs,
sterilisation of restricted breeds, seizure and destruction) is a separate piece
of work.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A dog must be registered from 3 months old, with the shire where it is
   ordinarily kept.** The only other exceptions are:
   - a dog registered under this Act or interstate;
   - a dog held by the RSPCA WA, the Dogs Refuge Home or RWWA in a rehoming place,
     or by a vet or an official (s 7(3), r 11).

   The fine is up to $5 000, or $10 000 for a dangerous dog. The infringement
   amount is $200, or $400 (r 33, item 1). An assistance dog must still be
   registered: s 15(4) only waives its fee. Asserted.
2. **Proven sterilisation cuts the fee by more than half.** It costs $20 instead
   of $50 for a year, $42.50 instead of $120 for 3 years, and $100 instead of $250
   for life. A pensioner pays half of these again. Sterilisation must be *proved*:
   a vet's certificate, a statutory declaration or the ear tattoo (s 15(3)(d),
   r 20(3)). A sterilised dog without proof pays the unsterilised rate. Asserted.
3. **A dog registered for one year in March is registered for only 7 months.**
   Every one-year registration ends on "the next 31 October" (s 15(2)(c)). The
   half fee in s 15(6) helps only a first registration taking effect from June to
   October. The regulations prescribe no concession for a registration of less
   than a year beyond that, though s 15(3)(c) allows one. Asserted (March: full
   $20; July: $10).
4. **"31 October in the final year of" a 3-year period is read literally.** The
   period is counted from the date of effect, so a 3-year registration from
   8 March 2035 runs to 31 October 2038, nearly 3 years 8 months. The Act does not
   say whether the period is counted that way or in registration years ending on
   31 October; the other reading gives 31 October 2037. Encoder's reading,
   asserted.
5. **A dangerous dog can be registered only year by year, at the full $50.** This
   follows from s 15(3A), which sets the term, and s 15(4B), which removes the
   concessions and the local discount. Yet the local government may refuse to
   register any dangerous dog at all (s 16(3)(d)), and keeping it unregistered is
   a $10 000 offence (s 7(1)). Asserted.
6. **The reductions are not said to combine.** The stock-dog quarter (s 15(5)),
   the first-year half (s 15(6)) and the shire's discount (s 15(4A)) are each a
   fraction of, or a discount on, "the fee that would otherwise be payable". The
   encoding applies them in turn. That is the encoder's reading. Asserted for the
   stock dog ($12.50) and the half fee ($10).
7. **The microchipping duty in s 21 covers every dog, despite its heading.** The
   heading says "dogs other than dangerous dogs", but s 21(2) says "a dog that has
   reached 3 months of age". s 22(6) keeps s 21 running while the dangerous-dog
   duty in s 22(2) is suspended after a declaration (s 22(3)). So a newly declared
   dog without a chip is still in breach, at the $5 000 level rather than $10 000.
   Asserted.
8. **The life event cites the wrong sections.** The scenario cites s 22 for
   microchipping and "s 16(4)" for the sterilised fee. s 22 is the dangerous-dog
   duty; an ordinary dog's is s 21. s 16(4) is the duty to notify a refusal. The
   sterilised fee is s 15(3)(d) with r 17 and r 20(3).
9. **The leash may be no longer than 2 metres, even when merely carried.** In an
   exercise area, or in a rural place that is not a rural leashing area, a dog may
   be off the leash only if a competent person nearby is carrying a leash of no
   more than 2 metres and can attach it (s 32(2), r 27). A place the shire
   prohibits is closed to the dog even on a leash (s 31(2A)). Asserted.
10. **The rural exemption reads "outside the metropolitan region *or* outside a
    townsite".** Taken as written, a public place in the metropolitan region that
    is not a townsite is exempt from the leash rule (s 31(2)(b)), but the s 32
    supervision rule still applies there. Paragraph (d) of the definition of
    "townsite" reaches only towns outside the metropolitan region, so whether a
    metropolitan suburb is a "townsite" turns on Land Administration Act
    designations and lodged plans. The encoding asks both as facts and encodes
    "or". Not separately asserted: the Byford cases take both as true.
11. **A declaration takes effect 7 days after the notice, objection or not.** The
    owner has 7 days to object to the local government or apply to the SAT
    (s 33F(2), (3)). A provoked attack is not an "attack" (s 3(1)), so it is no
    ground. The local government's opinion decides the rest. Asserted.
12. **The regulations refer to subsections that are no longer in the Act.**
    Table item 17 of r 33 gives a modified penalty for "s. 33(3) Greyhound not
    muzzled", and r 28 prescribes a training programme "for section 33(1)(b)".
    Both s 33(1) and s 33(3) are marked deleted in the deposited compilation. Not
    encoded.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Read the uncommenced provisions of the Dog Amendment (Stop Puppy Farming) Act
  2021. If they are proclaimed before 2035, the s 26I "approval to breed" and
  whatever goes with it may change what Chloe must do with Mochi. Those provisions
  are not in the deposit.
- Deposit the Shire of Serpentine-Jarrahdale's dog local law, and check whether
  Byford is a "townsite" and in the metropolitan region. The cases assume both.
- Settle how a 3-year registration's "final year" is counted (finding 4) and how
  the reductions combine (finding 6). Practice or a decision would answer both.
- The column layout of r 33's Table is flattened in the deposit, so a lone amount
  on a row could belong to either the "other dogs" column or the "whether or not"
  column. Only rows with a clear pair (item 1) are used.
- Encode the dangerous dog regime (s 33GA-33M) and dog attacks (s 33D) from the
  same seat, if a life needs them.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added on 6 November 2026, after the graffiti encoding's design (D2, D4, D8). The baseline's files are unchanged; the layer is added beside them.

- `doga-qa.l4` imports `prelude` and `doga-owner`. It has 19 exported questions.
- `doga-qa-cases.l4` imports `prelude`, `daydate` (for its dates), `doga-owner` and `doga-qa`. It has 101 cases.

### Roles

`my role` is the first input of every question. A question about another role answers `does not arise for you` and reads nothing else.

- **`I own or keep a dog`**: the baseline's seat (Chloe and Mochi, c075). s 3(1) "owner" includes a keeper. It has 16 questions, one for each baseline export. Each calls the baseline's function and wraps the answer:
  - `yes if` for a BOOLEAN;
  - `amount` for dollars;
  - `by` and `it takes effect on` for the two s 33F dates;
  - `the answer is` for the baseline's eight enumerations.
- **`A dog has attacked, chased or threatened me or my animal`**: the person on the other side of an incident. It has 3 questions:
  - can the dog be declared dangerous (s 33E, with s 3(1)'s provocation rule);
  - is it already a dangerous dog (s 3(1));
  - was it lawfully in the public place (ss 31, 32).

  Each calls the baseline's function, with the dog input renamed `the dog` and described as the person knows it.

The local government, its registration officer, an authorised person and the State Administrative Tribunal are not seats.

### Situation inputs

All are the owner's, and all are BOOLEAN. Each comes straight after `my role`. FALSE gives `does not arise for you` without reading anything else.

| Input | Questions |
|---|---|
| `I have been given notice that my dog is declared dangerous` | the s 33F(2) objection deadline and the s 33F(3) day of effect (2) |
| `I had my dog sterilised after registering it` | the r 19 refund (1) |
| `my dog has been microchipped` | the s 23 notice of the microchip details (1) |
| `I am said to have committed an offence under this Act` | the s 7(1) fine, the r 33 infringement amount, and the ss 21(2), 22(2) fine (3) |

These questions take no situation input, because they arise for every owner:

- is my dog dangerous;
- must it be registered, and can the shire refuse;
- the fee and when the registration ends;
- must it be microchipped;
- public places and the collar;
- could it be declared dangerous.

The person attacked has no situation input: every question of that role arises for everyone in it.

### Decisions

- **The baseline's facts about the dog stay in its `Dog` record.** These are whether it is registered, sterilised, microchipped or declared dangerous. None is made a situation that could contradict the record. The microchip situation is taken only by the s 23 question, which does not take the record.
- **The microchip fine keeps the baseline's $0 for no breach.** Under the offence situation, the baseline's "What is the most I can be fined for not microchipping" gives $0 where there is no breach (a microchipped dog). The layer keeps that answer rather than turning it into `does not arise for you`.
- **The question "could my dog be declared dangerous" keeps the incident as an ordinary input.** Its `none of these` value answers `no ground in s 33E(1)`. The baseline answers it, and an incident situation would repeat the same fact.
- **The person attacked has no question on the owner's liability for the attack.** That is s 33D, which the baseline does not encode. The layer adds no rule for it.

For the person attacked:

- s 33D (liability and penalties for an attack);
- the duties of a dangerous dog's owner (ss 33GA-33M);
- damages at common law;
- the local government's seizure and destruction powers.

### Fix after verification (6 November 2026)

An independent check through a local jl4-service form engine found these, fixed here (no answer changes; the 101 cases and the baseline's 49 are unchanged and satisfied):

- `the term` (3 labels), `date of effect` (2) and `in a s 22(3) period` (2) now carry one @desc each. The form shows one label per name.
- `doga-qa.l4` no longer imports `daydate`; it uses only the prelude's DATE.

Not changed:

- **Record inputs in questions of another role.** The owner's questions take the `Dog` record, and the attacked person's "Is the dog already a dangerous dog" takes one too, so the form engine keeps them for the other role: it cannot settle a question while a record input is unanswered. The code already answers `does not arise for you` for the other role without reading the record; the gap is in how the engine decides what to drop (it should evaluate with the role alone first). Flattening the baseline's record into separate fields would restate the baseline, against the layering rule.
- **The jl4-service build** rejects some baseline syntax and fails on modules with a `GIVEN ... YIELD` lambda (`doga-owner.l4` has one). That is a service bug; the stable l4 runs every file cleanly.
