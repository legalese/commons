# School Education Act 1999 (WA) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE, no BECAUSE.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** the compilation published on legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 49345, retrieved 7 September 2026), deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49345.txt`.
The deposit gives no compilation number.
Its compilation table ends with the Education and Care Services National Law Application Act 2026 Pt 6 Div 7 (in force 1 May 2026), and the last reprint it lists is Reprint 4 as at 19 February 2016.
The uncommenced provisions table lists only the Act's own s 182(3) and s 208.

The numbers the Act leaves to regulations come from the **School Education Regulations 2000 (WA)**, compilation mrdoc 47427 (version 03-h0-00, currency from 1 July 2024, retrieved 19 October 2026), deposited at `../../../school-education-regulations-2000/registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_47427.txt`.
Its compilation table ends with the School Education Amendment Regulations 2024 (SL 2024/118, in force 1 July 2024).
Used: reg 3 ("pre-primary programme", "Schedule 3A visa holder"), regs 11B and 11C (full-time participation), regs 12, 13, 14 and 14A with Schedule 2 (who else may enrol at a government school), regs 15 and 16 (priority when a school is full), reg 18 (noted), reg 61 (the $60 contribution and $235 charge limits, and the distance-education table), reg 62 with Schedule 3A (overseas students), reg 63 (the $25,000 limit on fees for instruction) and reg 64 (the adult-student exemption).

**Checks:** one module (`sea-schooling.l4`, 47 exported questions) and one case file (`sea-cases-schooling.l4`, generated by the scratchpad script `gen_wa_school-education-act-1999_baseline.py`).
70 assertions written, 70 satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings, on the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
A deliberately wrong expectation was confirmed to fail.
Expected values were written by hand from the text.

This baseline replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`.
It covers everything that one covered (ss 4-6, 9-11H, 23, 25, 38-42, 75-79) and reuses its hand-checked results where they still hold: the five children's first compulsory years, Jake's last day, the final-years window, the s 25 and s 38 results, the Panel's powers and the s 78 result for Oliver.
It adds the seat's other questions: s 11A, s 11E and s 11F (when a notice is in force), s 29 (work in school hours), s 27 and s 30 absences, s 26 referrals and s 39 disqualification, s 77 (kindergarten enrolment), the regulations' residence criteria, visa lists and priority orders, and the fees and charges of ss 97-106 with regs 61-64.
Exported questions with no direct assertion: `Am I a parent under this Act`, `Am I a parent for the final-years options`, `Do the arrangements satisfy the duty to enrol`, `May someone represent us before the Panel`, `Is my child entitled to enrol at a school without an intake area`, `my child's priority group from outside the intake area`, `Is my child an adult student` and `the most a fee for instruction can be`; several others are exercised only through the decisions that call them.

## Seat

**The parent of a school-age child (and the child).**
Part 2 of the Act is applied to the parent: the duty to have the child enrolled (s 9(2)), the duty to ensure attendance (s 38(1)), the notices that let a child leave school early (ss 11D, 11H), and the charges a school may recover (s 106).
It is applied to the child too: the attendance requirement (s 23) and the child's own offence (s 38(2)).
The plan entry names both. Every exported question is phrased as the parent would ask it ("Must my child be enrolled?", "Is my child excused that day?", "May I be prosecuted?", "What is the most the school can charge me?").

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The School Education Regulations 2000 are deposited and used for every number and list the cases need. The Minister's s 11B(2) orders, the s 60 intake-area orders and the s 117 school-year orders are not deposited, so whether a course is prescribed, whether a home is in an intake area, and whether the school was open that day are facts.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the parent (with s 11A's extended parent), the child or student, the principal, the attendance officer, the chief executive officer, the Minister, the Attendance Panel, the provider of an option, the school Council.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are asked as facts:
  - the Minister's exemption (s 11), acknowledgment (s 11E), approval (s 11G) and participation values (reg 11C(4)), and the opinion in s 11A(1)(c);
  - the chief executive officer's certificates (ss 9(3), 42) and decisions on residence, programme and accommodation (s 82);
  - the principal's request for a medical certificate (s 25(2)(c)) and public health requirement (s 27);
  - the Panel's advice and its decision on representation (ss 39(7), 41).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out from dates of birth: the precompulsory and compulsory education periods (ss 5, 6), the final years (s 4), the first charges payment year and "adult student" (s 97). "Overseas student" (s 97 with reg 62) and the residence qualification (s 76 with regs 12-14A) are worked out from residence and visa facts.
  Borrowed and asked: who has legal responsibility for a child (the s 4 "parent"), graduation requirements (School Curriculum and Standards Authority Act 1997), "approved VET course" (VET Act 1996), the Public Health Act 2016 s 141D bar.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one child, on one date (a period question), one day away (an attendance question), or one school year (a charges question).
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments: the precompulsory period glossed as kindergarten, the compulsory period's first year as pre-primary, the first charges payment year as the year charges may start.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out: the powers of authorised persons and attendance officers (ss 12-15, 33-37) beyond their penalties; enrolment paperwork and registers (ss 16-21); home education (Division 6) beyond registration as a fact; school governance, curriculum, discipline and exclusion (ss 55-74A, 81-96I); school funds (ss 109-116); Parts 4 onward.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names ss 6, 9, 11B, 23, 38, 39-40 and 78, and gives the seat as the parent's question: from when until when must my child be at school, at which school, and what happens if they miss it?
The prompt adds enrolment, leaving early for training or employment, and fees and charges.
So the encoding takes those sections and what they need to answer: s 4 and s 5 (definitions, kindergarten), ss 10, 11, 11A and 11C-11H (how the duty to enrol is met or lifted), s 29 (the work a child leaving early may do), ss 25, 27 and 30 (excused absence), s 26 and ss 41-42 (the road to a prosecution), ss 75-77 and 79 (beside s 78), and ss 97-106 (what a government school may charge), each with the regulation that sets its number.
It stops short of discipline, suspension and exclusion (ss 88-96I), which the seat could meet but no event asks about; they would be the next addition.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Year 1 is not the first compulsory year.** s 6(1) counts from "the beginning of the year in which the child reaches the age of" 6 years 6 months (to 2012) or 5 years 6 months (from 2013), and reg 3 calls the programme for "the first year of their compulsory education period" pre-primary.
   There is no "by 30 June" in the text, although c011 says there is.
   Chloe's first compulsory year is 2009, Daniel's 2002, Oliver's 2036; each life's Year 1 is the second.
   Luca's is 2035, two years before d054 puts him in Year 1: on the text his Year 1 would be 2036 (an inference from reg 3's pre-primary year). Asserted.
2. **Jake's compulsory period ends on his 18th birthday.** k024 cites s 6 as "compulsory to the end of the year he turns 17". That is paragraphs (a) and (b), which govern only to 31 December 2013. Paragraph (c) ends the period at the end of the year the child reaches 17 years 6 months "or" at 18, "whichever happens first". Jake reaches 18 on 30 August 2024, so his period ends that day; asked in 2013 it would have ended on 31 December 2023. Luca, born in February, reaches 17 years 6 months in August 2047, so his ends on 31 December 2047, before his birthday. Asserted.
3. **A 16-year-old may leave school, but only into a listed option, full time, with notice.** On 10 November 2022 Jake is in "the final 2 years of the compulsory education period" (s 4), which on this reading began on 31 August 2022. s 11B(1) lets him, "despite section 9(1)", take a university or higher education course, an approved VET course, an apprenticeship, employment with the Minister's prior approval (s 11G), or a prescribed course. It must be full time (s 11C): regs 11B and 11C make that a participation value of 100%, with the Minister setting part-time values. The parent gives the Minister notice as soon as practicable (s 11D(1)). The notice is in force when given (s 11D(7)), or for a combination of courses only once the Minister acknowledges it (s 11E(8)). While it is in force, s 9(1) does not apply (s 11F(2)). Employment without approval, part-time participation, or no option at all does not qualify. Whether a TAFE pre-apprenticeship is an "approved VET course" is a question for the VET Act 1996. Asserted.
4. **Which paragraph applies turns on the date asked about.** ss 5, 6 and 97 are written "until 31 December 2012", "from 1 January 2013", and so on. Read (an inference) as choosing the paragraph by the date of the question, Jake's first compulsory year is 2013 when asked in 2012 but 2012 when asked in 2013. Asserted on that reading.
5. **Kindergarten is not compulsory, and a kindergarten place is not guaranteed.** Chloe's 2008 falls in her precompulsory period (2007-2008 under s 5(a)), and s 9(1) speaks only of the compulsory period. s 77 entitles a child to a pre-compulsory place only where there is an appropriate programme **and** classroom accommodation, wherever the child lives; reg 15 orders a full kindergarten by intake area, then sibling, then distance. Asserted.
6. **The attendance duty is the child's; the parent's duty is to ensure it.** s 23 binds the student. s 38(1) makes the day-to-day parent of an enrolled child of compulsory school age liable to a fine of up to $1,000 if the child does not comply, and s 38(2) the child, up to $10. A day away is excused for illness or another reasonable cause only if the principal is told as soon as practicable and within 3 school days, with a medical certificate if the principal asks (s 25(2)). A religious or cultural day is excused only if the principal was told beforehand, or as soon as reasonably practicable where that was not possible (s 30). Asserted.
7. **The Attendance Panel advises; it cannot fine, order or prosecute.** A principal, an attendance officer or the chief executive officer may refer a persistent case (s 40(1)); a class teacher may not unless designated an attendance officer. Only the principal may refer for persistently doubtful reasons (s 26). Nobody may refer a child for missing a notified apprenticeship (s 40(1b)). The Panel inquires, advises, may recommend a responsible parenting agreement under the Children and Community Services Act 2004 (s 40(3A)), requests attendance and reports. It cannot hear the case if a member teaches at the child's school or has a child there (s 39(5)). A s 38 prosecution needs a s 42 certificate that reasonably practicable steps were taken and breaches continued, and, once a Panel has advised, a failure to follow its advice (s 41(1)). Asserted.
8. **"Parent" in the enrolment and attendance offences means only the day-to-day parent.** s 4 confines ss 9(2), 25, 27 and 38(1) to paragraph (b). A parent with only long-term responsibility commits neither offence. Asserted.
9. **The local-intake school must take a child from its intake area, with or without a free classroom.** s 78(1) asks only for residence in the intake area and an appropriate programme; classroom accommodation is a condition for children from outside (s 78(2)). The deposited regulations prescribe no further criterion for s 78(2)(b). The principal "is to enrol" an entitled child on a duly made application (s 75(1)). Asserted.
10. **A child on a temporary work visa may enrol, but pays as an overseas student.** A child in WA on a subclass 482 visa meets reg 14A (Schedule 2 Division 1), so qualifies under s 76(2). The same visa is in Schedule 3A, so reg 62 makes the child an "overseas student", who may be charged fees for instruction (s 98(1)), up to $25,000 a year (reg 63(1)), and excluded for unpaid charges (s 105). A visitor visa (subclass 600) qualifies under neither. Asserted for the qualification and overseas-student results; the fee and exclusion consequences follow from ss 98(1) and 105 as encoded.
11. **Charges start in the first charges payment year, and the cap ends with the year of turning 15.** Before the first charges payment year a government school may impose no charge for non-optional materials and services (s 98(3)) and may seek only a voluntary contribution of up to $60 a year (reg 61(1)). From that year until the end of the year the student turns 15, charges are capped at $235 (reg 61(2)). Reg 61 sets no cap after that year. Jake's first charges payment year is 2019, his Year 7. Chloe's is 2015, her Year 6 on the life's chronology (which has her move to high school in 2016), because s 97(c) lowered the age to 11 years 6 months from 1 January 2015. A child born on 1 July 2010 starts paying in 2022, one born a day later in 2023. Asserted.
12. **A school may not keep a child out of class for unpaid charges, and recovery has preconditions.** s 105 bars exclusion for non-payment, except for overseas and adult students. A charge may be recovered from a parent as a debt only after inquiries into why it was unpaid, every practicable step to recover it, and regard to the parent's capacity to pay (s 106(2)). Asserted with the inquiries missing.
13. **An adult returning to school under 19 years 6 months pays no fee for instruction.** Reg 64(2) exempts an adult student on a curriculum-based programme who has not reached 19 years 6 months by 1 January of the year. Jake, his compulsory period ended in 2024, would pay nothing in 2025. Asserted, on hypothetical facts.
14. **This year's charges must be settled two months before the school year.** s 99(5) requires charges and contributions to be determined, and approved by the Council where there is one, "not later than 2 months before the beginning of the school year"; one settled on 15 December 2022 for a year beginning 1 February 2023 is out of time. Asserted.
15. **The fines.** s 9(2) $2,500; s 11AA(2) $2,500; s 13(3) $500; s 27(4) $500; s 29(1) $5,000 (which catches a parent who lets the child work in school hours); s 36(3) $200; s 37 $500; s 38(1) $1,000; s 38(2) $10; s 43(2) $500. Asserted for ss 9(2), 38(1) and 38(2).

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The 2002-2010 events are answered from paragraph (a) of the **current** compilation. s 5 and s 6 were inserted in their present form by No. 46 of 2012. The text in force in 2002-2003, before the 2005 amending Act raised the leaving age, was not deposited or read; Daniel's 2002 answer rests on a text that did not then exist in this form.
- "Final 2 years" is read as the dates after the date two years before the period's last day, and each paragraph of ss 5, 6 and 97 as chosen by the date asked about. Both are inferences. A 29 February birthday is not handled.
- The regulations used are the compilation current from 1 July 2024. The $60, $235 and $25,000 figures are applied to every year, including 2008 and 2016, without checking the regulations in force then.
- The Minister's s 11B(2) orders, the s 60 intake-area orders and the s 117 school-year orders are not deposited.
- s 106's parent route is read as closing at 18, because "parent" is defined only in relation to a child. That is an inference.
- Discipline, suspension and exclusion (ss 88-96I), home education (Division 6) and non-government schools (Part 4) are not encoded.
- No case law was searched. The Vocational Education and Training Act 1996 (for "approved VET course" and "apprentice") is not encoded.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added on 6 November 2026 (batch qa1), on Michael's approval of the design of the graffiti encoding's seat layer
(`subjects/au/wa/graffiti-vandalism-act-2016/encodings/baseline-2026-10/`, its D2, D4 and D8). The layer is
`sea-qa.l4`, and its cases are in `sea-qa-cases.l4`. It imports `prelude`, `daydate` and `sea-schooling`. The baseline's
files are unchanged: the layer adds to them and replaces nothing.

**Roles.** Every exported question takes `my role` IS A `Role` first ("Which of these describes you"). A question about
another role answers `does not arise for you` without reading any other input.

- `I am the child's parent, or the adult responsible for the child`: the baseline's seat. Its 47 questions are the
  baseline's 47 exports. Each calls the baseline function and carries its answer into an answer type that has
  `does not arise for you`.
- `I am the child or student`: s 23 binds the student, and s 38(2) ($10) and s 36(3) ($200) are the student's own
  offences. ss 41-42 bar a prosecution under s 38, which includes s 38(2). An adult student, or a student who has
  turned 18, meets ss 97, 98, 105 and 106(1)(b) in person. 14 questions.
- `I employ a child, or would like to`: s 29 binds "a person" who employs a child of compulsory school age in school
  hours ($5 000). 3 questions.

The principal, the chief executive officer, the Minister, attendance officers and Attendance Panels are not seats.
Nor is a non-government school: the baseline does not encode Part 4.

**Situation inputs.** Each one is a BOOLEAN placed second. FALSE gives `does not arise for you`.

**Decisions.**

- QA1. **The situations are what the Act ties the rules to.**
  - The excused-absence, s 23 and s 38 questions turn on a day the child did not attend.
  - The Panel questions turn on a referral (s 40(1): "may be referred").
  - ss 11A-11H turn on the child taking up an option instead of school.
  - ss 75-79 and 97-106 turn on a government school, which is all Part 3 covers.
  - The s 6 periods, who is a parent (s 4), the duty to enrol (ss 9, 10) and the penalty table arise for every parent, so
    they take no situation input.
- QA2. **The prosecution question for not enrolling (ss 9(3), 41(1a), 42(1a)) takes no situation input.** It covers two
  cases: a child who is not enrolled, and a child who is not following a notified option. Its own inputs say which, and
  neither fact on its own decides whether the question arises.
- QA3. **The baseline's records are taken as the baseline takes them** (`My child's arrangements`, `My child's absence`,
  `My child's residence`, `The school's offer`). They are not broken into plain fields.
  - The student's questions reuse `My child's absence` and `My child's residence`, under the inputs `my absence` and
    `my residence`, with the student's `@desc`.
  - The records' own field names stay in the parent's words ("I told the principal").
  - Breaking the records into fields worded from each seat is left open.
- QA4. **A shared input carries one `@desc`.**
  - Where the baseline gave one input two texts (`on`, `the arrangements`, `the school`,
    `a Panel advised or offered assistance`, `of compulsory school age`), the layer gives one text that covers both uses.
  - The student's and the employer's inputs have their own names (`my date of birth`, `the date`,
    `the child's date of birth`, `the day of the work`), so no input name carries two texts.
- QA5. **Fork, s 29(2): work that is not for gain.** The employer's offence question asks whether the child works for
  the employer "for the purpose of gain", paid or not, and answers no when the child does not. s 29(2) extends
  "employed" to unpaid work for gain, and nothing extends it further. The other reading is that any work done for
  someone in school hours is employment, which would answer yes. Asserted on the first reading.
- QA6. **New helpers.** The layer adds two rules the baseline does not have:
  - `s 106(1)(b), (2) -- the fee or charge may be recovered from the student`: the student has turned 18 or is a
    prescribed child, and the three s 106(2) steps were taken. The steps are read as conditions, as the baseline reads
    them for the parent.
  - `s 29(1) -- the employer commits the offence`: the child works for gain, and the baseline's
    `May my child work during school hours` answers no.
  - The student's fine question maps the student's two offences onto the baseline's penalty table. It lists every value
    and has no OTHERWISE.
