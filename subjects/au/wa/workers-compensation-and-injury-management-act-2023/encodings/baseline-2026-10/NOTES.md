# Workers Compensation and Injury Management Act 2023 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the
individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. Checked with the
stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

It replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. It covers everything that encoding covered, reuses its
hand-checked figures where they still hold (the s 55 rates, the s 69 percentages, the Table percentages, the s 204
fine, the s 423 reductions), and adds the regulations, which the first pass did not have, and the claim process.

## Seat

**The injured worker.** The Act decides what a worker is owed after an injury and when the worker may sue, so the
worker is the person it is applied to. Every decision is a question the worker would ask: `Am I a worker`, `Is my
employer liable to compensate me`, `Is my claim made within 12 months`, `Is liability taken to be accepted`, `How much
income compensation do I get this week`, `Has my income compensation run out`, `How much can I be paid for medical and
health expenses`, `How much is my lump sum`, `Must my employer insure me`, `May I start court proceedings`. Provisions
addressed to the insurer, the employer or the Director (ss 28, 29, 36, 47, 76, 202) are encoded as what they mean for
the worker: when liability is taken to be accepted, when provisional payments start, when the first payment is
overdue, when the worker must be told 60% of the medical limit is used, whether the employer must be insured.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed: the deposited Regulations 2024 supply the
  starting figures and methods for the general maximum amount (reg 134), the maximum weekly rate (reg 23) and the
  maximum damages amount under 25% (reg 122), the rounding rule (reg 4), the claim-process days (regs 19, 22), the
  minimum-rate exclusions (reg 24), the prescribed and excluded classes of worker (regs 13-18) and who pays for an
  assessment (reg 90). The index figures those methods use (ABS earnings, the WA Wage Price Index) are not in any
  deposit and are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the worker, the employer, the principal (s 215), the insurer, the
  arbitrator, the approved permanent impairment assessor, the Director, the court, a third party.
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally.** Followed: misconduct "proved before an arbitrator"
  (s 20), the arbitrator's satisfaction for a special increase (s 78), the impairment "the court is satisfied" of
  (ss 421(4), 424), whether a third party is liable in damages (s 434) are inputs.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed: "worker" (s 12(2)), "employer"
  (s 12(3)), "injury from employment" (s 6(2)), the medical limits (s 69) are worked out; "contract of service",
  "trade or business regularly carried on", "industrial instrument applies" are asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one worker, one injury, one claim.
- **The Act's own terms with a plain gloss.** Followed, with the gloss in the `@desc` of every input.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed: WorkCover WA, licensing of insurers, the regulation-making power
  and transitional provisions (other than s 566, which sets the number) are out.

## The lives

- **Jake k044** (12 April 2029): a delivery rider on an ABN, his wrist broken by a car door. *Am I a worker?* No, if
  the riding is a business he regularly carries on in his own name; yes if it is not (s 12(2)(c)). Both asserted.
- **Jake k085** (5 October 2053): employed by Sentinel, he falls on a wet stairwell on patrol; eleven weeks off;
  14% whole person impairment. *Is my employer liable? How much income compensation do I get? Who assesses me? How
  much is my lump sum and when is it paid? May I sue my employer?* Eleven weeks at an illustrative $1,500 is $16,500;
  the assessment must be by an approved assessor; at 14% he cannot start proceedings against Sentinel, and gets no
  special increase in his medical limit.
- **Daniel d044** (1 June 2031): Jarrah's first three employees, seen from an employee's seat. *Am I a worker? Must
  my employer insure me?* Yes and yes, from the first worker; the employer faces up to $30,000 for three.
- **Daniel d080** (5 October 2053): the same fall, from the tenant's side, asked from Jake's seat. *Who is my
  employer?* Sentinel, the party to his contract (s 12(3)); the tenant is not (no contract), and is not a s 215
  principal (it did not contract Sentinel).

## Why this scope

The four life events ask who is a worker, what is paid, who assesses, what 15% unlocks, who must insure and who the
employer is. An ordinary injured worker also meets the claim deadline, the insurer's decision deadlines, the medical
limits and the lump sum, so those are in. Left out: journeys, diseases and dust disease, labour hire, jockeys, working
directors, the s 54 pre-injury rate calculation and the s 57(3) minimum amount (both inputs), board and lodging,
additional income compensation (s 52), suspension and discontinuance, miscellaneous and rehabilitation expenses,
hearing loss, death benefits, settlement agreements (their registration is an input), injury management, dispute
resolution, double recovery, choice of law, and administration. 18 of the 45 Table items to s 101 are encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The 15% threshold guards only the employer.** It sits in Part 7 Division 2, which s 415 confines to damages
   "against a worker's employer". s 416(d) takes out a person who is an employer only because of s 215, and s 434 lets
   the worker sue a third party (s 411: "a person other than the employer") while claiming compensation. At 14% Jake
   cannot sue Sentinel, but nothing in this Act stops a negligence claim against the building's occupier or manager, if
   one was at fault (the general law decides that). Asserted.
2. **A contractor can be a worker; k044 turns on a fact the scenario does not state.** Under s 12(2)(c) an individual
   who contracts to do the work himself is a worker unless the work is part of a business he regularly carries on in
   his own or a business name, he sublets, or he employs and does none of it himself. The scenario's "no workers'
   compensation for a contractor" is plausible, not compelled. Both answers asserted.
3. **The regulations replace the Act's own figure.** s 538(1) says $243 991; s 566 and reg 134 note 2 replace it with
   $252 724, which reg 134 then varies each year by the WA Wage Price Index, never downwards (s 538(5)), rounded to the
   whole dollar (reg 4). Every limit in the Act is a percentage of this figure, so no limit for any year can be stated
   without the WPI. The maximum damages amount under 25% starts at $530 724 (reg 122), and the maximum weekly rate is
   twice the average of the May and November ABS earnings figures for WA (reg 23). Asserted on illustrative index figures.
4. **Fifteen per cent is the price of a costly election, not a prize.** It also needs an election registered by the
   Director (s 421(1), regs 120-121), and the court must itself be satisfied of 15% (s 421(4)(b), (5)). Under 25%, the
   election cuts income compensation to 70% then 50% and ends it after six months, ends the lump sum and ends medical
   expenses for later expenses (s 423); and damages are capped (s 424). 15% also opens a special increase in the
   medical limit (s 78(3)(a)). Asserted.
5. **Eleven weeks off is paid at the full pre-injury rate.** The first 26 weeks are paid at 100% (s 55(2)), then 85%
   (s 55(3)), within the maximum weekly rate (s 56) and above any minimum (s 57); income compensation stops when the
   total paid reaches the general maximum amount (s 51). Asserted.
6. **The lump sum is not measured by the 14%, and is paid only on settlement.** It is the degree of impairment of the
   body part times the Table percentage times the general maximum amount on the day of injury (ss 98(2)(a), 101): 10%
   of the back is $18,954.30 on $252,724. s 97(3): payable "only when the employer's liability ... is commuted by a
   settlement agreement registered under Division 12". Asserted.
7. **Silence is acceptance.** If the insurer gives neither a liability decision nor a deferral within 14 days of
   receiving the claim, it is taken to have accepted liability (s 28(6)); after a deferral, liability is taken to be
   accepted on day 120 (s 29(3), reg 19), and provisional payments start on day 28 (s 36, reg 22). Asserted.
8. **Who assesses, and who pays.** An approved permanent impairment assessor, not the treating doctor (s 186); the
   insurer pays for an assessment it asks for, the worker for one the worker asks for unless it is a miscellaneous
   expense under s 91 (reg 90). Asserted (the insurer-pays case).
9. **Insurance from the first worker.** s 202(2) has no size threshold; s 204(1) fines "$10 000 in respect of each of
   the employer's workers": $30,000 for Jarrah's three (d044). The scenarios cite "Part 8 (compulsory insurance)" and
   "Part 3 (permanent impairment)": in this compilation insurance is Part 5 and assessment Part 4. Asserted.
10. **A strange minimum.** Reg 24 removes the minimum weekly rate if *either* no industrial instrument applies *or* no
    Minimum Conditions of Employment Act minimum applies. Read literally, a worker covered by an award but not by that
    Act's minimum gets no minimum at all. Encoded literally. Asserted (the no-instrument case).

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added 6 November 2026, after the graffiti encoding's design (Graffiti Vandalism Act 2016, `gva-seat.l4`, its NOTES D2,
D4 and D8). The layer is `wcima-qa.l4` (imports `prelude` and `wcima-compensation`), with its cases in
`wcima-qa-cases.l4` (imports `prelude`, `wcima-compensation` and `wcima-qa`). The baseline's files are unchanged: no rule
or export of `wcima-compensation.l4` was touched, and its 70 cases still pass. The layer imports no daydate of its own;
its cases write dates with the built-in `DATE_FROM_DMY`.

**Why.** The site's question form asks only the inputs that can still change an open question. Every exported question
takes `my role` first ("Which of these describes you"); for the role it is not about, it answers `does not arise for
you` without reading any other input. Within a role, a question that arises only in a situation takes that situation's
fact second, and answers `does not arise for you` when it does not hold, again reading nothing else.

**Roles** (`Role`):

- `I was injured at work, or in work I do for someone`: the baseline's seat, the injured worker (or a person who may be
  one: the first questions ask whether they are a worker at all).
- `I employ someone who was injured at work, or contracted their employer for the work`: the employer, on whom the Act
  puts the duties to pass on a claim (ss 26, 31(2)), to make provisional payments (s 36) and the first payment of income
  compensation (s 47), to insure (s 202) and to have an injury management system (s 159); and the principal, whom s 215
  makes an employer of the contractor's worker, jointly and severally liable with the contractor.

The insurer and self-insurer, WorkCover WA, the Director, the arbitrator, the approved assessor and the court are not
seats: what they decide is asked as a fact. A dependant of a worker who died is not a seat: the baseline does not encode
death benefits, and the layer keeps to the subjects the baseline encodes.

**Situation inputs.** All are BOOLEAN; FALSE gives `does not arise for you`; each is the second input wherever it is a
situation, with the same name, type and `@desc` in every question.

| Name | Role | Questions that take it |
|---|---|---|
| `I have claimed compensation from my employer` | worker | Did I make my claim within 12 months of my injury; Does the timing of my claim leave it valid; May I give my claim to the insurer myself; Is my employer's insurer taken to have accepted liability; Must my employer make provisional payments to me; Is my first payment of income compensation overdue |
| `my election to seek damages has been registered` | worker | How much income compensation do I get after my election is registered; Does my election end my compensation for later medical expenses (and, as an ordinary fourth input, Is my lump sum for permanent impairment payable) |
| `my worker has claimed compensation from me` | employer | What must I do with my worker's claim; Am I late in passing on my worker's claim; What is the most I can be fined for not passing on my worker's claim in time; Is my insurer taken to have accepted liability for my worker's claim; Must I make provisional payments to my worker; What is the most I can be fined for not making a provisional payment; Is my first payment of income compensation to my worker overdue; What is the most I can be fined for each income compensation payment not made when due |

The Act ties ss 26-29, 31, 36 and 47 to a claim made under s 25 ("within 7 days after a worker claims compensation",
s 26(1); "within 14 days after a claim is given to an insurer", s 28(1)), and the s 423 consequences to the registration
of the election. Nothing else in the encoded text turns on a situation of that kind: liability, income compensation,
medical expenses, the lump sum and suing arise for every injured worker, so those questions take none.

**Decisions.**

1. **reg 4 rounding is not a question.** The baseline exports `rounded to the nearest whole dollar`, a step inside the
   reg 122, 134 and 23 amounts. No person asks it, so the layer asks the two amounts it feeds (the WPI-varied amount and
   the maximum weekly rate) and not the rounding. Each of the other 35 baseline exports is asked by the worker, through
   the baseline function.
2. **The lump sum question takes the election as an ordinary input.** "Is my lump sum for permanent impairment payable"
   arises whether or not an election is registered (ss 97(3), 423(1)(c)), so the election is not its situation. It
   takes `my election to seek damages has been registered` fourth, with the same type and `@desc`, so the form asks it
   once (as the graffiti layer does with consent, its D8.10 (2)). The baseline's input for the same fact has another
   name (`I have a registered election to seek damages`); the layer passes its answer there.
3. **s 27 is new.** The worker's "May I give my claim to the insurer myself" has no baseline function; a helper in the
   layer works it from ss 26(1) and 27(1). Day 0 is the day of the claim, so the employer's 7 days are days 1-7 and the
   worker may act from day 8.
4. **The employer's 7 days are a count, not a date.** "What must I do with my worker's claim" answers a number of days
   after the claim (7). The claim day is left out (Interpretation Act 1984 s 61(1)(b)), and a last day on a weekend or
   public holiday would move (s 61(1)(e)), which this layer does not work out.
5. **Fixed answers settle on the role.** "Must I have an injury management system" (s 159(2)) and the s 159 fine read
   nothing but the role; the three claim fines read the role and the situation.
6. **The employer's damages questions fix the defendant.** "Can the court award my worker damages against me" and "Are
   the damages my worker can get from me capped" call the baseline's s 415/416 function with `my employer`, by the
   questions' own terms, and then its ss 421(4), (5) and 424 functions.
7. **Names.** Each question's name is a plain first-person question, different from every baseline function's name
   even after the site shortens names to their first 60 characters (letters and digits, the rest as hyphens), so the
   site never runs the baseline's function in the question's place. The first version broke this once: "How much income
   compensation do I get for my first weeks off work" shortened to the baseline's "... for my first weeks off", so the
   site answered the employer with the baseline's function and could not drop the question. It is now "For my first
   weeks off work, how much income compensation do I get" (fixed 6 November 2026, after verification).
8. **The police officer's death is part of the class** (fixed 6 November 2026). "Am I a worker under the Act" and "Is
   this person my worker" took the `My engagement` record (7 yes/no fields), the `Prescribed class` (7 values) and a
   yes/no for whether a police officer died of the injury: 1792 combinations, more than the 1024 the site's form tries
   before it gives up dropping a question for the other role, so each stayed open for the role it is not about. The
   death matters only for the police class (reg 16(2)), so the layer asks `Class under the regulations`, whose 8
   values split the police class in two, and passes the baseline's class and yes/no through two helpers that list
   every value: 2^7 x 8 = 1024 combinations.
9. **The s 26(1)/31(2) fine depends on how the employer is insured** (fixed 6 November 2026). The first version
   answered $5000 to every employer with a claim. Only an insured employer (s 26(1)) and an uninsured employer
   (s 31(2)) have the duty the fine is for; a self-insurer has neither, and s 31(3) takes out an employer uninsured
   because its insurer refused indemnity under s 241. The question now takes `how I am insured` and answers `a fine of
   at most` 5000 or `no fine: you have no duty under s 26(1) or s 31(2) to pass the claim on` (`Fine answer`).
10. **One description per input name** (fixed 6 November 2026). Inputs that share a name now share one description,
   worded for either role: `a liability decision notice has been given`, `a deferred decision notice was given`, `a
   deferred decision notice was given within 14 days`, `my assessed whole of person impairment`, `number of workers`
   and `the impairment the court finds`.

**Not covered for the employer.** Return to work programs (s 160); reducing, suspending or discontinuing income
compensation (ss 62-67); agreeing or disputing the degree of permanent impairment (s 105); dust disease claims
(Division 9); settlement agreements (ss 149-157); information to the insurer and records (ss 203, 209); the certificate
of currency (s 212); indemnity from a third party (ss 435-437). Nothing the baseline covers is missing for the worker.

70 satisfied; `wcima-qa.l4`; `wcima-qa-cases.l4`); `l4 check wcima-qa.l4` succeeds. A script listed every `@export`

role branch reading its situation first, no DEONTIC or EVENT input, one type and one description per input name,
