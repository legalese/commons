## Seat

**The person summonsed for jury service: "Must I serve, and can my employer penalise me?"**
The Act is applied to that person. It decides whether they are liable, eligible and qualified, whether and how they can be excused, whether they must attend and for how long, what they risk by not attending, what their employer may not do, and what they are paid.
Every exported question is phrased as that person would ask it ("Am I liable to serve as a juror?", "Must I attend?", "Can my employer excuse me?", "Does my employer commit an offence?", "How much does the State pay me?").
Provisions addressed to the sheriff, the summoning officer, the judge and the employer are encoded as what they mean for the person: whether they must or may be excused (ss 34E-34J), whether the summons reached them in time (ss 31, 32D), when they can be sent home (ss 32E, 32I), what the employer risks and what a court can order for them (s 56).

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed, with a gap. The regulations that set the s 58B amounts are not deposited, so the amounts and the prescribed classes are inputs. The Electoral Act 1907 enrolment categories that s 4 refers to (ss 17AA, 17A, 17B) are asked as facts; that Act has its own baseline.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the summoned person, the summoning officer, the jury pool supervisor (through ss 32FA and 32H only as comments), the sheriff, the judge, the employer (including "a person acting on behalf of an employer", s 56(1)), the State as payer.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are asked as facts:
  - the judge's or summoning officer's satisfaction of a s 34G ground, of a s 34H good reason and of partiality (s 34I);
  - the sheriff's satisfaction of permanent incapacity (s 34E);
  - undue hardship or serious inconvenience (s 34H(2));
  - a "reasonable excuse" for not obeying (s 55(1));
  - whether the employer can prove its act was not because of the summons (s 56(6));
  - the earnings the employee "could reasonably expect" (s 56(3), s 58B(3)), as a daily figure.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out: "conviction" (s 5(1), excluding quashed, pardoned and spent convictions); the s 5(3) eligibility and qualification limbs; the Schedule 1 classes as data; "does jury service" (ss 56(4), 58B(1)); "prejudicially" (s 56(2), (3)); "protected information" (s 56A). Asked: whether a conviction is spent (Spent Convictions Act 1988), whether the person is an involuntary patient, a represented person or covered by the Criminal Law (Mental Impairment) Act 2023, whether the Jury Exemption Act 1965 (Cth) applies, and whether an offence is against a "road law".
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one summoned person (`Person`, with their convictions as a list) and one summons.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments and `@desc` lines, each tied to its section.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out: jury districts, jury lists and the jurors' book (Parts III-IV); how panels and pools are chosen and juries empanelled (ss 20-30, 32A-32C, 32F-32H, 35-40); challenges; verdicts and the conduct of trials (ss 41, 43-54); s 57 (photographing jurors, an offence by others that protects the juror); s 59 onward.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names ss 5, 31, 56 and 58B, and its seat asks "Must I serve, and can my employer penalise me?".
Answering "must I serve" needs s 4 (liability) beside s 5, Schedule 1 (who is ineligible), the whole of Part VC Division 2 (excusal, ss 34C-34J), the service rules (ss 31, 32D(2), 33(5)), withdrawal and discharge (ss 32E, 32I), the attendance limits (ss 32I, 42), the disclosure duty (s 33A and Schedule 2), and the offence of not obeying (s 55).
"Can my employer penalise me" is s 56, with s 58B for who is paid.
Part IXA (jury confidentiality) binds the person once they serve, so ss 56A, 56B and 56E are in as well.

Not encoded:

- the deferral date itself (s 34H(5), (6): a panel or pool "to attend on a date within the 6 months after" the original date, or the first one after that period); the outcome is encoded, the date is not;
- s 56(7)(b)(ii), (c), (9), (10) (the other compensation routes, enforcement, contempt), beyond the cap in s 56(8);
- s 56C, 56D (soliciting and publishing protected information, offences mostly by others);
- the regulations' amounts and the prescribed classes.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Liability follows the State roll, not age.** s 4(1): a person "enrolled on any of the rolls of electors entitled to vote at an election of members of the Legislative Assembly" is liable. An eligible overseas elector (Electoral Act s 17A) and an itinerant elector (s 17B(1)) are not (s 4(2)). A 16- or 17-year-old enrolled provisionally under Electoral Act s 17AA "is taken not to be an elector" and "is not entitled to vote", so is read as not liable (INFERENCE). Chloe at 18, enrolled, is liable. Asserted.
2. **The upper limit is 75, and there is no other age test.** s 5(3)(a): "not eligible ... if he or she has reached 75 years of age". Asserted.
3. **Lawyers are out of every jury; police only of criminal juries.** Schedule 1 Division 1 (civil and criminal) covers viceregal and parliamentary officers, judicial and court officers, and, since 2022, every legal practitioner (cl 3). Division 2 (criminal only) covers Corruption and Crime Commission officers and the WA Police, including cadets. Asserted.
4. **A criminal record disqualifies in three ways, and spent, quashed and pardoned convictions never count.** s 5(1), (3)(b):
   - any sentence of death, life, or "imprisonment for a term exceeding 2 years or for an indeterminate period", anywhere and at any time;
   - in the 5 years before the first attendance date, any prison sentence or parole, juvenile detention after a finding of guilt, or a probation or community order, anywhere;
   - in those 5 years, 2 or more WA convictions for imprisonable offences, or 3 or more WA road law convictions.

   An interstate conviction does not count towards the two WA tallies. Exactly 2 years is not "exceeding 2 years". Asserted (18 months: qualified; 3 years: not; one WA and one NSW imprisonable conviction: qualified).
5. **Being ineligible does not by itself cancel the summons.** s 5(3) says the person "is not eligible" or "not qualified", but s 34G(2) says the judge or summoning officer "must excuse" them once "satisfied" of it, and s 33A(a) tells them "the manner in which a claim that he or she is not eligible or not qualified ... may be made". So the encoding has such a person answer the summons and make the claim (INFERENCE). The person must also disclose the matter (Schedule 2 items 1-6). Asserted.
6. **A first good-reason application can win only a deferral.** s 34H(3)(a) allows "a deferral of jury duty" unless the summons itself followed a deferral. s 34H(4) forbids outright excusal under (3)(b) unless the summons followed a deferral and the reason "was not reasonably foreseeable" then, or there are "exceptional reasons". Even with a good reason, both are discretions ("may"). Asserted.
7. **For partiality, the summoning officer needs an application; a judge does not.** s 34I(1), (2). Once satisfied, excusal is mandatory ("must", s 34I(3)), but only from "that trial". Asserted.
8. **Only a judge or the summoning officer can excuse.** ss 34F-34J name no one else. An employer cannot excuse a summoned employee. Asserted.
9. **A panel summons needs 5 clear days; a pool summons 5 days, unless a judge orders otherwise.** s 31; s 32D(2). A posted summons is served when it would arrive in the ordinary course of post (s 33(5)). Reading a late-served summons as not "served on the person under this Act" for s 55 is an INFERENCE. Asserted.
10. **Attendance is capped at 5 days, except on a jury still sitting.** s 32I(1), (2) (pool: at most 5 consecutive court sitting days as the summoning officer sets); s 42 (panel: no more than 5 days at the same sittings "except for the purpose of finishing a part heard case"). Asserted for a pool.
11. **Not obeying a served summons without reasonable excuse is an offence: a fine of $5 000.** s 55(1). Asserted.
12. **The employer may not sack, stop or cut pay, otherwise prejudice, or threaten any of these.** s 56(2), (5). This covers contractors too ("employee includes a person employed under a contract for services", s 56(1)). A bare "you cannot go", with no threat and nothing done, is not one of the listed acts (READING on the words); it does not excuse the employee either (finding 8). Asserted.
13. **Not paying the employee's usual earnings during jury service is itself prejudicial action, with no limit on days.** s 56(3) deems an employer to act prejudicially "because the employee has done or is doing jury service" if it does not pay "the earnings that the employee could reasonably expect to have been paid while doing the jury service". The Act sets no cap on days. The Fair Work Act 2009 s 111, as encoded in that Act's baseline, requires make-up pay only for the first 10 days, less jury service pay. For Chloe's HYPOTHETICAL 15 days at $400, that is $3,700 under the Fair Work Act and $6,000 of earnings under s 56(3). The two answer different questions: the Commonwealth minimum, and what this Act makes an offence. Whether the Fair Work Act affects s 56(3) for a national system employee was not examined. Asserted ($6,000).
14. **The employer carries the onus on the reason.** s 56(6): once the other facts are proved, the employer must prove its act "was not actuated" by the summons or jury service. Asserted (an employer that proves it: no offence).
15. **The penalty is $10 000 for an individual and $50 000 for a body corporate. Compensation is capped at 12 months' remuneration, and re-employment is available only after a dismissal.** s 56(5), (7), (8). Asserted ($50 000 for Jarrah; the cap at Chloe's $110,000; no re-employment order after a pay cut).
16. **The State's fees follow the earnings.** s 58B(3): an employer that keeps paying an employee on a contract of service gets the fees, unless it is in a prescribed class. (4): an employee who is not paid gets them. (5): anyone else (including a contractor, since (3) says "contract of service" where s 56 also covers contracts for services) gets them. Allowances and expenses go to the person in every case (s 58B(2)). Asserted.
17. **After the trial a juror may say they were on the jury, but never publish the deliberations.** s 56A(1) "protected information", s 56B(1) (an offence to disclose it if aware it will or is likely to be published: $5 000), s 56E(b) (after the trial, information identifying oneself as a juror, or another juror who consents). Disclosure to a court and the other listed bodies is not prohibited (s 56B(2)). Asserted.
18. **The scenario's citations need correcting.** The life's facts cite "s 32 (summons)" and "ss 58B-58C (employer must not dismiss or disadvantage)". Section 32 was deleted (No. 13 of 2011 s 22); the summons provisions are s 31 (panels) and s 32D (pools). There is no s 58C; employer protection is s 56, and s 58B is payments. Comment only.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added on 6 November 2026, after the graffiti encoding's design (D2, D4, D8). The baseline's files are unchanged; the layer is added beside them.

- `ja-qa.l4` imports `prelude` and `ja-juror`. It has 38 exported questions.
- `ja-qa-cases.l4` imports the same and `ja-qa`. It has 200 cases.

### Roles

`my role` (`Role`, "Which of these describes you") is the first input of every question. A question about another role answers `does not arise for you` and reads nothing else.

- **`I am liable for jury service, or have been summonsed for it`**: the baseline's seat (Chloe, c025 and c077). It has 30 questions, one for each baseline export. Each calls the baseline's function and wraps the answer:
  - `yes if` for a BOOLEAN;
  - `amount` for dollars;
  - `items` for the Schedule 2 list;
  - `the answer is` for the baseline's own enumerations.
- **`I employ someone who has been summonsed for jury service`**: the person s 56 binds. It has 8 questions:
  - may I excuse them;
  - is my act prejudicial;
  - do I commit the offence;
  - the earnings I must keep paying;
  - my maximum fine;
  - the most compensation a court can order;
  - can I be ordered to take them back;
  - do I get the fees (s 58B(3)).

  Each calls the baseline's function. What the employer did is asked in the employer's words (`What I did to my employee`) and put into the baseline's `EmployerAction` by `in the employee's words`. That helper lists every value, with no OTHERWISE.

The sheriff, the summoning officer, the jury pool supervisor, the judge and the State are not seats.

### Situation inputs

All are BOOLEAN. Each comes straight after `my role`. FALSE gives `does not arise for you` without reading anything else.

| Input | Role | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| `I have been served with a jury summons` | person | 13: the Schedule 2 disclosure, s 34G, "can my employer excuse me", s 34F(4), the two of s 34H, s 34I, s 34J, served in time, must I attend, the attendance day, the s 55 offence and fine |
| `I have attended to serve, or served, as a juror` | person | 4: who gets the fees, how much the State pays me, and the two of s 56B |
| `I work for an employer` | person | 4: is it prejudicial, does my employer commit the offence, the earnings, my employer's fine |
| `my employer has been convicted under s 56(5)` | person | 2: compensation and re-employment |
| `I have been convicted under s 56(5)` | employer | 2: the same two, from the employer's seat |

These questions take no situation input, because they arise in every situation of their role:

- liability, eligibility, qualification and standing (ss 4, 5);
- the s 34E certificate;
- "Am I doing jury service";
- the employer's other six questions. The role is an employer of a person summonsed.

### Decisions

- **Payment is tied to service done.** "How much does the State pay me" takes the served situation. It calls the baseline with `I do jury service` TRUE, so the baseline's 0 for no service is now `does not arise for you`.
- **Re-employment is tied to the conviction.** "Can the court order my employer to take me back" takes the conviction situation and calls the baseline with the conviction TRUE. The baseline's own input is now the situation.
- **"Can my employer excuse me" takes the summons situation.** It answers no wherever it arises.
- **"I work for an employer" covers contractors.** s 56(1) brings contractors in. The same input settles the person's four s 56 questions. Compensation and re-employment take the conviction instead, which implies an employer.

### Not covered for the employer

- the employer's own records and notices: the Act sets none;
- the Fair Work Act 2009 s 111 make-up pay (finding 13);
- the prescribed classes and amounts (no regulations deposited).

### Fix after verification (6 November 2026)

An independent check through a local jl4-service form engine found that some inputs of the same name had different @desc values, or two meanings. The form shows one label per name. Fixed here (no answer changes; the 200 cases and the baseline's 70 are unchanged and satisfied):

- `I am in a prescribed class` meant the juror in the s 58B(3)-(5) question and the employer in the employer's s 58B(3) question. The employer's input is now `I am in a prescribed class of employer`.
- `I have applied` was three different applications. It is now `I have applied to be excused for good reason` (s 34H), `I have applied to be excused for not being indifferent` (s 34I) and `I have applied to be excused for recent jury service` (s 34J).
- `days from service to attendance`, `act` and `days of jury service` now carry one @desc each.

Not changed:

- **Record inputs in questions of another role.** The juror's eligibility questions take the `Person` record `me`, so the form engine keeps them for the employer: it cannot settle a question while a record input is unanswered. The code already answers `does not arise for you` for the employer without reading the record; the gap is in how the engine decides what to drop (it should evaluate with the role alone first). Flattening the baseline's record into separate fields would restate the baseline, against the layering rule.
- **The jl4-service build** fails on modules with a `GIVEN ... YIELD` lambda (`ja-juror.l4` has one). That is a service bug; the stable l4 runs every file cleanly.
