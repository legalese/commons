## Seat

**The child before the court (Jake, at 15).**
The Act is applied to the child charged with an offence: which court hears the charge, whether the child can choose a jury trial, how much a magistrate can give, who may sit in the courtroom, whether the child can be named in the media, who may be told the outcome, and how the child can have an order looked at again.
Every exported question is phrased as the child would ask it ("Which court deals with my charge?", "May a report identifying me be published?").
Provisions addressed to the Court, the President, a magistrate or a publisher are encoded as what they mean for the child. That covers what the child must be told (ss 19B(1), 22(3)), what the Court must make sure of (s 34), and who may learn the child's name (ss 31(2), 35, 36).

## Why this scope

The plan entry names s 19 and the seat asks "Which court deals with me?".
The answer runs through ss 19A-19D (the election, the joint trial, the transfer at 18) and s 30(1).
The prompt also asked who may be present, publication of the child's identity, and the child's rights at the hearing as far as the Act states them. Those are ss 23, 31, 33, 34, 35, 36, 36AA and 36A, with the sentencing limits of ss 21-22 and the child's own review routes in ss 28 and 40.

Not encoded:

- s 20 (care and protection, school attendance, restraining orders, prohibited behaviour orders);
- s 19(9), s 19E, s 19B(5), (6), s 19C(3), (4);
- s 21(4A)-(6) (unfitness referral; referral up for sentence);
- s 27, s 29 (contempt), s 30(2) (a decision made in the mistaken belief the person was under 18);
- s 35(3) (child victims of sexual offences), s 36A(2), (4), (5), s 40A;
- ss 41-43 (appeals, which run under the Criminal Appeals Act 2004);
- the Young Offenders Act 1994 Part 5 Division 2 and the Sentencing Act 1995 provisions that s 19(1a) makes s 19(1) subject to.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The Children's Court has the charge whatever Jake's age when it comes to court.** s 19(1) gives it "exclusive jurisdiction" over a charge against a child, and s 19(2) extends that to a person now 18 or over for an offence "before attaining the age of 18 years". The test is the age on the day of the offence, as in the Young Offenders Act 1994 s 4. Where the age is uncertain and the charge says he "might have been a child", it keeps jurisdiction even on evidence he was 18 (s 19(2AA), (2AB)). Asserted.
2. **Had Jake been charged, the Children's Court would have heard his theft.** Stealing is indictable, but The Criminal Code s 426(4) gives a summary conviction penalty for property worth up to $1 000, so it is an indictable offence that may be tried summarily (Criminal Code s 5, read in its own deposit; an inference applied to the 2021 facts with the compilation's current values). The scenario cites s 19 correctly. Asserted.
3. **A child gets to choose a jury only after the Court has decided on indictment, for an either-way offence.** s 19B(1)(b) opens the election only where "the Court ... decides that it is to be tried on indictment"; for an offence an adult must be tried for on indictment it is open at once. The Court must tell the child (s 19B(1)). A child who does not elect is heard in the Children's Court "as if" on indictment, but "is not thereby entitled to have any issue tried by a jury" (s 19B(4)(c)). Asserted.
4. **Two things can take the charge out of the Children's Court without the child's choice.** s 19C sends it up for a joint trial with an adult co-accused who is to be tried on indictment, if the Court thinks that appropriate. s 19D lets the Court transfer the charge of someone who "has attained the age of 18 years" to the Magistrates Court. Both apply "Notwithstanding section 19B", and only to indictable offences (s 19A). Reading s 19D's "has attained" at the date the Court deals with the charge is an inference. Asserted.
5. **A magistrate's sentencing power is capped, and the cap depends on who sits and on age at sentencing.** s 21(2): no more than 12 months' detention; imprisonment no more than 3 months if under 18 at sentencing, 6 months if 18 or over; for one offence or in aggregate on one occasion. JPs alone cannot impose detention or imprisonment (s 21(4)). A magistrate directed by the President under s 22 sentences "as if that magistrate were a judge", and must say so when the trial begins (s 22(3)). This Act sets no cap for a judge. Asserted.
6. **The parent-attendance duty in this Act does not apply to Jake's criminal charge.** s 23(1a) confines s 23 to proceedings other than those under the Young Offenders Act 1994 s 45, which covers "proceedings against a young person for an offence". For a charge, the responsible adult duty is the Young Offenders Act's. Asserted.
7. **The courtroom can be closed to anyone, but a closed hearing does not keep the child's name from a victim.** s 31(1) lets the Court exclude "any persons". A "prescribed officer" is entitled to be present and heard (s 33(2)); reading that entitlement as surviving a general exclusion order is an inference. After a closed hearing the Court "shall" give the child's name, age, address and the charge to anyone who satisfies it of loss from the offence or other good cause (s 31(2)). Asserted.
8. **The Court must make sure the child understands the proceedings.** s 34(1). An order is not defective for falling short if the Court "substantially complied" (s 34(3)); the Act does not say what follows otherwise. Asserted (s 34(1)).
9. **Identifying the child is banned automatically in the Children's Court and on appeal from it, but only by order in the Supreme or District Court.** s 35(1) bans reports likely to identify the child, unless a Supreme Court order under s 36A (or the Prohibited Behaviour Orders Act 2010 s 34) allows it. For a child who elected trial in the Supreme or District Court, s 35(2) leaves it to that court to order a ban. Only the Attorney General or the Commissioner of Police may apply for a s 36A order (s 36A(3)). Breach: a fine of $10 000 or 12 months (s 35(4)). Asserted.
10. **No one but the child may tell an employer how the Children's Court case ended.** s 36(1) bars "a person other than the child" from disclosing, in a way that identifies the child, a dismissal, conviction, finding of guilt or order, except to a court, a person performing duties under a written law, a person concerned with the child's custody or welfare, the Developmental Pathways Project (s 36AA), or under a s 36A order. Asserted.
11. **The child has a month to ask the President to reconsider a magistrate's or JPs' order.** s 40(2)(a): "within one month after the date of the order", and not once leave to appeal has been granted (s 40(5)). It is not available against an order made by a judge (s 40(1)). The CEO (young offenders) may apply on the child's behalf at any time (s 40(2)(b), noted). Asserted.
12. **A rehearing is open only for orders under the Young Offenders Act 1994 or the Children and Community Services Act 2004, and not after conviction on indictment.** s 28(1), (2)(c). The President decides, on cause shown. Asserted.
