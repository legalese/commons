# Vocational Education and Training Act 1996 (WA) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.

No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** the compilation published on legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 47549, retrieved 7 September 2026), deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_47549.txt`.
The deposit gives no compilation number.
Its compilation table ends with the Vocational Education and Training Amendment Act 2024 (No. 8 of 2024), whose s 6 (inserting s 60J, delegation by the chief executive) came into force on 11 July 2024.

## Seat

**The apprentice or trainee**, meaning "the person who is named in a training contract as the person who will be trained under the contract" under any title (s 60A), and the person about to become one, such as a school leaver going to TAFE.
Part 7 is written mostly to the employer, the chief executive and the registered training provider. This encoding turns each of those provisions into what it means for the apprentice:

- the employer must lodge the contract, and the contract has not started until it is registered;
- after probation, the employer cannot end the contract without the apprentice's consent or the chief executive's approval;
- the chief executive must approve a termination on four grounds;
- the provider may confer the qualification only on stated conditions;
- any dissatisfied person may appeal to the Industrial Relations Commission.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed as far as the deposit allows. No regulations are deposited, so everything they settle is asked as an input (see above). The Act's own fines ($3,000 and $10,000) are encoded.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the apprentice, the employer (or employers, s 60E(2)), the chief executive, the Minister, the registered training provider, the Training Accreditation Council and the Western Australian Industrial Relations Commission.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed for each of these:
  - the chief executive's satisfaction (ss 60F(3)(d), 60G(3)), which gives "must approve, if satisfied";
  - the chief executive's approval of more than one employer (s 60E(2)) and of a termination (s 60G(2)(b));
  - the provider's assessment of competence (s 60I);
  - the Minister's classification (s 60C).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. These are worked out: "approved VET course", "registered training provider" (as the provider's facts), "training contract" (from the five agreements in s 60E(1), the stated qualification and its class) and "apprentice". "Probation period" is not defined by the Act and is asked as a fact.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one training contract, or one course or qualification.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed in the comments and the `@desc` of each input.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Out: Parts 2 to 6 (the Minister, the Board, the Council, colleges), Part 7A beyond s 58A (registration and accreditation from the provider's side), Part 8A (inspectors) and Part 8.

## Why this scope

The plan names ss 60E and 60F for Jake's two events. The prompt adds the rest of what an apprentice meets in Part 7: who signs, probation, cancellation and suspension, transfer, completion and disputes.
Part 7 is short: its Divisions 1 to 4 run from s 60A to s 60J, with the regulation-making power in s 60. All of it is in scope except the Minister's procedure for classifying (s 60C(1), (2), (5)-(7)) and delegation (s 60J).
s 5 and s 58A are added because the school leaver's first question is whether the course and the provider are the real thing.
Everything else in the Act concerns how the training system is organised, so it sits outside this seat.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A pre-apprenticeship is not an apprenticeship.** Under the Act, an "apprentice" is a person named in a training contract (s 60A), and a training contract is a promise by an employer to employ and train (s 60E(1)(a)). A school leaver at TAFE with no employer has neither. Jake in 2022-23 is a student on an approved VET course (if it is accredited), not an apprentice. Asserted.
2. **The contract does not start until it is registered, and lodging it is the employer's job.** "A training contract does not commence until it is registered by the chief executive" (s 60F(1)). The employer must lodge it, on pain of a $3,000 fine (s 60F(2)). The apprentice has no lodging duty. Asserted.
3. **Time at TAFE is time at work, and paid.** Every training contract must include the employer's agreement that time spent training and being assessed, "whether at the employer's workplace or not", counts "for all purposes (including the payment of remuneration)" as time working (s 60E(1)(a)(iv)). A contract without that agreement is not a training contract. Asserted.
4. **The Act does not say a parent must sign.** The parties are the employer and the employee (s 60E(1)). Capacity under 18 and the contract's form are left to the regulations (s 60(b), (c)), so whether a 17-year-old signs alone turns on regulations not deposited here. Asserted, conditionally.
5. **During probation either side may end the contract; after it, the employer may not without consent or approval.** "Subject to the regulations, a party ... may terminate it" (s 60G(1)). After probation, an employer who terminates without the apprentice's consent or the chief executive's approval commits an offence, with a fine of up to $10,000 (s 60G(2)). The restriction binds only the employer. "(if any)" is read so that a contract with no probation period is protected from the start, which is an inference. Asserted.
6. **Four grounds compel approval.** The chief executive "must approve" if satisfied the employer has ceased or is about to cease business, cannot perform because of a substantial change of circumstances, the apprentice has engaged in serious misconduct, or a prescribed matter applies. Otherwise the chief executive "may refuse" (s 60G(3)). Asserted.
7. **Only the employer can transfer the contract, and only to someone who employs the apprentice.** It "may be assigned by the employer to another person who employs the apprentice" (s 60E(4)(c)). The Act requires no consent from the apprentice, though the regulations may. A variation needs both parties and must be lodged like the contract (ss 60E(4)(a), 60F(4)). A suspension may be made by either party alone (s 60E(4)(b)). Asserted.
8. **When the contract ends, so does the job under it.** Cancellation of registration, termination or expiry ends the contract and "the employment of the apprentice by the employer under the contract" (ss 60F(6), 60H(1)). The parties may agree a new arrangement (s 60H(2)). Asserted.
9. **A class A trade can be had without finishing the apprenticeship, but not without an employer.** A provider may confer a class A qualification on someone who "has only partly fulfilled" the contract, if satisfied they have the competency "as a result of training received from an employer and other learning" (s 60I(1)). A course alone does not give it (s 60C(3)(a), an inference from "cannot obtain except"). Class B, class C and approved qualifications carry no employer condition (s 60I(2)-(4)). Asserted.
10. **Appeals go to the Industrial Relations Commission, which rehears.** Any decision of the chief executive under s 60F or s 60G may be appealed by a dissatisfied person. The Commission "must rehear the matter" and may confirm the decision, substitute its own, or remit it (ss 60F(8)-(9), 60G(4)-(5)). The Act gives the Commission no power to award compensation on such an appeal. Disputes under the contract itself, and refusals of a qualification, have a forum or appeal only if the regulations create one (s 60(f), (i)). Asserted.
11. **Part 7 overrides awards.** Where Part 7 or its regulations conflict with the Industrial Relations Act 1979 or an award or industrial agreement, Part 7 prevails (s 60B). Asserted.
12. **The fines.** s 58A, $10,000 (a provider offering a course or conferring a qualification it is not permitted to); s 60D, $10,000 (a training contract for a class C qualification, or training for a class A or B qualification outside one); s 60F(2), $3,000; s 60G(2), $10,000. Asserted for ss 60F(2) and 60G(2).

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The Vocational Education and Training (General) Regulations 2009 need retrieving and depositing. Until then, these are all inputs:
  - capacity under 18 and a parent's signature;
  - the probation period;
  - the lodging deadline;
  - eligibility;
  - the grounds for cancellation;
  - dispute resolution;
  - qualification appeals.
- The chief executive's s 60C(7) register of class A and class B qualifications, and the conditions on them, are not deposited. Whether an electrical or engineering qualification is class A, B or C is a fact.
- Jake's two events give no facts about accreditation, registration or classification, so those are hypothetical.
- No case law or Industrial Relations Commission decisions were searched (for example on "serious misconduct" or "substantial change of circumstances").
- Commonwealth law that may bear on an apprentice is not encoded: the Fair Work Act 2009, and the national VET regulator for providers registered under the National Vocational Education and Training Regulator Act 2011.

### Roles

`DECLARE Role IS ONE OF`:

### Decisions

- **QA-1. The situation is the contract's registration, not its signing.** s 60F(1): "A training contract does not commence until it is registered." So the questions of a running contract wait for registration, and the questions of lodging and refusal stop with it. "Is my training contract in effect" and "Am I still employed under it" arise for an unregistered contract too, and answer no from the baseline's status record.
- **QA-2. The employer's inputs are in the employer's voice, mapped onto the baseline's.** The baseline's types speak as the apprentice (`my employer`, `I consented`, `I have engaged in serious misconduct`, `training me for ...`). For the employer the layer declares its own types (`The reason I give`, `The decision I am dissatisfied with`, `The employer's offence`, `Who may make the change, for the employer`) and builds the baseline's `My termination` from the employer's own facts (`the apprentice consented` and so on). Every map lists every value with no OTHERWISE. Where the baseline's record has neutral fields (`My contract`, `My lodgement`, `My contract's status`) the employer is given the record itself, with a `@desc` saying who "I" is.
- **QA-3. Inputs that shared a name with different `@desc` texts are renamed.** The baseline's `the regulations permit it` means three different things in three questions; the layer asks `the regulations permit the transfer`, `the regulations permit the suspension` and `the regulations permit the termination`.
- **QA-4. The Commission's powers question serves both appellants.** "May the Commission do this on my appeal" (ss 60F(9), 60G(5)) is the same for the apprentice and the employer, so one question has a branch for each.
- **QA-5. The provider's view of the person is its own record.** `The person's completion` (they fulfilled a registered training contract; completed an approved course; I assessed them as competent; their skills come from training with an employer) maps onto the baseline's `My completion`.

### Not covered for the new roles

- Employer: what the regulations require for the form, lodgement and probation (not deposited); the employer's obligations under the award; more than one employer (s 60E(2)) is in the contract record but not asked as its own question.
- Provider: registration and accreditation under Part 7A beyond s 58A, and the Council's powers (out of the baseline's scope).
