# Tobacco and Other Products Control Act 2006 (WA) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no
coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as
advice.

**Edition:** the compilation "with editorial changes as at 14 May 2026", which
includes the Tobacco Products Control Amendment Act 2026 (No. 1 of 2026; Part 1 in
force 17 March 2026, Part 2 in force 13 May 2026). Deposited at
`../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49390.txt` (retrieved 7 September 2026). No
regulations are deposited. The personal use, commercial and large commercial
quantities (s 30B(1)), the "prescribed document" (s 15(1)(c)) and the therapeutic
conditions (ss 30A(3), 30B(6)) are inputs.

**Checks:** one module (`topca-minor.l4`) and one case file
(`topca-cases-minor.l4`). 44 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings, run with
the stable l4 from VS Code extension 1.6.98. The case file is generated by
`gen_wa_tobacco-and-other-products-control-act-2006_baseline.py` in the session
scratchpad. Its expected values were written by hand from the text.

## Seat

**The minor: a person who has not reached 18 years of age, found with, or trying to
get, a vape or tobacco.** The life event that cites this Act asks that person's
question: "Is my possession an offence, or only the sale?" Most of the Act speaks to
sellers, licensees, investigators and police. Those sections are encoded for what
they mean for the minor:

- whether the sale to me was an offence;
- whether the seller has a defence;
- whether my things may be taken, and whether I get them back;
- what I must tell an officer;
- whether my parent may be told.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The
  regulations that set the quantities, the prescribed document and the therapeutic
  conditions are not deposited, so those are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: seller, investigator, police officer,
  parent, young person.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** Followed. These are
  asked as facts:
  - the investigator's reasonable suspicion that I am under 18 (s 98);
  - whether the seller "had no reason to believe" I was under 18 (s 13(2)(a));
  - whether the therapeutic conditions are met.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed for "prohibited
  product", "tobacco product" (only at the level of the kind of item) and s 15(1)
  proof of age. Whether an item is a vaping product, or breaks a packaging
  requirement, is asked as the kind of item. The Commonwealth packaging law it
  borrows is not read.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one item, or one holding of
  one kind.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed.
- **Operative sections in, machinery out.** Followed. Licensing, the SAT,
  administration and investigations of premises are out.

## The lives

- **Jake, k031 (2 June 2023, aged 16): "Caught with a vape outside the campus; the
  shop that sold it is the one that offended."**
  - Decisions asked: `Is my possession an offence` and
    `Was the supply to me an offence`.
  - On the event's own date, both return "not answered". The deposited edition
    begins on 13 May 2026, and the 2023 text is not deposited.
  - On the same facts dated 2 June 2026:
    - possession of one vape is no offence, because it is within the
      (hypothetical) personal use quantity (s 30B(3));
    - the shop's sale is an offence under s 30A, not s 6;
    - the vape may be seized and must be destroyed (s 99(5)).

## Why this scope

The event turns on possession against sale. The plan names ss 6 and 106. The
minor's other natural questions are:

- may I buy it;
- what happens to what is found on me;
- what counts as proof of age.

These bring in ss 7, 12, 13 and 15, and ss 98-101. Everything else in the Act is
addressed to businesses or regulates places, and is left out:

- licensing;
- display and signage;
- advertising;
- smoking zones, including s 107D (smoking in a car with a child under 17);
- closure orders.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A minor's possession of a vape is an offence in the same way an adult's is, and
   age plays no part.** s 30B(2): "A person who possesses a prohibited product commits
   a crime", and a vaping product is a prohibited product (Glossary). The only escape
   is s 30B(3): no more than the prescribed personal use quantity. The same five vapes
   give the same answer at 16 and at 30. This contradicts the scenario's note "no
   possession offence for the minor". That note was true of tobacco, but under the
   deposited edition it is not true of vapes above the personal use quantity.
   Asserted.
2. **A minor's possession of ordinary tobacco is no offence at all.** No section
   makes possession of a non-illicit tobacco product or a smoking implement an
   offence. s 18AA reaches only possession "for the purposes of sale". Asserted.
3. **Selling a vape to me is an offence because selling a vape to anyone is, not
   because I am under 18.** s 6 reaches only "a tobacco product or a tobacco smoking
   implement". A vaping product is neither:
   - "tobacco product" excludes nicotine in a poison form;
   - vaping products are a separate defined class.

   So the offence for the k031 shop is s 30A (tier 4: up to 10 years and $600 000 on
   a first offence), not s 6. Illicit cigarettes sold to a minor offend both s 6 and
   s 30A. Asserted.
4. **s 106 does not reach a real vape.** It covers a "food, toy or other product that
   is not a tobacco product or a vaping product" designed to resemble one. The plan
   cited s 106 for the vape sale. Under the deposited edition it applies only to
   look-alikes. Asserted.
5. **Buying is not my offence, but lying about my age is.** No section punishes the
   minor for buying. But using another person's ID, or a forged one, to obtain tobacco
   is an offence with a $1 000 fine (s 15(2)). The adult who buys tobacco for me
   offends s 7. For a vape, the adult who hands it on "supplies" it (s 30A). Asserted.
6. **Proof of age is narrow.** Only these count, if they carry a photograph and show
   18 or over (s 15(1)):
   - a *current* passport;
   - a *current* Australian driver's licence;
   - a prescribed document.

   A student card or an expired licence does not count. The seller's s 13 defence
   fails outright if I was under 14, whatever ID I showed. Asserted.
7. **What is taken from me.** An investigator or police officer may seize the item
   only after asking for a s 15(1) document and not being shown one (s 99(3)). Police
   may use reasonable force. A seized vape or illicit tobacco is destroyed (s 99(5)).
   Legal tobacco or an implement is returned only if, within 7 days, I prove I was 18
   at the time; otherwise it is destroyed (s 99(4)). So for a real minor it is always
   destroyed. Asserted.
8. **From 14, I must answer.** If I am 14 or older, I must give:
   - my name and address;
   - my parent's name and address;
   - who supplied me.

   Refusing or lying carries a fine of up to $1 000 (s 100(2)). A parent may be told,
   and given an approved guide (s 101). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- **The Tobacco Products Control Regulations are not deposited.** Their personal use
  quantity decides whether a minor with one or two vapes has committed a crime. The
  figures used in the cases are placeholders.
- **The Act as it stood on 2 June 2023 is not deposited.** Jake's event cannot be
  answered for its own date. That needs the pre-2026 compilation, and probably the
  Medicines and Poisons Act 2014 (WA), which then governed nicotine vapes.
- **The Commonwealth tobacco packaging and product requirements are not read.**
  Whether a packet is "illicit" is asked, not worked out.
- **No case law or enforcement guidance was searched.** In particular, whether
  police in practice charge minors under s 30B was not checked.
- **Consistency with the Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 baseline.** It uses the same
  placeholder commercial quantity (60). It also treats the minor's personal-use
  possession as no federal offence below the commercial quantity. The WA personal
  use quantity is a separate line, set by WA regulations that are not deposited.
  Its relation to the Commonwealth figure is not known here.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added 6 November 2026 (batch qa11), following the graffiti model
(`../../../graffiti-vandalism-act-2016/encodings/baseline-2026-10/`, D2, D4, D8). The baseline files are unchanged;
the layer is added beside them: `topca-qa.l4` (imports `prelude` and `topca-minor`) and `topca-qa-cases.l4` (which
also imports `daydate`, for the dates in its cases, as the baseline cases do).

### Roles

Every exported question takes `my role` IS A `Role` first ("Which of these describes you"), and answers
`does not arise for you` for a role it is not about without reading any other input.

| Role | Why | Questions |
|---|---|---|
| `I am under 18, and have or want tobacco, a smoking implement or a vape` | the baseline's seat | 18 |
| `I sell or supply tobacco products, smoking implements or vapes` | ss 6, 12, 13, 15(1), 30A and 106 are the seller's | 8 |
| `I am a parent or other adult asked to buy, give or pass on tobacco or a vape for someone under 18` | ss 6, 7 and 30A reach the adult who buys for, gives to or passes on to a young person ("supply" includes giving); s 101 lets a parent be told | 5 |

Investigators and police officers are not seats. There are 25 questions; two (the edition, and whether an item is a
prohibited product) are asked of all three roles, one (s 101) of the young person and the adult, and one (the sale or
supply offence) of the seller and the adult.

### Situation inputs

Each is placed straight after `my role`; when it does not hold the question answers `does not arise for you` and reads
nothing else. A shared input has one name, type and `@desc`.

| Input | Type | Does not arise when | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `someone sold or supplied the item to me` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | Was selling or supplying this item to me an offence; Was selling me this look-alike an offence; What is the most the seller who sold to me can be fined under s 6; Does the seller who sold me tobacco have the s 13 defence |
| `who found the young person` | `Who found me` (the baseline's) | `someone else` | May the item be taken from me by an investigator or police officer; May force be used to take the item from me; Must I give my details when asked; What is the most I can be fined for refusing or lying when asked; May a parent be told of the finding (young person, adult) |
| `the item was taken from me` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | What happens to my item after it is taken |
| `I am said to have sold or supplied tobacco to someone under 18` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | Do I have the s 13 defence (seller) |

### Decisions

- **Who found the young person is the situation of ss 99-101.** The baseline's own `Who found me` enum is the input; only
  an investigator or a police officer (s 79(1)) has the powers, so `someone else` gives `does not arise for you`. The
  test reads the enum with EQUALS (no OTHERWISE, graffiti finding 31). Where it holds, the baseline is called with the
  same value, so its answers are unchanged.
- **The seller's and the adult's sale or supply question** builds the baseline's `My item` from the item, the day, the
  age of the person it goes to and the s 30A(3) conditions, with the quantities 0 (the sale and supply rule reads no
  quantity), and runs the baseline's `Was the supply to me an offence`. Mirror cases show it answers as the young
  person's question on the same facts.
- **The adult's words.** One of the four answers of the baseline's s 7 question speaks as the young person ("s 30A, by
  supplying it to me"). For the adult, a helper rewords that one answer "s 30A, by supplying it to the young person";
  the young person's question keeps the baseline's words.
- **The edition and prohibited-product questions** arise for every role and take no situation.
- **The young person's possession questions** take no situation: the role is someone with, or wanting, the item, and
  the baseline's `My item` record states it.
- **The seller's s 6 fine** takes the seller's own inputs ("I am a body corporate", "it would be my second or later
  offence"); the young person's question keeps the baseline's wording about the seller.

### Not covered for the new roles

- **Seller:** licensing (Pt 2 Div 2, Pt 4), possession for sale (s 18AA), display and signage
  (ss 22-26), advertising (Pt 3), prosecutions and closure orders; the seller's own s 30B possession.
- **Adult:** smoking in a car with a child under 17 (s 107A-107D, not in the baseline), and what a parent is told or
  given under s 101(b) (the approved guide).
