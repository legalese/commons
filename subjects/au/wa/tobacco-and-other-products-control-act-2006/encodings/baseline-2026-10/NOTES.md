## Seat

**The minor: a person who has not reached 18 years of age, found with, or trying to
get, a vape or tobacco.** The life event that cites this Act asks that person's
question: "Is my possession an offence, or only the sale?" Most of the Act speaks to
sellers, licensees, investigators and police. Those sections are encoded for what
they mean for the minor:

- whether the sale to me was an offence;
- whether the seller has a defence;
- whether my things may be taken, and whether I get them back;
- what I must tell an officer;
- whether my parent may be told.

## Why this scope

The event turns on possession against sale. The plan names ss 6 and 106. The
minor's other natural questions are:

- may I buy it;
- what happens to what is found on me;
- what counts as proof of age.

These bring in ss 7, 12, 13 and 15, and ss 98-101. Everything else in the Act is
addressed to businesses or regulates places, and is left out:

- licensing;
- display and signage;
- advertising;
- smoking zones, including s 107D (smoking in a car with a child under 17);
- closure orders.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A minor's possession of a vape is an offence in the same way an adult's is, and
   age plays no part.** s 30B(2): "A person who possesses a prohibited product commits
   a crime", and a vaping product is a prohibited product (Glossary). The only escape
   is s 30B(3): no more than the prescribed personal use quantity. The same five vapes
   give the same answer at 16 and at 30. This contradicts the scenario's note "no
   possession offence for the minor". That note was true of tobacco, but under the
   deposited edition it is not true of vapes above the personal use quantity.
   Asserted.
2. **A minor's possession of ordinary tobacco is no offence at all.** No section
   makes possession of a non-illicit tobacco product or a smoking implement an
   offence. s 18AA reaches only possession "for the purposes of sale". Asserted.
3. **Selling a vape to me is an offence because selling a vape to anyone is, not
   because I am under 18.** s 6 reaches only "a tobacco product or a tobacco smoking
   implement". A vaping product is neither:
   - "tobacco product" excludes nicotine in a poison form;
   - vaping products are a separate defined class.

   So the offence for the k031 shop is s 30A (tier 4: up to 10 years and $600 000 on
   a first offence), not s 6. Illicit cigarettes sold to a minor offend both s 6 and
   s 30A. Asserted.
4. **s 106 does not reach a real vape.** It covers a "food, toy or other product that
   is not a tobacco product or a vaping product" designed to resemble one. The plan
   cited s 106 for the vape sale. Under the deposited edition it applies only to
   look-alikes. Asserted.
5. **Buying is not my offence, but lying about my age is.** No section punishes the
   minor for buying. But using another person's ID, or a forged one, to obtain tobacco
   is an offence with a $1 000 fine (s 15(2)). The adult who buys tobacco for me
   offends s 7. For a vape, the adult who hands it on "supplies" it (s 30A). Asserted.
6. **Proof of age is narrow.** Only these count, if they carry a photograph and show
   18 or over (s 15(1)):
   - a *current* passport;
   - a *current* Australian driver's licence;
   - a prescribed document.

   A student card or an expired licence does not count. The seller's s 13 defence
   fails outright if I was under 14, whatever ID I showed. Asserted.
7. **What is taken from me.** An investigator or police officer may seize the item
   only after asking for a s 15(1) document and not being shown one (s 99(3)). Police
   may use reasonable force. A seized vape or illicit tobacco is destroyed (s 99(5)).
   Legal tobacco or an implement is returned only if, within 7 days, I prove I was 18
   at the time; otherwise it is destroyed (s 99(4)). So for a real minor it is always
   destroyed. Asserted.
8. **From 14, I must answer.** If I am 14 or older, I must give:
   - my name and address;
   - my parent's name and address;
   - who supplied me.

   Refusing or lying carries a fine of up to $1 000 (s 100(2)). A parent may be told,
   and given an approved guide (s 101). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- **The Tobacco Products Control Regulations are not deposited.** Their personal use
  quantity decides whether a minor with one or two vapes has committed a crime. The
  figures used in the cases are placeholders.
- **The Act as it stood on 2 June 2023 is not deposited.** Jake's event cannot be
  answered for its own date. That needs the pre-2026 compilation, and probably the
  Medicines and Poisons Act 2014 (WA), which then governed nicotine vapes.
- **The Commonwealth tobacco packaging and product requirements are not read.**
  Whether a packet is "illicit" is asked, not worked out.
- **No case law or enforcement guidance was searched.** In particular, whether
  police in practice charge minors under s 30B was not checked.
- **Consistency with the Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 baseline.** It uses the same
  placeholder commercial quantity (60). It also treats the minor's personal-use
  possession as no federal offence below the commercial quantity. The WA personal
  use quantity is a separate line, set by WA regulations that are not deposited.
  Its relation to the Commonwealth figure is not known here.
