# Public and Bank Holidays Act 1972 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Edition

- **The Act:** the compilation including amendments to the Industrial Relations
  Legislation Amendment Act 2021 Pt 6 (No. 30 of 2021, in force 12 February 2022),
  as deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_44571.txt` (retrieved
  7 September 2026).
- **No regulations.** The Act's own Second Schedule holds its dates.
- **The Sovereign's birthday is an input.** The Schedule leaves the "Celebration
  Day for the Anniversary of the Birthday of the Reigning Sovereign" to be
  "appointed for each year by proclamation published in the Government Gazette".
  No proclamation is deposited. The cases use 23 September 2024, 26 September 2022
  and 28 September 2026: these are the encoder's understanding of the proclaimed
  days, **not read in the deposit**. Elsewhere 28 September of the year is a
  neutral placeholder; no other holiday depends on it.
- **Proclamations under ss 7 and 8 are inputs**, stated by the employee from the
  Gazette.
- **Easter is computed, by an encoder's choice.** The Act names Good Friday, Easter
  Sunday and Easter Monday but does not say how Easter is fixed. The encoding uses
  the Gregorian computus (the Western churches' Easter).

## Seat

**The employee in Western Australia**, who asks: which days are public holidays
this year, is this day one for me, and does my award or the Act govern? The Act
addresses no person directly; it fixes days "throughout the State", and the person
it is applied to is the one who works, or does not, on them. The Governor (ss 7, 8)
and banks (s 10) are the actors it names; what they do is encoded as what it means
for the employee.

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed.
  The Schedule is in the Act. The yearly proclamation of the Sovereign's birthday
  is not deposited, so it is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the Governor, by proclamation; banks.
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Followed. Whether the
  Governor has proclaimed a special or substituted day, and whether it reaches the
  place where I work, are asked. So is whether an industrial instrument is
  "inconsistent" with the Act.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed: every Schedule
  day, and each weekend rule, is computed from the year. "Industrial instrument"
  (s 3(1)) is asked as whether the instrument was made under the Industrial
  Relations Act 1979. Easter is a borrowed term; the brief asked for it computed
  from the year, so it is computed and labelled as the encoder's choice.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one day, or one year.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed: "the Sovereign's birthday"
  is glossed as the King's Birthday; the weekend extra days carry the Schedule's
  "also".
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Followed, with three small
  departures: s 7(1)(b) half-holidays, the district limits of ss 7 and 8 beyond a
  "where I work" input, and the 3-week Gazette notice periods are left out.

## The lives

- **Chloe, c042 (6 May 2024): three months served.** The scenario asks "which days
  are public holidays in WA?", citing "s 3 and Schedule (Western Australia Day,
  Labour Day)". This Act answers it: in 2024 there are eleven days — 1 January,
  26 January, Labour Day 4 March, Good Friday 29 March, Easter Sunday 31 March,
  Easter Monday 1 April, Anzac Day 25 April, Western Australia Day 3 June, the
  King's Birthday (23 September, as input), 25 and 26 December. No weekend extra
  day arises in 2024. Whether she is paid for them is the Fair Work Act 2009
  s 116; this Act supplies the State-law limb of s 115(1)(b).

## Why this scope

The Act is ten short sections and a Schedule. The employee meets s 5 and the
Schedule every year, ss 7 and 8 whenever the Governor proclaims, s 3 if employed
under a WA award, and ss 6 and 10 if working in a bank. All are in. Ss 1, 2 and the
omitted s 4 and First Schedule are machinery.

**Consistency with the Fair Work Act baseline** (`../../../../fair-work-act-2009/encodings/baseline-2026-10/fwa-employee.l4`,
s 115): there, Western Australia Day and Labour Day count "if my State's law
declares it a public holiday". This encoding is that State law, and both days are
declared every year. That baseline's `Holiday` type has no Easter Sunday and no
weekend extra days; they reach a national-system employee through s 115(1)(b) as
days "declared or prescribed by or under a law of a State".

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Australia Day moves; the others double.** When 26 January falls on a weekend,
   the holiday is "the first Monday following" instead, and the 26th itself is no
   holiday. When New Year's Day, Anzac Day or Christmas Day falls on a weekend, the
   day itself stays a holiday and "the next following Monday is also" one.
   Asserted.
2. **Boxing Day has its own rule.** On a Saturday, the next Monday is also a
   holiday. On a Sunday or a Monday, the next Tuesday is. The Monday case matters
   when Christmas falls on a Sunday. Asserted.
3. **When Christmas falls on a Sunday, its extra Monday is Boxing Day itself.** The
   two land on 26 December, so the Schedule's answer is three days (25, 26, 27
   December), not four. Asserted (2022).
4. **Two holidays on one date give one day off.** Easter Monday fell on Anzac Day in
   2011, and the Monday after Anzac Day will be Easter Monday in 2038. The Schedule
   gives no further day for the clash; any extra day would need a proclamation
   under s 7 or 8. Asserted.
5. **Easter Sunday is a public holiday in its own right**, and s 3(3) stops a WA
   industrial instrument from moving it "solely because Easter Sunday falls on a
   weekend" — which, Easter Sunday being a Sunday, it always does. Under s 3(2) an
   instrument's public holiday terms reach Easter Sunday even if they do not name
   it. Asserted.
6. **Labour Day and Western Australia Day are the first Mondays of March and June.**
   "Monday on or first Monday following the 1st" puts them on the 1st when the 1st
   is a Monday (Labour Day 2027). Asserted.
7. **A WA award beats the Act; a proclamation beats a WA award.** s 3(1) lets an
   instrument under the Industrial Relations Act 1979 prevail to the extent of any
   inconsistency; s 9 makes a ss 7–8 proclamation prevail over the award. Neither
   speaks to a Fair Work Act instrument. Asserted.
8. **Every Saturday is a bank holiday**, but a bank need not close on a Saturday
   that is a bank holiday only by s 6. It must close on one that is also a Schedule
   holiday (Christmas Day 2021). Asserted.
9. **The Act does not fix the Sovereign's birthday at all.** The day is left to a
   yearly Gazette proclamation, so no year's full list can be computed from the Act
   alone. Asserted (as an input).
