## Seat

**The person given an infringement notice**, an individual. The Act calls them
"the alleged offender" (s 11). This is the person the Part 3 machinery is applied
to. Their question is "What happens if I do not pay?" The Registrar, the
prosecuting authority and the Sheriff are the actors who do things to that person.
Their powers are encoded as what can be done to me, and when.

How each 2026-10-18 default was applied:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The
  deposited regulations supply the fees, the remote-area test and the seizure
  limits. The principal enactment is not encoded: the modified penalty, the time
  to pay it, the road-traffic instalment scheme and demerit points are inputs or
  are left out.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the Registrar, the prosecuting
  authority, the Sheriff, the Magistrates Court and an approved sponsor.
- **Judgments handed to others answered conditionally.** Followed. These are
  inputs, never guessed:
  - the Registrar's satisfaction under s 20A(5) and s 21C(3);
  - the Registrar's "reasonable grounds to believe" under s 19(2A)(b);
  - the Court's satisfaction under s 101AA(7), asked as the fact the person would
    state: "I received none of the notices".
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed for "remote area"
  (r 2B, from the address's ASGS class and postcode), "unpaid infringement amount"
  and "registered". Borrowed terms are asked as facts: the ASGS class, the
  metropolitan region and the time limit for prosecuting.
- **One case is one infringement notice.** Followed. s 17(2A) and s 17A(2) say an
  order relates to only one notice.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a gloss.** Followed.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Part 2 (the Registry),
  s 13 (approving authorities), the regulation-making power and the transitional
  Parts are out.

One departure: s 4A, s 28(1) and ss 46A to 46C belong to Part 4 (court fines), but
they are encoded. The seat asks about work and development permits, and the
honest answer to that question needs Part 4 (finding 1).

## Why this scope

The plan names Part 3 (infringement notices), and the seat names the steps of
non-payment. Part 3 is encoded nearly whole, with Schedule 2 Division 1.

From Part 7, only two provisions are taken:

- s 68B with s 101AA, the 14-day right to have a warrant cancelled;
- s 75 with r 8, what the Sheriff cannot take.

The rest of Part 7 is procedure the person does not decide anything about:
vehicle clamping, number-plate removal, garnishee orders, land and sale. It is out.

Part 4 enters only to show that a work and development permit is a court-fine
remedy. Publication of names (Part 4A) and the court-fine regime are left for a
later encoding of Part 4.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A work and development permit is not available for an infringement notice.**
   s 46A lets an offender discharge "the amount owed in respect of a fine". s 28(1)
   defines "fine" as a penalty "imposed on an offender by a court". s 46C(1)-(2)
   requires "an enforceable registered fine" and a s 42 (Part 4) notice of
   intention to enforce. Part 3 has no permit. An unpaid infringement notice
   becomes permit-eligible only if the person elects, is convicted and is fined.
   For a fine, eligibility is hardship (s 46B, s 4A). A mentoring programme is
   open to anyone under 25 (s 46A(e)). Asserted.
2. **"Remote" includes Inner Regional Australia, and it bars licence suspension
   but not a warrant.** r 2B(2) counts Inner Regional, Outer Regional, Remote and
   Very Remote areas, and their postcodes, as remote, except the metropolitan
   region and any postcode it shares. s 19(2A)(a) forbids a licence suspension
   order at a remote last known address. s 20(2A) requires one to be cancelled on
   notice of a remote current address. s 21A has no such bar, so the same person
   can be sent straight to a warrant. Asserted.
3. **The right to elect to go to court is easy to lose.** The final demand offers
   it (s 14(4)(b)), and s 21(1) keeps it open after registration. It is lost:
   - once "any part" of the penalty and fees is paid, even by a dishonoured
     payment (s 21(7));
   - once a time to pay order is made;
   - once an enforcement warrant is issued.

   A road-traffic instalment payer never has it under this Act: s 14A(4)(d) says
   so, and s 21(1)(a) covers only s 14 final demands. Asserted.
4. **The fees climb.** On top of the penalty, the fees are:
   - $28.30 at the final demand;
   - $142.90 once the notice is registered ($28.30 + $24.10 + $90.50);
   - $202.40 with a licence suspension order;
   - $484.40 with an enforcement warrant.

   The $59.50 fee for the notice of intention to enforce is charged only when a
   suspension order is made or a warrant issued, "but not twice". The Sheriff's
   Division 3 fees and disbursements come on top and are not encoded. Asserted.
5. **The registration fee is charged even when police issued the notice (a
   reading).** r 10 exempts the Commissioner of Police and other listed
   authorities "from payment of the fee for registering". r 9(1) imposes the
   Division 1 fees "on an alleged offender" without exception, so the encoding
   charges the $90.50 whoever the authority is. The text does not say outright
   that an exempt authority's notice carries no fee. Asserted as encoded.
6. **For a road-traffic notice, the order to pay or elect counts as a
   conviction.** s 23 says an order to pay or elect is not a conviction "except as
   provided in sections 25 and 26". s 26(2) deems a conviction for the Road
   Traffic (Authorisation to Drive) Act 2008 s 16(1)(a). Asserted.
7. **A time to pay order is mandatory on three findings, and it halts
   enforcement.** s 21C(3) requires the Registrar to make one if he is satisfied
   of three things: the person cannot pay within 28 days, the offer is reasonable,
   and there is no unexcused earlier breach. Once it is made:
   - the Registrar must cancel any licence suspension order (s 21H(1));
   - he may not issue a notice of intention to enforce, a suspension order or a
     warrant (s 21H(2)).

   The person cannot apply once a warrant is in force or after electing
   (s 21B(3)(b)). The decision is final (s 27A). Asserted.
8. **A warrant can be undone only by proving that no notice ever arrived.** The
   person must apply within 14 days of the Sheriff's s 68B notice (s 101AA(3)),
   and only once (s 101AA(4)). The Court may cancel only if the person received
   none of the five documents, from the infringement notice to the notice
   confirming suspension (s 101AA(7)). Asserted.
9. **Little is protected from seizure.** Under s 75 and r 8, these are protected:
   - clothing to $1,250;
   - kitchen furniture to $1,250;
   - the debtor's bedding to $500;
   - entertainment electrical goods to $300;
   - tools of trade to $2,500;
   - photographs, medical aids and trust property, whatever their value.

   A car is not protected. Reading: an item over its cap loses the protection.
   Asserted.
10. **Part 3 does not reach anyone under 17 at the time of the offence**
    (s 12(3)). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The principal enactment needs encoding. The Road Traffic (Administration) Act
  2008 sets the modified penalty, the time to pay it, the instalment scheme and
  the election before a final demand. Here they are inputs.
- The registration-fee reading (finding 5) should be checked against Registry
  practice.
- Several parts are not encoded:
  - s 20B, the Registrar's request for information;
  - ss 21D-21G, amending and cancelling time to pay orders;
  - s 101, the court cancelling a suspension order for non-receipt;
  - s 101B, appeals;
  - s 22(5)(b)-(e), refunds after seizure;
  - the vehicle and garnishee powers of Part 7, including the protected earnings
    amount, which needs the published WA Wage Price Index.
- Part 4 (court fines, work and development permits and orders, fine expiation,
  warrant of commitment inquiries) deserves its own encoding from the seat of the
  offender.
- No decided cases were searched.
