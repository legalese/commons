## Seat

**The accused person (Jake at 15, charged with stealing), and the victim of threats and criminal damage (Jake at 29).**
The plan entry names both seats, because the two events put Jake on opposite sides of the Code.
At 15 the Code is applied to him: whether he can be responsible at his age, whether what he did is stealing, and what he can be made to suffer for it.
At 29 the Code is applied to the people at his door, and he is the person it protects. A provision that addresses only the offender is encoded as what it means for him: what offence was committed against him, and how far the offender is exposed.
Questions are phrased in each seat ("Can I be criminally responsible at my age", "Did I steal it", "What threat offence was committed against me", "What is the most imprisonment for the damage").

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. No instrument sets a number used.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the offender and the victim. The court appears only through the judgments it is handed.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are asked as facts:
  - whether capacity to know is "proved" (s 29);
  - whether a taking was fraudulent on grounds outside the s 371(2) intents;
  - what a statement or behaviour "may reasonably be regarded as" threatening (s 338);
  - whether paint or glue "damages" property (s 444);
  - whether the court sends a charge for trial on indictment (s 5(3)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out: "capable of being stolen" (s 370, the inanimate-thing paragraphs), "steal" (s 371), "circumstances of aggravation" (s 221), "circumstances of racial aggravation" (s 80I), "threat" (s 338). Asked, because they are borrowed: "family relationship" and the restraining order (Restraining Orders Act 1997), dangerous driving (Road Traffic Act 1974), "firearm" (Firearms Act 2024).
  One departure, for size: the five animal paragraphs of s 370 are asked as a single fact.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one thing taken (`What I did with the thing`, `How I stole it`), one threat, one act of damage.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments; field names are in the speaker's words and tied to their subsections.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed, but the Code is very large, so "operative" is narrowed to what the two events and the seat need. See below.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names ss 371, 378, 338B and 444. The brief adds s 29.
Answering "what offence is this?" for each event also needs:

- s 370 (what can be stolen), s 7 (the two friends) and s 22 (the excuse that most often meets a stealing charge);
- ss 426 and 426A and s 5, because for $89 the real exposure is the summary conviction penalty, not the 7 years;
- s 338 (the definition s 338B uses) and s 338A, because a threat made to collect a debt is s 338A before it is s 338B;
- ss 221 and 80I, which set the aggravated penalties in ss 338A, 338B and 444;
- s 445, the listed alternative to s 444.

Not encoded, in a very large Code:

- attempts and incitement (s 4, ss 552-555A) and common purpose (s 8);
- the other excuses in Chapter V (accident, mistake, duress and the rest) beyond ss 22 and 29;
- the offences analogous to stealing and the other alternatives to s 378 (ss 379-424, 429), robbery (s 392), receiving (s 414), and stealing a motor vehicle by use (s 371A);
- s 371(3), (4), (7) beyond comments; ss 372-376;
- false threats (s 338C), stalking (s 338E), and the rest of Parts V and VI;
- ss 444A and 445A;
- sentencing (the Sentencing Act 1995; the Young Offenders Act 1994) and procedure (the Criminal Procedure Act 2004);
- the Criminal Code Act Compilation Act's own sections (short title, the civil-remedies saving).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Being stopped at the door does not make it an attempt.** s 371(6) completes stealing when the thing is "actually" moved "by some physical act", and s 371(3) says the taking may be fraudulent "without secrecy". Jake had moved the goods. The scenario's "stealing" is right. Asserted.
2. **At 15, s 29 gives no protection.** It protects only those under 10 entirely, and those under 14 unless capacity "is proved". Jake is past both. Asserted, with the boundary on the 14th birthday.
3. **For $89 the Code's real figure is a fine of $6 000, not 7 years.** s 378 makes stealing a crime carrying 7 years. But s 426(4) gives a summary conviction penalty of "a fine of $6 000" where the value does not exceed $1 000, and s 5(2) says the court "is to try the charge summarily" unless it decides otherwise under s 5(3). Asserted. This is the 2026 text; see Edition.
4. **Two earlier stealing convictions within a year end the fine-only penalty.** s 426A (from 1 July 2024) withdraws s 426(4) where the person has at least 2 stealing convictions in the year before. Convictions for offences on the same day count once. A finding of guilt under the Young Offenders Act 1994 s 55 counts even without a recorded conviction. The penalty then rises to 2 years and $24 000. Asserted.
5. **The fine-only penalty reaches further than s 426(1).** s 426(4) is stated for "an offence under section 378" worth $1 000 or less, without the s 426(1) limits. Read literally, $500 taken from a forced cash box (14 years on indictment, s 378(5)(f)) has the fine-only summary penalty, while $3 000 from the same box has no summary penalty at all. This is the reading encoded; it is an inference from the words. Asserted.
6. **Stealing with friends is not aggravated stealing.** No s 378 circumstance turns on company. Each of the three is a principal offender under s 7 if he took, aided, enabled, or counselled or procured; mere presence is not on s 7's list (an inference). Asserted.
7. **Where several s 378 special cases apply, the Code does not say which wins.** The encoding takes the highest (an inference). Asserted.
8. **An honest claim of right leaves the definition met but removes responsibility.** s 22 says the person "is not criminally responsible"; it does not say there was no stealing. Asserted.
9. **A painted demand for a debt is s 338A, not only s 338B.** s 338 counts "behaviour" that "may reasonably be regarded" as a threat, including a threat to "cause a detriment of any kind". s 338A applies to a threat made "with intent to ... compel the doing of an act by a person who is lawfully entitled to abstain". The household is entitled not to pay a friend's debt. So, if the paint was a demand, it is s 338A (7 years), with s 338B (3 years) as the listed alternative. The scenario cites s 338B. s 338A has no summary conviction penalty, so it is indictable only. Asserted, on the assumed reading of the paint.
10. **A child at home aggravates the threat and the damage.** s 221(1)(b) makes it a circumstance of aggravation that "a child was present", and s 444(1A) borrows that meaning. With Ruby (3) at home, the s 338A maximum rises from 7 to 10 years and the s 444 maximum from 10 to 14. s 221(1A) removes this where the offender was also a child and it is the only circumstance. Asserted.
11. **Glue in a lock is criminal damage if a court calls it damage.** s 444 punishes whoever "wilfully and unlawfully destroys or damages any property": 10 years, or summarily 3 years and $36 000 where the injury is $50 000 or less. Without wilfulness, s 445 (damaging another's property without consent, 2 years and $24 000) is the alternative. Whether paint and glue are "damage" is for the court and is asked. Asserted.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added 6 November 2026 (batch qa13), on the pattern of the Graffiti Vandalism Act 2016 encoding's `gva-seat.l4` (its
design D2, D4 and D8). The layer is `cc-qa.l4`, with cases in `cc-qa-cases.l4`. It imports `prelude`, `daydate` and
`cc-offences`; `cc-offences.l4` and `cc-cases-offences.l4` are unchanged. Every exported question takes `my role`
first (`@desc Which of these describes you`) and answers `does not arise for you`, reading nothing else, for the role
it is not about. Every answer type carries that value: `Yes or no`, `Years`, `Amount`, and wrappers round the
baseline's `Responsibility at my age`, `Maximum imprisonment`, `Summary penalty`, `Threat offence` and `Damage
offence`. No exported question takes a DEONTIC or EVENT input.

### Roles

The baseline already has two seats, the accused (k021) and the victim (k063). They are the two roles here, and each now
asks about all three offences the baseline encodes.

- `I am accused of an offence, or did the act`: 16 questions. Ten are the baseline's: s 29 twice, s 7, and the seven
  stealing questions. Six are new from this seat, the threat and the damage the person is accused of: what offence,
  the longest imprisonment and the summary penalty, for each (ss 338, 338A, 338B; ss 444, 445; with ss 221 and 80I).
- `an offence was committed against me or my property`: 8 questions. Seven are the baseline's: s 221, the three
  threat questions and the three damage questions. One is new: whether what was done with my thing was stealing
  (ss 370, 371).
- Not seats: the police, the prosecutor and the court. What they must prove or may decide (capacity under s 29, "may
  reasonably be regarded", "damages", s 5(3)) is asked as a fact, as in the baseline.

### Situation input

One input, shared by both roles with one name, type and `@desc`: `what the offence is about` (`What the offence is
about`), placed second. Its values are `a thing taken or kept (stealing)`, `a threat` and `damage to property`.

| Questions | Arise for | Do not arise for |
|---|---|---|
| the accused's 7 stealing questions; the victim's "Was what was done with my thing stealing" | stealing | `a threat`, `damage to property` |
| the accused's 3 threat questions; the victim's 3 threat questions | a threat | stealing, `damage to property` |
| the accused's 3 damage questions; the victim's 3 damage questions | damage | stealing, `a threat` |
| the victim's s 221 aggravation question | a threat, damage | stealing |

The accused's s 29 and s 7 questions arise whatever the offence, so they take no situation input. The situation is
read only through four helpers in the layer, which list every value with no OTHERWISE.

### Decisions

- **Q1. The situation is the offence, not a yes/no per offence.** One person meets one charge at a time, and the
  baseline's questions divide cleanly by the offence they are about, so one shared enumeration settles every question
  at once.
- **Q2. s 221 does not arise for stealing.** It defines aggravation "in this Part" (Part V, which has the threats), and
  s 444(1A) borrows it. Stealing's special cases are s 378's own, which the accused's s 378 question already reads.
- **Q3. New questions from the other side use the baseline's records, built from plain inputs.** The layer has helpers
  that build `The circumstances`, `The threat made to me` and `The damage done to my property` from the accused's
  inputs, and `What I did with the thing` from the owner's. A question that does not read part of a record passes a
  neutral value for it, with a comment: what offence the accused committed, and the summary penalty, pass no
  aggravation. The damage maximum passes $0 of injury, which only the summary penalty reads.
- **Q4. The owner's stealing question asks the six s 371(2) intents as one fact,** carried in the first intent field.
  Any one of them makes the taking fraudulent, and nothing in `Did I steal it` tells them apart. "The property of any
  person" is TRUE by the question's own terms. s 22 is not read by `Did I steal it`, so it is passed as FALSE.
- **Q5. Shared inputs have one text.** The accused's threat and damage questions share the six circumstance inputs
  (`I am in a family relationship with the victim` and the rest), so the form asks them once. `the date of the act`
  has one text in both s 29 questions and the stealing guilt question.

### Not covered for the new questions

- The victim of a stealing: the thief's maximum imprisonment and summary penalty (ss 378, 426, 426A). They turn on the
  baseline's 18-field `How I stole it`, written from the thief's side. Asking it from the owner's side would need its
  own helper, and was left out.
- The accused of a threat or damage: s 5 (summary or indictment) for those offences, s 338C, s 338E, s 444A, s 445A.
- Sentencing and the Young Offenders Act 1994, as in the baseline.
