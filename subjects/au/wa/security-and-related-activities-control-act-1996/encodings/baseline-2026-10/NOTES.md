## Seat

**The security officer and licence applicant (Jake, at 27, joining Sentinel Security Services Pty Ltd).**
The Act is applied to the person doing the work. It decides:

- whether the work makes them a security officer;
- which licence the work needs;
- whether a past matter makes them a prohibited person;
- whether a licence may be issued, for how long, and how it is renewed;
- what they must do while working and afterwards;
- what can be done to the licence.

Every exported question is phrased as that person would ask it ("May I do this work without a security officer's licence?", "Am I a prohibited person on this day?", "Can I be issued a security officer's licence?").
Several provisions are addressed to an official: the licensing officer (ss 52, 55, 57), the Commissioner (s 67A, s 80) or the employer (s 20). Each is encoded as what it means for the officer. That covers whether the licence may be issued, renewed, revoked or suspended; what notice and reasons they must be given, and by when; and whether they may be put on the roster.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The Regulations 1997 set the disqualifying periods, the course, the examination, the fees and the modified penalties, and are used as deposited.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the applicant or licensee, the licensing officer, the Commissioner, the security agent (employer), the State Administrative Tribunal, the court.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are asked as facts:
  - "good character and ... fit and proper" (s 52(1)(c));
  - "extenuating circumstances" (s 52(1)(cb));
  - whether the applicant will be able to comply with conditions (s 52(1)(e));
  - "no other good reason" (s 52(1)(k)) and "not ... contrary to the public interest" (s 52A(2));
  - the s 53 training exemption;
  - "such later time as the licensing officer ... allows" (s 49(3));
  - the grounds in the officer's opinion, and "appropriate", under s 55;
  - the Commissioner's conclusions under s 67A.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out from facts:
  - "security officer" (s 12, with the in-house exception);
  - the other Part 3-5 definitions, far enough to say which licence a job needs;
  - "employment" (s 4);
  - "finding of guilt" (s 4B);
  - "prohibited person" (s 4A with reg 25).

  "Disqualifying offence" is the Act's term, but which Division of Schedule 2 an offence falls in means classifying the whole Criminal Code by maximum penalty. It is asked as a fact, and the comments list the entries (a departure, for size).
  Whether a matter is spent (s 4B(3), "permitted not to disclose" under any law) is a borrowed question and is asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one job (`My work`), one past matter (`My past matter`, gathered into a list for "am I a prohibited person"), one application (`My application`).
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments. Field names use the applicant's words, each tied to its provision.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out:
  - Part 2 (administration, secrecy, the register);
  - the parallel inquiry and crowd control licensing in Parts 4 and 5 (only their definitions are used);
  - Part 6 (armed bodyguards);
  - licences held for partnerships and companies (ss 44, 45, 56);
  - records (ss 78, 84-87);
  - the drug-testing procedure;
  - regulation-making.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names s 12 (security officer), s 15 and s 16. The seat asks:

- may I work unlicensed;
- which licence do I need;
- what makes me eligible, and which offences disqualify me (with spent and youth matters);
- how long the licence lasts and how it is renewed.

So the encoding covers:

- the Part 3 definitions and offences, with the neighbouring definitions only far enough to say which licence a job needs;
- the employment rules that decide whether a licensed officer may work for a given business (ss 19, 20);
- ss 4A and 4B with regs 24 and 25, for eligibility;
- the issue test (ss 52, 52A, 53 with regs 16 and 17);
- the term and renewal (ss 42A, 49, 55, 57, 60);
- the fees;
- the conditions and duties a working officer meets every shift (Sch 1 cl 7, 9, 11B, ss 61, 77, 77A);
- what can happen to the licence, and review (ss 67A, 79B, 80, 72).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. A security officer is anyone paid to watch, guard or protect property (s 12(1)). The exception in s 12(2) is narrow. It covers a person employed by one employer only, not a security agent, to guard that employer's own property, and it is lost if they carry a firearm or baton. Asserted.
2. Working as a security officer without the licence is an offence with a fine of up to $15 000 (s 16), or a $500 infringement notice (Sch 5 item 2). Asserted. Police and Defence Force members on official duty, public officers on official duty, court security, custodial and prison contract workers, and holders of a temporary licence need none (s 5, regs 5A, 5B, s 42A(3)). Asserted for police on duty.
3. A licensed officer may act only "as an employee of a security agent" (s 19), meaning under a contract of service or an apprenticeship (s 4). A guard working as an independent contractor breaches s 19 even when licensed. Asserted. The test is the definition of a security agent (one who supplies officers' services), not whether the agent is licensed. The unlicensed agent's breach is its own (ss 15, 20).
4. The security contractor in d04b needs a security agent's licence (ss 11, 15). Only a natural person can hold a licence, so one of the company's officers holds it on the company's behalf (s 43). The scenario cites ss 12-13, which define security officer and security consultant. Asserted.
5. A finding of guilt (s 4B(1)) is always a court's act: a formal finding, a conviction, an accepted plea of guilty, or a mental impairment finding. A police caution or a juvenile justice team referral is none of these, so (an inference) Jake's 2021 matter is not a finding of guilt. Asserted. A finding later quashed does not count (s 4B(2)(a)). Asserted.
6. A spent conviction still counts (s 4B(2)(b)), but it shortens the period. A Division 1 finding disqualifies for 10 years, or 5 if spent (reg 25(2)). Asserted. Whether a matter is spent is decided by "a law in any jurisdiction" (s 4B(3)). A youth conviction that the Young Offenders Act 1994 s 189 treats as spent would therefore take the 5 years. That is an inference, and the Act says nothing more about youth matters.
7. A Division 2 offence (which includes plain stealing under Criminal Code s 378) disqualifies for 5 years. It does not disqualify at all if it came from the person's first Division 2 incident and the total penalty was under $500 (reg 25(3)). Asserted. Findings before 1 January 1996 never count (s 4A(2)). Asserted.
8. Section 52 is written as a bar: the officer "is not to issue a licence ... unless" satisfied of each limb. Nothing says the officer must issue, so the best answer the encoding can give is that the officer may. Asserted. The limbs for a security officer's licence are:
   - identity;
   - age 18;
   - good character and fit and proper;
   - not a prohibited person;
   - extenuating circumstances if a disqualifying charge is pending;
   - able to comply with conditions;
   - the course (unless monitoring-only duties, reg 16(1A), or a s 53 exemption);
   - the written examination (reg 17(1)(f), with no monitoring exception);
   - a medical examination for a firearm endorsement;
   - the public interest (s 52A(2)).

   Asserted.
9. The s 53 training exemption is unavailable to someone applying for a firearm or baton endorsement (s 53(3)). Asserted.
10. Section 60 sets 3 years (or less on request, or a special event) for a licence issued or renewed "except for the first issue". The Act never states the first term. Schedule 4 prices a new licence for up to 1 year or up to 3 years, so 3 years is the most the fees contemplate (an inference). Asserted. A temporary licence for an interstate resident runs at most 1 year and cannot be renewed (s 42A(2)). Asserted.
11. Renewal must be applied for at least 28 days before expiry, unless the officer allows later (s 49(3)). Asserted. A refused renewal must be notified, with reasons, at least 14 days before expiry (s 57(2)), and a refused issue within 28 days (s 57(1)). Asserted. A prohibited person cannot be renewed (s 55(1)(b)). Despite a ground of a minor kind (s 67(1a)(c)-(e)), the officer may still renew if the officer thinks that appropriate (s 55(2)); without that view, no renewal. Asserted.
12. Fees from 1 July 2026 (Schedule 4, SL 2026/115): a new licence for up to 3 years costs $434, or $405 for a year or less. Renewal is $272, and fingerprints add $135. Asserted for Jake.
13. Every security officer's licence carries conditions (Sch 1 Div 2):
    - a current first aid certificate (cl 11B);
    - an approved uniform, except in retail loss prevention or an approved escort (cl 7);
    - no weapon other than an authorised firearm or baton (cl 9).

    Asserted. Breach is an offence under s 64, with a fine of up to $15 000 or a $500 infringement notice (Sch 5 item 21).
14. A licensee must report in writing within 7 days a charge for a disqualifying offence, or a finding of guilt for any offence (s 77A(1)(b) is not limited to disqualifying offences). Asserted.
15. A licensee who becomes a prohibited person loses every licence when the Commissioner's notice is received (s 67A(1)(b)). A pending disqualifying charge without extenuating circumstances brings suspension of every licence and referral to the Tribunal within 14 days (s 67A(2)(c), (4)). Asserted for the suspension. A court sentencing for a disqualifying offence cannot itself revoke under s 69 (s 69(1a)).
16. Random drug testing (Part 9) reaches only crowd controllers and security officers with a firearm or baton endorsement or a firearm permit (s 79B). An unendorsed officer like Jake is outside it. Asserted.
17. Refusing a training exemption is not a reviewable decision under s 72(2). Asserted. Refusing, revoking, suspending, the period and conditions are reviewable.
18. The scenario cites s 8 (secrecy) for k060. That section binds people who administer the Act, not the applicant, so it is not encoded.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Schedule 2 classifies offences by Criminal Code maximum penalties. An encoding that worked the Division out from the offence would need the Criminal Code's penalties.
- How s 4B(3) "spent" meets the Spent Convictions Act 1988 and the Young Offenders Act 1994 ss 189-190 should be encoded in those Acts and imported, not asked.
- Only a security officer's licence is worked through the issue test. The other licence types share s 52, but differ in courses, examinations and age (s 52(1)(b) sets 18 only for agent's, security officer's and crowd controller's licences).
- The State Administrative Tribunal's powers (s 67), the court's powers (s 69) and the endorsement cancellation procedure (s 68) are named, not worked out.
- There has been no independent test pass and no human gate. The fixture facts the scenario does not state (course, examination, no baton) are assumptions.

### Roles

`my role` (`Role`, "Which of these describes you") is the first input of every question. A question about another role answers `does not arise for you` and reads nothing else.

- **`I work, or want to work, as a security officer`**: the baseline's seat (Jake, k060). It has 31 questions, one for each baseline export. Each calls the baseline's function and wraps the answer: `yes if` for a BOOLEAN, `amount` or `disqualified for` for a number, and `the answer is` for the baseline's own enumerations.
- **`I run a business that supplies security officers to others`**: a security agent (s 11). The Act puts four duties on it:
  - s 15: its own licence, held for a company by a director or officer (s 43);
  - s 19: the licensed officers it supplies must be its employees;
  - s 20: it must not employ an unlicensed officer;
  - s 21: it must not advertise without the licence.

  It has 6 questions.
- **`I hire or employ security for my own property`**: the client (d04b's Jarrah). It meets three provisions:
  - s 12(2): an in-house guard is not a security officer unless the guard has another employer or carries a firearm or baton;
  - s 20: such a guard may be employed only with a licence;
  - s 15: the firm it hires must be licensed.

  It has 5 questions.

The licensing officer, the Commissioner, the State Administrative Tribunal and the courts are not seats.

### Decisions

- **One input with two meanings of not arising.** The licence input serves both kinds of question. The s 20 question takes it inverted, because it asks about the time before issue. Its name, type and @desc are the same as everywhere else, so the form asks it once.
- **The offence input is shared by two roles.** The officer and the business use the same input, word for word.
- **Two helpers read the client's situation.** They are `I employ my own guard, in` and `I hire a security firm, in`. Each lists every value, with no OTHERWISE (graffiti finding 31).
- **The client's s 12 question runs the baseline's test.** It builds a `My work` record (`the work of my own guard`) and runs the baseline's `Am I a security officer` on it. It does not restate s 12.
- **The business's s 19 question runs the baseline's s 19 function.** It passes the role's facts: the officer is licensed, and the business supplies officers' services.
- **New helpers give the business's fines.** s 15, s 20 and s 21 each carry $15 000. The modified penalty is $500 under Sch 5 Div 1, items 1, 7 and 8. The fine is the one each section states. Whether the Sentencing Act 1995 sets a higher maximum for a body corporate is not in the deposit and is not encoded.
