# Long Service Leave Act 1958 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent
test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

One module, `lsl-employee.l4`, holds every rule. Each question is a function named as the
employee would ask it and is published with `@export`, with `@desc` on every input. One
case file, `lsl-cases-employee.l4`, is generated by a script (scratchpad
`gen_wa_long-service-leave-act-1958_baseline.py`) whose expected values were written by
hand from the text.

There was no first-pass (`naive-2026-10`) encoding of this Act to replace.

## Edition

- **Act:** Long Service Leave Act 1958 (WA), No. 44 of 1958, the compilation including
  amendments to the Industrial Relations Legislation Amendment Act 2024 Pt 4 Div 3 (No. 43
  of 2024, in force 31 January 2025). It was retrieved on 7 September 2026 and is deposited
  at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_48247.txt`.
- **Regulations:** none used. The Act sets every number encoded here. The one prescribed
  rate, for board and lodging (s 7C(2)(b)), is asked as an input.

## Seat

**The employee.** The plan's question is "What long service leave have I earned, and what
do I lose on changing employer?" Both life events that cite the Act ask it from the
employee's side: Liam leaving his employer (c056), and Chloe leaving Ironbark (c072). The
Act's duties fall on the employer (grant the leave, pay on termination, keep and show
records). Each is encoded as what the employee may demand: how many weeks, how many
dollars, when the leave may be taken, and by when the records must be shown.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. No regulation is
  needed for any figure encoded.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the employee, the employer, the old and new
  employer on a transfer of business, and a person the employee authorises (s 26A).
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** Followed. Two inputs are
  named "if ...":
  - "if that entitlement is at least equivalent to the Act's" (s 4A(4));
  - "if the dismissal was made to avoid long service leave or annual leave" (s 6(2)(a),
    which s 11(1)(d) gives to the industrial magistrate's court).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed. These are
  worked out from facts:
  - continuous employment and its length (ss 6, 6A);
  - ordinary pay (ss 7-7C);
  - transfer of business and connection (ss 7E, 7G);
  - separate LSL entitlement (s 4A(2), (3)).

  These are asked as facts:
  - whether a person is an employee (the s 4(1) note points to IR Act ss 7A, 7B);
  - serious misconduct;
  - related body corporate (the Corporations Act).
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one employment with one employer, or
  one change of employer.
- **The Act's own terms, glossed.** Followed in every `@desc`.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. The industrial magistrate's court,
  inspectors, representation and regulation-making (ss 11, 12, 37-39) are out.

**Departures.**

- **Length of service is asked in years**, as a number already counted under s 6A, not
  computed from dates. The s 6 and s 6A questions tell the employee which absences to
  leave out.
- **A transmission of business before 20 June 2022 is reported, not answered.** For
  those, s 41 keeps the former s 6, which is not in the deposit. The date is read from the
  compilation table: IRLA 2021 Pt 4, which contains s 84, commenced on 20 June 2022.

**Consistency with the Fair Work Act baseline.** That encoding
(`../../../../fair-work-act-2009/encodings/baseline-2026-10/`) leaves long service leave
out and points c056 and c072 to this Act. This encoding is the other half, and it agrees
with that one: neither the Fair Work Act's s 22(5) (service) nor this Act's ss 7E-7H
carries Liam's service to an unconnected employer. Whether Fair Work Act s 113 gives
either employee long service leave under a pre-2010 award instead was not read here. The
s 4A question asks it as a fact (another law giving an at least equivalent entitlement).

## Why this scope

The Act is short, so the encoding takes nearly all of its operative provisions an
employee meets:

- who it applies to;
- continuity and its length;
- ordinary pay;
- service carried over on a transfer of business;
- the entitlement and payment on termination;
- taking leave;
- cashing out;
- leave in advance;
- forfeiture for working during leave;
- access to records.

Left out:

- the 2006 transitional rules in s 8(4)-(10), which matter only for employees with 9 to
  15 years' service before 4 July 2006;
- s 7(3), the rate for leave postponed by agreement;
- s 7I, the transfer of records between employers;
- s 26, the employer's record-keeping duties, beyond what s 26A lets the employee see;
- the machinery in ss 11, 12 and 37-39.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Nothing is "lost at 10 years"; the line on leaving is at 7.** s 8(3) gives an
   employee with "at least 7 years but less than 10 years" who leaves "in any
   circumstances other than by the employer for serious misconduct" a proportionate amount
   of 8 2/3 weeks. A resignation is one of those circumstances. Before 7 years, leaving
   earns nothing. Asserted (6.9 years: 0; 8 years: 104/15 weeks; 9 years: 7.8 weeks).
2. **Chloe has not reached 10 years.** Ironbark employed her from 5 February 2024 to 31
   January 2034 (facts.json), five days short of ten calendar years. Her unpaid parental
   leave (c061 in 2030, and again for Ella in 2033) keeps her employment continuous
   (s 6(1)(d)) but is not counted (s 6A(2)(a)). So she is paid a pro rata amount under
   s 8(3), not the 10-year 8 2/3 weeks. The scenario's "crystallised at ten years" is
   wrong, but she loses nothing by resigning. The 9-year figure is an assumption. Asserted.
3. **Changing employer starts the count again, unless the business moved.** s 8(1) earns
   leave "with the same employer". ss 7E-7H carry service over only if all four hold:
   - the old employment ended;
   - the new employer took the employee on within 3 months;
   - the work is the same or substantially the same;
   - the employers are connected (assets, outsourcing either way, or a related body
     corporate).

   Liam's move to an unconnected employer (c056) starts from zero. Asserted.
4. **What Liam keeps from the old employer depends on facts the scenario does not give.**
   Two readings of facts.json:
   - The cast note says "apprenticeship 2017-2021", and Southern Sparks is "Liam's
     employer from 2021". But c063 places him at Southern Sparks in 2030, after the c056
     change in 2029. The Fair Work baseline read Southern Sparks as the new employer from 2
     September 2029.
   - If he spent 8 years with the employer he leaves, he is paid 104/15 (about 6.93)
     weeks. If his apprenticeship was with that employer and he signed on within 52 weeks
     (s 6(7)), he has 12 years and is paid 10.4 weeks.

   Both are asserted, each labelled with its assumption.
5. **Accrued leave survives a dismissal for serious misconduct; only the pro rata part is
   lost.** Serious misconduct is excluded only from s 8(2)(c) and s 8(3). s 9(2) and
   s 9(2A) still require leave already accrued under s 8(2)(a), (b) to be paid on
   termination. Asserted (9 years, serious misconduct: 0; 12 years: 8 2/3 weeks).
6. **After 10 years, the termination top-up counts whole years only.** s 8(2)(c) uses "the
   number of years ... completed since the employee last became entitled". 12.5 years
   gives the same 10.4 weeks as 12 years. s 8(3) lacks the word "completed". The encoding
   reads it as proportionate to the whole length, part years included (8.5 years: 221/30
   weeks). Both are readings, and both are asserted.
7. **Leave is 8 2/3 weeks at 10 years, then 4 1/3 weeks for each further 5.** 15 years
   gives 13 weeks and 20 years gives 17 1/3. Nothing more accrues between the marks while
   the employee stays. Asserted.
8. **Unpaid leave keeps continuity but does not count; paid leave counts.** s 6(1) and
   s 6A. A dismissal followed by re-employment within 2 months (6 months for slackness of
   trade) keeps continuity, but the gap does not count. A dismissal made to avoid long
   service leave counts in full. An unexplained absence breaks continuity only if the
   employer gives written notice within 14 days. A resignation is not an absence, so
   nothing preserves continuity through it (a reading). Asserted.
9. **Ordinary pay leaves out overtime, penalties and allowances, but keeps a casual's
   loading.** s 7A excludes them. s 7B includes the casual loading. s 7(4) averages
   result-based pay over 365 days. Asserted.
10. **After 12 months the leave is the employee's to schedule.** Without an agreement, the
    employer "is not to refuse" leave the employee became entitled to more than 12 months
    before, on at least 2 weeks' notice (s 9(1a), (1b)). Asserted.
11. **Double at half pay, or half at double, is only a request.** s 9(1C), (1D) say "may
    request". The encoding answers what the leave looks like if the request is granted (a
    reading). Asserted.
12. **Cash-out needs accrued leave, writing, and at least full ordinary pay.** s 5 allows
    no agreement before the leave accrues (s 5(3)), and so none for a pro rata termination
    entitlement. Asserted.
13. **Working for someone else during the leave forfeits the rest of it, except for
    termination leave.** s 27(2), (3). Asserted.
14. **The employee may see the records for 7 years after leaving.** s 26A(3)(a), with
    s 26(2)(b). They must be shown by the end of the next pay period after a written
    request. A failure is a civil penalty provision, not an offence. Asserted.

  employer he leaves in 2029, and which side of that move Southern Sparks is on. Ask them

  electrical employers, to answer s 4A rather than ask it.
