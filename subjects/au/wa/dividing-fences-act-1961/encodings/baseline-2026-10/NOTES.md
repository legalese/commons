# Dividing Fences Act 1961 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Seat

**The neighbouring owner**: one of the two owners of adjoining lands, either the
one who wants a fence built or repaired or the one asked to pay. Their question is
"who pays for the dividing fence?" The Act speaks throughout of "the owner of land"
and "the owner of the adjoining land". It is applied to both, and both are in the
same seat. The only official is the Magistrates Court, and what it does is encoded
as what the owner may do: apply to it, and when.

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. No
  regulations set a number. Local laws prescribing a sufficient fence are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the owner, the adjoining owner, the
  court, landlord and tenant, a person building or repairing (s 21).
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Followed. The court's
  determination that a fence is sufficient (s 5 limb (d)) is an input. What the
  court would order under ss 9, 13(5) and 15(5) is not guessed: the answer is
  "the Magistrates Court decides".
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed: "owner" (from the
  interest held, the years left on a lease, and the public-reserve exclusion) and
  "sufficient fence" (from the local law, the agreement, and the cattle-and-sheep
  test). "Neglect", "substantial building", "reasonable time" are asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one dividing fence between
  two adjoining lands.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed: "sufficient fence",
  "owner", "repair" (includes re-erect and realign), "court" (the Magistrates
  Court).
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Partly departed from. Left out,
  though operative:
  - s 11 (an owner who cannot be found);
  - s 12 (defining the boundary by surveyor);
  - s 16 (a road-boundary fence another owner uses);
  - s 20 (purchaser under an option);
  - the community-titles rules (s 5(2), s 6(2)).

  These are rarer than the questions the seat asks first, and the caller asked for
  a small baseline. Machinery (ss 1-3, 23, 24) is out.

## The lives

- **Chloe, c07b (15 August 2039): a dividing fence with the new neighbour.** The
  scenario asks "Who pays?" and answers "half each". Chloe and Liam own their
  Byford house, so they hold the freehold in possession and are owners under s 5.
  On the encoder's assumptions (the lots are unfenced, there is no agreement about
  the cost, a $4,000 fence), `What share of a new dividing fence must I bear` gives
  "half: we join in or contribute in equal proportions (s 7)", and
  `How much must I contribute to the new fence` gives $2,000. The scenario gives no
  cost and does not say whether any fence already stood.

## Why this scope

The scenario cites ss 7 to 9. The brief added the notice, urgent repairs, damage
caused by one owner, the Magistrates Court, and what counts as a sufficient fence.
These take in ss 5, 7-10, 13-15 and 22. s 19 (landlord and tenant) and s 21
(entry) are short and answer questions an owner asks at the same time, so they are
in. Everything else is either rare for the seat or machinery.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Owners of adjoining lands not divided by a sufficient fence share a new fence
   equally.** s 7 says they "are liable to join in or contribute in equal
   proportions". An agreement between them about the cost is not affected by the
   Act (s 6(1)). The Crown is not bound (s 4). Asserted.
2. **"Owner" reaches a tenant with 5 or more years left, and stops short of a
   shorter lease.** The unexpired term is measured when the notice is given (s 5
   owner (b)). A trustee of a public reserve or park is not an owner. Asserted.
3. **What is "sufficient" is first a local-law question.** A fence prescribed by a
   local law, or one the owners agreed that does not fail the local law, is
   sufficient. Only "where no such local law or agreement is made" does the
   fallback apply: a substantial fence "ordinarily capable of resisting the
   trespass of cattle and sheep", or one the court determines. Read literally, the
   cattle-and-sheep test cannot make a fence sufficient where a local law exists.
   Asserted.
4. **The notice is how you compel a contribution.** It must name the boundary (or
   another line where the boundary is impracticable), contain a proposal, and
   state the kind of fence (s 8). It must be in writing, signed, and either handed
   over personally or sent by registered mail (s 22). Ordinary post or email is
   not a method s 22 names. Asserted.
5. **Either owner may go to the Magistrates Court once 21 days pass without
   agreement** on the need, the line or the kind of fence (s 9(1)). The order is
   final (s 9(4)). The encoding reads the 21 days as needing to run in full.
   Asserted.
6. **If the other owner does not do their part, you may build the whole fence and
   recover half the cost** (s 10). That is after the time the agreement or order
   names, or 3 months if it names none. Asserted.
7. **Building without notice does not lose you the half, but it can delay it.**
   You may claim half the value only once your neighbour has completed a
   substantial building on their land, occupies one, or lets someone occupy one
   (s 13(2)). The value is taken "as estimated at the date of the claim", not the
   cost. Asserted.
8. **A claim for half must be answered within one month.** If the neighbour
   neither pays nor gives notice disputing it within a month, they are liable for
   the amount of the claim (s 13(7)). A dispute sends it to the court (s 13(4)).
   Asserted.
9. **Repairs are also half each, but the ordinary route needs a notice and 14
   days.** If the neighbour gives no written reply within 14 days, you may repair
   and recover half (s 15(7)). If they dispute or object, you may apply to the
   court (s 15(4)). Where each owner built part of the fence, each repairs their
   own part (s 15(7)(a)). Asserted.
10. **Urgent repairs need no notice.** Damage "by flood, fire, lightning, storm,
    tempest or accident" may be repaired at once, and half recovered (s 15(7)(b)).
    Asserted.
11. **Damage you caused is yours alone.** Where a fire or a fallen tree came
    through one owner's neglect, that owner must repair "as soon as practicable".
    If they do not, the other may repair and recover the whole cost (s 15(7)(c),
    (8)). Fire appears in both (b) and (c). The encoding treats (c) as governing
    where there was neglect, and reads "neglect" as qualifying the tree as well as
    the fire. Both are the encoder's readings. Asserted.
12. **Between landlord and tenant, the share follows the length of the lease**,
    unless they agree otherwise (s 19(1)). Under 5 years, the landlord pays all.
    From 5 to under 7, the tenant pays a quarter. From 7 to under 12, half. From
    12 years, all. Asserted.
13. **Whoever is building or repairing may enter the neighbour's land** at
    reasonable times, to do what the work needs (s 21). Asserted.
14. **The s 13(2) claim is read as the route for a fence built without a notice.**
    Where a notice was given, ss 9 and 10 apply. The text of s 13(1) is general,
    so this division is the encoder's reading.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added on 6 November 2026, following the graffiti encoding's design (D2, D4, D8).

- **Files.** The layer is `dfa-qa.l4`. Its cases are in `dfa-qa-cases.l4`.
- **Imports.** It imports `prelude` and `dfa-fences`. It changes none of the baseline's rules or exports.
- **The role comes first.** Every exported question takes `my role` first ("Which of these describes you").
- **Other roles.** For a role a question is not about, the answer is `does not arise for you`, and no other input is read.

**Roles.** The baseline's one seat, the neighbouring owner, is two people who ask different questions. The Act adds a third.

- `I own land and want a dividing fence built or repaired`. This owner gives the notices, builds, claims half, and enters
  the neighbour's land.
- `I own land and my neighbour wants me to share the cost of a dividing fence`. This owner receives the notices and claims.
  They can be made to pay under ss 7, 10, 13 and 15.
- `I rent land that a dividing fence borders`. s 19 divides an owner's contribution between landlord and tenant. A tenant
  with 5 or more years left is an owner (s 5).

The Magistrates Court is never a seat.

**Situation inputs.** All three are BOOLEANs, and FALSE gives `does not arise for you`. Each comes straight after `my role`.
Each is identical in name, type and `@desc` in both owner roles.

| Name | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| `a notice to fence has been given between my neighbour and me` | s 9, applying to the court (both owners). s 10, building the whole fence (wanting the fence), and its mirror, the neighbour building it against me (asked to pay). 4 questions. The baseline's `a notice to fence was given by me or to me` is TRUE by it |
| `my neighbour has claimed half the value of a fence they built` | s 13(3), (4), (7): must I pay the claim (asked to pay) |
| `my land is let to a tenant` | The landlord's part of the contribution, s 19(1) (both owners) |

**Decisions.**

- **D-QA1. The baseline's 13 questions are spread over the roles.** 11 go to the owner wanting the fence. "I have
  received a claim for half ... must I pay it" (s 13(3)) goes to the owner asked to pay. s 19's tenant question goes to the
  tenant. The questions about who is an owner, the sufficient fence, the share, the contribution, the notice's validity,
  s 9 and s 21 are asked in both owner roles, by the same functions.
- **D-QA2. The s 15 rule is turned round for the owner asked to pay.** "What can my neighbour recover from me for
  repairing" uses three helpers, each listing every value with no OTHERWISE:
  - the first turns the person's own facts into the baseline's frame. "My neglect" becomes "my neighbour's neglect", and
    the person's reply becomes the baseline's reply;
  - the second calls the baseline's function;
  - the third turns its answer back into the person's frame (`What my neighbour can recover from me for a repair`).

  The amount question for this role takes the facts and the cost. The baseline's amount question takes the answer itself
  as an input, which the layer keeps for the owner wanting the fence.
- **D-QA3. s 10 against me.** "Can my neighbour build the whole fence and recover half from me" is the baseline's s 10
  function. My own part not done is the part the other owner has not done.
- **D-QA4. Added from the text.**
  - The landlord's part is one less the tenant's part under s 19(1): the whole, three-fourths, one-half or none.
  - The tenant's recovery or set-off of an excess comes from s 19(2).
  - "Am I, as a tenant, treated as an owner" is the baseline's s 5 function with `I hold a lease`.
- **D-QA5. No situation for s 13(2) or s 15.** The s 13(2) claim settles on `I gave a notice to fence before building` as
  the baseline does. The s 15 questions settle on the cause: a storm or accident needs no notice (s 15(7)(b)). Both arise
  for every owner in their role.

**Not covered for the new roles.**

- The owner asked to pay: s 11 (an owner who cannot be found, and what follows for the owner found later), s 12 surveys,
  s 16 road fences, and s 20 (a purchaser under an option, which the baseline leaves out).
- The tenant: what a tenancy agreement says, beyond "unless otherwise agreed" (s 19(1)).
- The tenant: the residential tenancy law.
