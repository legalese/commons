# Casino Control Act 1984 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Seat

**The casino patron**: the adult who goes to the casino, and the child who goes
in with a parent. The Act is mostly addressed to the Minister, the Commission and
the casino licensee. A patron meets it at three points:

- when someone wants them kept out (s 26, s 26A);
- when they are under 18 (s 27, s 27A);
- when they play (s 22, s 23).

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The
  Casino Control Regulations 1999 are deposited and supply the review fee and the
  infringement penalties.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the casino licensee, the Commissioner
  of Police, the Commission and authorised persons. A family member appears as an
  input only because the life asks about one. The answer is that the Act names
  none.
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Followed. Three are inputs:
  - the authorised person's reasonable suspicion that the patron is under 18
    (s 27A(1));
  - whether the stated age appears false to that person (s 27A(2));
  - the prosecutor's application for forfeiture (ss 23(2b), 27(3b)).

  The casino's decision whether to ban someone is unconstrained in the text. It
  appears only as a fact: whether a ban was given.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed for "authorised
  game" (as far as s 22(1)(a) goes) and for the s 27(4) exceptions. "Licensed
  casino", "casino licensee" and "Commission" are taken as given.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One visit, with one ban (or none) and
  one age check.
- **The Act's terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments.
- **Operative sections in, machinery out.** Followed. The casino's own duties
  (ss 26(7), 27(1), (1a)) bind the licensee, not the patron, and are left out.

## The lives

- **Jake, k080 (1 September 2045, aged 39): the casino on paydays.** The
  questions:
  - Is the game lawful?
    - An authorised game played to the approved rules: yes.
    - A poker machine: it cannot be an authorised game.
  - Is it an offence for him to enter or play? Not while no ban is in force.
- **Jake, k081 (8 May 2046): Chloe looks for a family exclusion order, and Jake
  self-excludes.** The questions:
  - What can each person do to keep him out? Only the casino and the
    Commissioner of Police can ban him; his family cannot, and he cannot by
    himself.
  - May his family apply for a ban? No.
  - Is his self-exclusion in force? Yes, until the casino revokes it.
  - What follows if he goes back?
    - Entering: up to $10,000, or $1,000 on an infringement notice.
    - Playing: up to $50,000, or $500 on an infringement notice, and his
      winnings are forfeited if the prosecutor applies.
    - He may be removed with reasonable force.
  - May he have the ban reviewed? Yes, for a $179 fee, and the ban stays in
    force meanwhile.

  The self-exclusion is taken to be a casino direction under s 26(2) given at his
  request. That is the encoder's assumption, because the Act does not mention
  self-exclusion.
- **Ruby (13 in 2046)** is used to illustrate s 27; she is not a scenario event.
  - At dinner with her father, she may be in the casino.
  - At dinner alone, she commits an offence: up to $1,000.
  - If she plays, she commits an offence: up to $2,000.

## Why this scope

The lives ask what a gambler and his family can do about the casino. The plan
expected the answer in the Casino Control Regulations 1999. It is in the Act's
s 26 and s 26A, and the regulations only set the review fee and the infringement
penalties.

Around that core the encoding takes the rest of what a patron meets: children,
proof of age, and lawful games. Licensing, the casino complex agreement, the
Independent Monitor, junkets, controlled contracts and suspicious-matter reports
are about the operator and are out. So is s 32 (detention of suspected offenders),
because the deposit omits the Table of the provisions it applies to.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Exclusion is in the Act, s 26, not in the regulations.** The life's facts
   cite s 25, which is "Records of gaming operations". The plan looked in the
   Casino Control Regulations 1999. Those regulations contain nothing on exclusion
   beyond the s 26A review fee (r 4) and the infringement penalties for ss 26(1d)
   and 26(6) (Schedule 1 items 4, 5). Asserted.
2. **A family cannot apply, confirmed.** No provision lets a family member apply
   for a ban, and none gives one a power. Only two can ban:
   - the casino (on the spot, s 26(1a); or by written direction, s 26(2));
   - the Commissioner of Police (by written direction, s 26(2)).

   Inference, not asserted: nothing in the text stops a relative asking the casino
   or the police, whose power is unconditioned. Asserted (as the absence of a
   right to apply).
3. **The Commission cannot ban anyone under the Act.** The life says "the casino
   and the Commission may exclude". The Commission's only role is to review a
   casino's written direction on the banned person's application, and confirm or
   revoke it (s 26A(5)). Asserted.
4. **There is no self-exclusion in the Act.** A self-exclusion from the casino
   can only be a ban the casino chooses to give. Asserted (as the answer for "I
   myself"). The Crown program the life cites is administrative.
5. **A patron has no right to be there.** s 26(1): no right to enter or remain
   "except by the licence of" the owner, occupier or licensee. Asserted.
6. **Bans last differently.**
   - An on-the-spot prohibition lasts at most 24 hours, or until revoked
     (s 26(1b)).
   - A casino direction has no time limit and lasts until the casino revokes it,
     or the Commission revokes it on review (s 26(4)(a)).
   - A police direction has no effect until the casino is told in writing
     (s 26(3)). It ends only when revoked and the revocation is notified to the
     casino (s 26(4)(b)).

   Asserted.
7. **Only a casino's written direction can be reviewed.** There is no review of
   an on-the-spot prohibition or of a police direction. The fee is $179 (r 4), and
   the ban stays in force during the review (s 26A(2)). Asserted.
8. **Playing while banned is the heavier offence in court but the lighter one on
   an infringement notice.**
   - Court maximum: $50,000 for playing (s 23(2)(ii)) against $10,000 for
     entering under a direction (s 26(6)).
   - Modified penalty: $500 for playing against $1,000 for entering (Schedule 1
     items 3 and 5).

   Asserted.
9. **Winnings can be taken, but only if the prosecutor asks.** On conviction for
   playing while banned or gaming under 18, the court "shall" order forfeiture of
   the winnings "on the application of the prosecutor" (ss 23(2b), 27(3b)).
   Asserted.
10. **A child may be in the casino for a meal with a parent, or to work, if they
    do not gamble.** s 27(4). A child there for any other reason commits an
    offence: $1,000 for a first offence, $2,000 after (s 27(3)), $200 on an
    infringement notice. Gaming under 18 is a separate offence: $2,000
    (s 27(3a)). Asserted.
11. **A child covered by s 27(4) cannot be put through the s 27A age check.**
    s 27A(1) excludes "a person to whom section 27(4) applies". Anyone else
    suspected of being under 18 who cannot prove their age must leave when told.
    If they stay, they may be removed by reasonable force and fined up to $2,000,
    unless they had a reasonable excuse. Asserted.
12. **Poker machines cannot be authorised games.** s 22(1)(a). Playing an
    authorised game to the approved rules is "not unlawful" (s 23(1)(b)), except
    for a banned person or an official (s 23(2)). Asserted.
13. **No entry levy.** No provision of the Act charges a patron to enter (the
    life's "no entry levy in WA"). The deposit was searched for "levy",
    "admission" and "entry fee", and nothing was found. Not asserted, because it
    is an absence.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The deposit's compilation table is empty, so the edition cannot be dated
  precisely. Fetch a fuller copy.
- The s 32 Table is missing from the deposit. Without it, detention of a
  suspected banned patron cannot be encoded.
- Whether a casino's self-exclusion program in fact operates through s 26(2)
  directions is an assumption. It should be checked against the program's
  documents.
- The Gaming and Wagering Commission Act 1987 (s 36 infringement modifications,
  s 44 offences) and the Criminal Procedure Act 2004 Part 2 were not read.
- The Interactive Gambling Act 2001 (Cth) Part 7B (BetStop), which k081 also
  turns on, is a separate subject.
- No case law was searched, for example on whether "over the age of 18 years" in
  s 27(4)(b) includes a person who has just turned 18.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added 6 November 2026, following the Graffiti Vandalism Act encoding's design (D2, D4, D8 and D8.1-D8.15 in `subjects/au/wa/graffiti-vandalism-act-2016/encodings/baseline-2026-10/NOTES.md`). The layer is `cca-qa.l4` (imports `prelude`, `cca-patron`); its cases are `cca-qa-cases.l4`. The baseline's files are unchanged: the layer adds, and replaces nothing.

**Roles** (`DECLARE Role`, in the first person; every exported question takes `my role` first, `@desc Which of these describes you`, and answers `does not arise for you` for a role it is not about without reading any other input):

- `I go, or want to go, to a casino in Western Australia`: the baseline's seat, the patron and the child who goes in with a parent. All 17 baseline exports are asked, each calling the baseline function (17 questions).
- `I work at a casino in Western Australia, as a casino employee or key employee`: s 23(2)(ia), (2a), (2b) bar the employee from playing; a person authorised by the casino licensee may ban on the spot and remove (s 26(1a)-(1c), (5)) and require proof of age (s 27A); a person concerned in managing the gaming must keep children out (s 27(1), (1a), (2)) (9).
- `I want to take a child under 18 into a casino with me`: s 27(4)(b) lets a child in for a meal with a spouse, parent, guardian or person in loco parentis over 18 (2).

The casino licensee (a company), the Gaming and Wagering Commission, the Commissioner of Police, the Minister and the courts are never a seat: what they may or must do appears as what it means for the person asking. The baseline left the casino's own duties out as binding the licensee; the layer takes s 27(1), (1a) for the employee because they also bind "any other person concerned in the organization or management of the gaming operations", an individual.

**Situation inputs** (all BOOLEAN, each straight after `my role`; FALSE gives `does not arise for you` without reading anything else; same name, type and `@desc` wherever taken):

| Input | Role | Does not arise when | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `I have been banned from the casino` | the patron | FALSE | whether the ban is in force, review, its fee, the ban during review (ss 26(1b)-(4), 26A) |
| `I have been convicted of a casino offence` | the patron | FALSE | forfeiture of winnings (ss 23(2b), 27(3b)) |
| `the casino licensee has authorised me to keep people out of the casino` | the employee | FALSE | prohibiting on the spot, how long it lasts, requiring a stated age, removing with reasonable force (ss 26(1a)-(1c), (5), 27A) |
| `I am concerned in organising or managing the casino's gaming operations` | the employee | FALSE | the s 27(1), (1a) offence and its fine |
| `I have been convicted under s 23(2) of playing in the casino` | the employee | FALSE | forfeiture of winnings (s 23(2b)) |

The other questions arise in every situation of their role and take none: who may keep me out, the family and the right of entry, the fines and infringement amounts, s 27(4), the offences of entering, playing and not leaving, the largest fine, the game, and removal for the patron; whether an employee may play and the fine; and the parent's two.

**Decisions.**

- **Q1. A ban is a situation of the four ban questions, not of removal or the offences.** "Is it an offence for me to enter", "to play", "the largest fine" and "may I be removed" arise also for a child who is not banned, so they take the ban's facts with `who banned me` (with `nobody` among its values) and take no situation.
- **Q2. The child's age is not made a situation.** "Does s 27(4) let me in although I am under 18" reads the age from the visit, as the baseline does; a separate `I am under 18` input could contradict it. An adult who asks is told no.
- **Q3. The employee's powers call the baseline's functions with the person's facts.** Removal is the baseline's `May I be removed with reasonable force` on the person's ban, visit and age check, asked about the person in the same way as Q9 asks the patron (mirror cases agree with the patron's). Requiring a stated age is reasonable suspicion and not a child let in by s 27(4), using the baseline's s 27(4) function. The 24 hours is the baseline's own constant.
- **Q4. s 27(1), (1a), (2) are helpers here.** "Permitted to enter or remain" is read as the child being in the casino; the s 27(2) defence (reasonable grounds for believing the child over 18) is an input. The fine is $50 000 if the child took part in gaming (s 27(1a)), else $20 000 (s 27(1)); where both happen, the larger is given, as the baseline does for the patron's visit.
- **Q5. The parent's questions build the child's visit.** The child's `My visit` record is made from the parent's facts; `the game` and `it is played to the approved rules` are placeholders no rule called reads. "Would the child commit an offence by playing" uses the baseline's `Is it an offence for me to play` with no ban (a `My ban` whose `who banned me` is `nobody`; its other fields are placeholders no rule reads then).
- **Q6. Answer types** follow gva-seat.l4: `Yes or no`, `Amount`, `Hours` (`up to` HAS hours) and `What they may do to keep me out`, which maps the baseline's five values one by one, with no OTHERWISE, from "me" to "you".
- **Q7. Not tested by an ASSUMEd value:** the baseline reads `Why I am there` through OTHERWISE in its s 27(4) function, and `Who banned me` and `Patron offence` with EQUALS; a read of those before the role or situation would not show in the cases (graffiti D8.8). The parameter script and the code show that each question reads the role, and then any situation, first.
- **Q8. Name clash avoided.** The baseline's `Does s 27(4) let me in although I am under 18` is called by the layer's `Does s 27(4) let me into the casino although I am under 18`; the question text is the baseline's.

- **Q9. The facts of a ban, a visit and an age check are asked as separate inputs, not as the baseline's records** (fixed 6 November 2026, after verification). Questions took `My ban` (4 values and 4 yes/no fields: 64 combinations), `My visit` (96) and `Age check` (32) together, up to 393 216 combinations. The site's form drops a question for a role it is not about only after trying every combination of its open yes/no and choice inputs, and gives up past 1 024, so the patron's five visit and ban questions stayed open for the employee and the parent, and the employee's removal question for the patron, the parent and an employee not authorised (verification: 289 ground-truth issues). Each question now takes only the facts the baseline rule it calls reads, and the layer builds the baseline's records from them (`the ban for`, `the visit for`, `the check for`) and calls the baseline's rules, which are not restated. Three folds keep the largest question within 1 024:

  | Input | Type | Baseline field(s) it stands for |
  |---|---|---|
  | `who banned me`, `hours since the ban was made` | `Who banned me`, NUMBER | the same fields |
  | `the ban has been revoked` | BOOLEAN | `the person who made it has revoked it`, `the Commission has revoked it on review and given notice` and, for a police direction, `the casino has been given written notice of the revocation`: the baseline reads them only as "revoked by whoever made it" (an on-the-spot ban), "by the casino or the Commission on review" (a casino direction), and "by the Commissioner and notified to the casino" (a police direction), so the one input, whose `@desc` says which counts, is put as the revocation and its notice, with no Commission revocation |
  | `the casino has been given written notice of the ban` | BOOLEAN | the same field (read for a police direction only) |
  | `my age in years` | NUMBER | `my age in years` |
  | `why I am there, and with whom` | `Why there, and with whom` (4 values) | `why I am there`, with `with my spouse, parent or guardian who is over 18` folded into the meal value, the only one for which the baseline reads it (s 27(4)(b)) |
  | `I play` | BOOLEAN | `I play` |
  | `the game`, `the game is played to the approved rules` | `Kind of game`, BOOLEAN | the same fields (read only by the game question) |
  | `an authorised person suspected I was under 18, my stated age appeared false to them, and I produced no evidence of my age` | BOOLEAN | the first three `Age check` fields, read only together (s 27A(1)-(3)) |
  | `I left when required`, `I had a reasonable excuse for not leaving` | BOOLEAN | the same fields |

  The employee's questions ask the same about the person (`who banned the person`, `why the person is there, and with whom`, `the person plays`, and so on). A fact a question's baseline rule does not read is passed as a fixed value (`no game`, FALSE). The largest question is the employee's removal question: 1 024 combinations in the patron's and the parent's states, where the authorisation is still open (the form's limit is "more than 1 024"); the patron's removal question has 512, the largest fine 256. `May I apply for a review of the ban on me` takes `who banned me` only, which is all the baseline reads. The cases give the fixtures in the separate inputs and assert the answer equal to the baseline export's on the fixture's own records; 18 new cases (8 of them equality cases) cover each fold the other way: a casino direction revoked by the Commission, a police direction revoked but not yet notified, a meal with a parent while playing, a child working in the amenities, an age check without a false-seeming age or with evidence produced.

**Not covered for the new roles.** The employee: the casino's own exclusion of banned people (s 26(7)), s 32 detention, cheating and forgery (ss 30, 31), employees' licensing, and an officer of the Commission or a police officer acting under s 27A (officials). The parent: whether any other law makes taking a child in an offence (not searched); a child's employment under s 27(4)(a), and the child's fines, are the child's own questions, asked as the patron.
