# Casino Control Act 1984 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Seat

**The casino patron**: the adult who goes to the casino, and the child who goes
in with a parent. The Act is mostly addressed to the Minister, the Commission and
the casino licensee. A patron meets it at three points:

- when someone wants them kept out (s 26, s 26A);
- when they are under 18 (s 27, s 27A);
- when they play (s 22, s 23).

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The
  Casino Control Regulations 1999 are deposited and supply the review fee and the
  infringement penalties.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the casino licensee, the Commissioner
  of Police, the Commission and authorised persons. A family member appears as an
  input only because the life asks about one. The answer is that the Act names
  none.
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Followed. Three are inputs:
  - the authorised person's reasonable suspicion that the patron is under 18
    (s 27A(1));
  - whether the stated age appears false to that person (s 27A(2));
  - the prosecutor's application for forfeiture (ss 23(2b), 27(3b)).

  The casino's decision whether to ban someone is unconstrained in the text. It
  appears only as a fact: whether a ban was given.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed for "authorised
  game" (as far as s 22(1)(a) goes) and for the s 27(4) exceptions. "Licensed
  casino", "casino licensee" and "Commission" are taken as given.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One visit, with one ban (or none) and
  one age check.
- **The Act's terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments.
- **Operative sections in, machinery out.** Followed. The casino's own duties
  (ss 26(7), 27(1), (1a)) bind the licensee, not the patron, and are left out.

## The lives

- **Jake, k080 (1 September 2045, aged 39): the casino on paydays.** The
  questions:
  - Is the game lawful?
    - An authorised game played to the approved rules: yes.
    - A poker machine: it cannot be an authorised game.
  - Is it an offence for him to enter or play? Not while no ban is in force.
- **Jake, k081 (8 May 2046): Chloe looks for a family exclusion order, and Jake
  self-excludes.** The questions:
  - What can each person do to keep him out? Only the casino and the
    Commissioner of Police can ban him; his family cannot, and he cannot by
    himself.
  - May his family apply for a ban? No.
  - Is his self-exclusion in force? Yes, until the casino revokes it.
  - What follows if he goes back?
    - Entering: up to $10,000, or $1,000 on an infringement notice.
    - Playing: up to $50,000, or $500 on an infringement notice, and his
      winnings are forfeited if the prosecutor applies.
    - He may be removed with reasonable force.
  - May he have the ban reviewed? Yes, for a $179 fee, and the ban stays in
    force meanwhile.

  The self-exclusion is taken to be a casino direction under s 26(2) given at his
  request. That is the encoder's assumption, because the Act does not mention
  self-exclusion.
- **Ruby (13 in 2046)** is used to illustrate s 27; she is not a scenario event.
  - At dinner with her father, she may be in the casino.
  - At dinner alone, she commits an offence: up to $1,000.
  - If she plays, she commits an offence: up to $2,000.

## Why this scope

The lives ask what a gambler and his family can do about the casino. The plan
expected the answer in the Casino Control Regulations 1999. It is in the Act's
s 26 and s 26A, and the regulations only set the review fee and the infringement
penalties.

Around that core the encoding takes the rest of what a patron meets: children,
proof of age, and lawful games. Licensing, the casino complex agreement, the
Independent Monitor, junkets, controlled contracts and suspicious-matter reports
are about the operator and are out. So is s 32 (detention of suspected offenders),
because the deposit omits the Table of the provisions it applies to.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Exclusion is in the Act, s 26, not in the regulations.** The life's facts
   cite s 25, which is "Records of gaming operations". The plan looked in the
   Casino Control Regulations 1999. Those regulations contain nothing on exclusion
   beyond the s 26A review fee (r 4) and the infringement penalties for ss 26(1d)
   and 26(6) (Schedule 1 items 4, 5). Asserted.
2. **A family cannot apply, confirmed.** No provision lets a family member apply
   for a ban, and none gives one a power. Only two can ban:
   - the casino (on the spot, s 26(1a); or by written direction, s 26(2));
   - the Commissioner of Police (by written direction, s 26(2)).

   Inference, not asserted: nothing in the text stops a relative asking the casino
   or the police, whose power is unconditioned. Asserted (as the absence of a
   right to apply).
3. **The Commission cannot ban anyone under the Act.** The life says "the casino
   and the Commission may exclude". The Commission's only role is to review a
   casino's written direction on the banned person's application, and confirm or
   revoke it (s 26A(5)). Asserted.
4. **There is no self-exclusion in the Act.** A self-exclusion from the casino
   can only be a ban the casino chooses to give. Asserted (as the answer for "I
   myself"). The Crown program the life cites is administrative.
5. **A patron has no right to be there.** s 26(1): no right to enter or remain
   "except by the licence of" the owner, occupier or licensee. Asserted.
6. **Bans last differently.**
   - An on-the-spot prohibition lasts at most 24 hours, or until revoked
     (s 26(1b)).
   - A casino direction has no time limit and lasts until the casino revokes it,
     or the Commission revokes it on review (s 26(4)(a)).
   - A police direction has no effect until the casino is told in writing
     (s 26(3)). It ends only when revoked and the revocation is notified to the
     casino (s 26(4)(b)).

   Asserted.
7. **Only a casino's written direction can be reviewed.** There is no review of
   an on-the-spot prohibition or of a police direction. The fee is $179 (r 4), and
   the ban stays in force during the review (s 26A(2)). Asserted.
8. **Playing while banned is the heavier offence in court but the lighter one on
   an infringement notice.**
   - Court maximum: $50,000 for playing (s 23(2)(ii)) against $10,000 for
     entering under a direction (s 26(6)).
   - Modified penalty: $500 for playing against $1,000 for entering (Schedule 1
     items 3 and 5).

   Asserted.
9. **Winnings can be taken, but only if the prosecutor asks.** On conviction for
   playing while banned or gaming under 18, the court "shall" order forfeiture of
   the winnings "on the application of the prosecutor" (ss 23(2b), 27(3b)).
   Asserted.
10. **A child may be in the casino for a meal with a parent, or to work, if they
    do not gamble.** s 27(4). A child there for any other reason commits an
    offence: $1,000 for a first offence, $2,000 after (s 27(3)), $200 on an
    infringement notice. Gaming under 18 is a separate offence: $2,000
    (s 27(3a)). Asserted.
11. **A child covered by s 27(4) cannot be put through the s 27A age check.**
    s 27A(1) excludes "a person to whom section 27(4) applies". Anyone else
    suspected of being under 18 who cannot prove their age must leave when told.
    If they stay, they may be removed by reasonable force and fined up to $2,000,
    unless they had a reasonable excuse. Asserted.
12. **Poker machines cannot be authorised games.** s 22(1)(a). Playing an
    authorised game to the approved rules is "not unlawful" (s 23(1)(b)), except
    for a banned person or an official (s 23(2)). Asserted.
13. **No entry levy.** No provision of the Act charges a patron to enter (the
    life's "no entry levy in WA"). The deposit was searched for "levy",
    "admission" and "entry fee", and nothing was found. Not asserted, because it
    is an absence.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The deposit's compilation table is empty, so the edition cannot be dated
  precisely. Fetch a fuller copy.
- The s 32 Table is missing from the deposit. Without it, detention of a
  suspected banned patron cannot be encoded.
- Whether a casino's self-exclusion program in fact operates through s 26(2)
  directions is an assumption. It should be checked against the program's
  documents.
- The Gaming and Wagering Commission Act 1987 (s 36 infringement modifications,
  s 44 offences) and the Criminal Procedure Act 2004 Part 2 were not read.
- The Interactive Gambling Act 2001 (Cth) Part 7B (BetStop), which k081 also
  turns on, is a separate subject.
- No case law was searched, for example on whether "over the age of 18 years" in
  s 27(4)(b) includes a person who has just turned 18.
