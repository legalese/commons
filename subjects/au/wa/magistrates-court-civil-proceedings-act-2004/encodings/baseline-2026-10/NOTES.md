# Magistrates Court (Civil Proceedings) Act 2004 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act. There is not yet an encoding of the
Magistrates Court Act 2004 to keep it consistent with. Its jurisdiction
questions belong here anyway, because this Act's s 5 says the Court's civil
jurisdiction "is set out in this Act".

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Edition

- **The Act:** the Magistrates Court (Civil Proceedings) Act 2004 (No. 48 of
  2004), compilation as deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_48918.txt`
  (legislation.wa.gov.au mrdoc 48918, retrieved 7 September 2026). Its s 7 carries
  the amendment by the Workers Compensation and Injury Management Act 2023.
- **The limits** are in the Act itself: the minor cases jurisdictional limit is
  $10 000 and the jurisdictional limit is $75 000 "on and after 1 January 2009"
  (ss 3, 4). The earlier figures are not encoded.
- **No regulations or rules of court.** Neither the Magistrates Court (Civil
  Proceedings) Regulations nor the Rules are deposited. So whether a hearing is a
  proceeding "prescribed by the regulations" (s 30(3)(a)) is an input. The time
  for an appeal from a registrar to a magistrate (s 32(2)(b)) is answered
  NOTHING.

## Seat

**The consumer claimant.** That person asks:

- "Can I bring a minor case for a small claim?"
- "Can the Magistrates Court hear my claim at all?"
- "What does the minor cases procedure mean for me?" That is: may I have a
  lawyer, what costs can I win or be made to pay, are the rules of evidence
  applied, and can I appeal?

The Act speaks to the Court: what it "must" or "may" order. Each rule is encoded
as what it means for the claimant.

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The
  numbers are in the Act. The regulations and rules are not deposited, so the
  one point that turns on them is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the claimant, the other party (the
  trader), the Court (a magistrate or a registrar), the District Court.
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Followed. The inputs are:
  - whether the case involves "an important principle of law or complex facts or
    issues" (s 28(3)(b)). Because s 28(3) is a "may", the answer is "the minor
    cases procedure, unless the Court orders the general procedure";
  - the Court's satisfaction for leave to be represented (s 30(3)-(5)) and
    "exceptional circumstances" (s 44(3));
  - the Court's costs findings under s 31(3);
  - whether a supplier acts "in the exercise of a discipline that is not
    ordinarily regarded as being within the field of trade or commerce" (s 7(2)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed for consumer,
  trader, consumer/trader claim, minor case and allowable costs. The borrowed
  terms are asked as facts: tenant and lessor under the Residential Tenancies Act
  1987, and Commonwealth jurisdiction.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One claim started by one claimant.
- **The Act's terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments.
- **Operative sections in, machinery out.** Followed. Left out: rules of court
  (ss 33, 47, 48), regulations (s 49), commencement (s 2), mediator protection
  (s 36).

## The lives

- **Chloe, c070 (20 May 2032, aged 29): a new laptop fails in month three; the
  retailer repairs it.** The scenario cites this Act for "minor cases up to
  $10,000, if it had not". The scenario gives no price, so the $2,000 claim is
  illustrative. That she bought the laptop for her own use is an assumption. The
  questions:
  - `Am I a consumer` and `Is the other party a trader`: yes;
  - `Is my claim a consumer/trader claim`: yes;
  - `Can the Magistrates Court hear my claim`: yes, as a consumer/trader claim;
  - `Can I bring my claim as a minor case` and `Is my claim a minor case` (if she
    elects): yes;
  - `Which procedure will my case follow`: the minor cases procedure;
  - `Will the Court first try to settle my case`: yes;
  - `Will my hearing be in private`: yes;
  - `Is the Court bound by the rules of evidence`: no;
  - `May a lawyer represent me`: only if all parties agree, or the Court is
    satisfied no other party is unfairly disadvantaged. "Interests of justice"
    alone is not enough;
  - `What costs can the winner recover`: court and service fees and enforcement
    costs only;
  - `Could I have to pay the other side's legal fees if I lose`: no, unless her
    claim was wholly without merit or exceptional circumstances apply;
  - `Can I appeal and to whom`: not because the magistrate got the facts wrong.
    For a denial of natural justice, yes, to the District Court within 21 days.

  Whether the laptop failure is a breach of the consumer guarantee is the
  Australian Consumer Law's question (Competition and Consumer Act 2010 Sch 2),
  not this Act's.

## Why this scope

The life cites the minor cases procedure (Part 4) and s 26. The seat's first
question, "can I bring a minor case", needs these:

- the two limits (ss 3, 4);
- the general jurisdiction and its exclusions (s 6);
- the consumer/trader claim (s 7), because a minor case must be "within the
  jurisdiction of the Court".

Part 4 refers outward to s 25 (costs), s 44 (representation) and Part 7
(appeals), so their relevant limbs are in. Left out:

- ss 8, 26(b): jurisdiction and minor cases under other Acts;
- s 9 and s 28(4): counterclaims;
- ss 11, 12: remedies and interest;
- ss 13-24: case management and the general procedure, except that s 16(1)(j)
  is noted;
- the wasted-costs powers against lawyers (s 25(10)-(12));
- mediation (Part 5) and transfer to a superior court (Part 6);
- ss 41-43 (further appeals) and s 46 (enforcement).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The $10,000 minor case is a choice, not a category.** A claim at or under
   $10 000 is a minor case only if "the claimant has elected" the minor cases
   procedure (s 26(a)), or the parties agree (s 26(c)). The Court may also order
   it (s 16(1)(j)), which is not encoded. Asserted.
2. **The parties can agree a bigger claim into the minor cases procedure.**
   Under s 26(c), any claim within jurisdiction, even $12,000, is a minor case if
   the parties agree. Then s 31's costs limits apply. Asserted.
3. **The costs cap follows the value, not the procedure.** By s 25(5), a claim
   at or under $10 000 that the claimant did not elect into the minor cases
   procedure still carries only the costs s 31 would allow. Electing the general
   procedure does not open lawyers' costs. Asserted.
4. **A winner in a minor case gets only fees.** "Allowable costs" are court
   fees, service fees and enforcement costs (s 31(1)). Lawyers' fees need
   exceptional circumstances or a claim or defence "wholly without merit"
   (s 31(3)). Asserted.
5. **The consumer/trader claim still has the $75,000 cap.** Each limb of
   s 7(3)(b) is "not more than the jurisdictional limit". It adds non-money
   remedies (work, services, return or replacement of goods) and "relief from
   payment" to the Court's reach. It is not a way past the limit. Asserted.
6. **Lawyers are harder to get in a consumer/trader minor case.** s 30(4), with
   its "interests of justice" ground, applies "Except in the case of a
   consumer/trader claim". s 30(5) allows a lawyer there only if all parties agree,
   or the Court is satisfied no other party "will be unfairly disadvantaged".
   Asserted.
7. **Consent cannot cure the s 6(5) exclusions.** s 6(1)(f) lets the other party
   consent to a claim above the limit. But s 6(5) applies "Despite subsection
   (1)", so defamation, title to land, motor vehicle personal injury and building
   claims stay out even with consent. Asserted.
8. **Residential tenants and landlords are outside s 7.** A tenant is not a
   "consumer" and a lessor is not a "trader". Their money claim may still come in
   under s 6(1)(a) (encoder's reading; the Residential Tenancies Act 1987 has its
   own jurisdiction, not encoded). Encoded, not asserted.
9. **Minor case appeals are confined to four grounds.** They are: outside
   jurisdiction, not a minor case, denial of natural justice, or judgment beyond
   jurisdiction (s 32(3)). There is no appeal on the facts or the merits, and none
   against orders made along the way (s 32(1)(a)). Asserted.
10. **Who decided the case decides where the appeal goes.** From a magistrate, it
    goes to the District Court under Part 7, within 21 days (ss 32(2)(a), 40(3)).
    From a registrar, it goes to a magistrate under the rules of court
    (s 32(2)(b)). Asserted.
11. **Minor cases are private, and the rules of evidence do not bind**
    (s 29(1), (4)). Relatives and friends may attend (s 29(2)). Ordinary cases
    are in open court (s 45(1)). Asserted.
12. **Commonwealth jurisdiction forces the general procedure.** The Court
    "must" order the general procedure (s 28(2)). The cases with complexity or a
    request by all parties are only a "may" (s 28(3)). Asserted.
13. **A non-lawyer agent may not be paid** (ss 30(10), 44(4)). Not asserted.
