# Commercial Arbitration Act 2012 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE, no BECAUSE.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

## Edition

The compilation published on legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 45283), deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_45283.txt` (retrieved 2026-09-07).
The deposit gives no compilation number.
Its compilation table ends with the Legal Profession Uniform Law Application Act 2022 Pt 17 Div 5 (in force 1 July 2022).

No regulations set its numbers. They are in the text: one arbitrator by default (s 10(2)), 30 days (ss 11(3)(a), 33(1)), and 3 months (ss 34(3), 34A(6)).

## Seat

**A party to a domestic commercial arbitration, or to a dispute mediated by an arbitrator under s 27D.**
In the lives, that party is Daniel's client in 2029 (d038) and Daniel's company Jarrah in 2035 (d051).
The Act is applied to the parties. It decides:

- whether they are bound to arbitrate;
- whether a court must send them there;
- who picks their arbitrator;
- what hearing they must be given;
- whether they can escape, appeal or enforce the award.

Most of its sentences address the court or the tribunal. The encoding turns each into what the party may ask for, must be given, or must do by a date.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. There are none.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the parties, the arbitral tribunal (arbitrator), the Court (Supreme Court, or the District or Magistrates Court by agreement, s 6), and the arbitrator acting as mediator.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are asked as facts:
  - the court's finding that the agreement is "null and void, inoperative or incapable of being performed" (s 8(1));
  - a "real danger of bias" (s 12(6));
  - the Court's findings on arbitrability and public policy (ss 34(2)(b), 36(1)(b));
  - each limb of the Court's satisfaction for leave to appeal (s 34A(3)).

  Where the Court keeps a discretion even when a ground is made out, the answer says "may" or "if the Court grants leave".
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. Worked out from facts:
  - "arbitration agreement" and "in writing" (s 7);
  - "domestic" (s 1(3)).

  "Commercial", which the Act leaves to the Model Law note, and "question of law" are asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one arbitration agreement, one appointment, one award.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments. Field names are in the party's words.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out: Part 1A, ss 2A-6, and Parts 9-11.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names ss 7, 8 and 27D. The seat asks more:

- who appoints the arbitrator;
- the right to be heard;
- the award, and its recognition and enforcement;
- the limited grounds to set it aside, and the time limit;
- an appeal on a question of law, by agreement and leave.

So the encoding adds:

- s 1 (whether the Act applies at all);
- ss 10-12 (number, appointment and challenge);
- ss 18 and 24 (equal treatment and hearings);
- s 30 (settlement during an arbitration, the other half of d051's question);
- s 31(3) (reasons);
- s 33(1)(a) (correction, which also moves the start of the 3-month limit);
- ss 34, 34A, 35 and 36(1).

Interim measures, confidentiality, consolidation, evidence, costs and interest, and the Court's powers once an application is made are left out.
They are what a lawyer or the tribunal does, not what a party asks at the threshold.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. An arbitration agreement must be in writing, but "writing" means only that its content is recorded in some form. An agreement concluded orally or by conduct counts if it is recorded (s 7(4)). So does an email (s 7(5)), an allegation in a statement of claim the defence does not deny (s 7(7)), or a reference to standard terms that makes their clause part of the contract (s 7(8)). An oral agreement never recorded is not one. Asserted.
2. The Act covers only domestic commercial arbitrations: both parties' places of business in Australia when the agreement was made, and the Model Law not applying through the International Arbitration Act 1974 (Cth) (s 1(3)). Most of it applies only if the seat is in WA. ss 8, 9, 17H-17J, 35 and 36 apply wherever the seat is (s 1(2)). Asserted.
3. A court in which an arbitrable matter is sued must refer the parties to arbitration if a party asks no later than its first statement on the substance. The only escape is the court finding the agreement null and void, inoperative or incapable of being performed (s 8(1)). Asking later loses the right. Asserted.
4. The default is one arbitrator, not the Model Law's three (s 10(2)). If no procedure is agreed and the parties cannot agree on a sole arbitrator, the Court appoints. With three arbitrators and two parties, each party appoints one; the Court steps in only after 30 days of silence or deadlock (s 11(3)). Day 30 itself is still within time on this reading. Asserted.
5. A party who appointed the arbitrator, or took part in the appointment, may challenge only for reasons learned afterwards (s 12(4)). The test is a "real danger of bias" (s 12(6)), not the Model Law's justifiable doubts alone. Asserted.
6. The opportunity owed is "reasonable", not the Model Law's "full" (s 18, note). A requested oral hearing must be held unless the parties agreed there would be none (s 24(2)). Asserted.
7. A settlement becomes enforceable as an award only if it is reached during arbitral proceedings, the parties ask, and the tribunal does not object (s 30). A settlement reached by mediation under a contract, outside any arbitration, is not touched by this Act; it binds, if at all, as a contract or deed. This is d051. Asserted.
8. An arbitrator who has mediated may go on to arbitrate only with every party's written consent given after the mediation ends. Otherwise a substitute must be appointed (s 27D(4), (6)). Asserted.
9. Setting aside is the only recourse besides the s 34A appeal (s 34(1)). The grounds are the closed list in s 34(2). An award that is merely wrong stands. The application must be made within 3 months of receiving the award, or of the tribunal disposing of a s 33 request (s 34(3)). Asserted. Reading an application on the corresponding day as in time, and a period from 30 November as ending with February, are INFERENCES (the Interpretation Act 1984 was not read).
10. An appeal lies only on a question of law, and only if the parties agree to it before the 3-month appeal period ends and the Court grants leave. Leave must be refused unless the Court is satisfied of all four limbs of s 34A(3), including that the decision is "obviously wrong" or, on a question of general public importance, "at least open to serious doubt". Asserted.
11. An award made in any State or Territory is to be enforced on written application with the original or a copy (s 35). Enforcement may be refused only on the closed grounds of s 36(1). Asserted, with the s 36(1)(a) grounds grouped as one fact.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added on 6 November 2026, following the design of the Graffiti Vandalism Act 2016 layer (its D2, D4 and D8). The
layer is `caa-qa.l4`, which imports `prelude`, `daydate` and `caa-arbitration.l4`; its cases are `caa-qa-cases.l4`.
`daydate` is imported as the baseline's rule file imports it, only to build placeholder dates. Nothing in
`caa-arbitration.l4` or `caa-cases-arbitration.l4` was changed. The layer builds the baseline's records (`My arbitration
agreement`, `My appointment facts`, `How I was heard`, `My setting-aside application`, `My appeal`) from plain answers
and calls the baseline's functions. Every exported question takes `my role` first (`@desc Which of these describes
you`) and answers `does not arise for you` for a role it is not about, without reading any other input. No exported
question takes a DEONTIC or EVENT input.

**Roles.**

- `I am a party to an arbitration agreement or an arbitration`: the baseline's seat (21 questions: the baseline's 21).
- `I am an arbitrator, or have been asked to be one` (9 questions).

The arbitrator is a seat because the Act puts duties on them by name (s 12(1), (2), s 18, s 24, s 30(1), s 31(3),
s 33(2)), and they are a private person the parties choose, not a public official. The Court is not a seat. Where the
baseline already encodes a duty from the party's side (s 24(2), s 27D(1), (4)-(6), s 31(3)), the arbitrator's
question calls the same rule, and a mirror case asserts the two seats agree.

**Situation inputs.**

| Name | Type | Does not arise for | Questions that take it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `a court case has been brought about a dispute my arbitration agreement covers` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | s 8(1) (party) |
| `an arbitrator has been appointed in my arbitration` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | s 12 challenge, s 24(2) hearing, ss 18 and 24 denied hearing, s 27D(1) mediator (party) |
| `the arbitrator has acted as mediator, and the mediation has ended` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | s 27D(4)-(6), asked of both roles |
| `we have settled our dispute` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | s 30 settlement (party) |
| `the award and me` | `The award and me` | `no award has been made yet`, for all 8 award questions; also `an award has been made, against me` for the enforcing question, and `an award has been made, in my favour` for the resisting question | s 31(3), s 33(1), s 34(3), s 34, s 34A(3), s 34A, ss 35-36, s 36(1) (party) |
| `the parties have settled during the arbitral proceedings` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | s 30(1) (arbitrator) |
| `a party has asked me to correct or interpret the award` | BOOLEAN | FALSE | s 33(2) (arbitrator) |

Each situation is what the provision ties the rule to: "an action is brought" (s 8(1)), the arbitrator (ss 12, 18,
24, 27D(1)), mediation proceedings "that are terminated" (s 27D(4)), the settlement (s 30), the award (ss 31, 33, 34,
34A, 35, 36), and the request (s 33(2)). The questions about the agreement, the Act's reach, the number of arbitrators
and who appoints them arise for every party and take none. `The award and me` is read only through three helpers,
`no award has been made`, `there is no award in my favour to enforce` and `there is no award against me to resist`,
which list every value with no OTHERWISE (graffiti finding 31).

**Decisions.**

- **QA1. Where a situation is the baseline's own input, it is passed TRUE within the situation.** For s 8(1), the
  baseline's `the court action is in a matter the agreement covers` is the situation. Outside it, the question does
  not arise, where the baseline answered no.
- **QA2. An award partly in my favour and partly against me opens both the enforcing and the resisting question.**
  Setting aside, appeal, reasons and correction arise for any award, whichever way it went, since s 34(3) and
  s 34A(6) speak of "the party making that application" without regard to who won.
- **QA3. Placeholders.** Where a baseline rule reads only some fields of its record, the others get fixed values it
  never reads, each commented: the agreement's later fields for the s 7 questions, the seat for s 8 (which s 1(2)
  applies wherever the seat is), the grounds for the s 34(3) time question, and the dates and agreement for the
  s 34A(3) leave question (dates built with `Date 1 1 2000`).
- **QA4. The arbitrator's own rules are helpers in the layer.** They are `s 12(1), (2) -- the arbitrator must disclose
  the circumstances` (approached: disclose; appointed: disclose unless already told), `s 30(1) -- what the tribunal must
  do on a settlement`, and `s 33(2) -- the days the tribunal has to correct the award` (30). s 18 and s 24(3)-(5) are
  fixed answers for the role.
- **QA5. s 33(2) answers 0 days for a request the arbitrator does not consider justified.** The duty arises only "if
  the arbitral tribunal considers a request ... to be justified". The question text says what 0 means.

**Not covered for the arbitrator.** The challenge procedure (s 13), withdrawal and substitution (ss 14, 15) beyond the
s 27D(6) consequence, jurisdiction (s 16), interim measures (s 17 onwards), procedure and evidence (ss 19-27C),
confidentiality (ss 27E-27I), the form of the award beyond reasons (s 31(1), (2), (4)), the correction on the
arbitrator's own initiative and additional awards (s 33(4)-(7)), costs and interest (ss 33A-33F), and the arbitrator's
immunity (s 39), none of which the baseline encodes.

**Checks.** `caa-qa.l4` runs and `l4 check` succeeds, with 0 errors and 0 warnings. `caa-arbitration.l4` and
`caa-cases-arbitration.l4` still give 0 errors and 0 warnings, and 69 of 69 assertions. `caa-qa-cases.l4` has **150
assertions, 150 satisfied**:

- 102 worked by hand (80 party, 22 arbitrator). Of these, 21 assert the party's answer equal to the baseline export's
  on the same facts, one for each of the party's 21 questions. 3 mirror cases assert the arbitrator's answer equal to
  the party's (s 24(2), s 27D(1), s 31(3)).
- 28 ask each question for the role it is not about, with every other input ASSUMEd.
- 20 ask each situation question, for its role, with each value under which the situation does not hold and the rest
  ASSUMEd.

`@desc` wherever it appears (`the award and me` in 8 questions), and that no input is DEONTIC or EVENT. It also
generated the 48 role and situation cases.
