# Cat Act 2011 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There is no earlier encoding of this Act in this subject. A full encoding of the
whole Act and the Cat Regulations 2012 (`legalese-aswathy`, with an independent
test pass and a fork register) was moved out of Commons to
`solutions/Legislate.4/examples/wa/cat-act-2011/encodings/legalese-aswathy/`.
It was read for its findings and readings, and it was not modified. Its L4 was not
copied. Where this baseline takes one of its readings, the reading is named below
and in the module's comments.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Seat

**The cat owner**, who asks: "What must I do before my cat is six months old?"
Also: what does registration cost, how long does it last, and how many cats may I
keep? The Act puts its duties on "the owner of a cat" (ss 5, 6, 14, 18, 25), and
that is the person it is applied to.

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The Cat
  Regulations 2012 set the exemptions, the fees and the registration periods. The
  Uniform Local Provisions Regulations set the number-of-cats rule. Local laws
  themselves are not deposited, so a local law's cat limit is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the owner, the local government, the
  veterinarian, the microchip implanter, the approved cat breeder, and the bodies
  named in reg 9. Only the owner is asked questions.
- **Judgments handed to others are asked as inputs.** Followed:
  - s 9(2) turns on what the local government "is satisfied" of, so each ground
    is a fact. The "not refused" answer says it holds "if the local government is
    satisfied of none of the s 9(2) grounds".
  - s 23 turns on what the transferor "is satisfied" of, asked as a fact.
  - Pensioner status (Rates and Charges (Rebates and Deferments) Act 1992) is a
    fact.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed: "owner" (s 4) is
  worked out from how the person stands to the cat. "Transfer" (s 3) is glossed.
  "Microchipped" (s 3, reg 8) and "sterilised" (s 3) are facts, because the owner
  knows them.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one owner, one cat.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Partly departed from. These
  operative sections are left out because they address someone other than the
  owner and give the owner nothing to do:
  - ss 15-17, 19-21: implanters, database companies, veterinarians.
  - Part 3: control notices, seizure, cat management facilities, breeding
    approvals.
  - Part 4: enforcement and review.

## The lives

- **Chloe, c076 (14 February 2036): "A cat, Biscuit: sterilised, microchipped,
  registered".** The scenario asks: "What must an owner do before a cat is six
  months old?" Biscuit's date of birth is not in the scenario, so the cases assume
  1 October 2035, making Biscuit about four and a half months old on the event
  day. On those facts:
  - Biscuit reaches 6 months on 1 April 2036 (`When does my cat reach 6 months of
    age`).
  - On 14 February 2036, s 5 does not yet require registration, s 14 does not yet
    require a microchip, and s 18 does not yet require sterilisation. From 1 April
    2036 all three are required (`Must I have my cat registered`, `... microchipped`,
    `... sterilised`).
  - Registering Biscuit early, already microchipped and sterilised, gives no
    ground for refusal (`Must my registration be refused`).
  - A one-year grant applied for on 14 February 2036 costs at most $20, and it
    runs to 31 October 2036.

## Why this scope

The scenario cites ss 5-6, 14 and 18. The brief adds the fees and periods, the
identification rules, and the number of cats. Around them, the baseline adds the
owner-facing rules an ordinary owner meets next:

- who counts as the owner (s 4);
- whether the registration will be refused (s 9);
- giving a kitten away or selling it (s 23, which has no 6-month threshold);
- telling the local government and the database of a change (s 25);
- the fines.

Breeding (s 35 onward) is left out, except as the s 18(2)(b) exemption.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Nothing is required before six months.** The registration, microchip and
   sterilisation duties fall only on the owner of a cat "that has reached 6
   months of age". Before that day the owner need do nothing under ss 5, 14
   and 18. Asserted.
2. **A kitten that is not yet sterilised or microchipped must be refused
   registration.** s 9(2)(c)-(d) make refusal mandatory for a cat that is not
   microchipped or not sterilised. s 9(3)-(4) save only a cat "exempt" under
   s 14(2) or s 18(2), and a kitten is not exempt: a veterinarian's certificate
   "cannot apply" under 6 months (ss 14(3), 18(3)). It is merely not yet required.
   So an owner who registers early must have the kitten microchipped and
   sterilised first. This is the same finding as `legalese-aswathy`'s fork F-09,
   and whether local governments apply it so is a question for a domain expert.
   Asserted.
3. **The Act does not say when a cat born on the 29th-31st reaches 6 months** if
   the sixth month lacks that day. The baseline infers the 1st of the following
   month. Asserted for 31 August 2035 (1 March 2036).
4. **No class of cats is prescribed as exempt from sterilisation.** s 18(2)(c)
   and s 23(2)(a)(iii) allow it, but the Cat Regulations 2012 as deposited
   prescribe none. The only sterilisation exemptions are a veterinarian's
   certificate and an approved breeder's breeding cat.
5. **Registration exemptions follow custody, not ownership.** reg 9 exempts a cat
   in the custody of Cat Haven, the RSPCA WA, a cat management facility,
   veterinary premises or the Commonwealth biosecurity department. It also exempts
   a cat in foster care placed by Cat Haven or the RSPCA WA, or by a SAFE entity
   for at most 12 weeks in total. Asserted (SAFE foster care, 10 weeks).
6. **The fee is a ceiling.** Sch 3: one year costs $20, or $10 for a grant applied
   for after 31 May to run to the next 31 October. Three years cost $42.50 and
   life costs $100. A pensioner pays 50%, and a local government "may reduce or
   waive" any fee (cl 1(4)). "After 31 May" is read as 1 June to 31 October, the
   same reading as `legalese-aswathy` F-10. Asserted.
7. **Registration years end on 31 October.** A one-year registration ends at "the
   next 31 October" and a three-year one at the third. A registration taking
   effect on 31 October runs a full year (`legalese-aswathy` F-03, F-04).
   Renewal to take effect from 1 November may be made 11-31 October (reg
   12(2)(b)). Asserted.
8. **The tag duty is only in a public place.** It has one prescribed exemption,
   while the cat is being exhibited at a show held by a named cat body or an
   affiliate (reg 10). Asserted.
9. **Giving away an unsterilised kitten is lawful with a free sterilisation
   voucher.** s 23(2)(b) allows it, but there is no voucher alternative for a
   microchip (s 23(1)). A transfer to a body named in reg 9 is outside s 23
   altogether (reg 19). Asserted.
10. **The Act sets no number of cats.** It lets a local law limit them (s
    79(3)(h)). The Uniform Local Provisions Regulations then provide three things:
    kittens under 6 months are not counted (reg 5); the limit is tripled where a
    member of a named cat organisation lives at the premises (reg 7); and an
    approved additional number is a defence (reg 6(2)). Asserted.
11. **Every owner offence carries a $5,000 maximum fine.** Each has a $200
    infringement notice amount (Sch 2; ULP reg 13). Asserted for s 5(1) and ULP
    reg 6(1).
12. **Ownership turns on registration.** Once a cat is registered, its owner under
    s 4 is only the registered owner: s 4(1)(b) and (c) speak of "a cat that is
    not registered". A keeper of a registered cat that is registered to someone
    else is not its owner. Asserted.
