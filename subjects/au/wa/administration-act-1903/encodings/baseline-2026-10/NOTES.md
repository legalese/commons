# Administration Act 1903 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

It replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`. That encoding was read, and
its scope is covered and widened here. Its hand-checked figures are reused where
they are still right (the s 14 arithmetic on $1,072,000 and $1,005,000, the s 15
splits, the s 139 and s 55 answers for Jake's estate). Its L4 was not copied. It
used no `REFUSE`.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Edition

The compilation incorporating amendments up to the Administration Amendment Act
2022 (No. 5 of 2022, in operation 30 March 2022), as deposited at
`../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49096.txt` (retrieved 7 September 2026). The
deposit states no compilation number. The last reprint it lists is Reprint 12, as
at 15 April 2016. One uncommenced amendment is listed without its text: the
Assisted Reproductive Technology and Surrogacy Act 2025, Pt. 17 Div. 1.

**No subsidiary instrument is used, because none is deposited.** Three numbers
are therefore inputs:

- **The legacy sums in the s 14 Table.** These are set by the Minister's orders
  under s 14A. The deposit's note 8 names the Administration (Intestacy Legacy
  Amounts) Orders 2023 and 2025 but gives no figures. `the original sums` is what
  s 14A(1) states: $472 000, $705 000, $56 500 and $56 500.
- **The s 139 bank-deposit ceiling.** The text says $1 200 "or such other amount
  as may for the time being be declared by proclamation". Note 4 says it "is now
  declared to be $50 000".
- **The period the probate rules allow for an inventory and accounts (s 43).**

## Seat

**The heir or next of kin, and the executor or would-be administrator.** The Act
is applied to two kinds of individual:

- the family of a person who dies: who takes on an intestacy, how much, and what
  the surviving partner may claim;
- the person who must take out a grant: which grant, who may have it, what it
  carries, and what can happen to them if they delay or fail to account.

The Court, the Principal Registrar, the Minister and the bank are the other side.
Where a provision addresses only them, it is encoded as what it means for the
heir or executor:

- s 25 becomes `May I be granted administration`;
- s 44 becomes `What happens if I have not filed my accounts`;
- s 139 becomes `May the bank pay me without a grant`.

The decisions are phrased as these people ask them: `How much do I take`, `Which
item of the Table applies`, `May I elect to take the home`, `Which grant does the
estate need`, `Does the grant vest this asset in me`, `Have I lost my right to be
executor`, `How much interest am I owed on my legacy`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, instruments brought in.** Departed from, of necessity. Neither
  the s 14A orders nor the Non-Contentious Probate Rules 1967 are deposited, so
  their numbers are inputs. See Edition.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the husband or wife, the de facto
  partner, issue, parents, brothers and sisters, grandparents, uncles and aunts,
  the executor, the administrator, the Public Trustee, a creditor, the Principal
  Registrar, the ADI and its manager.
- **A judgment handed to someone else, answered conditionally.** Followed:
  - s 12A(2)(a): the relationship is recognised by the Act's rule "if the Court is
    satisfied" of the facts;
  - Fourth Schedule cl 2: `only if the Court so orders`;
  - Fourth Schedule cl 3: `only if the Court extends the 12 months`;
  - s 139: the relation is the one the manager is satisfied of.

  s 36 is a power ("may"), so `Which grant does the estate need` names the grant
  the Act points to, not the Court's decision.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked as facts.**
  Followed. These are worked out from facts:
  - "intestate property" (s 14(1));
  - "household chattels" and "net value" (s 14(2));
  - "entitled issue" and the division by lines (s 14(2a)-(2b), (3a));
  - "declared sum" and "original sum" (ss 14(1A), 14A(1));
  - the de facto partner's entitlement (s 15).

  These are asked as facts: survivorship and superannuation nominations (other
  law), "fit to be so entrusted", "willing and competent", and the cl 2 kinds of
  dwelling.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one death and one estate.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in comments beside
  each type and field.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Followed. Out as machinery: ss
  1-5, 14B (the Minister's review), 54, 56-60, 140-142 and 144. The operative
  sections left out are listed under "Why this scope".

## Why this scope

The lives need:

- the s 14 Table, with its legacy sums, the division by lines, and s 15;
- which grant a death calls for, and who may have it (ss 6, 7, 25, 33, 36);
- what a grant carries (s 8);
- the small-estate and bank-deposit routes (ss 55, 139);
- confirmation that this Act imposes no duty.

The heir and executor in those lives also meet these provisions, so they are
encoded:

- the surviving partner's household chattels (item 1);
- the s 14(4) interest on the legacy;
- the Fourth Schedule election to take the home;
- s 12A for a child of unmarried parents;
- s 13(1a) for land shared by heirs;
- the executor's loss of place (ss 32, 37);
- the duty to account and its follow-up (ss 43-44), with a regulative rule for
  s 43(1)(b);
- the beneficiary's remedy (s 42);
- the legacy interest rate (s 143A);
- the will in evidence (s 71).

Left out, with the reason:

- **Operative, but not met in these lives:** insolvent estates (s 10A, Fifth
  Schedule); s 14(5) income; s 14(7) partition; s 15(4) two or more de facto
  partners; infants' shares (ss 17, 17A); sureties (s 26); revocation (s 29);
  attorneys, receivers and special letters (ss 34-35, 38-41); ss 45-47A; resealing
  of foreign grants (Part III); caveats (Part IV); and how the Fourth Schedule
  election is made and valued (cll 4-8).
- **In other Acts:** family provision claims (preserved by s 60A), survivorship,
  will validity, superannuation and the Public Trustee Act 1941.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Section 25 is not about probate, and Part IV is caveats.** Seven of the ten
   events cite "s 25" for a death with a will. But s 25 governs only "the estate
   of a person dying intestate". Probate comes from s 6 and s 7. Where there is no
   fit executor, s 33 or s 36 gives administration with the will annexed instead.
   Part IV, cited in c087, is "Caveats" (ss 63-64). Asserted.

2. **Sue's estate: half each, as the lives expect, and the house held as tenants
   in common.** Item 5 and s 14(2b)(b) divide the estate "into as many equal parts
   as there are children" who survived or left surviving issue. Under s 13(1a),
   land held on the statutory trust for two or more is held "as if it had been
   devised to those persons as tenants in common". Asserted.

3. **Jake's estate: Ruby takes all and may administer it. The Act gives the Public
   Trustee no route while she is available.** "Any other person" may be granted
   administration only if no entitled person is "resident within the jurisdiction
   and fit to be so entrusted", or that person "fails, when duly cited, to
   appear". The scenario's Public Trustee therefore rests on the Public Trustee
   Act 1941, not this Act. The s 55 small-estate route is for property "not
   exceeding $10 000". Asserted.

4. **The bank may pay without a grant: $1,200 in the text, $50,000 in the notes.**
   Under s 139, an ADI may pay a widow, widower, parent, child or de facto partner
   once a month has passed, if in that month no grant was produced and no notice
   of a will or application was given. Whether Jake's $38,000 is a bank deposit is
   the encoder's assumption. The apartment still needs a grant: s 8 vests real
   estate only "upon the grant". Asserted for the deposit.

5. **The legacy sums come from Minister's orders under s 14A, not from the
   Administration Regulations 1913.** The plan names the 1913 Regulations as
   setting the spouse's prescribed amount. The Act names neither them nor anything
   they prescribe. The Table's sums are "original sums" (s 14A(1)), replaced by a
   declared sum in an order "that has effect at the time the intestate dies"
   (s 14(1B)). A declared sum is indexed to average weekly earnings and "rounded up
   to the nearest $500" (s 14A(3)). The 2023 and 2025 Orders are not deposited.
   For deaths in 2052-2088 the real figures will be far above the originals.
   Asserted with the original sums and one invented declared sum.

6. **The statutory legacy, worked.** A spouse and two children sharing $1,072,000
   (chattels aside):
   - the spouse takes $472,000 plus one third of the $600,000 residue, $672,000;
   - each child takes $200,000.

   With one child, or the issue of one dead child, s 14(3) reads the residue split
   as halves: $772,000 for the spouse. Item 3 is the case of a spouse, both parents
   and a sister with $1,005,000:
   - the spouse takes $855,000;
   - the "other half" of $150,000 exceeds $56,500, so the parents take $56,500 plus
     half the rest, $51,625 each;
   - the sister takes $46,750.

   s 14(4) adds 5% a year on the unpaid legacy. Asserted.

7. **De facto partners: two years to take, five to displace a spouse (s 15).** A
   de facto partner of at least two years, with no spouse, takes what a spouse
   would take. Where the deceased had a spouse but did not live with them during
   that period:
   - after two years, the de facto partner and the spouse take half each;
   - after five years, the de facto partner takes it all.

   If the deceased lived with the spouse during the period, the de facto partner
   takes nothing. A partner of one year takes nothing. Asserted.

8. **The surviving partner may take the home only when not taking everything.**
   The Fourth Schedule election is open only if the partner "is not entitled under
   sections 14 and 15 to the whole of the intestate property". It is also closed
   for a tenancy that could end within 2 years. It must be made within 12 months
   of the first grant unless the Court extends that time. A farmhouse, part of a
   building, a lodging house or partly business premises needs a Court order.
   Asserted.

9. **A WA grant carries only WA property that has not passed outside the
   estate.** s 6 and s 8 reach property "in Western Australia". Giuseppe's house
   in Calabria (d074) is beyond this Act whatever Italian law does with it. The
   houses that passed by survivorship and the super that passed by nomination
   (c087, c096, d093) are not carried by the grant. That they pass outside the
   estate is other law, asked as a fact. Asserted.

10. **No death duty in this Act.** Part V keeps its heading, "Duties on deceased
    persons' estates and succession duties". But every charging section (ss
    65-70A, 72-137) is "Deleted: No. 80 of 1973 s. 3". Only s 71 remains: a will
    is not admissible before a grant except in criminal proceedings, on an
    application for a grant, or in proceedings on its validity. Asserted.

11. **A will that disposes of everything leaves nothing for the Table (k084).**
    s 14(1) distributes only "the property as to which they die intestate". Any
    Family Provision Act 1972 claim is preserved by s 60A and is outside this
    encoding. Asserted.

12. **An executor who sits on the will can be passed over, and one who does not
    account is chased.**
    - Under s 37, an executor who neither obtains nor renounces probate within 2
      months of the death (or of turning 18) exposes the estate to a grant of
      administration with the will annexed to any interested person or creditor.
    - Under s 44, one month after the rules' period expires the Principal
      Registrar must have the executor notified, and after a further month must
      apply for an order. The rules' period itself is not deposited.

    Asserted.

13. **A typographical slip in the deposit.** s 14(4)(a) prints "$$472 000". Not
    asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Retrieve and deposit the Administration (Intestacy Legacy Amounts) Orders 2023
  and 2025. Then decide what sums to use for deaths in 2052-2088, which the
  indexation formula of s 14A(3) cannot settle in advance.
- Deposit the Non-Contentious Probate Rules 1967, for the accounts period and the
  grant procedure. Check whether the Administration Regulations 1913 the plan
  names still exist and what they prescribe.
- Encode the Public Trustee Act 1941 route that k098 relies on.
- Encode Fourth Schedule cll 4-8 and s 15(4).
- Read the uncommenced 2025 surrogacy amendment, which may change s 12A.
- Survivorship (Transfer of Land Act 1893), will validity (Wills Act 1970) and
  family provision belong in their own encodings. The lives' answers on the house
  and the super come from there.
- s 14(4) interest is simple interest on the sum unpaid for the time given. Part
  payments and appropriation dates are not modelled.
- The s 43(1)(b) regulative rule is declared but not traced. No case law or
  Supreme Court probate practice was consulted.
