# Residential Tenancies Act 1987 (WA) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18.
No REFUSE, no BECAUSE.
No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate.
Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** the compilation published on legislation.wa.gov.au (mrdoc 49316), "Residential Tenancies Act 1987 with editorial changes as at 29 Apr 2026", deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49316.txt`.
The deposit gives no compilation number.
Its compilation table ends with the Statutes (Repeals and Minor Amendments) Act 2025 s 35 (in force 26 September 2025).
It shows the Residential Tenancies Amendment Act 2024 Pt 2 in force in three tranches: 16 May 2024, 29 July 2024 (which includes s 25, the new s 30) and 28 March 2026 (which includes s 43, the new Part 5A on bond release).

The numbers come from the **Residential Tenancies Regulations 1989**, consolidated version 05-ad0-00 (currency start 3 September 2026), deposited at `../../../residential-tenancies-regulations-1989/registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_49840.txt`:

- reg 10AB and reg 10ABA: the information a tenant must be given;

## Seat

**The tenant.**
The Act is applied to the tenant: what they may be charged, where their bond goes, when the rent may rise, what they must be handed, how the tenancy may be ended against them, and how they end it.
The plan entry puts the seat in the tenant's words: "Where must my bond go, how often may rent rise, what must I be given, and how may the tenancy end?"
Every exported question is phrased that way.
Most of the operative provisions are addressed to the lessor or the property manager (ss 27B, 27C, 29(4), 30, 61-64). Each is encoded as what it means for the tenant: a deadline they can hold the lessor to, a limit on what they can be asked for, or the effect of a notice they have been given.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The deposited Regulations supply every dollar figure and the bond-payment rule. Other Acts are not brought in: the Strata Titles Act for "strata lease", the Restraining Orders Act for family violence, and the Interpretation Act 1984 for the reckoning of days.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the tenant, the lessor, the property manager, the bond administrator, the Commissioner and a competent court.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed. These are asked as facts or left to the court:
  - whether a court authorised a notice under s 65(2);
  - whether the tenant "reasonably believes" the lessor's action was retaliatory (s 26B(2));
  - what a court orders under s 26B(4) or s 64(4): the encoding says only that the tenant may apply, and by when;
  - the Commissioner's decision on a disputed bond (s 81R): the encoding says only that the application goes to the Commissioner.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed.
  - Worked out from facts: "residential tenancy agreement" (s 3), "expiry day", the s 5(4) presumption against a holiday letting, and "continuation" of an agreement (s 31B).
  - Asked as facts: "boarder or lodger", "hotel", "club", "strata lease" and "fair wear and tear", none of which the Act defines.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one residential tenancy agreement, or one notice given under it.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments and `@desc` lines: "lessor", "security bond", "bond administrator", "expiry day", "possession day".
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed. Left out:
  - Part II (the Commissioner, investigations);
  - Part III's procedure;
  - s 88 (regulations);
  - the Schedule 1 accounts;
  - the transitional Part 7, except s 99. That section decides which s 30 governs an older fixed term.

## Why this scope

The plan entry names s 27C, s 29, s 30 and ss 61-64. The seat asks four things: the bond, rent rises, what must be given, and how the tenancy ends. So the encoding also covers:

- s 5, whether the Act applies at all;
- ss 27, 28, what may be charged up front;
- s 27B, the prescribed information;
- s 31, the bond rising with the rent;
- s 31B, a renewed lease does not restart the 12 months;
- s 99, older fixed terms;
- s 26B, retaliation;
- ss 60, 65, 70A, 76C and 80, which give the termination notices their effect;
- ss 67-69, the tenant's own notice;
- ss 81C-81K, getting the bond back.

Not encoded:

- s 27A and s 27AA;
- s 28(2);
- s 29(3), (6), (9);
- s 31A (an increase where rent is set by income returns "not covered");
- s 32 (excessive rent), except as a fact for s 65;
- ss 33-36;
- the standard terms of Part IV Divisions 2, 2A and 2B (repairs, entry, quiet enjoyment, pets, modifications), except the pet amount of the bond;
- Part V Divisions 2A (family violence), 3 (social housing) and 4 (court orders);
- ss 76-79 (abandonment) and ss 81A-81B;
- Part 5A Division 3 and s 81T;
- Part VIA (tenancy databases);
- service under s 85.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The bond never sits with the lessor.**
   s 29(4)(b) requires whoever receives the bond to pay it to the bond administrator "as soon as practicable, and in any event within 14 days". The penalty is a fine of $20 000, the highest in Division 1.
   The receipt must state the date, the payer, the amount and the premises (s 29(4)(a)).
   Chloe's bond received on 1 September 2027 must reach the bond administrator by 15 September 2027, and Jake's received on 1 November 2033 by 15 November 2033. Asserted.
2. **Where a property manager is involved, the bond goes through BondsOnline.**
   Under reg 11A(3) it goes through BondsOnline unless the tenant lacks an exclusive mobile number, an exclusive email address, internet access, competence with them, or "a sound understanding of the English language" (reg 11A(4)), or the bond administrator agrees otherwise. Asserted.
3. **Four weeks' rent, plus $350 for a pet, and no limit above $1 200 a week.**
   - Chloe can be asked for at most $1 920.
   - Jake can be asked for at most $1 680 (not asserted), or $2 030 if permitted a cat.
   - At $1 250 a week, s 29(2) and reg 11 lift the limit entirely, though s 29(1)(a) still allows only one bond (encoded, not asserted).

   Up-front rent is capped at two weeks (s 28(1)): $960 for Chloe. Asserted.
4. **Once in 12 months, on 60 days' notice, since 29 July 2024.**
   s 30(1) as amended by the 2024 Act requires both:
   - a start day at least 60 days after the notice;
   - a start day at least 12 months after the tenancy began or the rent last rose.

   For Chloe, a notice given on 1 July 2028 can take effect on 1 September 2028, the later of the two days. A notice for 15 August 2028 fails the 12 months even though it gives 75 days' notice.
   During a fixed term there can be no increase unless the agreement sets out the amount or the method (s 30(2)(a)).
   s 31B stops a lessor restarting the clock with a new lease to the same tenant. Asserted.
5. **Fixed terms signed before 29 July 2024 keep the old rule, which is not in the deposit.**
   s 99 continues "Section 30, as in force from time to time before the commencement" until the current term ends. The deposit does not reproduce the old section, so the encoding returns "not covered" for such a term. Asserted.
6. **The bond can rise only with the rent, or with a pet.**
   Under s 31, as widened by reg 5CB, the extra amount is payable no earlier than 60 days after the bond notice and not before the rent rise. The new total is capped at four weeks of the new rent plus $350.
   s 31(2) has no exception for rents above $1 200, unlike s 29(2). Read literally, a high-rent bond that started above four weeks cannot be raised at all. That is an INFERENCE, not asserted. The rest is asserted.
7. **The lessor's notice periods: 7, 30, 60, and 30 at the end of a fixed term.**
   - 7 days for an unremedied breach (s 62(2)), after a 14-day notice to remedy for any breach other than rent (s 62(3));
   - 30 days after a contract of sale with vacant possession (s 63);
   - 60 days with no ground (s 64);
   - 30 days before the possession day to end a fixed term (s 70A), and never earlier than the expiry day.

   A no-ground or sale notice cannot end a fixed term during its currency (ss 63(4), 64(5)).
   A no-ground notice is ineffectual while excessive-rent proceedings are pending (s 65). Asserted, except the 30 days on a sale, which is encoded but not asserted.
8. **A rent-arrears notice can be given on the day of the breach, and paying up stops it.**
   s 62(5) displaces the 14-day prior notice for rent. If the rent is paid in full before the possession day, the lessor may not apply to court.
   The encoding reads any rent notice without a prior 14-day notice as a s 62(5) notice. That is an INFERENCE from "may be given immediately the breach occurs". Asserted.
9. **A notice does not end the tenancy; leaving, or a court, does.**
   s 60(1)(a) ends the agreement only when the tenant gives vacant possession or a court terminates it.
   s 80 forbids anyone to enter to recover possession without a court order, "whether entry is effected peaceably or otherwise", on pain of a $20 000 fine. Asserted.
10. **Without a ground, the tenant gives 21 days, but not during a fixed term.**
    - s 68 gives 21 days, and s 68(3) excludes it during a fixed term.
    - s 69(3) gives 2 days where the premises are destroyed or uninhabitable, even during a fixed term (the 2 days encoded, not asserted).
    - s 70A gives 30 days to end a fixed term at its expiry.

    Chloe and Liam's notice given on 1 July 2029 for 22 July 2029 is valid; for 21 July it is not. Asserted.
11. **A fixed term does not simply end.**
    Without a s 70A notice the term does not end on the expiry day (s 70A(2)), and it continues as a periodic tenancy on the same terms (s 76C(2)).
    Whether a periodic tenancy continued under s 76C is still "during the currency" of the fixed term for s 30(2)(a) is not said. The encoding treats the currency as ending on the expiry day. That is an INFERENCE. Asserted for Chloe's 1 September 2028 rise.
12. **The condition report: 7 days for the lessor, then 7 days for the tenant, or the tenant is taken to agree.**
    - s 27C(1): two copies within 7 days of moving in. Jake moves in on 1 November 2033, so by 8 November.
    - s 27C(3): if the tenant does not give back a marked copy within 7 days of receiving it, they are "taken to accept the report as a true and accurate description".
    - s 27C(4): a final report within 14 days after the tenancy ends.

    The encoding treats a copy returned late as not returned "under subsection (2)". That is an INFERENCE. Asserted.
13. **The option-fee table in reg 10AD overlaps.**
    The $100 row (weekly rent over $500) and the $1 200 row (south of the 26th parallel, $1 200 or more) both describe a $1 300 Perth tenancy. The encoding takes the more particular $1 200 row. That is an INFERENCE. Asserted.
14. **Bond release since 28 March 2026.**
    A tenant cannot be asked to sign a release form before the agreement ends, unless:
    - all tenants have left and the parties agreed in writing to end it; or
    - it is a partial release after a rent reduction or the end of a pet.

    In every case the form must state the amounts (s 81C(5), $5 000 penalty).
    If every known party applies or agrees, the bond administrator must pay as stated (s 81K). Otherwise the application goes to the Commissioner (s 81I).
    Fair wear and tear is expressly outside the lessor's s 81E(1) entitlements. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- Settle whether s 30(2)(a) reaches a fixed term continued under s 70A(5) or s 76C.
- Encode the standard terms (Part IV Division 2), pets and modifications (Divisions 2A, 2B), family violence (Part V Division 2A), court orders (Division 4), abandonment, and the Commissioner's bond decisions (Part 5A Division 3).

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added 6 November 2026, following the Graffiti Vandalism Act encoding's design (D2, D4, D8). Nothing in `rta-renting.l4` or `rta-cases-renting.l4` was changed: the layer is added, nothing is replaced.

- **Files.** `rta-qa.l4` (imports `prelude`, `daydate` and `rta-renting`) and `rta-qa-cases.l4` (imports those, `rta-qa`, and `rta-cases-renting` for its fixtures only; an imported module's assertions are not run).
- **Roles** (`DECLARE Role`). Every exported question takes `my role` first, with `@desc Which of these describes you`:
  - `I am the tenant: I rent the home`. This is the baseline's seat. All 35 baseline exports are asked. Each calls the baseline function and wraps its answer (`yes`/`no`, `amount`, `by`/`on` a date, `at least`/`up to` days, `the answer is` for the baseline's enumerations).
  - `I am the lessor, or the property manager acting for the lessor`. ss 27, 27B, 27C, 28, 29, 31, 61-64, 80 and 81C put the duties on "a person", "a lessor or property manager" or "the lessor and property manager". 33 questions: the same rules from the other side, plus the fine for the rules a lessor most often meets.
  - Not seats: the bond administrator, the Commissioner and the court. What they must or may do (pay the bond as stated, decide a dispute, extend a notice) appears as what it means for the tenant or the lessor.
- **Situation inputs.** All are BOOLEAN and all come straight after `my role`. FALSE gives `does not arise for you`, and nothing else is read. Each has one name, type and @desc for both roles, so the form asks it once.

  | Name | Questions (tenant / lessor) |
  |---|---|
  | `the lessor has given a notice of termination` | what the notice does; by when the tenant may ask a court to extend a no-grounds notice; the most extra time (s 64(3), (4)): 3 / 3 |
  | `the tenant has given a notice of termination` | is the tenant's notice valid: 0 / 1 |
  | `the tenancy is for a fixed term` | the last day to give notice for the expiry day; what happens at expiry; which possession day counts (s 70A): 3 / 3 |
  | `the tenancy has ended` | the final condition report (s 27C(4)): 1 / 1 |
  | `the tenant has been given the condition report` | the tenant's 7 days to return a marked copy; taken to accept (s 27C(2), (3)): 2 / 1 |
  | `a rent increase notice has been given or is proposed` | does the increase take effect (ss 30, 99): 1 / 1 |
  | `a bond increase has been proposed` | may the bond rise as proposed (s 31): 1 / 1 |
  | `a bond release application has been made` | what happens to it (ss 81I, 81K): 1 / 1 |

  Each situation is what the Act ties the rule to: a notice of termination (s 61 onwards), a fixed term (s 70A), the termination (s 27C(4)), copies of the report given (s 27C(2)), a notice of increase (s 30(1)), a bond notice (s 31(1A)), an application (s 81C). Some questions arise in every situation of their role and take none: the tenant's own "Is my notice to end the tenancy valid", the earliest day the rent can rise, what may be charged, the bond limit and the notice periods.
- **Decisions.**
  - The lessor's questions take the baseline's records (`My arrangement`, `My tenancy, for a rent increase`, `My lessor's notice`, `My tenancy, for a notice`, `My bond increase`) and enumerations (`My lessor's ground`, `A claim on my bond`, `A payment asked of me`, `My ground for ending`). Their items are worded as the tenant would answer them, and the @desc says so. Restating the records from the lessor's side would restate the baseline's rules, which the brief bars. A lessor-worded form is for a later pass.
  - Two baseline answers are worded for the tenant ("my lessor must apply to a court"). The lessor gets them reworded value for value (`What becomes of the rent increase I give`, `What my notice of termination does`), through helpers that list every value with no OTHERWISE (graffiti finding 31). Neutral answers (where the bond goes, the end of a fixed term, the bond application) are wrapped unchanged.
  - The lessor's fine question takes an enumeration of nine rules (`A rule the Act puts on me`) and gives the Act's penalty: $5 000 for ss 27(1), 27B, 27C(1), 27C(4), 28(1), 29(1) and 81C(5), and $20 000 for ss 29(4) and 80. These are new values, read from the deposit.
  - Some baseline inputs are worded "I" or "my" (the day I moved in, the day I received the copies). The layer renames them to name the tenant, so both roles share one input where they ask the same fact.
  - `Date answer` has the graffiti shape plus `on` a day, for "the earliest day" and "which possession day counts". `Days` has `at least` (a notice period) and `up to` (the court's extension).
- **Not covered for the lessor:**
