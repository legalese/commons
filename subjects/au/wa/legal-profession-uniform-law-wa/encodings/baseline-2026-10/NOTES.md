# Legal Profession Uniform Law (WA) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the
individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE: a case the encoding does not
cover returns a value that says so. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public
use as advice. There was no first-pass encoding of this subject.

**Edition:** two instruments, each named in the comments and the exported questions.

- **Uniform Law** — the Legal Profession Uniform Law (WA), legislation.wa.gov.au mrdoc 46913, retrieved 7 September 2026,
  deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/mrdoc_46913.txt`. The deposit does not show its as-at date.
- **General Rules** — the Legal Profession Uniform General Rules 2015, mrdoc 45732, version 00-a0-02, current from 1 July 2022,
  deposited in the subject `wa/legal-profession-uniform-general-rules-2015`.

Two numbers are inputs because they are not deposited:

- the value of a penalty unit, which s 6 takes from "an Act of Victoria";
- the Reserve Bank's Cash Rate Target (General Rules r 75).

The Continuing Professional Development Rules are not deposited either, so s 52 appears only as a comment.

**Checks:** three modules (`lpul-practice.l4`, `lpul-trust.l4`, `lpul-costs.l4`) and three case files. 69 assertions are
satisfied (25 practice, 15 trust, 29 costs), with 0 errors and 0 warnings. The checks used the stable `l4` of VS Code extension
1.6.98, and `runcases.py` gave written = satisfied for each file. The case files are generated by the scratchpad script
`gen_wa_legal-profession-uniform-law-wa_baseline.py`. Every expected value in it was written by hand from the text.

## How this baseline and the Application Act baseline divide the ground

The Application Act baseline (`../../../legal-profession-uniform-law-application-act-2022/encodings/baseline-2026-10/`)
already brings in the Uniform Law and the two sets of Uniform Rules wherever Daniel's events ask about them. Its scope is:

- admission (ss 16-19, Admission Rules);
- "Australian lawyer" and the day of admission (Sch 4 cl 11);
- the grant, renewal, currency and carry-over of the practising certificate (ss 44, 45, Sch 4 cl 12, General Rules rr 17, 18);
- `May I engage in legal practice` (ss 6, 10, 43, General Rules r 10), with the s 10 penalties;
- titles (s 12(1), (2), General Rules r 9);
- supervised legal practice (s 49, General Rules rr 7, 14);
- whether Part 4.3 governs a matter (Sch 4 cl 18(1));
- costs agreements: conditional costs agreements, uplift fees, contingency fees, voidness (ss 180-185).

**None of that is repeated here.** This baseline takes the rest of the Uniform Law the lawyer meets:

- **Holding out:** s 11 and the s 12(3), (4) presumptions, with "qualified entity" worked out.
- **What my certificate allows:**
  - its category of practice (s 47);
  - whether I am a solicitor or a barrister (s 6);
  - whether my firm may receive trust money (ss 47(2), 150);
  - one certificate only (s 48(2));
  - the barrister's reading program (s 50);
  - what I must report and by when (ss 51, 86, 88, General Rules r 15);
  - the civil penalty for breaching a condition (s 54).
- **The client's money:**
  - what is trust money (s 129);
  - the warning for money that is not (s 134);
  - where each kind of money must go (ss 137-143);
  - protection from the firm's creditors (s 145);
  - paying the firm's bill out of trust (s 144(2)(b), General Rules r 42);
  - trust account statements (General Rules r 52).
- **Legal costs, other than costs agreements:**
  - commercial and government clients (s 170, General Rules r 71);
  - the client's right to a costs agreement (s 179);
  - disclosure and its thresholds (s 174, Sch 4 cl 18(3), (4), General Rules r 72);
  - disclosure before a settlement (s 177);
  - what follows a failure to disclose (s 178, General Rules r 72A);
  - when an agreement is prima facie evidence (s 172(4));
  - itemised bills, progress reports and the free bill (ss 187, 190, 191);
  - suing for costs (ss 194, 198(7)(b));
  - interest (s 195, General Rules r 75);
  - costs assessment (ss 197, 198, 204);
  - when an unfair bill is a conduct matter (ss 188, 207).

The two meet in one place, and neither repeats the other's logic. Whether an arbitration is a "litigious matter" is decided
there (`Is my matter a litigious matter`) and asked here as a fact for s 177.

## Seat

**The lawyer: Daniel in the Western Australian lives**, an employee solicitor at Swan & Partners from 2020 to 2031. The plan
entry gives his question: "What may I do only once admitted and certified, and what costs agreements may I make?" The Uniform
Law is applied to him. It decides what he may say about himself, the capacity he may practise in, what he must report, what he
and his firm must do with a client's money, what he must tell a client about costs, and when a bill can be held against him.
The Legal Practice Board (the designated local regulatory authority in WA), costs assessors and the designated tribunal only
administer it.

Most of Parts 4.2 and 4.3 put duties on the "law practice" and give rights to the "client". Daniel meets them as an associate:
the firm's duties are what he must do, and the client's rights are what he must allow for. So the questions read:

- `Must my firm warn the client that this is not trust money`
- `Can my client still ask for an itemised bill`
- `May my firm sue for its costs`
- `Can my client have my costs assessed`

How each 2026-10-18 default was applied:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers or dates — followed.** The General Rules set the disclosure forms,
  the reportable offences, the trust withdrawal procedure and the interest rate, so they are brought in. The thresholds
  ($750 / $3000) come from the Law itself (Sch 4 cl 18(3), (4)). The Admission Rules are not needed here.
- **The actors the text names — followed.** The lawyer, the law practice, the principal and responsible principal, the client,
  the Board, the costs assessor and the designated tribunal.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally — followed.** These are inputs, and the answer turns on them:
  - the Board's exemptions and directions (s 50);
  - the satisfaction required by General Rules r 72A;
  - the costs assessor's view that the firm should not pay (s 204(2));
  - the designated tribunal's "just and fair" finding (s 198(4));
  - the Board's s 152 determination.
- **The Act's own definitions are worked out; borrowed terms are asked as facts — followed.**
  - Worked out: "qualified entity" (paras (a), (b), (d)), "solicitor", "barrister", "trust money", "non-trust money" (s 134(2)),
    "notifiable summary offence" and "commercial or government client" (most limbs).
  - Asked as facts: "indictable", "bankruptcy-related event", "business day" counts, and "litigious matter".
- **One case is the text's natural unit — followed.** One certificate, one sum of money, one bill, one matter.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a gloss — followed.**
- **Operative sections in; machinery out — followed.** Out:
  - the Council, the Commissioner and the Board's own machinery;
  - external examinations and investigations;
  - fidelity fund administration;
  - registers;
  - Chapter 5 procedure.

## Why this scope

The plan entry for this subject names ss 10, 16-25, 43-49, 181 and 183, and Part 2.2. Every one of those is already encoded in
the Application Act baseline, and the brief directs that they not be duplicated. What is left is what the brief names, plus
what the lawyer meets as a matter of course:

- unqualified holding out;
- certificate classes and conditions;
- costs disclosure and the client's rights on costs, including costs assessment;
- trust money as it affects the client.

Sections 172(1)-(3) and 200 are left out because they are lists of factors for the assessor's judgment, not rules that can be
worked out. Third party payers (ss 171, 176) are left out because they are a second client relationship the lives do not reach.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Admitted is not qualified, and saying otherwise is its own offence.** Between admission and certificate, Daniel is an
   Australian lawyer but not a "qualified entity" (s 6). Representing that he may practise is an offence of up to 250 penalty
   units (s 11(1)), separate from practising (s 10). Using "solicitor" or "lawyer" raises a rebuttable presumption that he made
   that representation (s 12(3)). An employee who says the same of an unqualified entity faces up to 50 penalty units
   (s 11(2)). Asserted.
2. **An employee certificate lets him work in-house only until it is renewed.** s 47(4) lets an employee certificate cover
   corporate and government practice "Until the Australian practising certificate concerned is renewed". A principal
   certificate covers both permanently (s 47(3)). Every (a) or (b) category also covers pro bono work (s 47(5)). Asserted.
3. **The firm's authority to hold trust money turns on a principal's certificate, not on Daniel's** (s 150). Asserted.
4. **A conviction for a serious offence is not reported under s 51.** s 51(3) removes "an offence to which section 86
   applies". s 86 makes a conviction for a serious or tax offence an automatic show cause event. That event needs a notice
   within 7 days and a statement within 28 (s 88(3)). A charge for one is still a s 51 matter. A bankruptcy-related event is
   both, because s 51(3) lifts only offences. Asserted.
5. **The General Rules' drink-driving limb names only New South Wales and Victorian Acts.** General Rules r 15(4)(d) names the
   Road Transport Act 2013 (NSW) and the Road Safety Act 1986 (Vic). A WA drink-driving conviction is therefore reportable only
   if it brought imprisonment, a licence disqualification, or a maximum of 6 months' imprisonment or more (r 15(4)(a)-(c)).
   A WA speeding fine is not reportable. Asserted, on hypothetical maximum penalties.
6. **The disclosure thresholds are still the transitional $750 and $3000.** s 174(4), (5) leave the amounts to the Uniform
   Rules. The deposited General Rules prescribe none, and their r 72 Note points back to Sch 4 cl 18(3), (4). "Not likely to
   exceed" is read as at or below. Asserted.
7. **A commercial or government client loses more than disclosure.** s 170(1) disapplies the whole of Part 4.3 except s 170 and
   the listed parts of ss 181-185. That removes:
   - the right to a negotiated costs agreement (s 179);
   - progress reports (s 190);
   - read literally, costs assessment under s 198.

   But s 198(5) deals with late applications by third party payers whom s 170(1)(b) has already removed from the Part. That
   is a tension the text does not resolve. The literal reading is encoded and asserted. It needs authority.
8. **A disclosure failure can be cured within 14 days, but only if a decision-maker is satisfied.** s 178(1) voids the costs
   agreement, suspends the client's liability until assessment, bars suit and exposes those involved. General Rules r 72A
   lifts all of that if the Board, an assessor, a court or a tribunal is satisfied of three things:
   - the firm took reasonable steps;
   - it rectified the failure within 14 days of becoming aware of it;
   - the failure was not substantial.

   Asserted, conditionally.
9. **A client who objects to a bill still loses the trust money after 30 days unless he takes it further.** Under General
   Rules r 42(3), the firm may pay itself from trust in two cases:
   - 7 business days after a bill that refers to the withdrawal, if there is no objection;
   - if the client objects but does not refer the bill to the Board or to assessment, once 30 days have passed after the later
     of the bill and any itemised bill.

   Written instructions plus a prior notice also suffice (r 42(4)). Asserted.
10. **The firm may not sue until 30 days after the later of the bill and the itemised bill** (s 194(2)(b)). A pending
    assessment (s 198(7)(b)) or a disclosure failure (s 178(1)(c)) bars suit until it is resolved. Asserted.
11. **Interest is capped at the Cash Rate Target on the bill date plus 2 points** (General Rules r 75). The bill must state the
    rate (s 195(3)). Without a costs agreement term, the costs must have been unpaid for 30 days (s 195(2)). Asserted, on a
    hypothetical 4.1% Cash Rate Target.
12. **The client has 12 months from the bill to apply for assessment** (s 198(3)). After that, only the designated tribunal can
    let a late application through (s 198(4)). The firm pays the assessment costs if its costs are cut by 15% or more, or if
    it failed to disclose, unless the assessor thinks that unfair (s 204(2)). Asserted at exactly 15% and at 14%.
13. **An employee who helps give an unfair bill has no defence; the responsible principal does.** s 207(1)(b) reaches "each
    legal practitioner associate ... involved in giving the bill". The s 207(3) defence (no reasonable ground to suspect) is
    given only to a responsible principal. Daniel, as an employee who sends the bill, is exposed without it. Asserted.

## Q&A layer (seat and situations as parameters)

Added on 6 November 2026, following the graffiti encoding's design (D2, D4, D8).

- **Files.** The layer is `lpul-qa.l4` and its cases are in `lpul-qa-cases.l4`.
- **Imports.** It imports `prelude`, `daydate` and the three baseline modules: `lpul-practice`, `lpul-trust` and
  `lpul-costs`. It changes none of their rules or exports.
- **The role comes first.** Every exported question takes `my role` first ("Which of these describes you"). For the role a
  question is not about, the answer is `does not arise for you`, and no other input is read.

**Roles.**

- `I am a lawyer: admitted to the legal profession, practising or about to`. This is the baseline's seat (Daniel). The
  role is admitted by its own terms, so the baseline's `I am an Australian lawyer` is passed TRUE.
- `I am the client of a law practice`. Parts 4.2 and 4.3 give the client its rights to trust money, costs disclosure, bills
  and costs assessment. The client is the other person who would ask the Law questions.

Some people get no seat:

- The Board, costs assessors and the designated tribunal are never seats.
- A principal is not a seat of their own. The baseline already asks a principal's facts as inputs of the lawyer's
  questions (the responsible principal in s 207, a principal's certificate in s 150).

**Situation inputs.** Each comes straight after `my role`. It has the same name, type and `@desc` wherever it is taken, in
both roles.

- **`I hold a current practising certificate`** (BOOLEAN).
  - FALSE gives does not arise for 6 lawyer questions: s 47 capacity, s 48(2), s 50, s 51/88 reporting, notice in time,
    s 54.
  - It is an ordinary input, not a gate, of three questions that arise with or without a certificate: qualified entity,
    s 11(1) holding out, and solicitor or barrister.
- **`the law practice holds money for the client`** (BOOLEAN). FALSE gives does not arise. It is taken by 12 questions,
  the six trust questions of each role: ss 129, 134, 137-143, 145, and General Rules rr 42, 52.
- **`the bill given`** (`The bill given`). Its values are `no bill has been given yet`, `a lump sum bill` and `an itemised
  bill, with no lump sum bill before it`. It is taken by 11 questions:
  - the s 187(1)-(2) request and the s 187(3) time, in both roles (4);
  - the highest interest rate and may interest be charged, in both roles (4);
  - s 207 (the lawyer);
  - suing, in both roles (2).

  `no bill has been given yet` gives does not arise for all of them except suing (D-QA2). The itemised value also gives
  does not arise for the four s 187 questions, since s 187(1) is about a lump sum bill.
- **`the costs have been assessed`** (BOOLEAN). FALSE gives does not arise. It is taken by "Who pays the costs of the
  assessment" (s 204), in both roles (2).

**Decisions.**

- **D-QA1. Records are taken apart.**
  - The baseline's exported questions take records: the money, the withdrawal, the bill, the offence, the disclosure, the
    interest claim and the ledger.
  - The layer takes each field as its own plain input and builds the record. The form can then ask for the fields one by
    one and share them; `the day the bill was given`, for example, serves both r 42 and s 194.
  - Where a baseline function does not read a field of its record, the layer passes FALSE and does not ask for it. This
    applies to cash and the written direction, in "is it trust money" and the s 134 warning.
- **D-QA2. Suing with no bill keeps the baseline's no.** "May my firm sue" and "Can my law practice sue me now" answer `no:
  no bill complying with the Law and the Rules has been given (s 194(1))` when no bill has been given. That answer comes
  from the bill situation alone and reads nothing else, just as the graffiti layer's D8.1 keeps a no that settles its
  question.
- **D-QA3. Shared wording.**
  - Inputs shared by the two roles are worded of "the client" and "the law practice". That way one name and one `@desc`
    serve both roles.
  - The answers wrap the baseline's own enumerations (for example `on suing` HAS a `Suing answer`). So for the lawyer, the
    answer is the baseline's answer, word for word.
- **D-QA4. Three client questions are new, worked from the text.**
  - "By what date must I be given the itemised bill": s 187(3), 21 days after the request. The days are counted as the
    baseline's "Is our itemised bill in time" counts them.
  - "By what date must I apply for a costs assessment": s 198(3), 12 months, counted with the baseline's `months after`.
    It does not arise for a commercial or government client (s 170(1), finding 7).
  - "Is the person acting for me a qualified entity": s 6, using the baseline's function on that person's facts.
- **D-QA5. Situations not made.**
  - The settlement in s 177 keeps `the law practice negotiates the settlement` as an ordinary input. Only one question
    would take it as a situation, and the baseline answers no without it.
  - The disclosure failure in s 178 keeps its contravention input as an ordinary input. FALSE already settles the
    question to "nothing".
  - The assessment question (s 198) takes no bill situation, because s 198(3)(b) counts from payment where there was no
    bill.

**Not covered for the client:**

- third party payers (ss 171, 176, 198(5), (6));
- the costs agreement rights beyond s 179 (ss 180-185 are in the Application Act baseline);
- complaints and Chapter 5 costs disputes;
- the fidelity fund;
- whether the lawyer's charging is a conduct matter (s 207 is the lawyer's question only);
- the s 187(4) limit for a part payer.

**Counts.** There are 60 questions: 36 for the lawyer (every baseline export) and 24 for the client. `lpul-qa-cases.l4` has
264 cases:

- 60 ask each question for the other role, with every other input ASSUMEd;
- 35 ask each situation input with each value under which it does not hold, with every later input ASSUMEd;
- 116 are for the lawyer where the situation holds. They were worked by hand, and 36 of them (one per question) also
  assert that the answer equals the baseline export's on the same facts;
- 53 are for the client, worked by hand.

**Reads that the cases cannot show.** The baseline reads `Kind of client`, `Statement occasion` and `What happened`
through OTHERWISE (graffiti finding 31). For those inputs, the "not read" cases rest on the parameter script's check that
each question reads the role first and then its situation. The layer's own helpers over `The bill given` list every value
and have no OTHERWISE.

**Checks.** These used the stable l4 (VS Code extension 1.6.98), with `JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` unset.

- All eight files: 0 errors and 0 warnings.
- 333 assertions are satisfied: 69 in the baseline and 264 in the layer.
- `l4 check lpul-qa.l4` succeeds.
- A parameter script listed every `@export` (session scratchpad, `check_params.py`). It found:
  - `my role` first in all 60 questions;
  - each shared parameter identical in type and `@desc` wherever it is taken;
  - each situation input straight after `my role`;
  - no DEONTIC or EVENT input.
- A negative control failed as it should. It gave one case a wrong expected value, and another an ASSUMEd value for a
  situation input that its question reads.
