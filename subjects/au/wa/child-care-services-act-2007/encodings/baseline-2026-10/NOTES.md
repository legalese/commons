# Child Care Services Act 2007 (WA) — baseline encoding

## Method

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

Every question is published with `@export` and a plain-words description, and
every input carries an `@desc`, so the module can be deployed to Legalese Cloud as
it stands.

## Seat

**The parent using child care**: the person who "at law has responsibility for"
the child's long-term or day-to-day care (s 3 "parent"), asking about the service
the child attends. The Act addresses providers, licensees, the CEO and the State
Administrative Tribunal; it names the parent only in the principles (s 7(b)(i)),
the Minister's exemption factors (s 45(4)(b)) and the records provision (s 46).
What its duties mean for the parent is encoded: whether the service must be
licensed, whether it is lawfully provided, whether the supervising officer must
be there, and whether fees can be ordered refunded.

Each 2026-10-18 default:

- **The Act alone, plus the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed, with
  one addition. The Act's own s 4(a) turns on "an education and care service under
  the national child care law", so the National Law's definition is worked out
  from facts rather than asked; that is the plan's named instrument. The
  regulations are not deposited, so they are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: provider (licensee), CEO, Minister,
  State Administrative Tribunal, supervising officer.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** Followed. The
  refund answer is "possible, if the CEO alleges and the Tribunal is satisfied
  grounds exist"; whether the CEO is no longer satisfied, and whether there are
  reasonable grounds of unacceptable risk, are inputs.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts.** Followed: "child care
  service" (s 4), and through it the National Law's "education and care service"
  and "personal arrangement". Borrowed terms ("regular basis", "instruction in a
  particular activity") are asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one service the child
  attends.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Partly departed from: only the
  operative sections that reach the parent are in. The grant and renewal tests
  (ss 10-17, 23A-24), discipline of managerial officers (ss 30C, 30D), review
  (s 30, open only to the applicant or licensee), licence documents (ss 31-35),
  compliance powers (Part 4) and offences of obstruction and impersonation are
  left out as the provider's or the regulator's business.

## The lives

- **Chloe, c066 (10 February 2031): Oliver in long day care three days a week,
  with the Child Care Subsidy.** The question this Act is cited for is licensing.
  The encoding answers: Oliver's long day care is an education and care service
  under the National Law, so it is not a "child care service" under this Act
  (s 4(a)); this Act requires no licence; the centre needs a provider approval and
  a service approval under the National Law (s 103); and the lawfulness,
  supervision and refund questions under this Act do not reach it. That the
  centre falls in none of the National Law's exclusions is assumed (the scenario
  says only "long day care"). The subsidy percentage and activity test are for the
  A New Tax System (Family Assistance) Act 1999 encoding.

## Why this scope

Since the Education and Care Services National Law (WA) Act 2012 (No. 11 of 2012,
in force 1 August 2012) inserted s 4 and s 5B, this Act governs only what the
National Law leaves behind. A parent's first question is therefore which law
governs the service, and the baseline is built around that one decision, with the
licensing and parent-facing consequences for the services the Act still covers.

**What has moved to the National Law.** Long day care, family day care and
outside school hours care — the National Law's own examples of an "education and
care service" — are governed by the National Law and its regulations: provider
and service approvals (s 103), advertising (s 104), quality ratings, staffing and
the rest. None of that is in this Act or this encoding. This Act now reaches only
a service that (a) gives regular education and care to children under 13, (b) is
not an education and care service under the National Law — because it falls in
one of the National Law's exclusions — and (c) is prescribed by the Child Care
Services regulations. An associated children's service (a children's service
run by an approved provider at the same place as an approved centre) is under
this Act so far as the National Law does not apply, with its service approval
standing as the licence (s 5B).

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The Act no longer covers long day care.** s 4(a): a child care service "is
   not an education and care service under the national child care law". Oliver's
   centre (c066) is governed by the National Law and needs no licence under this
   Act. Asserted.
2. **Being prescribed is not enough.** A service prescribed under this Act that
   the National Law does not exclude is still governed by the National Law: s 4
   needs both limbs. Asserted.
3. **This Act covers only what the National Law excludes and the regulations
   pick up.** A swimming school (instruction in a particular activity) or a
   grandmother minding a grandchild (a personal arrangement) falls under neither
   law. A family friend who advertises the offer is no longer in a personal
   arrangement and falls under the National Law. Asserted.
4. **An associated children's service runs on its National Law service
   approval.** s 5B(2)(a): the approval is treated as a licence, and the
   nominated supervisor as the supervising officer. Asserted.
5. **A licence awaiting renewal still has effect.** s 22(3): if the renewal was
   lodged in accordance with s 22(2), the licence continues until the application
   is decided, unless suspended or cancelled. A service whose licence period ended
   with no renewal, or whose licence is suspended, is provided without a licence
   (s 9). Asserted.
6. **Running unlicensed is a serious offence.** s 9: first offence $12 000 and
   one year's imprisonment, plus $600 a day; subsequent offence $24 000 and two
   years, plus $1 200 a day. Asserted (fines and one daily penalty).
7. **The supervising officer must be there whenever the service runs**, as a
   condition of every licence, unless the regulations provide otherwise (s 18).
   Asserted.
8. **A parent can get fees back, but only through the CEO and the Tribunal.**
   s 29(4)(b)(ii) lets the State Administrative Tribunal order a refund to "a
   specified person or body", and s 29(5) lets it do so after the provider has
   stopped being a licensee. The parent cannot bring the allegation; the CEO does
   (s 29(3)). Asserted.
9. **Suspension by notice is capped at 60 days**, and within 21 days the CEO must
   take the matter to the Tribunal unless the suspension is revoked (s 25(3)(b),
   (4)). Asserted.
10. **The CEO may name services and people, but never a child** (s 45A(3)).
    Asserted.
11. **The age limit can change.** s 4 says 13 "or such other age as may be
    prescribed"; the National Law's limit is a flat 13. The encoding uses one
    input, "the children are under 13", for both, so a prescribed age other than
    13 would need a second input. Not asserted (inference).
