# Housing Australia Act 2018 (Cth) — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent
test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

There was no earlier encoding of this Act.

## Edition

Compilation No. 7, compilation date 11 December 2024 (register id C2024C00857). It
includes amendments up to Act No. 136, 2024. It is deposited at
`../../registers/source-bundle/C2018A00065.txt`.

**The instrument that matters is not deposited.** The Home Guarantee Scheme lives in
the Investment Mandate (s 12): the Housing Australia Investment Mandate Direction 2018.
That instrument holds the scheme's eligibility criteria, price caps, place limits and
minimum deposit. Everything it sets is an input here:

- whether the buyer meets the Mandate's criteria for the guarantee;
- whether the guarantee is within the Mandate's limits;
- whether the Mandate provides for the lender's application (s 10(2));
- the minimum deposit percentage.

No determination under s 51(3) is deposited either. The determined day, if any, is an
input (0 means none).

## Seat

**The first home buyer.** The question is "Can I buy with a 5% deposit under the Home
Guarantee Scheme?".

The Act is addressed to Housing Australia, its Board and the Minister. Nothing in it is
addressed to a home buyer. The guarantee is issued to a constitutional corporation, the
lender, not to the buyer (s 8(1B)). So the decisions are phrased as what the Act means
for the buyer:

- `Can Housing Australia issue a guarantee to this party`
- `Can I be issued a guarantee myself`
- `Is a guarantee on my loan within what the Act allows`
- `Is my loan guaranteed by Housing Australia`
- `What does the Act say about my deposit`
- `How much deposit do I need` and `Does my deposit meet the minimum`
- `Am I a party to the guarantee agreement`
- `Must I comply with the guarantee terms`
- `Can Housing Australia recover a debt from me under the agreement`
- `Is the guarantee backed by the Commonwealth`
- `Can Commonwealth money meet the guarantee`
- `Can the Minister direct a guarantee for my loan`
- `Am I one of the buyers the Act is for`
- `Does the Act give me a right to a guarantee`

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed, but the one
  instrument that sets the numbers (the Investment Mandate) is not deposited. Its
  criteria, limits and deposit percentage are inputs.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: Housing Australia, the Minister, the
  constitutional corporation (the lender), the entity that applies, the Commonwealth.
  The buyer is not named; the buyer appears only in the s 3 object.
- **Judgments handed to others are answered conditionally.** Followed. Housing
  Australia's decision to issue (s 10(4) "may") is an input. So are whether the
  guarantee is for improving housing outcomes and assists the lender's business
  (s 8(1B)), and whether the buyer meets the Mandate's criteria (s 10(3)(b)).
- **The Act's own definitions are worked out; borrowed terms are asked.** Followed.
  "Guarantee function" (s 8(1B)) and "guarantee liabilities" (s 48A(2)) are worked out.
  "Constitutional corporation" (s 5) points to the Constitution, so whether a lender is
  one is asked. "First home buyer", "single parent", "dependants" and "legal guardian"
  (s 3) are not defined in the Act and are asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one home loan and the guarantee
  behind it.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed.
- **Operative sections in, machinery out.** Followed. Almost the whole Act is machinery:
  Parts 3 to 6 (Board, CEO, staff, the Special Account, capital and reserves, dividends,
  borrowing, tax, delegation, annual reports, reviews, rules) are out, except s 48A and
  s 51, which say what stands behind the guarantee. The financing and capacity building
  functions (s 8(1A), (1C)) do not reach a home buyer and are out. The Help to Buy
  functions (s 8(1)(ca)) belong to the Help to Buy Act 2024 and are out.

## The lives

- **chloe c051** (2028-02-20). Chloe (24) and Liam sign a house-and-land package in
  Byford for $620,000, with a five per cent deposit under the Home Guarantee Scheme, and
  ask "Does the Home Guarantee cover the deposit?". The life does not name the lender;
  a participating bank that is a constitutional corporation is **assumed**, and so are
  the Mandate's criteria being met. Asked:
  - `Can I be issued a guarantee myself`: no; the guarantee goes to the lender;
  - `Is a guarantee on my loan within what the Act allows`: yes, on those inputs;
  - `Is my loan guaranteed by Housing Australia`: yes if Housing Australia decides to
    issue it;
  - `How much deposit do I need`: $31,000, at the scenario's 5% (an input);
  - `Am I a party to the guarantee agreement`, `Can Housing Australia recover a debt
    from me under the agreement`: no;
  - `Is the guarantee backed by the Commonwealth`: yes; no cut-off day can fall before
    1 July 2028;
  - `Am I one of the buyers the Act is for`: yes, as a first home buyer (s 3(e)).

  The grant and the duty in the same event are in the WA baselines (First Home Owner
  Grant Act 2000, Duties Act 2008).

## Why this scope

The plan entry says the scheme is administrative and cites no section. The brief asks
for what the Act itself says that reaches the buyer, with the eligibility criteria as
inputs. That is: the guarantee function and who it serves (s 8(1B)); the conditions the
Act puts on issuing a guarantee (s 10); the Mandate's role in setting criteria and
limits (ss 12, 13) and the bar on directing a particular guarantee (s 14); the buyer's
position under the agreement (s 10(5)-(7)); what stands behind the guarantee (ss 48A,
51); and the object's named buyers (s 3). The rest is the corporation's constitution.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The Act gives the first home buyer almost nothing to decide.** It does not mention
   the Home Guarantee Scheme, a deposit, a price cap or an income test. It never
   addresses a buyer. The only place a buyer appears is the object (s 3(e)-(g)).
   Asserted (`What does the Act say about my deposit`, `Does the Act give me a right to
   a guarantee`).
2. **The guarantee is the lender's, not the buyer's.** The guarantee function is "to
   issue guarantees to constitutional corporations" (s 8(1B)). An individual buyer
   cannot be issued one. It is a **reading** that this holds even if an individual were
   an "entity" under the inclusive s 5 definition, because s 10(4) limits guarantees to
   the performance of the s 8(1B) function. Asserted.
3. **The 5% deposit is not in the Act.** Any minimum deposit is a "decision-making
   criteria" or "limit" the Investment Mandate may set (s 13(b)(iia), (c)(ii)). The
   Mandate is not deposited, so the percentage is an input. Asserted.
4. **Housing Australia may only guarantee on the lender's application, under the
   Mandate, by written agreement** (s 10(2)-(5)). It must consider the application by
   the Mandate's criteria and limits and within any timeframe the Mandate sets
   (s 10(3)); the timeframe is not encoded. Even when all is met, "may" leaves the
   decision to Housing Australia. Asserted.
5. **The buyer is not bound by the guarantee agreement, and cannot be sued under it,
   by this Act.** The duty to comply and the statutory debt fall on "the entity", the
   party to the agreement (s 10(6), (7)). That is a **reading**; the buyer's loan
   contract with the lender may say otherwise, and is not this Act. Asserted.
6. **Housing Australia's payments are guaranteed by the Commonwealth, for now**
   (s 51(1)). The Minister may end that for contracts entered into on or after a day
   determined by legislative instrument, which cannot be earlier than 1 July 2028
   (s 51(2), (3)). Chloe's February 2028 contract falls before any possible day. It is
   a **reading** that a purported day before 1 July 2028 has no effect. Asserted.
7. **Commonwealth money is appropriated to meet guarantee liabilities** (s 48A).
   Asserted.
8. **The Minister cannot direct a guarantee for a particular loan** (s 14(1)(a)(iia),
   (v)). The Mandate may set criteria for classes of buyer, never for one. Asserted.
9. **The object names four kinds of buyer**: first home buyers; single parents with
   dependants; single legal guardians of children; and those who have not held an
   ownership interest in Australian real property in the last 10 years (s 3(e)-(g)).
   The object guides the Minister's directions (s 12(2)) but gives no one a right.
   Asserted.
