# Patents Act 1990 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the
inventor or founder, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** Compilation No. 52, compilation date 14 October 2024 (includes amendments up to Act No. 39, 2024), register id
C2024C00455, as deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/C2004A04014.txt` (retrieved 13 October 2026). The deposit's
own caveat says a version in force from 19 September 2026 has no registered compilation yet, so later amendments may be
missing. The Patents Regulations 1991 are not deposited. The prescribed circumstances and period of s 24(1), the prescribed
period of s 9(e), the regulation dates of ss 43(2)(a) and 65(b), the other formalities requirements of s 52, and renewal
fees and periods (s 143) are all inputs. The amending Act No. 9, 2020 is not deposited either.

**Checks:** one module (`pa-patentability.l4`) and one case file (`pa-cases-patentability.l4`, generated by the scratchpad
script `gen_patents-act-1990_baseline.py`). 40 assertions satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings, with the stable `l4` of VS Code
extension 1.6.98. Every decision carries `@export` and `@desc` for Legalese Cloud.

There was no first-pass encoding of this Act to replace.

## Seat

**The inventor or founder seeking a patent, alone or through the company.** In the WA lives this is Daniel, for Jarrah
Legal Technologies Pty Ltd and its matching engine. The plan's question is "Is my software patentable?". Patentability,
the grace period, the term and entitlement are all applied to the applicant and patentee. The Commissioner's and the
court's judgments are encoded as inputs that say what has been found about the invention. Decisions are phrased that way:
`Is my invention patentable`, `Must my disclosure be disregarded`, `Did I apply within the grace period`, `Can my
innovation patent application pass the formalities check`, `When does the term of my patent end`, `Do I hold the
exclusive rights on this day`, `May the patent be granted to me`, `Can my patent be revoked for want of entitlement`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates.** Followed, but the instruments are missing. The
  plan names no regulations, and the Patents Regulations 1991 are not deposited, so every prescribed period and
  circumstance is an input. In the cases the grace period is a HYPOTHETICAL 12 months. The day s 52(3) commenced is fixed
  at 26 August 2021 as an inference from endnote 3 (finding 4).
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the inventor, the applicant or nominated person, the patentee, a person who
  derives title, the legal representative, co-patentees, the Commissioner and the court.
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally.** Followed. All of these are inputs: manner of manufacture;
  novelty, inventive step and innovative step (asked leaving aside my own disclosure, and then whether that disclosure
  would defeat them); whether a credible use is disclosed; whether a use was secret; and the court's "just and equitable"
  satisfaction under s 138(4).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out; borrowed terms asked.** Followed. The following are worked out: priority date
  (s 43(2)), date of patent (s 65), "useful" as far as s 7A goes, "secret use" as far as s 9 goes, the s 24 disregard, and
  the s 18 limbs. Two terms are asked as facts: entitlement to an assignment, which turns on contract and general law, and
  "prior art base", which is defined in Schedule 1 but feeds a judgment.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one application (`Application`), one invention as claimed
  (`Invention`), one earlier disclosure (`Disclosure`) and one claimant (`Claimant`). The encoding treats the invention "so
  far as claimed in any claim" as a single claim.
- **The Act's own terms, with a plain gloss.** Followed.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed, and narrowed hard because the Act is very large (see below).

## The lives

- **Daniel, d046 (1 September 2031: the JARRAH mark, the code owned by the company, and a patent application for the
  matching engine).** The decisions are these:
  - `Is my invention patentable`: patentable if found a manner of manufacture, and not patentable if found "a mere scheme
    run on a computer". The Act leaves that judgment to the cases.
  - The grace period: a hypothetical conference demonstration in March 2031 is disregarded; one in March 2030 is not; a
    disclosure after a provisional application has fixed the priority date does not count.
  - `Can my innovation patent application pass the formalities check`: no, for a patent dated 2031.
  - `When does the term of my patent end`: 1 September 2051.
  - `May the patent be granted to me`: Daniel as inventor, and Jarrah if it holds an assignment or is entitled to one.

  The copyright half of the event (s 35(6) of the Copyright Act 1968) belongs to another Act.

## Why this scope

The Act runs to about 300,000 characters. The plan's event cites only s 18. The brief asked for the inventor's core
questions: patentability, standard against innovation patent, term, entitlement and the grace period. Those need ss 7,
7A, 9, 15, 18, 23, 24, 43(2), 52(3), 65, 67 and 68. Four more provisions come with them because they answer the very next
question the inventor asks:
- s 13(1) and s 143: do I hold the rights today?
- s 16(1): we are co-owners; what do I hold?
- ss 22A and 138(3)(a), (4): what if the wrong person was granted it?

The following are left out:
- application, examination, opposition and grant procedure;
- PCT and Convention routes;
- specifications and amendments;
- pharmaceutical extensions of term;
- infringement and its exemptions;
- compulsory licences and Crown use;
- revocation on other grounds;
- attorneys, the Commissioner and courts;
- fees.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The Act does not say whether software is patentable.** s 18(1)(a) requires "a manner of manufacture within the meaning
   of section 6 of the Statute of Monopolies" and says nothing more. Whether a computer-implemented invention qualifies is
   for the cases, so the encoding takes it as an input and gives both outcomes. Asserted.
2. **The grace period depends on regulations that are not here.** s 24(1) disregards my own disclosure only if it was made
   "in the prescribed circumstances" and only if "a complete application for the invention is made within the prescribed
   period". Both terms are left to the Patents Regulations 1991, which are not deposited. A disclosure by me in other
   circumstances counts against me. A disclosure by someone who got the invention from me without my consent is
   disregarded on the same time condition. A disclosure only to a government or its authorised investigator (s 24(2)) is
   disregarded with no time limit. Asserted.
3. **The grace period runs to the complete application, not the provisional.** s 24(1) asks for the *complete* application
   within the prescribed period. On the inferred reading that the period runs from the disclosure, a provisional filed in
   time does not save a disclosure if the complete application comes too late. A disclosure on or after the priority date
   never counts, because of s 23. Asserted.
4. **The Act's text gives no closing date for innovation patents.** s 52(3) makes it a formalities requirement that "the date
   of the patent (if granted) would be a date before the day this subsection commences". The day itself is not in the text.
   Endnote 3 says items 2-9 of Schedule 1 to Act No. 9, 2020 commenced on 26 August 2021. That s 52(3) is among those items
   is an INFERENCE; the amending Act is not deposited. On that inference, an innovation patent dated 1 July 2021 can pass
   the check, and one dated 26 August 2021 or later (Daniel's, in 2031) cannot. The compiled Act still contains the
   innovation patent provisions (ss 18(1A), 52, 62, 68, Ch 9A). Asserted.
5. **A standard patent lasts 20 years and an innovation patent 8, both from the date of the patent** (ss 67, 68). That date
   is the filing date of the complete specification, unless the regulations fix another (s 65). So the term runs from the
   complete application, even where an earlier provisional fixed the priority date. Daniel's patent, dated 1 September
   2031, runs to 1 September 2051. Ending the term on the anniversary is an inference. Asserted.
6. **The Act does not give an employee's invention to the employer.** s 15(1) lets a patent be granted to the inventor, to
   anyone who "would, on the grant ... be entitled to have the patent assigned", to anyone who derives title, or to a legal
   representative. Whether an employer is entitled to an assignment depends on the contract and the general law. The Act has
   no counterpart to the Copyright Act's s 35(6). An employee-inventor is still a person to whom the patent may be granted.
   Asserted.
7. **A grant to the wrong person is not invalid for that reason alone** (s 22A). A court may revoke it for want of
   entitlement only if satisfied it is "just and equitable" (s 138(4)). Asserted.
8. **Plants and animals are excluded only for innovation patents** (s 18(3)), and not when the invention is a
   microbiological process or its product (s 18(4)). Human beings and their generation are excluded for both kinds
   (s 18(2)). Asserted.
9. **Secret commercial use before filing defeats the patent unless a complete application follows within the prescribed
   period** (ss 9(e), 18(1)(d)). Reasonable trial, confidential disclosure and non-commercial use are not secret use (s 9).
   Asserted.
10. **Co-owners hold equal shares unless they agree otherwise** (s 16(1)(a)). No co-owner may license or assign without
    the others' consent (s 16(1)(c)). The equal share is asserted.
