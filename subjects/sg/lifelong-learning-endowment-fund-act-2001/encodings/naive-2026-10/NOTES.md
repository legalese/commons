## Why this Act, and why scoped

This is requirement **REQ-0181** in `subjects/sg/requirements.jsonl`: Tier 3 of the
remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by everyday-life relevance. The requirement asks what
the Act decides for a person or business it applies to; no scenario has asked a sharper
question yet.

The Act is 17 sections and a Schedule, nearly all about the Fund itself. This row takes
what a learner, a training provider, a donor, or someone questioned by the auditor
meets: ss 4(2)-(4), 5, 7, 8, 10, 11(1), (2), 12, 14, 15, 16 and Schedule paras 1(3), 3
and 6. Not encoded: the establishment, banking and investment of the Fund (ss 3, 4(1)),
the Council's establishment (s 6), the Minister's accounting and reporting duties
(ss 9, 13), the auditor's access, report contents and reporting (s 11(3)-(6)),
regulation-making (s 17), and the rest of the Schedule.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A company officer is guilty by default, and must prove two things to escape

s 15(1): when a body corporate, partnership or unincorporated association commits an
offence under the Act, every director, manager, partner, secretary or similar officer
(or anyone purporting to act as one) "shall also be guilty ... unless the person
proves" both that the offence was committed without their consent or connivance **and**
that they exercised all due diligence. Proving only one limb is not enough. This is a
harder rule than the consent-or-neglect test in the sibling Community Care Endowment
Fund Act 2005 row. Asserted.

### 2. The Act puts no nationality, residence or age condition on who may be helped

s 5 speaks only of "persons" and their "employability", and every head of s 5(2) says
"whether in Singapore or elsewhere". A foreigner learning abroad is within the Act's
words. Any eligibility limits on actual schemes must come from elsewhere (regulations
under s 17 or administrative scheme rules; none were retrieved). Asserted.

### 3. Nobody has a right to be paid

s 5(2): the Minister "may" authorise the income to be paid out; s 10: no payment
"unless the payment is authorised by the Minister". Learning unrelated to employability
fits only if prescribed under s 5(2)(f). Asserted.

### 4. A gift can never be spent, whatever the donor says

s 4(2): "Despite the terms of any gift or bequest", every gift for the Fund's purposes
is paid in as capital, and s 4(3) lets capital be used only for investment. A permanent
loss of capital must be made good from income before any income is paid out (s 4(4)),
and administration and audit costs also come from income (s 7). How the two rank
against each other is not stated; the encoding takes both off income and floors at nil
(an inference). Asserted.

### 5. The offences are fines only

Failing without reasonable excuse to give the auditor information, or hindering the
auditor: up to $1,000 (s 12). Knowingly false statements or documents to the Minister,
the auditor or their authorised persons: up to $5,000 (s 14). No imprisonment.
Prescribed offences may be compounded for up to $500, after which no further
proceedings may be taken (s 16). Asserted.

### 6. The Council's quorum is one-half

Schedule para 3: "one-half of the number of members constitutes a quorum"; para 6:
simple majority of members present, with a casting vote for the person presiding. For an
odd-sized Council, half is read as rounded up (an inference). Asserted.
