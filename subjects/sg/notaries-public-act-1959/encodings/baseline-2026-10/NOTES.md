## Seat

**The advocate and solicitor who is, or wants to be, a notary public in Singapore**
and, for s 7, anyone who exercises a notary's functions in Singapore. Every
operative provision of the Act is applied to that person: who may be appointed
(s 3), how long an appointment lasts and when it lapses (s 3(1), (6)), what a
notary may do (s 4), when the appointment must or may be revoked (ss 5, 6), and who
commits an offence by acting as one (s 7). The notary's client is not addressed by
the Act; what the client can have done is the same question as what the notary may
do (s 4), asked from the other side. The Senate of the Singapore Academy of Law is
the decision-maker throughout and is not the seat: its powers are encoded as what
they mean for the lawyer.

Decisions are phrased as that person asks: `Am I eligible to be appointed a notary
public`, `Can I be appointed a notary public`, `Can I be appointed a notary public
temporarily`, `Can I appeal against the Senate's decision on my appointment`, `Am I
a notary public`, `May I administer the oath for, take or attest the affidavit`,
`Must my appointment be revoked`, `Can I have my appointment revoked at my own
request`, `Do I commit an offence under section 7` (and `... on the day`), `What is
the most I can be fined under section 7`, `Who can convict me under section 7`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, instruments brought in:** followed as far as the deposit allows.
  The Notaries Public Rules made under s 8 (fees, further prescribed powers) are not
  deposited, so they are not brought in; where an answer turns on prescribed powers
  (s 4(3)(c)) the decision says `not covered by this encoding`. The Act's own
  numbers (7 years, 12 months, one month, $10,000) are in the text.
- **The actors the text names:** followed. The Senate, the Council of the Law
  Society, the notary public, the absent notary, a District Judge.
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally:** followed. "Fit and
  proper" (s 3(1)), whether a notary "appears" to the Senate to be about to be
  absent (s 3(5)), and misconduct "in the opinion of the Senate" rendering one unfit
  (s 5(c)) are inputs. For s 5(a) and (b) the answer carries "if it so appears to
  the Senate". Where every condition of s 3 is met, the answer is only that the
  Senate may appoint, because s 3(4) gives it absolute discretion.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts, borrowed terms asked as
  facts:** followed. "Notary public" (s 2) is worked out from the appointment and
  the day. "Practising advocate and solicitor", "bankrupt", "arrangement with
  creditors", "struck off the roll" and "affidavit or statutory declaration" are
  borrowed and asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit:** followed. One candidate, one
  appointment and a day, one affidavit, one notarial act.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss:** followed, in comments beside
  each type and field.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out:** followed. Ss 2 to 7 are encoded.
  S 1, s 3(7) (Gazette publication, with no consequence attached) and s 8
  (rule-making) are out.

## Why this scope

The Act is eight short sections and every operative one reaches the lawyer, so all
of them are encoded. Two things the Act borrows rather than states are left out:
the powers "ordinarily exercised by notaries public in England" (s 4(1)), and the
powers and fees prescribed by rules under s 8. Any question that turns on them
(an affidavit with no stated place of use, a notarial function other than oaths and
attestations) answers `not covered by this encoding`. Month-counting for the
"exceeding one month" absence in s 3(5) is not done: the Senate's view of it is an
input.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Seven years' practice, counted as "not less than".** s 3(2): a practising
   advocate and solicitor in Singapore with 7 years is eligible; 6 years is not, and
   20 years without current practice is not. Asserted.

2. **Eligibility does not entitle.** Even when every condition is met, the Senate
   "shall have absolute discretion in making or refusing to make any such
   appointment and there shall be no appeal from its decision" (s 3(4)). It must
   consult the Council of the Law Society first (s 3(3)) and may appoint for no
   more than 12 months (s 3(1)). Asserted.

3. **A temporary stand-in needs neither 7 years nor a "fit and proper" finding.**
   s 3(5) asks only for "a practising advocate and solicitor in Singapore", while a
   notary is about to be absent for more than one month. A lawyer of 6 years can be
   appointed temporarily. That s 3(2) does not reach s 3(5) is a reading (s 3(2)
   says "No person shall be appointed as a notary public"); the Council must still
   be consulted because s 3(3) covers "any appointment under this section". Asserted
   on that reading.

4. **A temporary appointment ends early on the absent notary's return or death.**
   s 3(6): it "shall lapse on the death or on the return to Singapore" of the
   absent notary, and in any case lasts no more than 12 months. The encoding reads
   the lapse as taking effect on the day of return or death. Asserted.

5. **A 12-month appointment from 1 January ends after 31 December.** The Act does
   not say how to count months; the encoding reads a period of N months as ending
   the day before the same date N months on, clamping to the month's last day (31
   August plus 6 months ends after 27 February 2027). That is a reading, not the
   text. Asserted on that reading.

6. **Section 2 excludes a person revoked under s 5, and says nothing of s 6.** The
   encoding treats a revocation at the notary's own request (s 6) as also ending
   the standing, because s 6 revokes "the appointment" in the same words. A reading.
   Asserted.

7. **A notary may not swear an affidavit for a Singapore court, with two
   exceptions.** s 4(2) takes out of the borrowed English powers any oath for an
   affidavit or statutory declaration to be used "in any court or place within
   Singapore", but only "except for the purposes of" s 4(3). So an affidavit
   confirming or proving the due execution of a document, or one by a vessel's
   master or crew about the vessel, may be sworn before a notary even for use in
   Singapore. An affidavit for use outside Singapore always may. Asserted.

8. **Revocation for bankruptcy, striking off or misconduct is mandatory.** s 5:
   "the Senate shall by order revoke" once a ground appears to it. Misconduct
   counts only if the Senate thinks it renders the notary unfit. Ceasing to
   practise is not a ground in s 5 (though it would bar reappointment under s 3(2)).
   Asserted.

9. **Revocation on request is discretionary.** s 6: the Senate "may" revoke if the
   notary requests it, by notification in the Gazette. Asserted.

10. **Acting as a notary without standing, or beyond s 4, is an offence with a
    $10,000 maximum fine.** s 7 reaches "any person who exercises within Singapore
    any of the functions of a notary public otherwise than in accordance with the
    provisions of this Act", on conviction before a District Judge; the Act
    provides a fine only. The encoding reads "otherwise than in accordance" as
    acting when not a notary that day (never appointed, period run out, revoked,
    lapsed) or beyond s 4(2). Acting outside Singapore is not within s 7. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The Notaries Public Rules made under s 8 should be deposited and brought in: they
  fix the fees and may prescribe further powers under s 4(3)(c), and breach of them
  may be "otherwise than in accordance with" the Act for s 7.
- The English notarial powers borrowed by s 4(1) are not stated anywhere in the
  deposit; whether a given protest, certificate or other act is within them is not
  encoded.
- The readings in findings 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10 have not been checked against any
  decided case; no case law was searched.
- No independent test pass has been run. The moved encoding's independent pass
  found that s 3(3) reaches temporary appointments; this encoding was written with
  that in mind but has had no pass of its own.
