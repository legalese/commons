## Why this Act, and why scoped

This is requirement **REQ-0134**: Tier 2 of the remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by
everyday-life relevance. The requirement asks what the Act decides for a person it
applies to — here mostly a school student in the National Police Cadet Corps (NPCC)
or the National Civil Defence Cadet Corps (NCDCC), and the teachers and volunteers
who run them. No scenario has asked a sharper question yet.

The row takes who is a member (s 2), eligibility (s 9), discharge and dismissal
(s 10), summary discipline of cadets (ss 11, 12), boards of inquiry into officers
(s 13), compensation (s 14), the weapons-law exemption (s 15) and the Commandant's
term (s 6(3)). Establishment, organisation, the Council, appointments, secondment,
orders, the regulation-making power and the transitional provisions (ss 3–5, 6(1),
(2), (4), 7, 8, 16–18) are not encoded. No regulations or orders made under the Act

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. There is no offence in this Act

Nothing in the Act is punishable by a fine or imprisonment. A cadet who defies a
lawful command, or breaks an order or regulation they knew or should have known,
"must be dealt with summarily by the cadet's officer-in-charge" (s 11), and the most
the officer-in-charge can do on a recorded finding of guilt is a reprimand, a
reduction in rank (only "where the cadet holds a rank") or suspension of membership
"for a period not exceeding 3 months" (s 12). A fine, detention or dismissal is not
among them. Asserted.

### 2. Dismissing a student cadet needs the school principal's agreement

s 10(2): a cadet may be dismissed only by the Commandant of the cadet's own Corps,
on the officer-in-charge's recommendation and, "where the cadet is a student of a
school, with the concurrence of the principal". A non-student cadet needs no
principal. An officer-in-charge cannot dismiss, and the other Corps' Commandant
cannot either. Asserted.

### 3. Defiance matters for commands; knowledge matters for orders

s 11(a) catches disobedience of a lawful command only when done "in such manner as
to show wilful defiance of authority"; s 11(b) catches a breach of an order or
regulation only if it was "known to the cadet or that the cadet might reasonably be
expected to know". Plain disobedience without defiance, and breach of an order the
cadet could not have known, fall outside s 11. Asserted.

### 4. Citizens and PRs may join one Corps, not both

s 9(1): a citizen or permanent resident is eligible "in one of the following Corps,
but not both"; s 9(2) lets the Minister approve anyone else "in special cases". The
Act sets no age (left to regulations, s 17(2)(d)). Whether the "not both" bar binds a
s 9(2) approval is not said; the encoding applies it to everyone, which is an
inference. Asserted.

### 5. A volunteer instructor is a "member"; a seconded police officer, on the words, is not

"officer" includes an honorary instructor appointed under s 7(3), and "member" means
an officer or cadet and includes someone on an officer cadet course (s 2). So an
honorary instructor counts for s 14 compensation and the s 15 exemption. A police or
civil defence officer seconded under s 8 is not appointed under s 7, so by the
definition is not a member — an inference, since s 8 seconds them "for ... service
in" the Corps. Asserted (as encoded).

### 6. Compensation is a power, and only for harm "solely" from duty

s 14 makes it "lawful for the Minister to compensate" a member or dependants for death
or injury "arising out of or in the execution of the member's duty and that is
attributable solely to the discharge of the member's duty". It is not an entitlement,
and the amount is left to regulations. Asserted.

### 7. Officers face a board, not summary discipline

For an officer, the Commandant may convene a board of inquiry (s 13(1)), chaired for
the NPCC by a police officer of at least Assistant Superintendent of Police or an
NPCC officer of at least Assistant Superintendent, and for the NCDCC by an SCDF or
NCDCC officer of at least Captain, with at least two others, one nominated by a
Permanent Secretary to the Ministry of Education (s 13(2)). It recommends reprimand,
reduction in rank or dismissal — not suspension (s 13(3)). Who then imposes the
punishment is not stated. Asserted.

### 8. Weapons laws stop at the parade ground

The Arms Offences Act 1973 and the Corrosive and Explosive Substances and Offensive
Weapons Act 1958 do not apply to members "while on parade or in the course of their
duty" (s 15) — and only then. A Commandant's term never exceeds 3 years, but is
renewable (s 6(3)). Asserted.
