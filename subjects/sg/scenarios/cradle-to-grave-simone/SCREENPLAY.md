# SIM ONE

*A life, executed.*

A documentary in ten chapters. Running time about twenty-four minutes. Present day: October 2088.

Built on [`ABRIDGED.md`](ABRIDGED.md), [`facts.json`](facts.json) and the rows in Commons. Every answer THE VOICE gives is the law as encoded, run on the facts of Simone's life. Where it says it does not know, the library did not hold the answer on the day the scene is set.

---

## Conventions

- **THE VOICE** is the interface to law-as-code. It has no name, no face, no age. Its body changes with the decade: a website, a text message, a chat window, a voice in a kitchen, a voice in a corridor. It is always the same voice when it speaks.
- **ON SCREEN** renders a typed exchange in the frame, the way text messages do in *Sherlock*: her words on the right, its words on the left, nothing else. When an answer carries a figure, the figure appears alone, large, for one beat, before the sentence that explains it.
- **LOWER THIRD** names the Act, once, small, bottom left, only in scenes where the name matters to her. Nobody ever says a section number aloud.
- **THE LOG** is the documentary's own device: a vertical scroll of dated entries, each a line of plain text, which the film returns to between chapters. It is the console's event log, and the present-day frame is Simone having it read back.
- Chapter cards: black, serif, the chapter title, the years, and her age in those years.

Nothing in this film shows the law stopping Simone from anything she wants. The law arrives as an answer. The forms disappear; the rules do not change.

## COLD OPEN

**INT. TWO-ROOM FLEXI FLAT, TAMPINES — LATE AFTERNOON, OCTOBER 2088**

A small flat kept very tidy. One armchair facing the window, a second chair at the table, pushed in. Rain on the glass. A pair of reading glasses folded on a thin book.

SIMONE NG (85) sits in the armchair. She is small now, and precise. She is listening to something we cannot yet hear.

The door. KAI EN (58), grey, carrying groceries, lets himself in. He has his mother's way of putting things down exactly where they go.

**KAI EN**
You started without me.

**SIMONE**
I asked it to wait.

**THE VOICE (V.O.)**
*(from nowhere in particular; warm, unhurried)*
I waited.

Kai En puts the kettle on. Sits in the second chair. Pulls it out from the table, which is clearly not usual, and looks at her.

**KAI EN**
Why do you want me to hear all of it?

**SIMONE**
So that you know how to do it. And so that you know it was mostly nothing.
*(to the room)*
From the start.

**THE VOICE (V.O.)**
Which start?

**SIMONE**
Mine.

A beat. Then THE LOG fills the screen: white text on black, scrolling up from the bottom, thousands of lines, too fast to read, and slowing, slowing, until it stops on the first.

> **14 March 2003.** A birth. KK Women's and Children's Hospital.

Cut to black.

**TITLE: SIM ONE**

## CHAPTER 1 — PRIMARY SCHOOL

**CARD: 1 · Primary school · 2006 to 2015 · ages 3 to 12**

**INT. NG FAMILY FLAT, TAMPINES, LIVING ROOM — NIGHT, 3 JANUARY 2010**

A desktop computer on a dining table. Boon Keng, reading glasses on his forehead, types with two fingers into a government website. The page is plain: a search box, a question. Behind him, on the sofa, SIMONE (6) is asleep in a school uniform one size too big, bought to last.

ON SCREEN, the website's answer box:

> **Does my daughter have to start school this year? She was born in March 2003.**

> Yes. A citizen child who is above six, living in Singapore, must attend a national primary school. From January 2010 she is of compulsory school age. Which school, and in which phase you registered, is a matter for the Ministry; I can show you the framework but I do not decide it.

Boon Keng reads it twice. Looks at the sleeping child. Does not print anything. Closes the laptop lid gently so as not to wake her.

**LOWER THIRD:** Compulsory Education Act.

**EXT. PRIMARY SCHOOL GATE, TAMPINES — MORNING, 4 JANUARY 2010**

**SIEW LAN**

SIMONE (8), on a stool too low for her, chin up, trying very hard not to smile and failing. Siew Lan outside the curtain with a phone, an early smartphone, typing with one thumb.

**MONTAGE — THE YEARS THAT KEEP RUNNING**

## CHAPTER 4 — FIRST JOB

**CARD: 4 · First job · 2026 to 2028 · ages 23 to 25**

**EXT. MRT PLATFORM, TAMPINES — EVENING, 31 AUGUST 2026**

Rush hour. SIMONE (23), lanyard now reading MERIDIAN LOGISTICS, stands on the yellow line looking at her phone, where a payslip has just arrived. Four figures she does not recognise. The train is two minutes away.

## CHAPTER 6 — FIRST CHILD

**CARD: 6 · First child · 2030 to 2031 · ages 27 to 28**

**INT. RENTED FLAT, KITCHEN — EVENING, 22 AUGUST 2030**

A small kitchen in a flat full of boxes that have never been fully unpacked, because it was never going to be for long. A paper calendar on the wall, August 2030, with a date two weeks away circled in red. SIMONE (27), very pregnant, in Marcus's shirt, a pen in her hand. MARCUS (31) at the stove, stirring something he has made before.

This is the first time THE VOICE speaks aloud. It comes from a small speaker on the counter, and we hear it as the voice we have heard in the 2088 frame.

**SIMONE (27)**
*(to the room, not the phone)*

**THE VOICE**
Now, if you want. You may begin up to four weeks before the birth. Sixteen weeks in all.

**SIMONE (27)**
Who pays?

**THE VOICE**
Meridian pays the first eight. The Government pays the last eight, at your pay, up to ten thousand dollars for every four weeks.

**MARCUS**
*(not turning from the stove)*
And me?

**THE VOICE**
Four weeks of paternity leave, in the first year. And ten weeks between the two of you to share, however you agree.

**SIMONE (27)**
Six and four.

**MARCUS**

**SIMONE (27)**
I take six. You take four. You're hopeless at the first weeks.

**MARCUS**
I am hopeless at the first weeks.

He brings the pot to the table. She writes M on four weeks in the new year, in the same red pen, and draws a box round them, and they look at the calendar together with the particular silence of people counting.

The same ward. The same light, but it is digital now. A NURSE clips a bracelet onto a wrist. Simone, wrecked, radiant. Marcus with both hands over his face, laughing or not. Nobody is holding a form.

**SIMONE (27)**
*(to Marcus, who has not seen it)*

**MARCUS**

**SIMONE (27)**

A pram. KAI EN, four weeks old, asleep in it with the absolute conviction of the very new. A BANK OFFICER (20s) across the desk with a tablet. Simone, tired in the way that has a floor under it now.

**BANK OFFICER**

**SIMONE (27)**
*(to her phone, as the officer waits, politely)*

> **$10,000** *the Baby Gift, now.*
> **$5,000** *into this account, the First Step.*

## Production notes

- **Runtime.** Twenty scenes plus three montages and the frame, at roughly a minute a scene: twenty-two to twenty-five minutes.
- **Casting Simone.** Five actors at least: 6, 15 to 27 (one actor can carry 21 to 28 with hair and costume), 32 to 49, 56 to 70, 79 to 85. In animation, one design aged in ten steps per the ages table in `ABRIDGED.md`.
- **The Voice.** One performer, cast for warmth and plainness, recorded dry. Never processed to sound synthetic; the point is that it is the same person in every decade. Do not give it a name in the credits.
- **Interfaces by decade.** 2010 a website on a desktop; 2011 a chat bubble on an early smartphone; 2018 and 2024 text; 2025 to 2028 a chat window; 2030 onward a voice in the room, and from 2068 a voice from nowhere we can see. The shift from text to voice happens in the kitchen in Chapter 6, on purpose: the first time it speaks aloud is the first time it is asked about a child.
- **Figures on screen.** Exactly nine: $840, $737.60, $10,000, $5,000, $2,000 a year, $32,000, $7,200, $36,600, and the nil answers. Every other number stays in dialogue or off screen.
- **The honest stops.** Four: the student exemption (2025), the notice period (2029), the HDB policy (2028), and the lease refund (2088). Two of them are later resolved on screen (2026, 2040). Do not add more; do not cut any.
- **What is not in the film.** No divorce, no court, no offence, no scam, no hospital bill, no dispute over any estate. If a producer asks for conflict, the answer is that the conflict is time.
