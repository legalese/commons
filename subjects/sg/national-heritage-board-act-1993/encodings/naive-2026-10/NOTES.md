## Why this Act, and why scoped

This is requirement **REQ-0190** in the Singapore requirement ledger: Tier 3 of the
remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by everyday-life relevance. It asks what the Act
decides for a person or business it applies to; no scenario has asked a sharper
question yet.

Most of the Act constitutes the Board and moves the old National Museum, National
Archives and Oral History Department into it in 1993. A person outside the Board
meets very little of it. This row takes what such a person can meet: entry on their
land for archaeological investigation (s 46), the symbol offence (s 47), the secrecy
duty (s 47A), the Board's immunity (s 48), gifts made before 1993 that take effect
later (s 14), what a donor's condition can and cannot stop (ss 15(3)-(5), 16(3)),
and the Schedule rules a Board member meets (paras 7, 9, 11), with s 4(2) and s 5(1).
Not encoded: functions and powers, committees, directions, museums and directors,
the 1993 vesting and transfers (ss 13, 37-45), staff, funds and borrowing,
regulations and transitional provisions.

s 34 deems the Board an institution under s 13(1)(e) of the Income Tax Act 1947 and
"an institution of a public character approved under the Charities Act 1994". What
that means for a donor's tax deduction is decided in those Acts, not here, so it is
noted and not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The Board can dig on your land with 24 hours' written notice — but not in your garden

s 46(1): a person "specifically authorised in writing by the Board", after "not less
than 24 hours' notice in writing to the occupier", may enter at reasonable times any
land the Board "has reason to believe contains any ancient monument" and "may make
excavations". The occupier may demand to see the authority. s 46(2) keeps the entrant
out of a dwelling house, or any "park, garden, pleasure ground or other land used for
the amenity or convenience of a dwelling house", without the occupier's consent; and
s 46(3) requires every consent an excavation would otherwise need. Exactly 24 hours
suffices; 23 does not. Asserted.

### 2. What is found may be taken away, but not kept

s 46(6) lets the entrant take "temporary custody" of any object of archaeological or
historical interest found. s 46(7): the Board "may not retain the object without the
owner's consent beyond the period that may be reasonably required" to examine,
record, test or treat it. Treasure trove is left to the Government (s 46(8), not
encoded). Asserted.

### 3. A donor's condition binds a loan for only 25 years, and binds a disposal not at all if the object rots

s 16(3): an object given subject to a condition may be lent inconsistently with it
once "25 years have elapsed" since it vested, or with written consent from the person
who imposed the condition or their personal representatives. s 15(4)-(5): an object
useless through "damage, physical deterioration or infestation by destructive
organisms" may be disposed of "by whatever means, including destruction", "despite a
trust or condition (express or implied)". Asserted. That a prohibiting trust binds
other disposals is an inference (s 15(5) overrides it only for s 15(4)), and is
encoded as such.

### 4. A treasure can leave the collection only by sale, exchange or gift, and only with the Minister

s 15(3): an object "of significant national or historical value" may be disposed of
only if a duplicate or, in the Board's opinion, unsuitable for retention, "and the
disposal is done with the prior approval of the Minister and by way of sale, exchange
or gift". Destruction of such an object on grounds of unsuitability is not allowed.
The money must buy new objects (s 15(6), not encoded). Asserted.

### 5. Copying the symbol costs more than leaking secrets

The fine for using the Board's symbol, or one likely to deceive or confuse, without
permission is up to $5,000 (s 47(2)); for wilfully obstructing an archaeological entry,
also $5,000 (s 46(9)); for disclosing information by a present or past member,
officer, employee, agent or committee member, $2,000 (s 47A(2)). All three carry up to
a year's imprisonment. The secrecy duty survives leaving the Board ("is or has
been"). Asserted. That obstructing an entry s 46 does not authorise is not the s 46(9)
offence is an inference from "in the exercise of the powers conferred by this section".

### 6. Pre-1993 gifts to the Government for the Museum go to the Board

s 14: a gift (including by will) in an instrument made before 1 August 1993 but taking
effect on or after it, which would have vested in the Government "for the purposes of
the National Museum", vests in the Board instead unless the testator or donor
expressed a contrary intention. Asserted.

### 7. Good faith is a complete shield

s 48: no suit lies against the Board, its members, officers, employees or anyone
acting under its direction for anything done "in good faith" in the execution or
purported execution of this Act or the Preservation of Monuments Act 2009. Asserted.

### 8. The Board as a body

A Chairperson, a Deputy and 10 to 25 other members (s 5(1)); quorum is one-half of
the total (para 11(2)); a tie is broken by the presiding member's casting vote
(para 11(3)); a member is disqualified for incapacity, bankruptcy, a creditors'
arrangement or an unpardoned conviction for dishonesty, fraud or moral turpitude
(para 9), and loses office after 3 consecutive meetings missed without leave
(para 7). A sealed document needs two authorised members, or one and the chief
executive officer (s 4(2)). Asserted.
