## Part A. The split-site (ii) table

### How I read "DECIDED" and "OPEN"

Guide labels used below (the wording is the Guide's, as copied in my `PROMPT.md`).

- **IS-1**, the "In short" sentence on `JUST FALSE`: "a means definition, a condition the text introduces with 'if', 'only if', 'as long as' or 'unless' (and plain variants such as 'while', 'until' and 'except'), a list the text closes with 'only', a flat negative ('does not', 'shall not', 'may not')".
- **IS-2**, the "In short" sentence on `NOTHING`: "where the text lists without closing the list, says 'includes', leaves a term undefined, is silent, points outside the sources, or limits a grant or prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit".
- **PR-a, PR-b, PR-c**, the periods ruling of 2026-11-05: (a) a period fixed in the text's own words gives `JUST FALSE` outside it; (b) a failed "where ..." gives `JUST FALSE`; (c) a scope limit with no "only" gives `NOTHING`.
- **DD-c**, the delegated-definitions ruling, item (c): "when", "upon" and "whenever" count as "where" under PR-b.
- **Six**, the six case-by-case questions (assumed, not ruled), of which two matter here: "an 'unless' or 'except' once it is met" and "a duty whose 'if' or 'where' is unmet".
- **GR-1, GR-2, GR-3**, the three limits of the grant ruling of 2026-11-12 (not confirmed by the maintainer): GR-1, a "may" function whose "if" gate failed answers `NOTHING`; GR-2, keep `JUST FALSE` where the text closes the gate itself ("only if", "only where", "may not ... unless", a list closed with "only") and for a bar, a duty or the provision's own question; GR-3, do not change, but list in the fork register, a "may" function whose gate is "where", "when" or "upon".
- **Ex1, Ex2, Ex3**, the three worked examples: a failed "if" is `JUST FALSE`; an open list is `NOTHING`; a scope limit with no "only" is `NOTHING`.

**DECIDED** means a Guide sentence settles the row and no other Guide sentence points the other way.
**OPEN** means the Guide sentences point both ways (almost always GR-3 itself, which lists the case and decides nothing) or the Guide leaves the case to site-by-site judgment (the two of the Six above).

**My lean on an OPEN row is a policy of mine, not a Guide sentence.**
For a power or a relief ("may", "Nothing ... prohibits"), `JUST FALSE` reads as "may not", which the text does not say, and a wrong "no" is the expensive mistake; `NOTHING` only withholds a "no".
For a duty, `JUST FALSE` reads as "not required", which is what an unmet trigger means.
I do not decide which OPEN answer is right; each OPEN row says which way I lean and what the other reading costs.

Where a row has two gates that the Guide treats differently, I split it into two rows, so one function can appear twice.

## Part B. The s 7(9)(ca) seam

### (1) What (ca) asks, and whether a failed condition leaves the question open

The text, `inputs/CoA1967.txt` 2250 and 2266-2268:

> (9) There is to be disregarded —
> ...
> (ca) an interest of a company in its own shares if that interest is purchased or otherwise acquired in accordance with sections 76B to 76G (including treasury shares); and

**The limb is introduced by "if".**
Under IS-1 and Ex1 a condition the text introduces with "if" gives `JUST FALSE` when it is not met, so for the (ca) limb a purchase that is not "in accordance with" the sections is the text's own no, and the keel's `FALSE` is the right value, not an open question.
GR-1 does not reach it: (ca) is not a function named for a grant or a power.

**Does a failed condition of 76B to 76G make the interest "not in accordance with" them?**
For the conditions the sections state as flat negatives, I read it that way, and the text itself names the failure in other words.

- 76B(1), text 8314: "a company may, in accordance with this section and sections 76C to 76G, purchase or otherwise acquire shares issued by it if it is expressly permitted to do so by its constitution". The sections use "in accordance with" as their own standard of compliance.
- 76B(3), text 8324-8330: the total "that may be purchased or acquired by a company during the relevant period must not exceed 20% ... unless" the share capital was reduced under section 78B or 78C or the Court approved a reduction under section 78I.
- 76E(1), text 8890-8895: "A company must not make a purchase or acquisition of its own shares on a securities exchange ... unless the purchase or acquisition has been authorised in advance by the company in general meeting."
- 76F(2), text 8962-8967: "If the requirements in subsection (1) are not satisfied in relation to an agreement — (a) in a case within subsection (1)(a) — no purchase or acquisition by the company of its own shares pursuant to that agreement is lawful".

**So the sga3 note's premise, "the sections never say what an acquisition that fails one is called" (`phase3/seam-7-9-ca.md`), is not true of 76F(2), which calls it "not lawful", and 76B(3) and 76E(1) say "must not".**
"Not in accordance with" is my word for that, not the text's; the text says "not lawful" and "must not".
I see no reading of "in accordance with sections 76B to 76G" that a purchase made in breach of a "must not" still satisfies.

**Where the question is genuinely open.**
Some requirements are procedural and the text does not say what their breach does to the purchase: 76B(7), text 8414 ("A private company may purchase or acquire any of its shares under section 76C, 76D, 76DA or 76E by lodging the following with the Registrar"), and 76D(2A), text 8611 (the company "must, before passing the special resolution ..., obtain the consent of at least 75% of the holders of the shares of that class"). For these a function that asks "is this in accordance" has no sentence of the text to answer from beyond the plain meaning of "in accordance with", and I would not claim `JUST FALSE` from the text alone.

**What sga3's function actually does at the seam** (`sga3-76b.l4:455-480`; `sg-interests.l4:46`, `148-153`).
It answers `JUST TRUE` when its four inputs hold and `NOTHING` otherwise; it never answers `JUST FALSE`.
So two errors arrive at the keel's BOOLEAN field.
A real no (for example the 20% cap exceeded) arrives as `NOTHING`.
A purchase that met the four inputs but missed a requirement the function does not ask (76B(7), 76D(2A) or 76F(2) are examples; I did not trace whether its fourth input, "authorised as section 76C, 76D, 76DA or 76E requires", absorbs them) arrives as `JUST TRUE`, which is an over-claim.
The function's name also lacks "(including treasury shares)"; I read that as harmless, since the sections' conditions do not turn on treasury holding, but a caller keyed by name will notice.

### (2) Fix options and their costs

**The same fact is asked as an independent BOOLEAN input at four consumers**, so any option should be judged for all of them: the keel field (`sg-interests.l4:46`; consumed at `:244` and `:257`; asked again by the seats at `sg-seat-keel.l4:171` and `:230`), `s 76(9)(c)` (`sga2-s76-exceptions.l4:426`), `s 76H(1)` (`sga3-76h-76k.l4:25`), and `s 78A(5)`, `s 78A(1)` and `s 78A(2)` (`sga3-78a-78c.l4:28`, `:50`, `:65`).
The three other consumers are MAYBE BOOLEAN functions that already answer `NOTHING` when their own gate fails, so a three-valued answer can pass through them; only the keel's chain, from `s 7(9)` up to the two interest functions, is BOOLEAN.

**(a) Make the keel field a MAYBE BOOLEAN and say what `s 7(9)` does with `NOTHING`.**
A workable rule: `s 7(9)` is `JUST TRUE` if any of (a), (b), (c), (d) holds, otherwise `NOTHING` if (ca) is `NOTHING`, otherwise `JUST FALSE`.
Costs:
- `s 7(9)` is consumed by two BOOLEAN functions that recurse through the body-corporate chain (`s 7 the person has an interest apart from subsection (4A)` and `s 7 the person has an interest`, `sg-interests.l4:242-260`), so the type change spreads to both, up to `s 7 the person has an interest in the shares`, which is already a MAYBE BOOLEAN.
- One unresolved (ca) limb out of five would make the whole interest question `NOTHING` unless another limb holds, which is a large effect for one limb.
- Sixteen lines of `enc/tests-independent.l4` build `s 7 interest facts` positionally with BOOLEAN literals (t:17, t:117, t:119-125, t:128-131, t:134-136) and would stop type-checking.
- The two seats that ask the field as an `@export` input (`sg-seat-keel.l4:171`, `:230`) would ask a user a MAYBE BOOLEAN question.
- It carries sga3's `NOTHING` for a real no across the seam unchanged, which is the error in (1).

**(b) Keep the BOOLEAN, and wrap the sga3 answer in one line at the place the field is filled, with a fork line.**
For example `(<the sga3 function> ...) EQUALS JUST TRUE`, which gives `TRUE` for `JUST TRUE` and `FALSE` for anything else.
Costs:
- With sga3 unchanged, this turns a `NOTHING` into `FALSE`, which the sga3 note says the rule of 2026-10-29 forbids (I have the rule only as that note cites it, and I have not read the rule itself), so the fork line would have to argue the exception; the argument is (ca)'s "if", but then the cleaner place to say it is in the function (option (d)), not in a wrapper that hides it.

- It leaves the over-claim from (1) untouched, and the other three consumers still take their own BOOLEAN.
- No type changes, so no test changes.

**(c) Leave it as an input and say so in the notes.**
The keel's BOOLEAN stays what the caller answers; the sga3 function is documented as one way the caller may answer it.
Costs:
- Nothing in the tree ties the four consumers to the function or to each other, so one acquisition can be "in accordance" at one consumer and not at another.
- The over-claim and the `NOTHING`-for-no problem are still there, only now a human sees them.

**(d) Fix at the source: have the sga3 function answer `JUST FALSE`, not `NOTHING`, when a condition it asks is not met, and feed the keel with the one-line wrapper of (b).**
The fork line would quote (ca)'s "if" (IS-1, Ex1), 76B(3) and 76E(1) ("must not ... unless"), and 76F(2) ("no purchase ... is lawful"), and cite GR-2 ("the provision's own question").
The wrapper then maps `JUST FALSE` to `FALSE`, so no `NOTHING` is converted, and the function can also feed `s 76(9)(c)`, `s 76H(1)` and `s 78A` if the lead wants the four consumers to agree.
