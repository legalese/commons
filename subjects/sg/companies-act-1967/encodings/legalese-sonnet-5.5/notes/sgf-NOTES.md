## Seat, scope and method

The seat is the one the brief fixes: an individual who is, or is about to become, a director, a company secretary or a member of a company, or who deals with one.
A minority member is the person most of this part is for: may I apply for an order, on what ground, what may the Court order, what must I lodge and by when, may I act in the company's name, what notice must I give the directors, what may the Court do about costs, can my case be stopped.
There is never a seat for the Registrar, the Minister or the Court: where a rule is about one of them it is encoded in layer 1 for what it decides, and the seat asks only what it means to the individual.
The Court's own judgments (that a ground is established, that a person is a proper person, that notice is not expedient, that an interest may be substantially affected, what order it "thinks fit") are plain inputs, named for the fact, never guessed.
Each seat question calls a layer-1 function and restates no rule.
`sgf-seat.l4` is generated from one table of inputs and one table of questions, so that one fact keeps one name and one `@desc` in every question.

**F-216-7** s 216(5), `s 216(5) the last day for lodging a copy of the order with the Registrar`, `s 216(5) the applicant has failed to comply with subsection (5)` and the rule `s 216(5) the applicant must lodge a copy of the order with the Registrar within 14 days`.

Taken: `JUST TRUE` for a member, and for a person to whom shares have been transmitted by operation of law; `NOTHING` for anyone else (silent).
Other reading: `JUST FALSE` for anyone else, reading the subsection as a closed statement of who counts.

**F-216A-4** s 216A(3), `s 216A(3) the bar on bringing the action or arbitration, or making the intervention, applies`.

### Cross-cutting forks

**F-X-1** "debenture".
The meaning of "debenture" is s 4(1), in the keel (`s 4(1) "debenture" the instrument is a debenture`).
The seat asks the fact "I hold a debenture of the company" and glosses it in the `@desc`; the keel function is not called, because it needs seven facts about the instrument that a holder asked "do you hold one" would not be asked here.
A caller unsure whether an instrument is a debenture runs the keel function first and passes its answer.

**F-X-2** "director" and "member".
The count of directors (s 216A(3)(a)) is a number the person states; who is within the word "director" (s 4(1)) is not recomputed.
"Member" is a plain fact (`the person is a member of the company`), as is the transmittee fact; the regulative rules use the keel's `Member`, `Creditor`, `Minister` and `Company` parties.

**F-X-3** the Minister.
There is no seat for the Minister.
Layer 1 encodes the Minister's limb in s 216(1) and s 216A(1)(b); the seat passes `FALSE` for "the person is the Minister" in every question but one, and that one asks what the Minister's power means for a member's company.

**F-X-4** `SHANT` derivation.
N2 derives a prohibition where the text names the bearer.
Only s 216(4) does ("the company concerned"), and its rule is encoded.
The bars in s 216B(1) and (2) name no bearer ("must not be stayed"), so they stay decision functions.

**F-X-5** the Court's judgments.
Each judgment the text hands to the Court is a conditional input named for the fact: the opinion that a ground is established, that notice is not expedient, that the interest of a complainant may be substantially affected, that a person is a proper person, that the Court is satisfied, what terms it thinks fit.
They are never guessed, and no `NOTHING` becomes a `FALSE` in a caller.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (sgf)

In this part a "yes" (`JUST TRUE`) means the text of ss 216 to 216B says yes to the case: a person may apply, a ground is established, a bar applies, a copy was not lodged in time.
A "no" (`JUST FALSE`) means the text says no in its own words: a "means" definition that none of its paragraphs satisfies (a complainant), an "unless" condition that is not met (s 216A(3)(a), (b), (c)), a "where" condition that fails (s 216(3), s 216A(6)), the provision's own question answered no (a ground is not established, the applicant has not failed to lodge), and the two "may" sites of split question (ii).
"Not covered" (`NOTHING`) means the text does not say: a grant-named function whose class or "if" gate is not met (the Court may not be made out, but the text does not say it may not), a bar outside its own limit ("by reason only", "without", "under section 216A", an inconsistent alteration), a list that is not closed (a buyer not named in s 216(2)(d), a matter off the s 4(8) list, a dismissal for another reason), an "includes" list, a person to whom s 216(7) says nothing, and a Court decision that has not yet been made.
A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`: "the Court may" answered `NOTHING` is not "the Court may not".
In the seat, a question named as a power ("may I ...", "may the Court ...") is never answered no by `NOTHING`, and a question named as a bar ("am I barred ...", "is the company barred ...") has `JUST TRUE` as "yes, barred" and `NOTHING` as "the section does not say it is barred".

## What the text turns out to say (numbered findings)

1. **The Minister's limb is not tied to "company" in s 4(1).**
   Section 216(1) gives the Minister a power "in the case of a declared company under Part 9", and s 216A(1)(b) a place among complainants on the same words.
   Section 229, for Part 9, says "“declared company” means a company or foreign company which the Minister has by order declared to be a company to which this Part applies" (21909-21911), so a declared company need not be a "company" under s 4(1).
   Both functions therefore take the Minister's limb on the declared-company fact alone (F-216-1); the other reading ties it to s 4(1).
2. **Section 216(7) speaks of "This section".**
   A transmittee of shares is treated as a member for s 216 only; nothing in the text says the transmittee is a "member" for s 216A(1)(a), "any member of a company".
   `s 216A(1)` does not apply `s 216(7)`.
3. **"The Court" is the General Division of the High Court (s 4(1)), and s 216A(6) sends the application for permission to a District Court.**
   "“Court” means the General Division of the High Court" (1353).
   Section 216A(2) lets a complainant apply "to the Court", and s 216A(6) says an application "under subsection (2) must be made in a District Court" where the action is in the State Courts.
   The text does not say whether "the Court" in s 216A(3), (4) and (5) is then the District Court.
   The functions ask the Court's judgments as inputs and do not name a court.
4. **Section 216A(6) speaks of "the action" and not of an arbitration**, though s 216A(2) says "action or arbitration".
   Whether (6) reaches an application to bring or take over an arbitration is not answered.
5. **Section 216(6) can only be committed by the applicant.**
   It makes guilty "Any person who fails to comply with subsection (5)", and subsection (5) binds only "the applicant".
   The offence function takes "the person is the applicant for the order" as an element.
6. **No consequence is stated for a forbidden alteration under s 216(4).**
   The text says the company "does not have power" without the Court's permission; it does not say whether a further alteration made without it is void, voidable or only a breach.
   The regulative rule's `BREACH` records the fact of the breach and no consequence.
7. **Two routes lead to a derivative action and the text does not relate them.**
   An order under s 216(2)(c) may "authorise civil proceedings to be brought in the name of or on behalf of the company", and s 216A gives a complainant a separate route by permission.
   Section 216A(2) says "Subject to subsection (3)", which does not mention s 216(2)(c).
8. **"Reasonable" appears in s 216A(5)(c) and not in s 216B(3).**
   The Court may order the company to pay "reasonable legal fees and disbursements incurred by the complainant" (s 216A(5)(c)), and interim costs "including legal fees and disbursements" (s 216B(3)); the text does not say whether "reasonable" limits the interim costs.
9. **"Including the applicant" can be read two ways.**
   In s 216(1)(a) and (b) the members or holders of debentures harmed are "one or more ... including the applicant".
   It can mean the applicant must be among those affected, or that the class may include the applicant.
   Each function asks the whole phrase as one fact and does not decide.
10. **The bars in s 216B(1) and (2) name no bearer.**
    "An application ... must not be stayed" does not say who must not stay it, so no `SHANT` is derived (N2) and both bars are decision functions only.
11. **The list in s 216(2) is not closed.**
    The order may include (a) to (f) "without limiting the foregoing", so a power off the list is `NOTHING`, never `FALSE`.
12. **The 14 days in s 216A(3)(a) are counted from the notice to the application.**
    The text says "14 days’ notice ... of the complainant’s intention to apply"; it does not say whether the days are clear (F-216A-3).
    `s 216A(3)(a) the latest day on which the complainant can give the directors notice` and `s 216A(3)(a) the earliest day on which the complainant can apply to the Court` count both ways round, and agree on three pairs of dates in `sgf-tests-s216a.l4` (a year end and a leap year among them).
