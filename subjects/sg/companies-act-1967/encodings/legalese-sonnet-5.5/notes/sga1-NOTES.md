## Seat (sga1)

The seat is a person asking one question about their own position under these provisions: a director or officer of a company, a member or holder of shares, a person who deals with the company, or the company itself.
The Registrar and the Court appear only as what they mean to the person: the question is whether the Court "may" validate shares (s 72), not a rule addressed to the Court; no regulative rule is addressed to either.
`sga1-seat.l4` holds 72 questions in 11 `§` groups in the order of the text.
Each question is named "Under section N, ..." (or "Under schedule 6 ..."), carries `@export`, asks its inputs with `@desc` worded as the person would be asked, and its body is one call of a layer-1 function: no body restates a rule.
75 of the 160 layer-1 decision functions that answer a `MAYBE` are asked in the seat; the rest are reached through layer 1 (definitions, savings, record constructors and the sub-functions a seat function calls).
`sga1-cases-seat.l4` holds 186 assertions, at least one case for every question, 39 of them cases outside the encoding (an explicit `NOTHING`, asserted).
**Direction.** Each question must ask the same way round as the layer-1 body it calls ("barred" asks barred, "late" asks late, "not yet taken effect" asks not yet taken effect), because a seat that flips the direction answers the opposite of the text.
`work/seat_direction.py` compares, for every question, the words that fix the direction in the layer-1 function name with the question; it is a word-level check, and all 72 pairs were also read by eye.
It flagged 1 pair: s 63A(2), where the question reads "need the return of allotment not state ..." and the function "need not state": the same direction, the words are not adjacent.

## Fork register (sga1)

Every place where the text can be read two or more ways with different outcomes, or where this encoding took a reading no ruling covers.
Each entry gives the provision, the function, the answer taken, the other reading, and what licenses the answer taken.
The ids are labels: **RS** restrictions on allotment and commencement of business (ss 59 to 62, `sga1-restrictions.l4`); **IS** issue and returns (ss 62A to 63C, `sga1-issue.l4`); **RT** rights attaching to shares (ss 64 to 68, `sga1-rights.l4`); **AL** alteration and redenomination (ss 71 to 73B, `sga1-alteration.l4`); **CL** classes and conversion (ss 74 to 75, `sga1-classes.l4`); **SC** the Sixth Schedule; **X** one reading used at sites in more than one module.
There is no F-IS1 and no F-RT6: the ids were renumbered once, on 2026-11-18, so that no two modules use the same id, and the number F-X3 took over what was F-IS1.
The Guide lines cited are lines of `inputs/ENCODING-GUIDE.md` (section 5).

### Periods used (the keel's conventions, not re-decided here)

The Act has no rule for counting "within N days after X".
This encoding calls the keel's `sg-periods.l4` for every period and writes no date arithmetic of its own, so the keel's conventions P1 to P6 apply (day of the event not counted; "not later than" includes the last day; a month ends on the corresponding date).
Where one of them decides a boundary, the entry says so (F-X3 for an early act, F-AL2 for "beginning on", F-IS2 for the six months).

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (sga1)

Almost every function of this encoding answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (or `MAYBE NUMBER`).
**`JUST TRUE`** is yes: the provision's own test is met on the facts given.
For a bar ("must not ... unless", "does not take effect until") the function is named for the bar: `JUST TRUE` means barred, `JUST FALSE` means the bar does not apply on these facts.
**`JUST FALSE`** is the text saying no to this case in its own words, and in these provisions it comes in these kinds: (1) a "means" definition the thing falls outside ("deemed allotment", "specified share", "redenomination", ss 63(3), 64(5), 73(10)); (2) a flat negative ("does not affect", "is not deemed", "may not be negated or altered", "not capable of being called up", ss 73A(1), 71(2), 64(4), 65(2)); (3) an "unless", "only if" or "until" gate that is met (ss 59(1), 62, 70(3), 70(4), 64A(3), 64A(1)); (4) a failed "where" or "if" of a duty or a deeming (ss 63A(1), 63B(1), 63C, 70(8), 73B(1), 74(5), 60(3), 61(1), 61(2), Sixth Schedule Part 2: reading (a) of the brief); (5) a period or date the text fixes in its own words, outside it (ss 59(4), 62B(2), 62B(7), 66(2), 66(4), 73(7), 73(8)); (6) the provision's own question: a duty not met (the "late" and "in default" functions) or an element of an offence that is unmet (split site (i)).
**`NOTHING`** is "not covered": the encoding does not say yes and does not say no.
It comes in these kinds: a scope limit with no "only" and the case outside it ("on or after 3 January 2016", "a private company", "issued before 29 December 1967", "a public company": ss 63(2), 70(7), 71(1C), 74A(4), 66(2), 64A(1)); a grant or power whose gate is unmet or unused (ss 62B(3), 62B(4), 63(1), 65(1), 65(2), 70(1), 70(6), 71(1), 72, 73(1), 74(4)); a "subject to" that hands the answer to another provision (ss 64(1), 64(3), 74A(1): fork F-X2); the text is silent (s 59(3)(a) states no fine and no term; a penalty function for a person the subsection does not make guilty); a list not closed with "only" (ss 62B(3), 65(1), 71(1)); and "includes" ("vendor", Sixth Schedule Part 3 paragraph 3).
**A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.**
In particular `JUST FALSE` from a function named "the person is guilty of the offence this subsection creates" means this subsection makes the person guilty of nothing on these facts; it does not say the person is innocent of any other offence.
`JUST FALSE` from a "late" function means the text does not find the duty broken on these facts (in time, or the period has not run out); it is not a finding that the duty was met in any other respect.
`JUST FALSE` from "this section gives the director a defence on these facts" (s 60(3)) means this section gives no defence on these facts, never "no defence at all" (Guide line 94, ruling (b)).
The tests assert every "not covered" branch as `EQUALS NOTHING`, so a change that turns one into a no turns a test red.

## Findings: what the text turns out to say

1. **The rendering prints six labels twice.**

   The coverage script counts a repeated label under the same parent as part of the row above, so the table has the units the Act has and not the units the rendering seems to have: 229 label tokens at the start of lines, 223 rows.
2. **s 59(2) says "or", s 60(3) says "or to both"** (lines 6158 to 6159 and 6215 to 6217).
   The two subsections are in the same Division and give the same fine and the same term (a fine not exceeding $5,000 or imprisonment not exceeding 12 months); only s 60(3) allows both.

3. **The offences of these provisions have five penalty shapes, one offence states none, and one has no amount for its default penalty.**
   s 59(2): a fine not exceeding $5,000 or imprisonment not exceeding 12 months, no default penalty.
   s 60(3): the same fine or term, or both.
   ss 61(6), 63A(3), 63B(6) and 73B(4): a fine not exceeding $4,000 **and** a default penalty of $250.
   s 74(5): a fine not exceeding $2,000 "and also to a default penalty", with no amount in the subsection: the penalty record carries the flag `TRUE` and the amount `0`, which means "not stated in this text", not "no default penalty"; a reader must read the flag first, and a record field of type `MAYBE NUMBER` would say it better.
