**F-173A-1 (s 173A(1)(a) to (d), the 14 days).**

**F-173B-1 (s 173B(1)(a), (1)(b), (3), "as soon as practicable but not later than 14 days").**

## Jural relations (sgb2)

Each row is one provision, stated once, as a relation between two parties.
Where the text names no one who may complain of a breach, the holder is given as "not named in the text" (an offence is enforced by prosecution; a duty owed to the company is held by the company).
A name in backticks is a top-level name in a layer-1 module; a short form without backticks (for example "s 168(5)(a)") stands for the functions of that provision, whose full names are in the coverage table.
A heading, a definition, a counting rule or a cross-reference creates no relation and is listed as `none`.
"Void" and "may be set aside" effects are written as a disability of the party who tried to act and the corresponding immunity of the party the act would have bound.

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (sgb2)

These are the readings where a lawyer's judgment, not the encoder's, should settle the answer.
Each is also a fork-register entry (the entry has both readings and the text that licenses each); the list here says what turns on it.

1. **A "may" whose gate is "where" or "on payment" (F-164-9, F-164-10, F-173F-1: split site (ii)).**
   F-164-9 and F-164-10 answer `NOTHING` when the gate fails (the grant rule), where the periods ruling's item (b) would answer `JUST FALSE`.
   The Guide's limit 3 of 2026-11-12 says to leave `JUST FALSE` alone for such a function and to list it; these two sites take the other answer, so they are the first to overrule.
   F-173F-1 answers `JUST FALSE` when the gate fails, by the S1b ruling (Guide line 101, 2026S1bC): the Registrar's power is a public body's power.
   What turns on it: for F-173F-1, whether the Registrar's lack of reasonable cause is read as "the Registrar may not amend the register" (the S1b ruling says so) or as silence; for F-164-9, whether an unpaid request is a "no" or just outside the duty.
2. **"Unless" and "except" once met (F-163-1, F-168-1, F-172B-1, F-173B-1, F-173D-1).**
   The Guide leaves "an 'unless' or 'except' once it is met" to the encoder.
   Taken: `JUST FALSE` (the prohibition does not apply once the "unless" is met).
   The other reading is `NOTHING`: the "unless" limits the prohibition in its own wording, and the case falls outside the limit.
   What turns on it: whether "it is not lawful ... unless there is prior approval" says that an approved dealing is lawful, or says nothing about it.
3. **A bare "must" not done (F-164-1, F-171-1, F-173-1).**
   Taken: `JUST FALSE` ("the company has not done what the section requires").
   The other reading is `NOTHING` (the text says what is required, not what follows).
   The rules for these duties carry no period and so cannot reach `BREACH` (see "Rules with no breach path").
4. **"Within 2 business days" and the 14 days: whether the day of the event itself is in time (F-165-2, F-173A-1).**
   Taken: the notice is in time through the last day, the 2nd business day (or the 14th day) after the day of the event, and a notice on the day of the event is in time; counted with the keel's "not later than the end of a period after".
   The other reading is the keel's window form, under which a notice given on the day of the event itself is early, hence late.
5. **"Until" and the approval's last day (F-161-3).**
   Taken: an approval under s 161(3) is in force through the day on which the earlier event falls (the day the next annual general meeting concludes, or the last day of the period within which it must be held).
   The other reading (the earlier pass) is that "until" excludes that day.
6. **The 14 days of s 164A(1)(c) (F-164A-1).**
   The paragraph does not say from what the 14 days run.
   Taken: from the day the company is served with the notice.
   Other readings: from the day the notice was sent, or the day it was received.
7. **"As soon as practicable but not later than 14 days" (F-173B-1).**
   Taken: both must hold, and "as soon as practicable" is a plain input for a person to judge.
   The other reading is that "as soon as practicable" is the only operative test and the 14 days is an outer bound that is never reached first.
8. **"Under this subsection" in s 171(1C) (F-171-1C).**
   Subsection (1C) has no functions to discharge; taken as a reference to subsection (1A).
9. **The bearer of s 171(4A) (F-171-4A).**
   "The office of secretary must not be left vacant for more than 6 months" names no bearer; the company is taken as the bearer of the regulative rule.
10. **"Sufficiently served if" (F-173-14).**
    Taken: a document that fails a condition is not sufficiently served (`JUST FALSE`); the other reading treats "if" as a safe harbour and answers `NOTHING` for service by another route.
11. **The offences' penalty is "a fine ... or imprisonment ..." with "or", not "or both" (F-162-6, F-163-7).**
    The penalty function gives both ceilings and does not say whether both may be imposed; the text does not say.
12. **An unmet element of an offence (the eight split sites (i)).**
    Where the text introduces the element with "If" or "Where", unmet gives `JUST FALSE`; where it describes the person with a relative clause ("who fails to comply"), unmet gives `NOTHING`.
    The answers follow the wording, not a policy; a lawyer may prefer one answer for all eight (see "Split sites").
13. **"Is a reference to" read as a definition (F-162-12).**
    A person who is not the borrower, or the person to whom the goods were supplied, is `JUST FALSE`; the other reading is `NOTHING`.
14. **Who may recover consideration under s 161(6) (F-161-6).**
    The text says it is "recoverable accordingly" and does not say by whom or from whom; no actor is invented.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (sgb2)

The convention is the Guide's (section 5, the rule of 2026-10-29 and the rulings after it), assumed here and not ruled by a lawyer.
A function in this part answers `JUST TRUE`, `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING` (a `MAYBE BOOLEAN`), or returns a number or a date.

- **`JUST TRUE`** means "yes, as the function is named": the bar applies, the person is guilty, the notice was in time, the exception applies.
- **`JUST FALSE`** means the text itself says no to the case in its own words: a flat negative ("does not include", "do not apply"), a condition the text introduces with "if", "where", "unless" or "except", or a period the text fixes ("within 14 days": a notice after it is `JUST FALSE`).
  It never means "the text is silent".
- **`NOTHING`** means the text does not answer the case: a scope limit with no "only" ("any director who authorises ..." says nothing of a director who did not), a list the text does not close, a grant limited by its own wording, a person or kind the text does not name.
  It is never read as no.
  A seat question that answers `NOTHING` says "the text does not answer this on the facts you gave".
- Every `FALSE` and `NOTHING` branch of layer 1 carries a `-- FALSE-kind (...)` or `-- NOTHING-kind (...)` comment on its own line, naming the words of the text that license it (checked mechanically: 268 such comments; every phrase quoted in them (351 in all) occurs in the text, or in the Guide where the comment cites the Guide; see "What check.sh prints").
- A function that returns a number or a date (a period's last day, the highest fine) is not `MAYBE`: a case outside the text's scope is carried by the `MAYBE` function that asks whether the offence or period applies, and the number function is asked only when it does.
  The penalty functions return `NOTHING` outside the offence.

**In the seat layer** the direction of a question is the direction of its body: "Under section 162(2), is the company barred from making the restricted transaction?" answers `JUST TRUE` when the bar applies; "Under section 165(10), does subsection (1)(a) and (b) not apply to me?" answers `JUST TRUE` when it does not apply.
A question that asks "must ..." answers `JUST TRUE` when the duty is owed.
34 of the 84 questions have no `NOTHING` case in `sgb2-cases-seat.l4`: 28 yes/no questions whose layer-1 function returns only `JUST TRUE` or `JUST FALSE` on every input (a period the text fixes, or a requirement stated whole) and 6 that return a number or a date.

## What the text turns out to say

These are things the text says (or fails to say) that an encoder would not guess; each is checked against the text at the line cited (lines of `inputs/CoA1967.txt`).

1. **The rendering prints a list label twice where a paragraph's tail resumes.** In s 162(1)(a) the tail "(called in this section a relevant director)" is printed under a second "(a)" (line 13421); the same happens at the tail of s 163B(a) (line 13963, "in connection with any alleged negligence, default, breach of duty or breach of trust") and at the end of s 165(2)(a), where "whichever last occurs" is printed under a second "(a)" (line 14298). Structure comes from the labels, but here the label repeats: each tail is taken as belonging to the paragraph above it (F-165-3 for the third; the coverage rows say "the label (a) is printed twice in the rendering").
2. **s 171(1C) speaks of "this subsection" for functions it does not have.** A person "is not to be regarded as being a person who is qualified to discharge the functions of secretary under this subsection", but subsection (1C) names no functions; they are subsection (1A)'s (F-171-1C).
3. **s 165(2) gives a period for notices (1)(a) and (1)(b) only.** Subsection (1)(c) (the events and matters the company needs for section 173A) has no period, yet subsection (9) makes failure to comply with subsection (1) an offence; the rule for (1)(c) cannot reach `BREACH` (F-165-1).
4. **Four offences give "a fine ... or imprisonment" with no "or both"**: ss 162(6), 163(7), 164(17), 165(9). Two give a fine only (s 164A(2): $10,000; s 173H: $5,000, with "also ... a default penalty" whose amount is s 4(1)'s and is not read here). s 164(17) and s 165(9) add "a further fine of $1,000 for every day during which the offence continues after conviction".
5. **s 168(3) makes the failure an offence and states no penalty** (line 14469); this part does not read a penalty from another provision, so no penalty function is written for it.
6. **s 164A(1)(c) does not say from what the 14 days run** (F-164A-1), and paragraph (e) has no period at all: the statement is to be laid "before the next general meeting of the company held after the statement is audited".
7. **The defence in s 164(12) is confined to a prosecution for failing to comply with subsection (1), (1A) or (5)** (line 14129). A prosecution for failing to comply with (6), (8), (9), (10) or (11) has no such defence in the text (F-164-12). Section 165(4)'s defence is likewise confined to (1)(a), (1)(b) and (2) (F-165-4).
8. **The awareness defence of s 165(4) has a 7-day edge and a conclusive presumption.** A person who became aware "less than 7 days before the date of the summons" is within the defence, so one who became aware exactly 7 days before is outside it (F-165-4); and s 165(5) makes a person "conclusively presumed" aware at a time of what the person would have known with reasonable diligence, or what an employee or agent with duties in relation to the person's interest in the company's shares, debentures or participatory interests knew or would have known. The presumption is a deeming rule (`s 165(5) the person is conclusively presumed to have been aware of the fact or occurrence at that time`).
9. **s 172(2) is void "except as permitted by section 172A or 172B"; s 172B(1) is itself a "does not apply where ... except when" with two listed exceptions**, so an indemnity against a liability to someone other than the company is permitted unless it is for a criminal fine, a regulatory penalty, the costs of a criminal defence ending in conviction, the costs of a civil action by the company or a related company ending in judgment against the officer, or an application for relief the court refuses; whether a conviction, judgment or refusal has become "final" takes the appeal rules in s 172B(2), (3).
10. **s 173(4) keeps a former name only for 5 years** ("need only keep ... for a period of 5 years from the date on which the name was furnished", line 14801 region): the function `s 173(4) the Registrar must keep the former name of the director in the register on the day` answers `JUST TRUE` through the fifth anniversary of the day the name was furnished, `JUST FALSE` after it, and `NOTHING` for a day before the name was furnished.
11. **s 163(1) is answered in an order that decides which answer a case gets** (F-163-1): an exempt private company (`NOTHING`), a kind of dealing the subsection does not name (`NOTHING`), a dealing within an exception (`JUST FALSE`), the interest condition unmet (`JUST FALSE`), prior approval given as required (`JUST FALSE`), otherwise `JUST TRUE`. The same facts reordered would give a different answer to a case that fails two of them.
12. **Deleted and repealed provisions**: ss 166, 167 and 170 are shown repealed; s 173(12), (13), s 173A(2), s 173B(4) and s 173H(3), (4) are shown `[Deleted by Act 21 of 2024 wef 09/12/2024]`. They are coverage rows `repealed or blank in this text`; no code.
13. **Two amendments are defined by the date of another Act's commencement**, which the text does not give: s 173E(1) applies to a disqualification on or after the commencement of s 66 of the Corporate and Accounting Laws (Amendment) Act 2025, and s 173G(6) names the commencement of s 40 of the ACRA (Registry and Regulatory Enhancements) Act 2024. Both are inputs.
