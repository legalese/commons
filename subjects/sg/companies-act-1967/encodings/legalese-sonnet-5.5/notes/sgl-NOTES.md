## Where PLAN.md and the brief differ

The brief governs where the two differ.
These are the differences I found, each checked against both documents:

1. **The range, in prose.** The brief's prose says "ss 173J to 195" and calls it the lead's reading made before the work started; PLAN.md's row for `sgl` and the brief's table both give lines 15411-17092, which end at s 189 (see "Range" above).
2. **The grant ruling of 2026-11-12.** PLAN.md (section 8, item 4, and the paragraph "What is assumed, not ruled") states the two status-quo readings: (a) a failed "if" counts as the text's no; (b) a prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not. The brief adds the three limits of the grant ruling of 2026-11-12, which narrow reading (a) for a function named for a grant or a power ("may", "is permitted to", "has power to"): a failed "if" gate there answers `NOTHING`, while `JUST FALSE` stays where the text closes the gate itself and for a bar, a duty or the provision's own question; and "where", "when" and "upon" gates on a "may", and "entitled"-named functions, are listed and not decided. PLAN.md cites the guide's lines 96 to 99 for the grant rule but does not restate the date or the three limits. I followed the brief.
3. **The register of members.** PLAN.md's row for `sgl` says the part "takes: the register of members (sgm) as an input". No rule of this Division asks for the register as such; s 174(6) asks whether "a list showing the names and addresses of the members and the number of shares held by them" was produced and kept open, and I took that as two plain facts (`the list ... was produced at the commencement of the meeting`, `the list remained open and accessible to any member during the continuance of the meeting`).
4. **The keel's inputs for ss 174 and 175.** PLAN.md's table A says the keel asks "one `BOOLEAN` each, named for the fact" for "annual general meeting", "statutory meeting" and "statutory report". The keel's functions (`sg-definitions.l4`) take such a fact as an input; layer 1 *gives* it as the answer of a function, not as a stored boolean: `s 174(1) the meeting is the statutory meeting the section requires`, `s 174(2) the report is the statutory report the section requires`, `s 175(1) the meeting is the annual general meeting the section requires`. A merge that wants a boolean calls these.

Nothing else in PLAN.md section 2 (naming), section 5 (the stable binary's traps), section 7 (the seat) or section 8 (the NOTES sections) contradicts the brief for this range.

## The seat layer (sgl)

`sgl-seat.l4` asks, in the words of the person in the seat, what the layer-1 modules decide; `sgl-cases-seat.l4` holds the cases.
It holds **214 questions** under 14 `§` headings (173J: 7; 174: 20; 175 and 175A: 25; 176 and 177: 17; 178: 7; 179: 14; 180: 6; 181: 18; 182: 3; 183: 17; 184: 11; 184A to 184G: 34; 185 to 187: 11; 188 and 189: 24) and **569 cases**, each an `#ASSERT` in `sgl-cases-seat.l4`; every question has at least one case, every case is satisfied on both builds, and all 214 titles are different.
Every question is `@export <title>?` with `GIVEN ... @desc` on each input; every title begins "Under section N," with the Act's own numbering; the questions are grouped under `§` headings in the order of the text.
Each question's body is one call (or, where the text itself builds one rule on another, a few calls) of a layer-1 function: no rule is restated, and inputs the module wants as a `MAYBE` are built in `WHERE` from a yes/no and a plain value (`IF b THEN JUST v ELSE NOTHING`).
The shared input names are exactly those of PLAN.md section 7 (`I am a director of the company`, `I am the secretary of the company`, `I am a member of the company`, `the company is a private company`, `the company is a public company`, `the liability of the company's members`, `the number of directors of the company`, `the date asked`), with the same `@desc`s; a script (`work/seatlib.py`) refuses to register one input name with two types or two `@desc`s.

**Direction audit (every title read against the layer-1 function its body calls; the list is `work/audit-list.txt`).**
Every title asks the question its body answers, in the same direction.
Where the layer-1 function is a bar, the title is the bar: `s 173J(8)` ("does this Act prohibit the meeting of the board or a committee from being held in the way proposed"), `s 174(7)` ("is the resolution barred because notice of it has not been given in accordance with the constitution"), `s 176(3)` ("is the meeting the requisitionists convene barred because it would be held after 3 months"), `s 181(1A)(a)` ("is a proxy barred from voting on the question"), `s 185` ("is the resolution not effective because notice of the intention to move it was not given to the company in time"), and the four invalidity functions `s 184B(2)`, `s 184D(2)(a)`, `s 184E(2)`, `s 184F(2)`, whose titles ask whether the resolution is *invalid* (and `s 184E(2)` and `s 184F(2)` answer `JUST FALSE` where the text says non-compliance does not invalidate it).
Where a layer-1 function is a permission, the title is the permission.
Titles that read as double negatives were rewritten in the same direction: "need the company not hold an annual general meeting" became "is the company excused from holding an annual general meeting" (the function says the company *need not* hold it, so `JUST TRUE` still means yes, excused), "need I not use all my votes" became "am I free not to use all my votes", "need the participants not be together" became "may the participants be at different places".
Two titles did not start "Under section N," and were rewritten ("Under sections 175(1) and (5)" and "Under sections 175(1) and (2)" became "Under section 175(1), ..., allowing for ..."), and one title said "the fine on conviction" where the body answers "the most I may be fined" (`s 186(4)`), now worded as the most.

**What the answers are for the person.**
`JUST TRUE` is yes; `JUST FALSE` is the text's no; `NOTHING` is "the text does not answer on these facts" (section "Reading a result" below).
The fine questions answer a dollar figure (`MAYBE NUMBER`): the highest fine only; the default penalty some offences also carry is not asked (header of `sgl-seat.l4`).
Two fine questions always answer `NOTHING`, because the text states no penalty: `Under section 181(2), what is the most I may be fined, in dollars` and `Under section 181(5), what is the most I may be fined, in dollars` (the subsections make the person guilty of an offence and say no more).
One question has no input: `Under section 184A(6), do subsections (3), (4) and (4A) require the requisite number of members to formally agree to the resolution on a single day`, whose answer is the text's flat negative (`JUST FALSE`) whatever the facts.

## Fork register (sgl)

One line per site where the text can be read two ways with different outcomes: the provision, the function, the answer taken, the other reading, and the text that licenses each.
Every fork id is also written in the module comment next to the branch it governs, so `grep -n 'F-12' sgl-*.l4` finds the branch.
The "rows" figure in the coverage table counts how many coverage rows carry the id.
"Answer taken" is `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING` unless the cell says otherwise; "grant limit 1" is the first limit of the 2026-11-12 grant ruling (Guide line 96-99: `NOTHING`, with a fork line, for a function named for a grant or a power whose "if" gate failed).
Nothing in this register has been ruled by a lawyer: every reading is "assumed".

## Jural relations (sgl)

Hohfeld's correlatives: a claim (right) is the other party's duty; a privilege (liberty) is the other party's no-right; a power is the other party's liability to have the position changed; an immunity is the other party's disability.
Each relation is stated once, from the holder's side, and the rule that encodes it is named.
Where the text names no holder or no bearer, the row says so: it does not invent one.
A definition, a deeming rule or a rule of evidence creates no relation and is marked `none`.
The Registrar, the Minister and the Court appear only as what they mean to the individual (the brief's seat assumption): a claim, power or liability the person has against or because of them.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (sgl)

For someone who is, or is about to become, a director, the secretary or a member of a company, a seat answer reads like this.

- **`JUST TRUE`** is yes: the meeting may be held that way; the company must give you notice; you are guilty of the offence; the resolution is passed.
- **`JUST FALSE`** is the text's no, in its own words: the section says you are not entitled, the company need not, the resolution is not effective, or (for an offence) one element of the offence is not met on these facts, so *this subsection* makes you guilty of nothing. It is **not** "you may not": see the next bullet.
- **`NOTHING`** is "the text does not answer on these facts". For a power or a permission ("may") whose "if" condition is not met, `NOTHING` means the text neither gives you the permission nor takes it away. For example `Under section 175(2), may the Registrar extend the period for holding the annual general meeting` answers `JUST TRUE` when the company has applied and the Registrar thinks there are special reasons, and `NOTHING` when neither that nor a prescribed class holds: it does not answer "the Registrar may not". Reading `NOTHING` as "no" turns a grant into a bar; reading it as "yes" turns silence into permission.
- **A "where", "when" or "upon" gate on a "may" is `JUST FALSE`** (periods ruling (b)), except where the S1b ruling reads it as a gate on a liberty with no bar to relieve (Guide line 101, 2026S1bC), and is listed as a split site; for example `Under section 173J(3), may the participants in the meeting be at different places` answers `NOTHING` for a meeting held at a physical place only, because the subsection speaks only of a meeting "under subsection (2)(b) or (c)" and the participants' leave is a liberty. A lawyer may prefer `JUST FALSE` there (F-2, and the other split sites (ii)).
- **A number or a date** (`NUMBER`, `DATE`, `MAYBE DATE`): the figure the section gives (days of notice, the last day for a meeting, the most a fine can be). `NOTHING` in a `MAYBE NUMBER` or `MAYBE DATE` means no figure can be given on these facts: for a fine, either you are not guilty on the facts you gave or the text states no fine (ss 181(2) and 181(5)).
- **A fine answers the highest fine only.** The default penalty that some offences carry as well is not asked (it is in the layer-1 `Penalty` record); no offence in these sections carries imprisonment.
- **Bars and invalidity.** Where the title says "barred", "not effective" or "invalid", `JUST TRUE` means the bar applies or the resolution is invalid; `JUST FALSE` means the text says it does not (for example `Under section 184E(2)`, `JUST FALSE` when the company did not notify the members: the resolution is **not** invalidated by that).
- **The conventions behind the answers** are `work/policy.md` (kept, not shipped) and the fork register: a duty's own question, an offence element and a "means" definition answer `JUST FALSE` when unmet; a grant, power or deeming limited by its own wording answers `NOTHING` outside the limit; "includes" answers `NOTHING` when the thing is not within it; a flat negative is the text's no.

## What the text turns out to say (numbered findings)

Each finding was checked against the raw text and the file named in it at the time of writing (the status line above).
None is a claim about the official text.

1. **The range has 25 regulative rules: 13 carry a clock and 12 do not.**
   The 13 are ss 174(1), 174(2), 174(5), 175(1), 176(1), 177(2), 179(7), 184E(1), 185, 186(1), 188(1)(a), 188(3A) and 189(2); the 12 are listed under "Rules with no breach path" (a script finds them by the absence of a `WITHIN` in the rule).
   Two of the rules without a clock, s 174(6) and s 189(1), were added after a last pass over every line of the range that contains "must": the text names the party and the act in each ("The directors must cause a list ...", "must be kept by the company"), so the brief asks for a rule beside the function.
   Every one of the 13 has a `#TRACE` that reaches `BREACH` and one that reaches `FULFILLED`; the 12 have none that can reach `BREACH`.
   The 65 traces in the tests (27 expect `FULFILLED`, 25 `BREACH`, 13 `RESIDUAL`) agree with their `-- EXPECT` lines on both builds.

2. **"Immediately", "without delay" and "within a reasonable time" give no clock.**
   The text uses them at s 176(1) ("immediately proceed duly to convene an extraordinary general meeting of the company to be held as soon as practicable but in any case not later than 2 months after the receipt by the company of the requisition"), s 183(4B) ("the directors must, without delay after it is first received by a director"), s 184C(2) and (3) ("without delay"), and s 188(3B) and (3C) ("within a reasonable time after the resolution is passed").
   Only s 176(1)'s "not later than 2 months" is a period, and it is the one encoded as a clock; the others are encoded as a duty with no `WITHIN`, or (s 184C(2), (3)) as a function asking whether the directors acted "without delay" as a fact.

3. **Section 185 holds three different periods in one sentence.**
   The resolution "is not effective unless notice of the intention to move it has been given to the company not less than 28 days before the meeting at which it is moved"; the company must tell its members at the time and in the manner of the notice of the meeting "or, if that is not practicable", "not less than 14 days before the meeting"; and "if after notice of the intention to move such a resolution has been given to the company, a meeting is called for a date 28 days or less after the notice has been given, the notice, although not given to the company within the time required by this section, is deemed to be properly given".
   The 14-day period is the regulative rule's clock (time 0 is a stated day; the last day is 14 days before the meeting); the 28 days and the deeming are in `s 185 the resolution is not effective because notice of the intention to move it was not given to the company in time` (`sgl-resolutions.l4:238`).

4. **Section 183 has two deposit periods and an exception for the annual general meeting, and two ways to count members.**
   A requisition requiring notice of a resolution must be deposited "not less than 6 weeks before the meeting", any other requisition "not less than one week before the meeting"; but if "an annual general meeting is called for a date 6 weeks or less after the copy has been deposited, the copy though not deposited within the time required by this subsection is deemed to have been properly deposited" (lines 16468 to 16484; `s 183(4) the conditions on which the company is bound to give notice or to circulate are met`, `sgl-circulation.l4:74`).
