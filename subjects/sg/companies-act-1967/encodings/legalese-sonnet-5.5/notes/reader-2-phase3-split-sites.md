## How this was made

  Every agent claim in `g2-split-sites.md` was confirmed by that run; the cells say "probe" where I rely on it.
- The Guide lines I use, from `PROMPT.md`:
  - In short: JUST FALSE only where the text says no in its own words; NOTHING where it "limits a grant or prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit".
  - Periods ruling of 2026-11-05: (a) a period the text fixes: outside it JUST FALSE; (b) "a 'where ...' condition: when it fails the answer is JUST FALSE"; (c) "a scope limit with no 'only'": NOTHING.
  - Delegated-definitions ruling of 2026-11-05, (c): "when", "upon" and "whenever" count as "where".
  - Example 2: a list of persons who may apply, not closed with "only", answers NOTHING for a creditor.
  - Grant ruling of 2026-11-12 (not confirmed by the maintainer): limit 1, a "may" whose "if" gate failed answers NOTHING; limit 2, keep JUST FALSE where the text closes the gate and for "a bar, a duty or the provision's own question"; limit 3, "where", "when" and "upon" gates keep JUST FALSE and are listed.
- Status words: DECIDED means the words of the text settle the answer without any convention for an unmet gate; OPEN means the answer depends on which Guide line governs.
  The Guide's rulings (b) and (c) name "where", "when", "upon" and "whenever", and name nothing else, so a site whose only gate word is one of those is OPEN: the text is silent about the outside case, and JUST FALSE comes from the ruling.

My lean rests on one distinction, stated here so each row can be read against it.
A gate that names a condition the actor must meet or supply (a "where" condition, notice, payment, an application, the Court's own satisfaction) leaves the power absent when it is unmet: lean JUST FALSE.
A clause that only names the proceeding or case it is about ("On the hearing of any application under subsection (6)", "In a case referred to in subsection (1)(b)") is a scope description: lean NOTHING.
A person who is not the named applicant or not within the named class gets NOTHING (Guide example 2).

A note on my own tests, since this changes what two of them mean.
My assertions are not uniform: S112b (L171, s154(6), 13 days' notice, an "upon" gate) expects JUST FALSE, but S158b (L251, s157B, a literal "Where") and S298 (L466, s173F, a literal "Where") expect NOTHING, because there I applied the In-short line.
Under the lean above, and under ruling (b) with limit 3, a literal "Where" gate gives JUST FALSE.
If the lead keeps limit 3, S158b is a SCENARIO error of mine (triage line 8 changes from AMBIGUOUS to SCENARIO) and S298, which passes today, would then fail against the encoding.
S128b (L196, a former director) and S165b (L261, a creditor's application) stand: those are a class limit and a different applicant, not an unmet "where".
