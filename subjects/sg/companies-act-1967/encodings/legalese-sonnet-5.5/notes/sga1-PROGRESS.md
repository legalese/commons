## How the `.l4` files are made

The layer-1 sources are `work/src/*.l4.in` (outside this folder; `work/` is not shipped).
`work/build.py` expands them into the seven `sga1-*.l4` layer-1 modules in this folder: it quotes the raw text mechanically (`-- src:N | ...` lines), computes each `@ref` locator, and generates the penalty record, guilty function and penalty function of an offence (the `@@OFFENCE` macro).
The tests (`sga1-tests-*.l4`) are written by hand; `sga1-seat.l4` and `sga1-cases-seat.l4` are generated by `work/gen_seat.py` and `work/gen_cases.py`.
`NOTES.md` is generated by `work/make_notes.py` from hand-written pieces in `work/notes/` and from tables made out of the files themselves; rerun it after any change to a `.l4` file, after `work/run_gates.sh` and `check.sh`.

- A grant gated by "if" answers NOTHING when the gate fails (grant ruling of 2026-11-12, limit 1); an "unless" or "only if" gate answers JUST FALSE (limit 2).

- "Where" and "If" gates of a duty answer JUST FALSE when they fail (periods ruling of 2026-11-05, item (b)): ss 63A(1), 63B(1), 63B(3), 63C, 64A(4), 70(8), 73B(1), 74(5).

- A period the text fixes answers JUST FALSE outside it (periods ruling, item (a)): s 62B(2), s 62B(7), s 66(2), s 66(4), s 73(7), s 73(8).

- A `MUST` with no time limit of its own (s 66(4), s 63B(1), s 63B(3)) is a rule with no `WITHIN`; it cannot reach BREACH (NOTES.md, "Rules with no breach path (sga1)").

## Open points (none blocks the report)

- s 74(5) names "a default penalty" and no amount: the penalty record carries the flag TRUE and the amount 0, which means "not stated" (NOTES.md, finding 3); a `MAYBE NUMBER` field would say it better.
- ss 63B(2), 64A(4) and the Registrar's duties (ss 60(2), 61(3), 61(7)) are decision functions with no regulative rule (NOTES.md, "Rules with no breach path (sga1)").
- Sixth Schedule row 29 asks a fact the keel could answer (`s 6 the corporations are deemed to be related to each other`); it is taken as an input (NOTES.md, "Seams (sga1)").

## Traps met

- `sed -i` is blocked in this sandbox ("-I or -i may not be used with stdin"): edits go through python or the editor tool.
- `srcrefs.py --strict`: an `@ref` with no quote run of its own is governed by the nearest run above, so a deontic rule placed after an offence block must carry its own `@@REF` (or sit before the offence).
- The first coverage script missed `@ref` locators written as one line (`(src:6334)`) and reported two encoded rows with no name; it now reads both forms.
