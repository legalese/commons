### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (sgh)

In this row, a `JUST TRUE` answer says the text says yes to the case put to it.
A `JUST FALSE` answer is the text saying no in its own words: a "means" definition, a condition the text introduces with "if", "only if", "unless", "until", "except" or a proviso, a list closed with "only" or "either ... or", a flat negative ("does not", "shall not", "may not", "is not allowed"), a period the text fixes in its own words (the periods ruling, item (a)), a failed "where", "when", "upon" or "on" (the periods ruling, item (b), and the delegated-defences ruling, item (c)), or the provision's own question on a duty, a bar or an offence.
Every `JUST FALSE` branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`, and the quote is mechanically checked against the text by `work/checkquotes.py` (all 124 FALSE-kind comments found; 9 NOTHING-kind comments join paragraph labels with lead-ins and are listed by the checker as warnings, not as missing).
A `NOTHING` answer is "the text does not say": it is not a no.
Every `NOTHING` branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, where the why is one of: silent, list not closed, includes, points outside the text, limited by its own wording (no branch of this row says "undefined term").
A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.

What a "no" means for a person at the seat.
On a question about a duty or a bar (for example "Under section 20(1), is the Registrar barred from registering the constitution"), `JUST FALSE` means the provision does not apply to the facts given, and the question's own text says no; it does not mean the person is free of every other rule.
On a question about an offence (for example "Under section 8A(8), am I guilty of the offence of giving a false or misleading statement"), `JUST FALSE` means this subsection makes the person guilty of nothing on these facts: an element of the offence is unmet; it does not say the person is innocent of any other offence (split site (i), fork line F-H-S2).
On a question about a power, a grant or a right (a "may"), `JUST FALSE` appears only where the text closes the gate in its own words ("only", "unless", a failed "where", "when" or "upon" under the periods ruling), and `NOTHING` is the answer where an "if" gate, "on payment" or "on application" fails or the case lies outside the words of the grant: the grant ruling of 2026-11-12, limit 1 (fork lines F-H-S1 and F-H-S3); the one exception is `s 21(7)`, whose failed "where" gate on a liberty answers `NOTHING` by the S1b ruling (Guide line 101, 2026S1bC).
On a question about a counting rule, a deeming rule or a definition, `NOTHING` means the text does not reach the case.

What "not covered" is in this row: the answer `NOTHING` (a `MAYBE` with no value); the Act's own words for the case are missing, or the matter is handed to an instrument or an authority outside the sources.
No constructor of this row is named for "not covered"; every constructor and type this row declares begins with its section label.
Where a person's facts are not enough to apply the provision (a Minister's or Registrar's state of mind, "satisfied", "considers necessary"), the fact is an input named for the fact, and the answer follows from it; the encoding never guesses it.

The seat's questions return the same `MAYBE BOOLEAN` or `MAYBE` value as the layer-1 function they call.
A caller who treats `NOTHING` as `FALSE` has made a legal claim the text does not make.

## What the text turns out to say (numbered findings)

1. **The heading of s 12E says something the text of s 12E does not.**
   The body heading above s 12E reads "Exclusion of residential address from public access if contact address is available" (lines 3215-3216), but s 12E(1) states no condition about a contact address (lines 3218-3231); the contact address appears only in s 12E(3)(a) (a change of contact address lodged) and in s 12F(8) (the bar on providing one).
   The functions follow the text of the subsections, not the heading (a comment at the head of `sgh-address.l4` says so).
   The arrangement table at the head of the file also prints the heading broken across two entries, so that "12D." sits against "Exclusion of residential address from public access if contact" and "12E." against "address is available" (lines 70-71); this is the garbling the brief warns of.
2. **Paragraph (a) of s 12(2), s 12(2A), s 12(2C) and s 12(2D) is shown as deleted.**
   The text shows "(a) [Deleted by Act 24 of 2025 wef 06/05/2026]" at lines 2811 (s 12(2)), 2877 (s 12(2A)), 2902 (s 12(2C)) and 2915 (s 12(2D)); the four rows are in the coverage table as `repealed or blank in this text`, with s 8(5), (6), (6A), s 16 and s 16A (nine rows in all).
   The encoding of s 12(2)(b), (c) and (d) and s 12(2A) to (2D) answers the text as it stands in this edition and says nothing of the deleted paragraphs.
3. **Three provisions govern the certificate of confirmation of incorporation.**
   Section 17(9) and s 19(7) say that "Upon the application of a company and payment of the prescribed fee, the Registrar must issue to the company a certificate of confirmation of incorporation"; s 12(2AA) says such a certificate "may only be issued to the company upon an application made in accordance with those provisions".
   The duty (17(9), 19(7)) and the closed gate (12(2AA), "only") are separate functions; the closed gate answers JUST FALSE for an issue without an application, and the duty answers JUST FALSE where "Upon" fails (periods ruling, item (b)).
   Whether the two together mean the Registrar must issue the certificate after an application and may not otherwise is a reading of the combination that no single provision states.
4. **The 14 days in s 13(1) are a condition of the Court's power, not a deadline for a duty.**
   The sentence is "If a corporation or person ... fails to make good the default within 14 days after the service ... of a notice requiring it to be done, the Court may, on an application by any member or creditor of the corporation or by the Registrar, make an order" (lines 3379-3397).
   No regulative rule is written for s 13(1) (fork F-H-D2); `s 13(1) the corporation or person failed to make good the default within 14 days after the service of the notice` carries the period, with a test on both sides of the last day.
5. **The two-year window of s 17(6) closed on 15 August 1986.**
   Section 17(6) says a guarantee company with a share capital registered before 15 August 1984 "must, within 2 years of that date, elect to convert and re-register".
   Under conventions P1 and P4 of `sg-periods.l4` the last day is 15 August 1986 (730 days: 1985 and 1986 have no 29 February); `s 17(6) the period of 2 years specified in section 17(6) has expired` is FALSE on that day and TRUE the day after (`sgh-tests-formation.l4:49-50`), and it is the seam for the keel's s 4(1) "limited company"; the core's fourth `Liability of members` constructor, `limited by shares and by guarantee`, exists only until the end of this period (PLAN.md section 3, `sg-core.l4`).
6. **Section 21(4)(b) is a "must" that s 21(4B) takes much of the sting out of.**
   Subsection (4)(b) says the subsidiary "subject to subsections (4A) and (4B), ... must, within the period of 12 months or such longer period as the Court may allow ... dispose of all of its shares in the holding company"; subsection (4B) says shares not disposed of "may, subject to subsections (4C) and (6E), be held or continued to be held by the subsidiary".
   Fork F-H-D4: the duty stands, with a rule that reaches BREACH when the period passes with the shares undisposed, and (4B) is encoded as its own function.
   The other reading (the saving removes the duty) is in the fork register.
7. **Twenty-four rules of the range have a duty with no time limit and no stated consequence.**
   The rules are s 12E(1), s 12E(3), s 12E(4), s 12F(2), s 12F(4), s 12F(5), s 12F(9), s 22(2), s 17(9), s 19(1)(a), s 19(1)(b), s 19(1)(c), s 19(2), s 19(3), s 19(4), s 19(7), s 20(2), s 8B(4)(d), s 8E, s 12B(1), s 12B(2), s 12D(2), s 12A(3), s 14(5) (provisions as in "Rules with no breach path (sgh)"): each is a "must" with no "within" and no stated consequence.
   They are listed under "Rules with no breach path (sgh)"; no trace can show them breached, and I did not invent a deadline or a consequence.
   Of the 42 rules of the range, 18 do have a breach path: a prohibition (`SHANT`) or a "must" with a stated period.
8. **The defence of s 8A(7) covers only a requirement to produce books.**
   It applies "Where a person is charged with an offence under subsection (6) in respect of a requirement to produce any books relating to the affairs of a corporation" (lines 2535-2539); a requirement to provide an explanation or make a statement under s 8A(3) is outside its words.

9. **Section 21(6E)(d) and (e) are not a closed pair.**
   Paragraph (d) says "where the subsidiary is a wholly-owned subsidiary of the holding company, no dividend may be paid, and no other distribution ... may be made"; paragraph (e) says "where the subsidiary is not a wholly-owned subsidiary ..., a dividend may be paid".

10. **Section 22(1AA) reaches only companies to which s 22(1)(e) applies.**
