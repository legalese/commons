### How the seat layer is built

The seat is an individual who is, or is about to become, a director, the company secretary or a member (shareholder) of a company, or who deals with one.
There is never a seat for the Registrar, the Minister, the Authority or the Court: where a provision gives the Court or the Registrar a power, the seat question is what that power means to the individual ("may the Court relieve me", "may I appeal").
Every question is named "Under section N, ..." with the Act's own number, has `@export` and an `@desc` on every input, takes plain inputs (yes or no, numbers, dates and the modules' own choices), and calls the layer-1 function of the same provision.
Three layer-1 functions take an optional value (`s 407(2)` a `MAYBE Penalty`, `s 408(1)` twice a `MAYBE NUMBER`).
The seat asks each of them as a yes or no plus a number and builds the optional value inside the question.
The questions are generated from `work/seat/questions.txt` and the input descriptions in `work/seat/desc*.txt`, so one fact keeps one `@desc` in every question that asks it.
The cases of `sgo-cases-seat.l4` are the facts and the expected answers that the layer-1 tests wrote from the text, asked through the seat question that calls the same function (a yes case, a case where the text says no, and a case the text does not answer, as far as the question has each).
The seat does not use the shared input names that `inputs/PLAN.md` section 7 reserves (`I am a director of the company` and the others): this row asks about other facts (whether a notice was published on a website, whether a dividend was paid out of profits), and where it asks about the person it asks in the words of the layer-1 input.

#### Seat questions whose name and body differ

Every seat question was compared with the layer-1 function it calls.
No question differs from its body in direction: none is titled as a permission while its body is a bar, and none is negated to make it fit.
Where the layer-1 function is a bar, the question is a bar: `s 392(6)` ("is the Court barred from making the order"), `s 393` ("is an inspector barred ..."), `s 403(1)`, `s 403(1A)`, `s 403(1C)` ("barred from being paid as dividends"), `s 405(2)` ("is the company barred from using the word Private or Sendirian"), `s 409A(7)` ("is the Court barred from requiring the undertaking as to damages") and `s 409B(3)` ("may no further proceedings be taken").
Where the function says "does not invalidate" or "does not apply", the question says so (`s 387A(5)`, `s 387B(4)`, `s 392(2)`, `s 392(2A)`, `s 392(3)`, `s 392(5)`, `s 403(1B)`, `s 409C(3)`).
Seven differences were found and fixed during this check: the questions for `s 387A(5)` and `s 387B(4)` had asked whether a failure "leaves the proceedings valid", which is not the same as "does not invalidate", and five officer questions (`s 396(3)`, `s 396A(4)`, `s 396B(1)`, `s 401(1)`, `s 405(2)`) had said "am I, as an officer" where the function's subject is "the officer" and its inputs ask about that officer.
What remains is a difference of voice and not of subject: the questions below speak in the first person where the layer-1 function says "the person", and the inputs of each are worded in the second person to match.

> The rule of 2026-10-29 for what `JUST FALSE` and `NOTHING` mean is the convention the rows written since then follow (applied in legalese/commons#30 and #37); it was assumed, not ruled as a whole, and the rulings below settle parts of it.
> In short, a function answers `JUST FALSE` only where the text itself says no to the case in its own words: a "means" definition, a condition the text introduces with "if", "only if", "as long as" or "unless" (and plain variants such as "while", "until" and "except"), a list the text closes with "only", a flat negative ("does not", "shall not", "may not").
> Everywhere else it answers `NOTHING`: where the text lists without closing the list, says "includes", leaves a term undefined, is silent, points outside the sources, or limits a grant or prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit.

> Each ruling is recorded again, word for word, in the `NOTES.md` of each row that carries that rule's `NOTHING-kind` tags: in its fork register where it has one, and in a section of its own where it has none.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (sgo)

A function of this row answers `JUST TRUE`, `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING`.
`JUST TRUE` is "yes": the facts meet the provision (the offence is made out, the notice is served, the Court may make the order).
`JUST FALSE` is the text's own "no" and nothing more: a definition that says "means" that the case does not meet, a condition the text introduces with "if", "unless", "except", "where" or "so long as" that the facts do not meet, a flat negative, or a period the text fixes in its own words that the date falls outside (for example "within 28 days after" in s 409C(1)).
In this Act that is, for example, "a dividend paid out of profits is not barred by s 403(1), which says 'except out of profits'", or "the person may not apply under s 391(2) unless the person has reason to apprehend a claim".
`NOTHING` is "the text does not say": the section is silent about the case, the list is not closed, the word is only "included", a term is undefined, the answer sits in another Act or in regulations that are not among the sources, or the provision limits a grant or a prohibition in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit.
In this Act that is, above all, an offence whose element is not met ("shall be guilty" says nothing of a person who did not do the act), and a duty whose "where" or "if" is unmet (the duty does not reach the case, which is not to say it was broken).
A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`: `NOTHING` is not "no offence" and not "no duty", it is "this section does not decide it".
Every branch that answers one of the two carries a `-- FALSE-kind (...)` or `-- NOTHING-kind (...)` comment in the module with the quoted words.
`REFUSE` does not exist on the stable binary, so "not covered" is `NOTHING` or a constructor, and it is asserted.

## Findings: what the text turns out to say

1. **An offence punishable with exactly 6 months is within both courts (s 409(3)).** The Magistrate's Court takes "any offence ... other than an offence punishable with imprisonment for a term exceeding 6 months", and the District Court takes "an offence punishable with imprisonment for a term of 6 months or more". An offence of exactly 6 months is in both. `s 409(3) the proceedings may be prosecuted in a Magistrate's Court` and `... in a District Court` both answer `JUST TRUE` for 6 (tested).
2. **s 401(1) states no penalty, so s 407(2) fixes it.** The subsection says "shall be guilty of an offence" and nothing about a fine or imprisonment; s 407(2) gives "a fine not exceeding $1,000" where a penalty "is not so mentioned". `s 401(1) the highest penalty on conviction` answers `Penalty OF 1000, 0, FALSE, FALSE`. (s 401(2) and (2A) state $50,000 or 2 years or both; s 402(1) $10,000 or 2 years or both.)
3. **s 403(2)(a) allows a fine or imprisonment, not both.** It says "a fine not exceeding $5,000 or to imprisonment for a term not exceeding 12 months", without the "or to both" of ss 401(2), 401(2A), 402(1), 404(3), 405(1), 405(3) and 406. The `Penalty` record's flag "the text allows both" is `FALSE` for it and `TRUE` for those.
4. **s 405(2)'s prohibition and its offence name different parties.** "A company must not use the word Private or Sendirian ... and every corporation and every officer of a corporation who is in default shall be guilty of an offence": the bar is addressed to "a company", the offence to "every corporation" and "every officer ... who is in default". The penalty is a fine of up to $5,000 "and also to a default penalty" (fork F-405-2).
5. **s 387B(3) counts one day more strictly in limb (a) than in limb (b) under the literal reading.** Limb (a) asks that the document be accessible throughout a period "beginning before the specified number of days before the date of a meeting"; limb (b) asks that the notification be given "not less than the specified number of days before". Read literally the period must begin strictly before the latest day, and the notification may be given on it (fork F-387B-3; tested on both sides).
6. **s 396B(1)'s period is the later of two start dates.** "The shorter of (a) the period of 2 years immediately preceding the commencement of the investigation, or (b) the period between the incorporation of the company and the commencement of the investigation" starts on the later of the day 2 years before and the day of incorporation. `s 396B(1) the first day of the period throughout which proper books of account must have been kept` computes it with sg-periods P10 and is tested over a leap year.
7. **s 397's duties carry no deadline except one interval.** Only s 397(3) says "at intervals of not more than 7 days"; s 397(1) says "at the same time" and s 397(2) says nothing about when (rules with no breach path above).
8. **s 408(1) fixes the daily amount twice over.** The additional penalty for each day is "not more than the amount expressed in the section as the amount of the default penalty or, if an amount is not so expressed, of not more than $200". The amount a section expresses is an input here (it belongs to the section that provides the default penalty), and $200 is the Act's own fallback.
9. **s 409B(1) and (2) cap the composition sum at the lower of two figures.** "Not exceeding the lower of one half of the amount of the maximum fine that is prescribed for the offence, and $20,000". `s 409B(1) the highest sum the Registrar may collect to compound the offence` takes the maximum fine as an input and returns the lower.
10. **The headings of s 404 outlast its first two subsections.** Subsections (1) and (2), under "Fraudulently inducing persons to invest money", are shown "[Deleted by Act 42 of 2001]"; the section's operative text is subsection (3) ("Whoever, being an officer or agent of any corporation ... shall be guilty of an offence") and the evidence rule of subsection (4).
11. **s 406(c) has a period on both sides of one date.** The concealment must be "since or within 2 months before the date of any unsatisfied judgment or order": on or after the date, or from the corresponding date 2 months earlier (sg-periods P6, month-end clamped by P4) to the day before. Tested with a 15 March and a 30 April judgment, and a leap year.
12. **s 392(6) is a bar written as an "unless".** "The Court is not to make an order under this section unless it is satisfied" of three conditions that depend on which of the orders of subsection (4) is sought. The question is named for the bar: `JUST TRUE` is "the Court is barred", `JUST FALSE` is "the conditions are met, so this subsection does not bar".
13. **s 399(2) lends a former officer his or her old status only for the order.** "For the purpose of complying with any such order a former officer is deemed to have the same status, powers and duties as he or she had at the time the act, matter or thing should have been done".
14. **The First Schedule lists three Cap. numbers and does not say "only".** The three rows are Cap. 15, Cap. 174 and Cap. 277, each "The whole". `sch 1 the written law is one of those the First Schedule lists` answers `JUST TRUE` for those three and `NOTHING` for any other number, because the Schedule's own heading refers to section 3(1), which governs the repeal, and the Schedule is not closed with "only".
15. **A deemed rule completes a power elsewhere in the same section.** s 387C(1) lets the company send a notice electronically "with the express, implied or deemed consent of the member"; subsections (2) and (3) define implied and deemed consent. The seat asks the facts of (2) and (3) inside the question for (1), so the person need not know the legal test.
16. **The words "officer" in ss 402, 403, 404 and 406 do not mean the same thing.** s 402(1) is for "an officer of a corporation" and (2) includes a former officer; s 403(2) is for "a director or chief executive officer"; s 404(3) is for "an officer or agent"; s 406 is for a person "while an officer of a company". Each takes the keel's definition (`s 4(1) "officer"`, `"director"`, `"chief executive officer"`) and adds its own widening.
17. **`Penalty` has no place for a default penalty's amount.** The record carries a flag that a default penalty also applies (s 396(3), s 396A(4), s 405(2)); the amount is s 408(1)'s ($200 a day unless the section says otherwise), computed by `s 408(1) the highest additional penalty for each day during which the offence continues`.

## What the text does not answer

- Who may apply under s 399(1): the subsection says "the Court may" and names no applicant (fork F-399-1).
- What a "contrary intention" in a section is, for ss 386A and 392(1): asked as a fact.
- Whether "registered accountant" in s 404(4) has a meaning of its own: the term is not defined in the text read, and is asked as a fact (F-404-1).
- The regulations under s 411, and which offences the Minister prescribes as compoundable under s 409B(4): not among the sources, so every use is an input.
- The words that need a judgment the text hands to someone else are plain inputs: "reasonable diligence" (s 390(1)), "credible testimony" (s 388(1)), "acted honestly and reasonably" and "ought fairly to be excused" (s 391(1)), "substantial injustice" (s 392), "reasonable cause to believe" (s 394(1)), "wilfully" and "knowingly" (ss 396 to 406), "reasonable precautions" (s 396(1)), "excusable" (s 396B(2)), "in default" beyond s 408(3), "intent to deceive" and "intent to defraud" (ss 402, 404, 406).
- The amount a section "expresses as the amount of the default penalty" (s 408(1)), the maximum fine "prescribed" for an offence (s 409B), and the "specified number of days" (s 387B(3)): numbers that sit in other sections or in an instrument, asked as inputs.

## What the stable binary does not accept

The brief's list was followed as given: no `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` or `BECAUSE` (except `LEST BREACH BY ... BECAUSE`, which is accepted and used), `WAITUNTIL` for `WAIT UNTIL`, `Date day month year` and no `YMD`, no `TYPICALLY` on a field, no sets, every `#ASSERT` on one line.
Two of its traps were met in this run, and a third is followed from the brief:
- **`NOT a AND b` parses as `NOT (a AND b)`, and `NOT a OR b` as `NOT (a OR b)`, when written on one line.** It was measured in `s 405(1)`: `IF NOT ((NOT x AND y) OR z)` answered something other than `NOTHING` for the facts where no limb holds, and two test assertions failed until the inner `NOT` was parenthesised. A probe on main-110 (`work/probe/not-probe.l4`, scratch, not in the folder) printed `FALSE` for `NOT a OR b` with a and b both `TRUE` (the intended reading gives `TRUE`) and `TRUE` for `NOT a AND b` with both `FALSE` (the intended reading gives `FALSE`). The same `NOT a` and `OR b` split over two lines inside a parenthesised group printed the intended answer, which is a layout effect this row does not rely on. So in the modules every `NOT` that has an `AND` or an `OR` beside it is in its own parentheses (`s 405(1)`, `s 396B(3)`, including its stocktaking limb); a `NOT` that stands alone as a whole `IF` condition (`IF NOT` followed by one name) has no neighbour and is not parenthesised.
- **A chain of `OR` between comparisons that end `EQUALS JUST TRUE` is ambiguous** (found in `s 387C(1)` when it was first written), so the two sub-answers are named in a `WHERE` and the chain is over booleans.
- **Positional `OF` needs its commas** (the brief's list), so `Penalty OF 1000, 0, FALSE, FALSE` and the constructors with fields are written with them.
Two limits of the checker, not of the compiler: `check.sh` counts assertions and error diagnostics and does not see a `#TRACE` outcome, so each trace's printed outcome was compared with its `-- EXPECT` comment by `work/tools/traces.py` on both builds (the output above); and a seat input must be plain, so an optional value (`MAYBE Penalty`, `MAYBE NUMBER`) is built inside the question from a yes or no and a number.
`@export` text with apostrophes, commas and parentheses is accepted (the seat file loads and runs on both builds).
