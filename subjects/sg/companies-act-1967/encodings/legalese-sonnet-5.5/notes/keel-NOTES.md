## Fork register (keel)

Every place where the text can be read two or more ways with different outcomes, or where the keel took a reading no ruling covers.
Each entry gives the provision, the function, the answer taken, the other reading, and what licenses the answer taken.
The ids are labels: **C** a defined term of s 4(1); **D** s 4 outside its list of terms; **G** a reading in ss 5 to 7A; **S** the sites the brief asks to be recorded one by one (section "Split sites (keel)"); **P** the counting of periods.
There is no F-D1: the id was never used.

### Periods (sg-periods.l4): P1 to P9, assumed, not ruled

The Act counts days in one place only, s 4(1) "business day" (implemented in `sg-periods.l4`), and has no rule for "within N days after", "not less than N days before", a week or a month.
The Interpretation Act 1965 is not among the sources, and no number is read from memory, so the convention below is the keel's assumption.
Every other function that counts a period calls these; no part writes its own date arithmetic.

- **P1** (`sg-periods.l4`, header, line 14). Taken: the day of the event is not counted: day 1 of "N days after X" is the day after X, so the last day is X plus N. Other reading: the day of the event counts as day 1, so the last day is one day earlier.
- **P2** (`sg-periods.l4`, header, line 15). Taken: "within N days after X" is a window that opens the day after X: an act on X itself is not "within" the window (period: the date is within a period after), while an act on any day up to the last day is "not later than" the last day (period: the date is not later than the end of a period after). Other reading: an act on the day of the event is "within" the period; and "not later than" excludes an early act.
- **P3** (`sg-periods.l4`, header, line 18). Taken: a period of weeks is 7 days a week. Other reading: none the text suggests (recorded because the Act is silent).
- **P4** (`sg-periods.l4`, header, line 19). Taken: a period of months or years ends on the corresponding date of the last month; where that month has no such date it ends on the month's last day (31 August plus 6 months is 28 February). daydate's `add months` clamps this way. Other reading: a month that has no such date rolls into the next month (`Date 31 2 2023` does that: it evaluates to 3 March 2023, checked 2026-11-18 on 1.6.110).
- **P5** (`sg-periods.l4`, header, line 21). Taken: "not less than N days before X": the last day on which the act may be done is X minus N (X not counted, the day of the act counted). Other reading: clear days (P7).
- **P6** (`sg-periods.l4`, header, line 22). Taken: "within N months before X" runs from the corresponding date N months earlier up to the day before X. Other reading: the window includes the day of the event itself.
- **P7** (`sg-periods.l4`, header, line 23). Taken: "N clear days" excludes both the day of the event and the day of the act (offered as the alternative to P1 and P5). Other reading: it is the default for every "N days", in place of P1 and P5.
- **P8** (`sg-periods.l4`, header, line 24). Taken: business days are counted from the day after the event, skipping weekends and the supplied public holidays. Other reading: the day of the event counts when it is itself a business day.
- **P9** (`sg-periods.l4`, header, line 25). Taken: a count that is not a whole non-negative number is not checked here: the caller supplies N from the Act's own words. Other reading: a validating function that refuses a negative or fractional count.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (keel)

Almost every function of the keel answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN`.
**`JUST TRUE`** is yes: the section's own test is met on the facts given.
**`JUST FALSE`** is the text saying no to this case in its own words, and in this Act it comes in these kinds: (1) a "means" definition the body or thing falls outside (for example, "public company" means a company other than a private company); (2) a "does not include" or a flat negative ("No person other than an accounting entity may ..."; "the Depository is not to be regarded as a holding company ... by reason only of the shares it holds ... as a bare trustee"); (3) an "unless the contrary intention appears" where one does appear; (4) a failed "if", "where" or "when" in a deeming provision (reading (a) above); (5) a date or period the text fixes in its own words, outside it (s 4(13)); (6) the provision's own question: a duty not met (s 7A(3)), or an element of the offence unmet (s 7A(6)).
**`NOTHING`** is "not covered": the keel does not say yes and does not say no.
It comes in these kinds: the term is introduced by "includes" and the list is open (s 4(1) "debenture", "accounts", "contributory"; s 4(7); s 4(8)); the text is silent (s 7(1B) with no restraint; s 7(10) for another ground); the answer points outside the sources (the regulations under s 4(1) "debenture" and s 7(9)(c) and (d); the other Acts a term hands its meaning to are supplied as facts); a grant or a deeming limited by its own wording and the case falls outside (s 7A(4)(b), s 7A(5), s 4(5A)); the lists of s 4(8) whose paragraphs carry a condition of their own; and a gap (an accounting limited liability partnership appointed in its own name, s 10(5) and (6)).
**A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.**
In particular `JUST FALSE` from `s 7A(6) the director is guilty of the offence this subsection creates` means this subsection makes the person guilty of nothing on these facts; it does not say the person is innocent of any other offence.
The tests assert a "not covered" branch as `EQUALS NOTHING` (for example `sg-solvency-tests.l4:60`), so a change that turns it into a no turns a test red.

## Findings: what the text turns out to say

1. **s 4(1) mixes closed and open definitions.** 92 terms in 91 entries carry a definition and 1 entry is shown deleted (lines 1141-1770). Each term is one function; a term the text writes with "means" answers `JUST FALSE` outside it and a term the text writes with "includes" answers `NOTHING` outside it. The two kinds are not always where a reader expects: "contributory" and "accounts" include, "public company" and "registered" mean.
2. **A foreign cooperative society is both in and out of "corporation".** "Corporation" "includes any foreign company but does not include ... (c) any cooperative society" (lines 1327-1351), and "foreign company" limb (a) takes in any "society ... incorporated outside Singapore" (line 1465). The keel lets the exclusion win (F-C1). The earlier pass found the same.
3. **An unincorporated foreign association is a "corporation".** "Foreign company" limb (b) (lines 1471-1477) takes in an unincorporated body that can sue or hold property in the name of its secretary, and "corporation" includes any foreign company, so such a body is a corporation for the whole Act although it is not a body corporate; "exempt private company" limb (a) (lines 1433-1436) asks that no beneficial interest be held by "any corporation", so a private company it owns is not exempt (`sg-definitions-tests.l4:198` builds the body). The six exclusions from "corporation" go the other way: none of a corporation sole, a cooperative society or a limited liability partnership is a "corporation", so a private company owned wholly by one of them can still be exempt.
4. **s 4(8) has two paragraphs lettered (d).** The first (line 1885) is about an act or thing done while a receiver is in possession, the corporation is under judicial management, a compromise is administered or it is being wound up; the second (line 1904) begins "(d) and, without limiting the foregoing, any conduct of such a receiver ...". The keel keeps both under (d) and names them apart by their words (two constructors of `s 4(8) matter`). Three paragraphs carry a condition of their own ((b) a trustee that is not a trustee corporation; (d) the four occasions listed; (i) the corporation has issued interests); outside it they answer `NOTHING`.
5. **s 4(5) lacks a closing parenthesis in this rendering** (lines 1822-1829): "(other than a corporation that is a prescribed entity mentioned in section 239(4) of the Securities and Futures Act 2001 is deemed to be an invitation". F-D3 records how the keel closed it.
6. **s 5(3)(b)(ii) is circular** (lines 2037-2043): it counts shares held by "a subsidiary of that other corporation" while s 5(1) is the question whether a corporation is a subsidiary. The Act gives no order of determination. The keel takes the holder's kind as a supplied fact (F-G3), which goes round the problem and does not solve it. A part that needs the fixed point has to supply it.
7. **"Wholly owned" does not travel up a chain.** s 5B(c) admits a subsidiary whose members are only the other corporation or its nominee (lines 2097-2100), and nothing in s 5B carries that up the way s 5(1)(b) carries "subsidiary" up, so in P, S2, S1, C a corporation whose only member is S1 is not wholly owned by P (`sg-groups-tests.l4:211`). s 5(1)(b) does make C a subsidiary of P. The earlier pass found the same.
8. **s 6(c) relates two subsidiaries of one corporation, not two parents of one subsidiary.** The two parents of a joint venture are each the venture's holding company and are not related to each other (`sg-groups-tests.l4:233`), and a venture whose board both control has two ultimate holding companies (`sg-groups-tests.l4:174`).

Two bodies corporate, each holding exactly 20% of the next, do not give the top person an interest by (4A) alone (F-G7). This is the largest difference from the earlier pass.
11. **s 7A(2) gives the company with the least machinery the fewest ways to comply** (lines 2327-2345): a company exempt from audit under s 205B or 205C must have a declaration signed by every director; any other company may have that or an auditor's report. 12. **The offence of s 7A(6) turns on the grounds when the statement was made** (lines 2381-2385: "without having reasonable grounds for the opinions expressed in it"). 13. **s 10(5) and (6) treat later joiners differently, and omit accounting limited liability partnerships** (lines 2778-2800).
