## Seat

**The person whose interest in a periodical payment ends part-way through a
period, or that person's executor, administrator or assign**: the landlord whose
life interest ends, the employee or pensioner who dies mid-month, the shareholder
who dies mid-year, and above all their estate. The Act is about what that person
(or their estate) gets for the broken period, when, and how. The payer (tenant,
employer, company) is the other side; s 5(2) is the only provision addressed to
them, and it is encoded as what it means for the person: whether they may go to
the payer or the land, or must sue whoever receives the whole rent.

Decisions are phrased as that person asks: `Is my payment apportionable`, `What
part of the payment had accrued to me`, `What can I recover after just
allowances`, `When can I recover my share`, `Can I recover my share on`, `Do I have
the remedies of section 5`, `How do I recover my share`, `May I go to the payer
directly for my share`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, instruments brought in:** followed. The Act has no subsidiary
  instruments and sets no numbers or dates; there was nothing to bring in.
- **The actors the text names:** followed. Persons, executors, administrators,
  assigns, persons liable to pay rent, and the person entitled to the entire rent.
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally:** not needed. The Act
  hands no judgment to an official.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts, borrowed terms asked as
  facts:** followed. "Annuities", "dividends" (with its return-of-capital
  exclusion) and "rents" from s 2 shape the `Kind of payment`. "Policy of
  assurance", "re-entry", "just allowances" and "rent reserved out of or charged on
  land" are borrowed terms, asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit:** followed. One payment, one period, one
  day on which the interest ended.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss:** followed, in comments beside
  each type and field.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out:** followed. Ss 2 to 7 are
  encoded; s 1 and the long title decide nothing. There is no other machinery.

## Why this scope

The Act is seven short sections and all of the operative ones reach the
individual, so all of them are encoded. What the encoding does not do is say what
the general law gives where the Act does not apportion (a return of capital, a
policy sum, an express stipulation, a lump sum): those cases answer `NOTHING`, not
zero, because the Act is silent on them. A day of ending that is not inside the
period (on the day it began, or on or after the due date) is also not a part-way
case and answers `NOTHING`.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Salaries and pensions are "annuities", so they are shared out by the day.**
   s 2 brings salaries and pensions within "annuities", and s 3 makes all annuities
   accrue from day to day. An employee who dies on 11 March has earned 10 of 31
   days of a $6,200 monthly salary, $2,000. Asserted.

2. **Writing does not matter.** s 3 applies "whether reserved or made payable under
   an instrument in writing or otherwise"; an oral tenancy apportions exactly as a
   written lease does. Asserted.

3. **A dividend accrues over the period it is declared for, not when it is
   declared or paid.** s 2 deems the divisible revenue to accrue "by equal daily
   increment during and within the period for or in respect of which" it is
   declared. A dividend for 2025 paid in March 2026, with the shareholder dying on
   2 July 2025, gives the estate 182 of 365 days. A payment "in the nature of a
   return or reimbursement of capital" is not a "dividend" at all and is not
   apportioned, whatever it is called. Asserted.

4. **Two express exclusions.** Annual sums under a policy of assurance "of any
   description" are never apportionable (s 6), and the Act does not extend to any
   case where "it is expressly stipulated that no apportionment shall take place"
   (s 7). Asserted.

5. **The share is not payable early, under either limb of s 4.** For a continuing
   payment it is payable when the entire portion falls due, "and not before"
   (s 4(a)); for one ended by re-entry, death or otherwise, when the next entire
   portion "would have been payable" had it not ended, "and not before" (s 4(b)).
   Both land on the same date: the estate of a landlord who dies on 1 February
   cannot recover the share until the quarter day of 1 April. A dividend share is
   not payable at the end of the year but on the date the dividend is paid.
   Asserted.

6. **The estate takes the same remedies as for the whole payment, less a share of
   the allowances.** s 5(1) gives persons, their executors, administrators and
   assigns "the same remedies" as for the entire portion, "allowing proportionate
   parts of all just allowances". With $900 of allowances on a $9,000 quarter, 31
   days gives $2,790, not $3,100. That the allowances are shared by days is an
   inference from "proportionate". Asserted.

7. **For rent on land, the estate cannot go to the tenant.** s 5(2): the tenant and
   the land "shall not be resorted to" for the apportioned part. The whole rent is
   received by whoever would have been entitled to it, and the estate recovers its
   part from that person "by suit". For a salary, pension or dividend s 5(2) does
   not apply and the payer can be pursued directly. Asserted.

8. **The Act does not say how to count days.** The encoding counts whole days from
   the start of the period to the day the interest ended, not counting the first
   day and counting the last, so that the whole period adds up to the entire
   portion. That is a reading, not the text. The calendar is used, so a leap year
   has 366 days. Asserted on the encoding's reading only.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The day-counting reading (finding 8) and the treatment of rent payable in
  advance have not been checked against any decided case; no case law was
  searched.
- "Just allowances", "re-entry" and "policy of assurance" are asked as facts; what
  counts as each is for the general law and is not encoded.
- What the estate gets where the Act does not apportion (ss 6 and 7, a return of
  capital, a lump sum) is not encoded.
- How s 5(2) applies to rent that has stopped (s 4(b)) rather than continued is
  encoded the same way for both: the text says "an entire or continuing rent", and
  the encoding reads "entire" as reaching the stopped case (asserted for a lease
  ended by re-entry). The moved encoding
  reached the same reading after an independent test pass; this one has had no such
  pass.
