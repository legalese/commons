## Why this Act, and why scoped

**REQ-0186** in `subjects/sg/requirements.jsonl`: Tier 3 of the remaining Singapore
Acts, ordered by everyday-life relevance. The requirement asks what the Act decides
for a person or business it applies to; no scenario has asked a sharper question yet.
The Act is almost entirely about the Polytechnic's own governance, so this row takes
the points where a student, a donor, a business contracting with the Polytechnic or a
Board member meets it: execution under seal (s 4(2)), late-payment charges and their
validation (ss 5(2)(c), 5A), diplomas (s 12, Schedule s 11(g)), ending a student's
studies (Schedule ss 6(3)(c), 11(j)), when regulations take effect (Schedule ss 6(4),
12(2)), the Board's size, terms, quorum and voting (Schedule ss 5, 8, 9), the Senate's
quorum (Schedule s 10(3)), donations (Schedule ss 16, 17), the Students' Union and the
Societies Act (s 15) and the financial year (Schedule s 14). Not encoded: objects and
powers beyond the fee power, the Schools, committees, appointment of officers and
staff, the Minister's power to amend the Constitution, grants-in-aid, shares to the
Minister for Finance, estimates and the Students' Union fee (Schedule s 18(3)).

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Late-payment charges collected before 2008 cannot be challenged in court

s 5A deems every amount collected before the commencement of the Statutes
(Miscellaneous Amendments) Act 2008 "as, or purportedly as" a late-payment charge to
have been "always ... validly collected", and "no legal proceedings shall lie". The
express power to charge for late payment (s 5(2)(c)) arrived with the same Act. The
deposit annotates both "wef 01/04/2024", which sits oddly with a 2008 Act; the
commencement date is not given in the deposit, so it is taken as a fact. Asserted.

### 2. A tied Board vote can fail for want of a casting vote

Schedule s 9(3) gives the casting vote to "the Chairperson or, in his or her absence,
the Deputy Chairperson". When both are absent, s 9(5) lets the members elect someone
to preside, but the text gives that person no casting vote, so a tie is lost. The
quorum is 6 (s 9(2)), and decisions are by a majority of those "present and voting",
so abstentions do not count. Asserted.

### 3. Two signatures are needed under seal, and not just any two

s 4(2): sealed instruments must be signed by "the Principal or a Deputy Principal and
by another member of the Board". The Chairperson cannot sign first; a Deputy Principal
counter-signed by another Deputy Principal, or the Principal counter-signed by the
Registrar, does not satisfy it, because neither is a Board member as such under
Schedule s 5(1) (an inference: either could be appointed a member). Asserted.

### 4. Academic failure and misconduct take different routes

The Senate may itself "require any student on academic grounds to terminate his or
her studies" (Schedule s 11(j)); expulsion for discipline is left to regulations made
by the Board (Schedule s 6(3)(c)). Asserted.

### 5. Senate regulations wait a semester; Board regulations do not

Board regulations operate "on the day on which they are made" unless the Board
provides otherwise (Schedule s 6(4)). Senate regulations, on admission, courses,
examinations and awards, "come into operation not earlier than one semester after"
they are made unless the Board approves otherwise (Schedule s 12(2)). Asserted.

### 6. The Board has 12 to 18 members on 3-year terms

A Chairperson, a Deputy Chairperson, the Principal and 9 to 15 others, all but the
Principal appointed by the Minister for 3 years unless the Minister directs otherwise
(Schedule s 5). A person filling a vacancy serves only the predecessor's remainder
(s 8). The Senate's quorum is one-quarter of its members (s 10(3)); the financial year
runs 1 April to 31 March (s 14); donations for a specific purpose must be applied to it
and separately accounted for (ss 16, 17). Asserted.

### 7. The Societies Act binds the Students' Union unless the Minister exempts it

s 15 applies the Societies Act 1966 to the Students' Union "despite anything to the
contrary" in it, subject to exemption by order. Asserted.
