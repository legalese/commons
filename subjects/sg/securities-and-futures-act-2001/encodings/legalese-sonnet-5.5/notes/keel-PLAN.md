# PLAN: the keel of the Securities and Futures Act 2001 (Singapore), Part 1

## 1. Purpose and how to read this plan

The keel is Part 1 of the Act (lines 1 to 4224 of `SFA2001.txt`: ss 1, 2, 3, 4, 4A and 4B) in L4, written first so that the four part agents share one vocabulary and one core.
The four parts are sfaa (Parts 7 and 8), sfab (Part 12), sfac (Part 13 Division 1 Subdivisions 1 and 2) and sfad (Part 13 Division 1 Subdivisions 3 to 5).
You read this plan and not the keel's code.
This plan gives the exact names, types and order of inputs of every function you may call, and the exact names of the inputs every seat must share.
The keel's modules are read-only to you: do not edit them.
If you lack a type or constructor, define it in your own module under your own provision label and record it in your `NEEDS-CORE.md`; the lead hoists it at the merge.
`IMPORT` finds only modules in the importing file's own folder, so import the keel's modules by name from the folder they are given to you in, with backticks around a name that has a hyphen: ``IMPORT `sfa-core` ``.
A name defined in two imported modules cannot be used at all (met in this run, section 5); every keel name begins with its provision label (section 2), so a name of yours that begins with your own provision label can never collide with one of these.
The text is a rendering (the Singapore Statutes Online informal consolidation, 2020 Revised Edition, "version in force from 29/6/2026 to 20/10/2026"), not the official text.
The keel's `NOTES.md` records what the keel decided and why: read its fork register and its numbered findings before you rely on a keel function in a way this plan does not describe.

How to call a keel function.
Write its name and then its inputs in the order listed here, as the keel's own tests do: ``` `s 4(1) the person has authority over the disposal of the thing` TRUE FALSE ```.
Or name the inputs: ``` (`name` WITH `input one` IS TRUE, `input two` IS 5) ```.
An input is a `BOOLEAN` (`TRUE` or `FALSE`), a `NUMBER` (Singapore dollars unless it says otherwise), a `DATE` (written `Date day month year`), an enumeration (a constructor of the type), a `LIST OF` something, or a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (`JUST TRUE`, `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING`).
A function answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` unless this plan says otherwise: `JUST TRUE` is yes, `JUST FALSE` is "the text says no in its own words", `NOTHING` is "the text does not say".
Never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.
Bracket a call that is compared: ``` (`f` TRUE) EQUALS JUST TRUE ```.
Where a keel function takes a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` input, it is the answer of a limb, so an unanswered limb stays `NOTHING` and a combining function answers `NOTHING` rather than guess.

## 2. Naming rule

- A name defined in two modules that are imported together cannot be used at all, so no two parts may define the same name.
- Every top-level name (function, constant, type, constructor not in the core) a part defines **begins with the label of the provision it implements, in the form `s 240(1)(a) ...`** (the Act's own numbering: `s 272B(1) the offer is exempt`, `sch 2 para 2 ...`; parentheses inside a backtick name parse fine on the stable binary).
- That covers every type and every constructor, not only functions: write ``DECLARE `s 240 offer status` IS ONE OF `s 240 offer permitted`, `s 240 offer not covered` ``, never a bare `Not entitled`.
- Types and constructors that several parts share live only in the core (`sfa-core.l4`) and in the keel's modules.
- The function's name carries the section it implements and does not claim more than the section says (`s 4(2) it is immaterial that the authority is subject to restraint or restriction`, not `is the authority restrained`).
- A seat question is the one exception to the label rule: it begins `Under section N, ...` (section 7).
- The keel's own exceptions: the names in `sfa-core.l4`, the period functions of `sfa-periods.l4` (named `period: ...`, with the type `Period unit`), and two test constants in `sfa-periods-tests.l4` (`the supplied public holidays`, `no public holidays`).
- Every module of part `X` begins with `X-` (`sfaa-`, `sfab-`, `sfac-`, `sfad-`); every module of the keel begins with `sfa-`.
- An input is named for the FACT it states, in the person's words, never for a default, a presumption or the rule it feeds (`the person is an individual`, not `individual by default`).
- The same fact keeps the same name and the same `@desc` everywhere; section 7 fixes the shared ones.
- Results are `MAYBE BOOLEAN`; `JUST FALSE` only where the text says no in its own words, `NOTHING` otherwise, and every branch carries `-- FALSE-kind (...)` or `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`.

### 3.2 `sfa-periods.l4` (tests: `sfa-periods-tests.l4`): the one place the Act's periods are counted

Parts never write their own date arithmetic: call these with a start date and a number of days, weeks, months or years.

Part 1 has no counting rule of its own, so the counting conventions in this module are assumptions recorded in NOTES.md (the fork register entries P1 to P10); say which one you rely on.

## 4. Seams

A seam is a provision in one part whose answer another part's provision needs.
A part cannot import another part before the merge, so the dependent part takes the answer as an INPUT fact, named for the fact and the section it comes from.
A provision that no part encodes (a later wave) is an input in the same way (the keel's own functions take `the Authority has prescribed ...` and `regulations under section 341 prescribe ...` inputs).

How these were found.
The starting point was `inputs/XREFS.md` and `inputs/HEADINGS.md`.
For the parts, a script searched every part's ranges for "section N" where N is a section another part owns, and for "Part 7", "Part 8", "Part 12", "Part 13", "Division n of Part n" and "Subdivision (x)".
Each hit was then read in the text at the cited line (a few lines each).
That reading of a hit is the only confirmation: a part must confirm its seams against its whole range before it relies on this list, because a script that finds a section number does not read meaning.

## 5. Traps on the stable binary that the keel met

Sorted as the failures show up: LOUD ones print an error and teach; SILENT ones return a wrong answer with no error, and are the ones that need a test.
The stable builds are 1.6.110 and 1.6.109; the keel's `check.sh` runs every module on both and compares four counts per module.

SILENT (no error, wrong answer):

- `NOT x OR y` parses as `NOT (x OR y)`: write `(NOT x) OR y`, and test the input that tells the two apart.
  The keel brackets every `NOT` (checked by script: no unbracketed `NOT` in code).
- Arithmetic that crosses a line break: `x DIVIDED BY w` at the end of a line followed by `TIMES 100` on the next divides by `w TIMES 100`; bracket it (from the Commons guide; the keel wrote none that crosses a line).
- A `JUST FALSE` where the text is silent: not an error of the binary but the commonest drift; every branch of the keel's carries its `-- FALSE-kind` or `-- NOTHING-kind` comment, and a script checks that each quoted FALSE-kind and NOTHING-kind fragment occurs in the text.

LOUD (an error, so it teaches):

- `ELSE IF ... THEN ...` after an `ELSE` is a parse error in the layout the keel used; use a `BRANCH` with `IF ... THEN ...` lines and `OTHERWISE`.
- `x NOT EQUALS y` gives "unexpected NOT": write `(NOT (x EQUALS y))`.
- `f WITH a IS FALSE EQUALS JUST TRUE` is a type error: bracket the call, `(f WITH ...) EQUALS JUST TRUE`.
- `<long backticked field> OF c` inside `any (GIVEN c YIELD ...)` gave "There are multiple definitions for the identifier" and argument-type errors; the possessive `c's <field>` works (the cause was not investigated).
- A module that uses a keel type without `IMPORT`ing the module that declares it gives unresolved-name errors; `IMPORT `sfa-core`` is needed in every module that names `Party`, `Corporation` or the other core types.
- A name defined in two imported modules cannot be used: "multiple definitions for the identifier".
- `srcrefs.py check --strict` reports a block whose first locator does not span exactly the lowest and highest quoted line, and a `more()` locator that falls outside the quote lines it follows (met: a combining function whose `more()` came after the next provision's block).

From the brief and not independently met by the keel: no `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` or `BECAUSE` (except `LEST BREACH ... BECAUSE`); each `#ASSERT` on one line; write dates `Date day month year` and never define `YMD`; a list of strings is a vertical `LIST` block, not `•` bullets; no section `GIVEN`, sets, `TYPICALLY` or opaque `DECLARE`.
Worked on both builds and used by the keel: the `%` literal in arithmetic (`TIMES 20%`), `@export` and `@desc` on `MEANS` functions, `LIST OF` inputs, `any (GIVEN c YIELD ...) list`, and a `WITH` value of more than one word in brackets (`IS (JUST FALSE)`; whether the bracket is required was not tested).

## 7. The seat layer

The seat is the individual who asks.
A seat question is a decision function whose `@export` line carries the question, ending in `?`, and whose inputs are plain facts the person can state, each with a `@desc` (the question the form asks).
The function's own name is the question without the final `?`.
Every question is named `Under section N, ...` (the Act's own numbering, then a comma, then the question in the person's words: `Under section 4(1), do I have authority over the disposal of these securities, contracts or units?`).
The name asks the question the body answers, in the same direction: `JUST TRUE` is yes, `JUST FALSE` is no in the text's own words, `NOTHING` is "the text does not say".

## 8. The NOTES sections every part must write, and the split sites

The two split sites (the keel's brief; do not harmonise with another part).
(i) An unmet element of an offence or a contravention: does the function answer `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING`?
