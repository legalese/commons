# NOTES: Supreme Court of Judicature Act 1969 (Singapore), whole text with an individual's seat

Status: draft. No lawyer or other domain expert has read this encoding against the text, and it has not been checked against the official text.
Written on 2026-11-05 by Sonnet 5.5 agents (`claude-sonnet-5-5`) working in parallel, repaired through 2026-11-05 and 2026-11-06 (the machine's local date; the clock passed midnight during the repairs), and merged by the lead agent of the session that ran them.
Target dialect: `main`; every module runs on the main builds 1.6.109 and 1.6.108 with the same four counts.
The source is a plain-text rendering made by a converter script (`SCJA1969.txt`, 7,314 lines), not the official text.

## How this folder was made

One **keel** agent first wrote the shared vocabulary (`scja-core.l4` and its tests) and a plan (`notes/keel-PLAN.md`) that gives each part a range of the text and lists the seams between the parts.
3 **part** agents then encoded their ranges in parallel against the same keel, none reading another's work: `scja1` (lines 46 to 2309: the Arrangement of Sections, Part 1 (ss 1 and 2), Part 2 (ss 3 to 8A) and Part 3, Divisions 1 to 9 (ss 9 to 29E)), `scja2` (lines 2310 to 4320: Part 4 (the Appellate Division, ss 30 to 47), Part 5 (the Court of Appeal, ss 48 to 60M) and Part 6 (officers and offices, ss 61 to 72)), `scja3` (lines 4321 to 7314: Part 7 (ss 73 to 83), the First to Tenth Schedules and the Legislative History).
The keel's range is lines 1 to 45 (the cover page, the consolidation banner, the running title and the first lines of the Arrangement of Sections: the shared vocabulary `scja-core.l4` (nouns and the Act's own figures and dates, no rule) and its tests).
Each part was then read by an independent **Opus** test author (a different agent from the encoder) who decided the answers from the text alone, before opening the encoding or its notes, and wrote tests of its own (`notes/` does not hold them: they were run, not shipped).
The part's encoder then repaired the findings it judged to be the encoding's fault, under Meng's rulings of 2026-11-05 (the two bullets under "The conventions" below), and the lead ran the reviewer's tests again on the repaired part.
The lead merged the parts with a script; nothing in a part's modules was edited at the merge.
Every note a part wrote is kept, unchanged except that the lead's scratchpad path is replaced by a placeholder and the two 2026-11-05 ruling bullets are replaced by the guide's text, as `notes/<agent>-NOTES.md`, with its `PROGRESS.md`, its brief as `notes/<agent>-BRIEF.md` and its `NEEDS-CORE.md` where it wrote one.
**A part's NOTES.md is where its coverage table, fork register, marginal readings, jural relations and findings are.** They are not repeated or summed here.

## The conventions this row follows

The family's rule of 2026-10-29 for `JUST FALSE` and `NOTHING` (the guide, section 5): a function answers `JUST FALSE` only where the text itself says no to the case in its own words, and `NOTHING` where the text is silent, open, undefined or points outside it.
Four rulings of Meng's, recorded in the Commons guide and repeated here word for word:

- **E1** (bench `commons-negation-1030`; should a legal effect be one definition gathered from every section that gives it?): option B, as guidance for gathering a legal effect's routes by hand only, with no pilot run; option A (gathering by bare name) is withdrawn as a direction for the language, not deferred; option C (per-section answers) applies everywhere else; the Magna Carta pilot in the printed recommendation (chapters 2, 3, 4, 5, 37, 43, 46 and 53, with a blind second reader) is not to be run. Meng's lean, verbatim: "B but generally uncomfortable with this much magic because it takes the language from unopinionated to opinionated in ways that are hard to teach an l4 novice." Meng's note, verbatim: "Fundamentally I feel like we are trying to make the language do work that the human l4 drafter should do. Too much magic. Can’t we just decline to be this opinionated and say the programmer needs to do this thinking?" Marked 2026-11-03T23:19:02.812Z, ruled 2026-11-04. Bench: https://claude.ai/artifact/49q6k2H5xtuJqy5ttkwZ23
- **N2** (bench `commons-negation-1030`; does a flat negative count as the text's no?): option A as printed: flat negatives count as a no, and where the text names the bearer the prohibition (`SHANT`) is derived from that answer; the Hohfeld kinds stay in each row's "Jural relations" table; option C's wording (the kind typed in the person-facing answer) comes only after a lawyer confirms the typing, decided together with the S/E choice for how "not covered" is said. Meng's lean, verbatim: "Why are we even trying to do this work? Some of these rules are metatextual and require more homoiconicity than we currently have in L4." Meng's note: none written. Marked 2026-11-03T23:22:49.971Z, ruled 2026-11-04. Bench: https://claude.ai/artifact/49q6k2H5xtuJqy5ttkwZ23
- **Periods, "where" and scope limits** (a question raised on 2026-11-05 while checking an encoding of the Retirement and Re-employment Act 1993, not a bench): (a) a period the text fixes in its own words ("within one month", "begins from ... until the employee attains the age", "on the last day of employment"): outside the period the answer is `JUST FALSE`; (b) a "where ..." condition: when it fails the answer is `JUST FALSE`; (c) a scope limit with no "only" (for example "below" the minimum age): outside the scope the answer is `NOTHING`. Meng's words, verbatim apart from the trigger word that ended the message: "i agree with your lean: no for a and b; nothing for c." Typed 2026-11-05T04:30:00.322Z, ruled 2026-11-05.
- **Delegated definitions, defences and "until" bars** (questions raised on 2026-11-05 while checking encodings of the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, the Terrorism Act 2000 and the Singapore Sale of Goods Act 1979, not a bench): (a) a definition the text itself hands to a list ("Schedule 9 has effect for determining what is a business in the regulated sector"): when no head in the list holds, the answer is `JUST FALSE`; a bare list with no such hand-off still answers `NOTHING`; (b) "it is a defence ... to prove that X": when X is not proved, the answer is `JUST FALSE` for that provision's defence, worded "this section gives no defence on these facts" and never "no defence at all"; (c) "when", "upon" and "whenever" count as "where" under rule (b) of the periods ruling above; (d) "the property does not pass until X": encode the bar ("the property cannot yet pass": `JUST TRUE` before X, `JUST FALSE` after X) and never claim that the property has passed. Meng approved these four readings as put to him by the general-manager session, in the word NOTHINGBURGER; his note on (d), verbatim apart from the bracket and the trigger word that ended the message: "in the case of Q5 [the "until" bar] i hope we are using the deontic mechanisms to examine a trace." The note is a request about how (d) is modelled, not part of the ruling. Typed 2026-11-05T06:04:54.553Z, ruled 2026-11-05.

## Merge decisions (assumed, not ruled)

- **One seat module for the Act.** Each part wrote its own seat questions and cases. The lead concatenated them, in part order, into `scja-seat.l4` and `scja-cases-seat.l4`, with one banner comment per part and the IMPORT lines merged; the text under each banner is the part's, unchanged. Each part's questions are still those of its own range, so a question about a regulation is found under the banner of the part that owns that regulation.
- **Seams stay inputs.** Where a part needs an answer that belongs to another part or to the keel's definitions, it takes it as an input named for the answering provision (the seams are listed in `notes/keel-PLAN.md`; each part's "Seams" section says what it asks and answers). The merge does not wire one part's output into another's input, so the seat asks the person for the fact.
- **NEEDS-CORE requests are not hoisted.** Two parts recorded requests. `scja1` (`notes/scja1-NEEDS-CORE.md`) suggested one shared input for "every issue already decided" (asked by ss 17C(1)(a) and 22B(1)(b) of `scja1` and, per the plan, ss 38(1)(b) and 56(1)(b) of `scja2`), one for the public holidays (ss 10B(1) and 29B(2)(b)), a time of day for the extraction of a judgment (s 29B(2)(a)), and a member of `Kind of Judge` for a person who is not a Judge, without which the `JUST FALSE` branch of `s 2 Judge` cannot be reached from a `Judge` record; `scja2` (`notes/scja2-NEEDS-CORE.md`) asked for a count of Judges unable to continue in the core's `Hearing`, which its ss 36(3) and 54(3) take as plain number inputs. `scja3` made none. The keel's `scja-core.l4` is byte-identical in every part and is shipped as the keel wrote it; none of these is hoisted, and nothing in a part is blocked on it (assumed, not ruled).
- **The Singapore International Commercial Court is read two ways across the parts (a divergence the merge does not resolve).** The keel's note, taken by `scja1` (fork K1) and `scja2` (fork F1), is that the SICC is its own `Court` and not "the General Division", so wherever the text says "General Division" a decision of the SICC answers `NOTHING` (`scja1` ss 29, 29A, 29B(2); `scja2` ss 35(2)(a), 43(1), 53(2)(a), 60A(1)(a)); in `s 29C(2)` of `scja1` a `JUST TRUE` Sixth Schedule input decides the answer for the SICC too. `scja3` (fork S3-K1) takes the other reading for ss 73A(1), 73A(4), 73B(5)-(6), 73C(6)-(7), 73D(6)-(7), 74(1) and the scope of the Fourth, Fifth and Sixth Schedules: the SICC is counted with the General Division, through s 18A and s 18C. A person asking about an SICC decision can therefore get `NOTHING` from one part and a definite answer from another. A lawyer should rule on which; each part's notes give both readings.
- **Seam 11 has two names.** `scja1` asks one input, `s 34(1)(a) or s 52(1)(a) the place is one the Chief Justice appointed`, for ss 8A(6) and 11(1); `scja2` answers it as two functions, `s 34(1)(a) the place is one that the Chief Justice appointed` and `s 52(1)(a) the place is one that the Chief Justice appointed`. The names differ, so nothing could be wired even if the merge wired seams. Every other name in the plan's table of 20 seams (`notes/keel-PLAN.md`) was checked by script against both sides: the answering function exists in the answering part and the input exists in the asking part, with the same name and the same type (seams 19 and 20 are plain facts a party states, with no answering function). The plan's seams 14 and 16 are joined by two more, which `scja2` added in the fix round and `scja3` answers under the same names: `sch 7 the Seventh Schedule allows fewer than 3 Judges` and `sch 10 the Tenth Schedule allows a single Judge`.
- **Prohibitions with a gate: the convention is stated in `scja2` (fork F8) and applied less evenly in the other two parts.** `scja2` answers `JUST FALSE` from the "bar applies" function when a "where" or "if" gate is unmet, and `NOTHING` from the function named for the grant ("may Y be done"). `scja3` answers `JUST FALSE` for the unmet "Where" at ss 73B(2)(b), 73C(2)(b), 73D(2)(b) and 79(4) and added twelve "bar applies" functions; `scja1` added a bar function at s 28(2) and keeps `NOTHING` for some bare scope limits (its fork F15). The lead did not re-read every prohibition of `scja1` and `scja3` against `scja2`'s rule (assumed, not ruled), so a lawyer should expect sites where those two parts' grant and bar functions are not shaped like `scja2`'s.
- **The merge lost nothing:** the merged folder was gated as it stands and its total is 3593 = 23 (the keel's tests) + 1034 + 1108 + 1428 (each part's test and seat-case assertions, in part order), which are the parts' own totals as the lead measured them after the repairs.

## The seat

The seat is the one the keel's brief gave the parts: a person who wishes to start, defend or appeal a civil case in the Singapore High Court or Court of Appeal, asking on facts they can state which court hears it, who may appear, what jurisdiction and powers apply, and the time limits the Act itself sets.
Each part's questions are phrased as that person asks them ("Under section 21(1), do I need permission to appeal from the decision?") and each has at least one case; the lead's gate found every one of each part's questions called by a case, 250 questions in all.
The notes of `scja1` and `scja2` (`notes/<agent>-NOTES.md`) have no "Seat" heading; this section is their seat section.
