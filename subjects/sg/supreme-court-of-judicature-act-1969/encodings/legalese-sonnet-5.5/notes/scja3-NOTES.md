**Where this brief and PLAN.md differ (the brief governs, as the brief says).**
1. File names: PLAN.md says "every module of yours begins with `scja-`"; the brief says `scja3-`.
   Every module of this part begins `scja3-`; the keel's `scja-core.l4` is unchanged.
2. Helper names in the seat layer: the brief says every top-level name begins with the label of its provision.
   The seat's wiring (the list types `scja3 Which court`, `scja3 Which appeal court`, `scja3 My answer`, and the functions `scja3 my court`, `scja3 the court I appeal to`, `scja3 what I say, as a fact`, `scja3 my decision`) implements no one provision, so these begin `scja3 ` (the part id) instead of a provision label.
   Layer-1 names all begin with the provision label (`s 73A(2) ...`, `sch 5 para 3(h) ...`).
3. Schedule questions in the seat: the brief's template is "Under schedule N paragraph M,", so they read "Under schedule 1 paragraph 5," and "Under schedule 3," even though the Act says "First Schedule", "Third Schedule".
4. `s 80(3)` (the make-up of the Rules Committee): PLAN.md lists it among the definitions of this range, the brief's reason 2 (boards and their staffing) would also let it stay inert, and a definition "is encoded".
   It is encoded (a head-count table, `s 80(3) the Rules Committee has this number of members of the kind`), so the row is `encoded`, not `inert`.
   It has no seat question, because it describes an official body.

### Conventions taken for the six open questions ("decide case by case", the last ruling above)

Each site gets one line in the Judgment calls further down; these are the conventions, so a reviewer can overrule a convention at once and the sites with it.

- **Q1 (QA), an "unless", "except" or "without" once it is met.**
  Where the sentence is phrased as a power or permission and the exception names the case where the power does not exist (s 73(2): "may, ..., exclude ... except when that person is a party to or a witness"), the exception being met gives `JUST FALSE` for the power and `JUST TRUE` for its mirror (`s 73(2) the person is to be allowed to remain ...`).
  Where the sentence is phrased as a prohibition and the exception lifts it (s 75(1) "Except with the approval of the Chief Justice", s 80(5) "without the consent of the Chief Justice", s 80(2)(j) "unless it has been otherwise agreed", s 73B(2)(a) "without the permission of that court", s 74(1) "without the permission of the General Division"), the function that asks "may the person do it" (the grant) answers `NOTHING` once the exception is met: the bar lifts and the text grants nothing.
  Each such bar also has a twin that asks "does the bar apply", which answers `JUST FALSE` once the exception is met and `JUST TRUE` while it is not (fork S3-B6).
  The other reading in each case is the opposite answer.
- **Q2 (QB), a bare "must" not done, and Q4 (QD), a duty or consequence whose "if" or "where" is unmet** (one bullet, because the sites overlap).
  A bare "must" or "is to" that the text does not follow with a consequence answers `NOTHING` when it is not done: ss 73C(4)(b) and 73D(4)(b) (the order "must specify every court"), s 80(4) ("all questions are to be decided by a majority") and s 81 ("is to assemble at least once in every year").
  The names of these functions end "as the section requires", so that the name tells the reader the function asks whether a requirement is met and not a fact the person states (fork S3-F7).
  A duty whose trigger is absent (s 73(6) when there is no list, s 74(4) when no order is made, s 80(6) for Rules not made under the section) gives `NOTHING`: the duty is scoped by its own wording.
  A condition the text states with "Where" or "If" that is unmet gives `JUST FALSE` for a permission or protection that the "Where" confers (ss 73B(2)(b), 73C(2)(b), 73D(2)(b), s 79(4), s 82(3); ruling (b) of 2026-11-05) and for a restraint, `JUST FALSE` in the twin; the grant that asks "may the person do it" and the functions that state a restraint the "Where" imposes (ss 73B(5)-(6), 73C(6)-(7), 73D(6)-(7)) answer `NOTHING`, because `JUST FALSE` would read "you may not" to a person nobody has restrained (fork S3-B3).
  The other reading: `JUST FALSE` for a bare "must" not done and for an absent trigger; `NOTHING` for an unmet "Where" that confers a permission or protection; `JUST FALSE` for the restraint grants.
- **Q3, "listed in Schedule N".**
  Outside the Schedule's own scope (its heading names the court or the decision) the answer is `NOTHING`.
  Inside the scope, a Schedule that closes its list ("in any of the following cases", Third, Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, Seventh, Ninth, Tenth) gives `JUST FALSE` when no limb holds (a delegated definition: the text hands the question to the list); where the Schedule's own exception takes the case out (Fourth para 2; Fifth para 4(2) for seams 5 and 6) the bar answers `JUST FALSE` and the exception `JUST TRUE`.
  The First Schedule is not closed (s 18(2): "Without limiting subsection (1)"): a power the Schedule does not set out answers `NOTHING` for seam 1, and `JUST FALSE` for seam 2 only when the power is set out under another numbered paragraph.
  The other reading for a closed list is `NOTHING` (a caller who wants it must say so).
- **Q5, "(other than X)".**
  Inside a prohibition (s 75(1)(b), "other than his or her salary and allowances") the excluded thing gives `NOTHING`, because the bar does not reach it and the text grants nothing.
  Inside the condition for a consequence (s 78(4), "other than any fees that are for the time being allowed"; s 73B(3) and the like, "other than for the permission of the court") the excluded thing gives `JUST FALSE` for the consequence: the condition fails.
- **Q6, a missing event in the state ledger.**
  Not met in this range: it has no ledger.
- **Descriptive grants.**
  A grant that describes its case (the First Schedule's descriptive paragraphs, for example paragraph 16, "in any action for damages for personal injuries") gives `NOTHING` when the case falls outside the description, and `JUST FALSE` only where the text itself says no.
- **Courts (S3-K1 and S3-B1).**
  The Singapore International Commercial Court is counted with the General Division through s 18C (S3-K1).
  The Family Division of the High Court answers `NOTHING` for the grants to "a court" (nothing in this part says whether it is part of the General Division).
  A subordinate court (a District Court, a Magistrate's Court, a Family Court, a Youth Court, a Syariah Court, or another subordinate court, tribunal or judicial or quasi-judicial body) answers `JUST FALSE`: s 2 reads "“court” means the General Division, the Appellate Division, or the Court of Appeal", a plain "means" with no "and includes" (lines 381-382), and a District Court is not among the three (S3-B1).

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (scja3)

Two readings are marked "for a lawyer" in the brief, and both are taken here as the status quo.
(a) A failed "if" counts as the text's no, clause by clause, within the provision that holds the "if", and flat negatives count as the text saying no.
(b) A prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not say no for the case outside the limit, so that case answers `NOTHING`.
Counted by `grep`, the layer-1 modules carry 207 `-- FALSE-kind` comments and the first table has 207 rows; they carry 136 `-- NOTHING-kind (limited by its own wording)` comments and the second table has 136 rows (`work/gen_marginal.py` stops with an error if either pair differs).
So every `JUST FALSE` branch of the layer-1 modules is in the first table with its quoted words, and every `NOTHING` branch that rests on reading (b) is in the second.
The rows marked "not marginal" (a "means" definition, a stated "unless", "except", "until" or "only", or a delegated definition) need no lawyer.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (scja3)

- **`JUST TRUE`** means the Act's own words give the answer yes on the facts stated: for example s 73A(2) lets a court stay proceedings it is satisfied are conducted in a vexatious manner.
- **`JUST FALSE`** means the Act says no in its own words to the case stated.
  In this part that is: a Schedule that closes its list with "in any of the following cases" and no limb holds; a bar the text states ("no appeal lies", "is final", "cannot be brought"); a condition the text states for a power (s 73B(1): at least 2 applications that are totally without merit) and the case does not meet it; a period the text fixes (the 2-year caps).
  It never means "the law does not allow this"; it means "this section answers no".
- **`NOTHING`** means this part's text does not answer: the case is outside the section's own wording (the Family Division of the High Court under s 73A(2)), the list is not closed (a power the First Schedule does not set out), a term is not defined (the Family Division of the High Court as "a court"; a subordinate court is not such a case: s 2 names the three courts, so it answers `JUST FALSE`), or the fact needed is outside the sources (the court of s 29C, the amount prescribed by the Rules of Court, the sum in s 29A(1)(b)).
  A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.
  In the seat layer `NOTHING` is shown as "not covered".
- A seat question about a protection (ss 79(1), (2), (4), (5)) is worded as a question about the protection: `JUST TRUE` means the person is NOT liable to be sued.

## What the text turns out to say (numbered findings)

1. **The Eighth Schedule is repealed, but the Seventh Schedule's cross-reference still names it.**
   The Eighth Schedule reads "[Repealed by Act 25 of 2021 wef 01/04/2022]" (lines 6483-6485).
   The Seventh Schedule's heading (lines 6272-6275) still cites "paragraphs 1(a) and (b) and 2(a) of Eighth Schedule".
   The row for the Eighth Schedule is `repealed or blank in this text`; no rule is built from it.
2. **Five limbs of Fourth Schedule para 1 are deleted, and the letters are left blank.**
   Limbs (a) and (c) to (g) read "[Deleted by Act 40 of 2019]" (lines 5840 and 5842-5846): the rows are `repealed or blank in this text`; the surviving limbs keep their letters (b), (h), (i), (j), (k).
3. **Subsection (8) of s 73A does not name the court's own motion, but subsection (7) does** (lines 4445-4450 and 4458-4462): fork S3-F2.
4. **The three civil restraint orders are three separate sections with the same shape but different caps.**
   A limited order lasts "for the duration of the legal proceedings ... unless the court orders otherwise" (s 73B(4)(b), lines 4548-4550; the answer is `JUST FALSE` where the court has ordered otherwise, S3-B2); an extended or general order lasts "a period (not exceeding 2 years) that is specified in the order" (ss 73C(4)(a), 73D(4)(a)), may be extended and "must not" be extended for more than 2 years on any one occasion (ss 73C(5)(b), 73D(5)(b)).
   Each is encoded on its own (ruling E1, option C), with the wording kept alike so the lead can compare them.
5. **Permission to appeal after a restraint or vexatious-litigant order is routed through s 29C, but an appeal from the Judge on the list of touts is closed.**
   ss 73A(9), 73B(5)-(6), 73C(6)-(7), 73D(6)-(7) and 74(2A) say "only with the permission of the court to which the appeal is to be made under section 29C", while s 73(5) says "The decision of the Judge sitting in chambers in the General Division is final" and s 74(3) says "No appeal lies from an order refusing permission under subsection (1)".
   The first group answers through the s 29C input (seam 9) and returns `NOTHING` without it; the second group answers `JUST FALSE` ("is final", "no appeal lies").
6. **Section 80(2)(j) caps the rate of interest the Rules of Court may fix at 8% per annum, "unless it has been otherwise agreed between the parties"** (lines 5133-5139).
   For a person this means that where the rate is above 8% and the parties have agreed it, the function that asks whether the Rules may regulate that rate says nothing (`NOTHING`) and the twin that asks whether the cap bars the rate says `JUST FALSE`; where they have not agreed, `JUST FALSE` and `JUST TRUE` (S3-B6).
7. **The First Schedule is headed "Additional powers of General Division"** (line 5466) and is made under s 18(2) and s 18I(1)(a) (line 5464): it leaves the Singapore International Commercial Court to s 18I(1), another part's section, so the First Schedule answers `NOTHING` for the SICC and for the Family Division (S3-K1).
8. **Paragraph 22 of the First Schedule counts the late filing charge in days "after the expiry of that period"** and leaves the amount to the Rules of Court: an input, never a guess (S3-F4).
