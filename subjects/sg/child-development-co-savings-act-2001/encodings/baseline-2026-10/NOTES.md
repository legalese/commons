## Seat

**The working parent:** an employee who is the mother or the father of the child. Part 3 of the Act is a set of things a
parent who works for someone else can claim:
- maternity leave;
- paternity leave;
- childcare and extended childcare leave;
- unpaid infant care leave;
- if the parent has no leave, a Government-paid benefit.

Every decision is phrased as that parent would ask it (`Am I entitled to paternity leave`, `What is the most my employer must
pay me for 16 weeks of maternity leave`). The facts are ones the parent can state: dates, months served, citizenship, marriage,
pay. Where the Act addresses the employer or the Government, the encoding gives what that means for the parent: what the
employer must pay, the employer's penalty if it refuses, and the dismissal notice that is unlawful.

The 2026-10-18 defaults, one by one:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers:** followed. The regulations under s 20 are not deposited. So the
  prescribed period for "total income" (s 9(5A)) and the notice period for paternity leave (s 12H(1A)) are not computed: the
  first is taken as a daily figure, and the second is only said to be required. The reductions by regulation (s 9A(5A)(b)) are
  not applied; the benefit is given as the most before any reduction. The First Schedule is part of the Act and is used.
- **The actors the text names:** followed. They are the employee, the employer and the Government (as payer).
- **A judgment handed to someone else, answered conditionally:** followed, with one input of that kind. "Prevented by any
  sufficient cause" (s 12H(1A)) is a fact the father states. For the employer offences, "without reasonable cause" is folded
  into the description of what the employer did.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts, borrowed terms asked as facts:** followed for "April 2025 Scheme child",
  the 2017 and 2021 gates, the weekly index for a regular work pattern, and "relevant period" (as a premise). There are three
  departures:
  - Which "specified event" a confinement is (s 2(2)) is asked as a fact, not worked out.
  - Citizenship is asked as a fact; it is borrowed from the Constitution.
  - Months of service are asked as a fact.
- **One case is the text's natural unit:** one confinement, one birth for a father, one relevant period for childcare.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss:** followed in comments ("weekly index", "April 2025 Scheme child",
  "qualifying child", "relevant period", "specified event").
- **Operative sections in, machinery out:** followed. Part 2 (the Scheme accounts), ss 13–22 and the regulation powers are out.
  Further operative parts are also out for size; see the next section.

## Why this scope

The Act runs to about 9,000 lines of print. The provisions an employed parent meets most often are the ones in scope:
- the maternity leave a mother takes for a child who is a citizen at birth;
- the Government-paid benefit for a mother with no leave;
- paternity leave;
- childcare and extended childcare leave;
- unpaid infant care leave;
- what the employer risks by refusing these.

Left out:
- the self-employed parent;
- a child who becomes a citizen after birth (s 9(1A), (1B)). These turn on Employment Act 1968 s 76 periods outside the
  deposit, and the encoding answers `section 9(1A) or (1B) applies, not covered by this encoding`;
- adoption;
- shared parental leave (ss 12DA–12G and the Second Schedule);
- the paternity benefit (s 12HA) and extra paternity leave (s 12JA);
- employers' reimbursement;
- the per-child lifetime caps on childcare leave;
- the order in which leave is taken;
- the irregular-pattern weekly index.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The "or 24 days" limbs never bite.** The Act repeatedly says "4 times the employee's weekly index or 24 days, whichever is
   the lower", and also "8 times … or 48 days" and "twice … or 12 days". But First Schedule note 3A says "the maximum number of
   work days is 6". So N times the index can never exceed 6N, and the day limb is never the lower one. A 6-day worker's flexible
   maternity leave is exactly 48 days, and a 5.5-day worker's is 44. Asserted.

2. **A third child can earn a high earner less maternity pay than a second.** For a first or second confinement, s 9A(4)(a) caps
   only the pay "after the first 8 weeks" ($10,000 a block, $20,000 in all). The first 8 weeks are paid at the full gross rate.
   For a third or later confinement, s 9A(4)(b) caps every block, at $10,000 a block and $40,000 in all. On $4,000 a week:
   - a second confinement is owed at most $52,000 by the employer;
   - a third is owed at most $40,000.

   Asserted.

3. **Dismissal does not forfeit continuous maternity leave; resignation forfeits all of it.** s 9(2A) takes all untaken leave
   from a mother who resigns. s 9(3), for dismissal, completion of contract or any other reason, takes only the later flexible
   periods. It does so "without affecting section 84 or 84A of the Employment Act 1968". Asserted.

4. **Having leave rules out the Government-paid benefit, unless the job ended by completion or redundancy.** A mother entitled
   to leave under s 9(1) cannot claim under s 9(5A) (s 9(6)(a)). s 9(6B) lifts the bar where she would have been entitled but
   for the completion of her contract or redundancy. The benefit is 56 days of total income (112 for a third or later
   confinement), capped at $10,000 for every 28 days. That is $20,000 or $40,000. Asserted.

5. **Paternity leave doubled for an April 2025 Scheme child, but not for a stillborn child.** s 12H(1)(a) gives 4 weeks to the
   father of an April 2025 Scheme child and 2 weeks otherwise. An April 2025 Scheme child must be "born alive" (s 2(1)), and
   s 12H(12) excludes (1)(a)(i) for a stillborn child. So the father of a stillborn child born in June 2025 gets 2 weeks.
   The pay cap is $2,500 a week, with a total of $10,000 or $5,000. Asserted.

6. **A father need not be married at conception.** s 12I(1)(c)(iii) is satisfied if the parents marry within 12 months after
   the birth. A father who never marries the mother has no paternity leave. Asserted.

7. **Childcare leave grows with service within the period, and the combined cap is 6 days.** Under s 12B(1), the days run
   from 2 (less than 5 months in the relevant period) to 6 (11 months or more), after 3 months' service. Extended childcare
   leave for a child aged 7 to 12 is 2 days. s 12B(2)(a)(iii) caps the two together at 6 a period. After 3 days of childcare
   leave, the employer's pay is capped at $500 a day (s 12B(10)). Extended childcare leave is capped at $500 from the first
   day (s 12B(10A)). Asserted.

8. **The "3 days" in s 12B(10) are read as counted within the relevant period.** The text does not say so; the entitlement is
   given per relevant period. This is a reading, labelled as one in the module. Asserted on that reading.

9. **Unpaid infant care leave is 12 days a period, with 24 for each child.** s 12D(1) and (2)(a) say so. The leave counts
   towards service under the Employment Act (s 12D(9)). Asserted.

10. **Untaken leave lapses and is never paid for.** Under ss 12B(8), (11) and 12D(5), (6), childcare and infant care leave
    lapse at the end of the relevant period or on termination "for any other reason", with no payment in lieu. Asserted.

11. **Every employer offence the parent meets carries the same maximum.** Refusing childcare, extended childcare, infant care or
    paternity leave without reasonable cause, failing to pay, and giving notice of dismissal during paternity leave (s 12H(6),
    (6A)) each carry $5,000 or 6 months. For a repeat offender or a subsequent offence the maximum is $10,000 or 12 months.
    Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- **Regulations under s 20** should be deposited: the prescribed periods for total income, the paternity notice period, and the
  benefit reductions.
- **Section 9(1A), (1B)** need the Employment Act 1968 s 76 periods brought in.
- **Specified events** should be counted by s 2(2), not asked.
- **Childcare limits not yet encoded:** the per-child lifetime caps (42 and 12 days) and the start date under s 12B(1C) and
  12D(1B) when the parents marry later.
- **Shared parental leave** (ss 12DA–12DD and the Second Schedule) is the April 2025 change most parents will now meet, and is
  the obvious next scope.
- **No independent test pass and no domain review** has been done. No case law or MOM guidance was read.
