## Seat

The seat is **a person who entered into a contract after a false statement was made to them**, asking, on facts they can state, whether they may claim damages or rescind, and whether the other party's clause excluding that liability works.
Why: the Act is written from that person's side. Ss 1, 2 and 3 each begin from "a person has entered into a contract after a misrepresentation has been made to him" (s 3 from the contract's term and the party it hurts), and the only other parties it speaks of are the person who made the statement (the other party to the contract), "the court or arbitrator" (s 2(2)) and "those claiming that the term satisfies that requirement" (s 3).
There is **no seat for the court or the arbitrator** (officials): s 2(2) is encoded in layer 1 for what the court or arbitrator may do, and in layer 2 only as the question "May the court or arbitrator keep the contract going and award me damages instead of letting me rescind?".
A second seat for the other party (the person who made the statement) was not written: the brief asks for one thin seat layer.
The seat is two files, `ma-seat.l4` (13 `@export` questions, grouped under `§§` headings in the text's order) and `ma-cases-seat.l4` (63 worked cases).
The Act's own words are asked as facts: the Act defines no term, so "misrepresentation", "rescind", "fraudulently", "loss", "equitable" and the reasonableness test in section 11(1) of the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 are plain inputs the person states or answers "I am not sure" to.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING

Every decision rule answers a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (the amount rule a `MAYBE NUMBER`).
In this Act:

* **`JUST TRUE`** is the text's yes: for a power, "may" (the court or arbitrator MAY declare the contract subsisting; it does not say it will); for a liability, "shall be so liable"; for a bar, "Nothing in this Act applies"; for an invalidity, "shall be of no effect".
* **`JUST FALSE`** is the text's own no, and only that: a "where" condition that fails (s 1, s 2(1), s 2(2) including "otherwise than fraudulently", s 2(3)), a date the text fixes in its own words and the case lies outside it (s 4 "made before 12 November 1993": a thing made on or after that day), an "if" that fails (s 1, s 2(1), s 2(2), s 3), an exception the text itself introduces and that is met ("unless he proves" in s 2(1), "except in so far as it satisfies the requirement of reasonableness" in s 3, "except in so far as it was applicable by virtue of section 5 of the Civil Law Act 1909" in s 4), and, for s 2(1)'s proviso, **"this section gives no defence on these facts"** when it is not proved (never "no defence at all").
* **`NOTHING`** is "the Act does not answer on these facts", never "no": the text limits a power in its own wording and the case is outside the limit (a body that is not "the court or arbitrator" in s 2(2); a matter the opinion did not have regard to; a party who is not one of "those claiming" in s 3; no award to take into account under s 2(3)); the Interpretation Act's "include" for "person" and "party" does not name an individual; or **a fact was not given** ("I am not sure" is `NOTHING`).
* A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`. A result of `NOTHING` on a question about a power says nothing about whether the power exists.
* Every branch that answers no carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"`; every branch that answers "not covered" carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, where why is one of: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording. A `NOTHING` that comes only from an input that was not given is tagged `silent`, with the clause "a fact the answer turns on was not given (an input is NOTHING)", because the six-word list has no heading for a fact not given.
* The names of the rules carry the section (`s 2(2) the court or arbitrator may declare ...`), not "is the instrument valid" and not "may I ...": a name must not claim more than the section says.
* Where a rule can answer `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING` from three-valued inputs, a known no on a limb the text joins with "and" wins over an unknown limb; a yes needs every "and" limb known true (where the text joins limbs with "or", one known true limb is enough).

2. **Who made the statement.** s 1 speaks of a misrepresentation "made to him" (by anyone); s 2(1) says "by another party thereto"; s 2(2) says nothing of who made it. Each is encoded as written.
3. **How the sections combine.** s 1 is "subject to the provisions of this Act" and does not say which provisions cut it down; the text does not combine ss 1, 2(2), 3 and 4 into one answer. Each section is its own question and none is gathered into a composite (F1).
4. **Who bears the s 2(1) burden, and how.** "Unless he proves" puts the showing on the person making the misrepresentation, in the Act's words; the text gives no standard of proof and no time for the showing, and the encoding takes the proviso as met only when both matters are proved (F3).
5. **How many matters the court weighs.** s 2(2) names three ("the nature of the misrepresentation", "the loss that would be caused by it if the contract were upheld", "the loss that rescission would cause to the other party"). The text does not say whether the court may weigh others, or how much weight each has. A matter not regarded gives `NOTHING` (F6); the opinion itself is the court's and is an input.
6. **Liability net of the s 2(2) award.** s 2(3) says an award "shall be taken into account in assessing his liability under subsection (1)"; it gives no formula and does not say "deducted". No net figure is computed (F8).
7. **Facts the Act points outside the sources for.** Reasonableness "as stated in section 11(1) of the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977" (s 3); the saving "by virtue of section 5 of the Civil Law Act 1909 in force before that date" (s 4); the law of deceit (s 2(1) "had the misrepresentation been made fraudulently"); the general-law right to rescind (ss 1, 2(2)). All four are inputs.
8. **Facts about time.** "Up to the time the contract was made" (s 2(1)) and "before the contract was made" (s 3(a)) are yes/no facts the caller states. They are not date comparisons: the Act gives no date for either. The only date the Act's text compares is 12 November 1993 in s 4.
9. **The showing in s 3.** The Act does not say to what standard, or at what stage, those claiming that a term satisfies the requirement must show it, or how a term divides into parts for "in so far as" (F9, F10).
10. **Money and time limits.** The Act fixes no measure of damages, no limitation period, no time limit for rescission, and says nothing of contributory negligence. These are outside the text and are not encoded.
11. **The short title.** s 5 creates no rule and has no function (row R43).
12. **The word "person" for an individual.** The Interpretation Act says "person" and "party" include a company, an association and a body of persons; it names no individual, so the answer for an individual is `NOTHING`.

## Checks on names, inputs and seat wording

* **The s 2(1) function's name.** The function takes no fraud input, and its earlier name ended "notwithstanding that it was not made fraudulently". It is now `s 2(1) the person making the misrepresentation is liable to damages`, which says what it does: the answer is the same whether or not the misrepresentation was fraudulent (F2). The seat question keeps the phrase "even though the false statement was not made fraudulently" because that is how the person would ask it.
* **"Up to the time the contract was made" and "before the contract was made".** Checked: both are yes/no inputs, not date comparisons (item 8 above). The only function that compares a date is `s 4 the date on which it was made is before 12 November 1993`.
