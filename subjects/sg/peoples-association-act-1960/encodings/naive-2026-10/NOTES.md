## Why this Act, and why scoped

This is requirement **REQ-0198** in `subjects/sg/requirements.jsonl`: tier 3 of the
remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by everyday-life relevance. The requirement asks
what the Act decides for a person or business it applies to; no scenario has asked a
sharper question yet. The Act is short (16 sections and a Schedule) and mostly sets up
the Association. This row takes the two provisions that reach outsiders (s 15, the
symbols offence; s 10, the bar on suits), the one that reaches course participants
(s 9(i), certificates), and the constitutional rules that can be computed: membership
and terms (ss 4, 5, Schedule), meetings (s 6), sealing (s 11), deposits (s 13(1)) and
the financial year (s 14). The objects (s 8), the rest of s 9, Ministerial directions
(s 5A), disclosure of interest (s 7), share issues (s 13A), investment (s 13(4)) and
the Chief Executive (s 16) are not encoded; ss 5A, 7 and 16 work through the Public
Sector (Governance) Act 2018, which was not read.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. Copying the PA's symbol is a crime, with no permission exception on the face of it

s 15(3): anyone who "uses a symbol identical with, or which so resembles, any of the
Association's symbols as to be likely to deceive or cause confusion" commits an
offence, fine up to $10,000 and no imprisonment. The reading taken is that an
identical symbol is caught without proof of likely confusion. s 15 has no exception
for use with the Association's consent; whether consent answers the charge is left to
the general law (an inference, not encoded). Asserted.

### 2. A symbol is the Association's once adopted and displayed — the Gazette is a duty, not a condition

s 15(1) gives the exclusive right to symbols the Association "may devise or adopt ...
and thereafter display or exhibit". s 15(2) says it "must publish" them in the Gazette,
but s 15(3) does not make publication a condition of the offence. Asserted (adopted
and displayed); the Gazette point is a reading of the text's silence.

### 3. Those who act for the PA cannot be sued for good-faith acts; the PA itself can

s 10 bars any suit against members, officers, employees, Board and committee members
and anyone "acting under the direction of" the Association, Board or a committee, for
anything done in good faith in the execution or "purported" execution of the Act.
The Association itself is not covered: s 2(3) says it "may sue and be sued". A
volunteer acting on his or her own initiative is outside s 10. Asserted.

### 4. The Association has 94 members by the count the text gives, and needs 32 for a quorum

s 4(1): the Prime Minister (Chairperson), a Minister as Deputy, 8 appointees, and one
member for each Schedule organisation. The Schedule numbers 85, one deleted, so 84
organisations and 94 members. The quorum is "one-third of the total number of members"
(s 6(8)): read as a floor, 31 is short and 32 suffices. The Board is 14 (the first 10
plus 4 elected by the organisations' members, s 5(1)) with a fixed quorum of 5
(s 6(9)). s 4(5) lets the Board vary these numbers by Gazette notification. Asserted.

### 5. Terms run three years, but a replacement only finishes the predecessor's term

s 4(3): three years from appointment; s 4(7): a person filling a vacancy holds office
"for so long as the member in whose place he or she is appointed would have held
office". The Chairperson may revoke any appointment "without assigning any reason"
(s 4(2)), and an elected Board member's seat falls with his or her Association
appointment (s 5(4)). Asserted.

### 6. Notice by post or messenger is deemed received; email is not mentioned

s 6(3) requires at least 7 days' notice of a general meeting, to all members, stating
date, time, place and business. s 6(4) deems receipt only for ordinary post or
messenger to the recorded address. Whether the 7 days are clear days the text does not
say; this row counts from sending. The presiding member votes and, on a tie, has "a
second or casting vote" (s 6(10)). Asserted.
