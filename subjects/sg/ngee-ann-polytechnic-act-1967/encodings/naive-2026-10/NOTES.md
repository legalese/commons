## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The power to charge for late payment waited sixteen years to commence

s 24(2)(e) lets rules provide for fees "including charges for the late payment of any
such fee or charge", and s 24A deems every amount collected "as, or purportedly as" a
late-payment charge before the Statutes (Miscellaneous Amendments) Act 2008 commenced
to have been "always ... validly collected", with "no legal proceedings" lying in
respect of it. Both carry "[Act 4 of 2008 wef 01/04/2024]", and the Legislative
History gives that Act readings in January 2008 but commencement on 1 April 2024. On
that date the validation reaches every late-payment charge collected up to 31 March
2024. Asserted (dates taken from the annotation and the Legislative History).

### 2. A will that names the "Ngee Ann Technical College" is read as naming the Polytechnic — but s 25 does not reach the "Ngee Ann College"

s 25: on and after 16 April 1982 a reference to the Ngee Ann Technical College "in any
contract, agreement, bequest, will, trust or other instrument" has effect as a
reference to the Ngee Ann Polytechnic. The Legislative History shows the Act began as
the "Ngee Ann College Act, 1967" and was renamed the Ngee Ann Technical College Act by
Act 17 of 1970. Section 25 says nothing about references to the original name; whether
the 1970 Act dealt with them is not in the deposit. Asserted.

### 3. The Principal is protected from removal and cannot resign the Council seat — but can still lose it

s 8(2) (revocation by the Minister) and s 8(3) (resignation) both say "except the
Principal". s 8(1), which vacates the office of "a member of the Council" on bankruptcy,
a composition with creditors or absence without leave from 3 consecutive meetings, has
no such exception. A vacancy in the Principal's seat is not filled under s 9 (which
excludes it); the Council appoints the Principal under s 19(1). Asserted.

### 4. A tied Council vote can fail for want of a casting vote

s 12(3) gives the casting vote to "the Chairperson or, in his or her absence, the
Deputy Chairperson". When both are absent, s 12(5) has the members elect someone to
preside, but the text gives that person no casting vote, so a tie is lost. The quorum
is 6 (s 12(2)) on a Council of 12 to 18 (s 7(1)); decisions are by a majority of those
"present and voting". Asserted.

### 5. The Ngee Ann Kongsi keeps three seats, and keeps them through vacancies

s 7(1)(d) reserves 3 seats for representatives nominated by the Kongsi's Committee of
Management, beside 6 to 12 persons the Minister determines. s 9(2): a person filling a
Kongsi seat "must also be a representative of the Ngee Ann Kongsi nominated by the
Committee of Management", for the predecessor's remainder (s 9(1)). And s 20(2)
disapplies section 20 of the Kongsi's own incorporating Ordinance to its gifts of land
to the Polytechnic; what that section requires is not in the deposit. Asserted.

### 6. Diplomas are sealed by two officers; everything else needs the Principal

s 14(1): the seal may not be affixed to any instrument "other than a diploma or academic
certificate" except in the presence of "the Chairperson or a member of the Council and
the Principal", both signing. s 14(2): a diploma may be sealed before "any 2 officers
... duly authorised by the Council". That the second signer under (1) must be someone
other than the Principal is an inference from "and the Principal". Asserted.

### 7. Smaller points

Minutes must be circulated within 14 days (s 13(4)); the Registrar does not vote unless
a Council member acting as secretary (s 13(5), read as an inference); the Principal may
speak but not vote on a committee of which he or she is not a member (s 19(5)); the
Societies Act 1966 binds the Students' Union unless the Minister exempts it by Gazette
order (s 23); rules need the Minister's approval and Gazette publication (s 24(1),
(3)). Asserted.

### 8. A drafting slip in s 19A (not asserted)

s 19A lets the Council appoint officers and employees "on the terms and conditions
determined by the Board". The Act has no Board; its governing body is the Council
(ss 5, 6). Presumably "Council" is meant (an inference).
