## Why this Act, and why scoped

Requirement **REQ-0092**: Tier 2 of the remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by
everyday-life relevance. It asks what the Act decides for a person or business it
applies to; no scenario has asked a sharper question yet.

The Act is short (25 sections). This row takes what lands on a private owner,
occupier, builder or utility supplier in a common services tunnel (CST) area: entry
on notice (s 9), obstruction (s 10), upkeep (s 11), engineering works in a
protection zone (s 12), laying utility infrastructure on public land (s 13), the
penalties (ss 10 to 15 and 25(3)), officers of a corporation (s 16(2)), composition
(s 19), appeal (s 20) and when posted service takes effect (s 22(5)). Not encoded:
appointments (ss 4, 5), declaring and changing CST areas (ss 6 to 8), s 17,
injunctions (s 18), other service rules, s 23, exemptions (s 24) and regulations. No
regulations were retrieved, so which offences are compoundable and where any
protection zone lies are unknown here.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. An owner can pay for removing an obstruction they did not cause

s 10(3)(b) lets an officer remove a structure, object or obstruction from an
ancillary structure (an entrance, exit, manhole, vent or shaft) at once "if access
... is required immediately". The cost is then recoverable under s 10(4)(b) "from
the owner or occupier of the land", not from whoever placed it. Only when removal
follows a disobeyed order (s 10(3)(a)) does the cost fall on the person served.
Asserted.

### 2. Owners of land with an ancillary structure must keep it in repair at their own cost

s 11(1): an owner or occupier "must take reasonable measures to keep and maintain
the ancillary structure ... in good and serviceable repair"; ordered works are at
their cost (s 11(3)), and failure "without reasonable excuse" is an offence. The
Government and public authorities are excluded (s 11(7)). Asserted.

### 3. Every main offence carries $20,000 plus $1,000 a day, and only obstructing an officer carries prison

ss 10(5), 11(4), 12(8) and 13(5) all set a fine not exceeding $20,000 and, for a
continuing offence, $1,000 "for every day or part of a day" after conviction. s 14
(obstructing an officer) is $20,000 or 6 months or both. s 15 information-notice
offences are $10,000. Regulations may create offences up to $5,000 or 6 months
(s 25(3)(b)). Asserted.

### 4. Composition is capped at $5,000, so the half-fine limb rarely matters

s 19(1): the sum is the lower of half the maximum fine and $5,000. For every
$20,000 offence the cap is $5,000; for the $10,000 offences both limbs give $5,000;
only a $5,000 regulations offence drops to $2,500. Reading "the maximum fine" as the
base fine without the daily add-on is an inference. Asserted.

### 5. Entry needs 7 days' notice only for occupied land, and not for an inspection

s 9(2): notice is needed before entering occupied land unless the officer thinks
there is an emergency or the entry is "only for the purpose of an inspection or a
survey". A notice affixed to the land counts (s 9(3)(b)), and no fee is payable to
the owner or occupier of private land for the entry (s 9(6)). Re-entry needs no
notice (s 9(4)). Asserted (the notice rule).

### 6. The utility-laying approval covers public land only, and an appeal does not suspend an order

s 13(1) forbids a supplier to lay infrastructure "on, under or over any public land"
in a CST area without approval; the officer may make a tunnel-use agreement a
condition precedent (s 13(4)). An appeal lies to the Minister within 14 days of
service (s 20(1)), but only against the listed refusals, orders and conditions,
and the order "must be complied with unless otherwise ordered by the Minister"
(s 20(2)). Posted service takes effect 2 days after posting (s 22(5)(c)), so the
window effectively runs 16 days from posting. Email service needs prior written
consent (s 22(6); not encoded). Asserted (except s 22(6)).
