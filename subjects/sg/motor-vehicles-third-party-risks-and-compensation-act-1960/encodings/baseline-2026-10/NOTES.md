# Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act 1960 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

It replaces the first-pass encoding `naive-2026-10`, whose notes and L4 were read
for scope, pitfalls and hand-checked expected values. Where those values were still
right they were reused (the $5,000 / $5,001 boundary, the two-part employment
exclusion, cancellation without a certificate dealing). No encoding of this Act moved

2026, carrying the Platform Workers Act amendment (Act 30 of 2024, in force

## Seat

**The driver or owner of a motor vehicle**: the person who uses it on a road, or
causes or permits someone else to use it. The Act is written to protect the person
that driver injures, but almost every duty and every consequence lands on the driver
or owner: the offence of using without cover, the disqualification, the presumption
that the registered owner is the user, the duty to say whether they were insured, to
surrender a cancelled certificate, to produce it and to report an injury accident,
and, through ss 7(2), 8(3) and 9(9), the insurer's right to come back to them for
what it paid the victim. The provisions addressed to the insurer (ss 7, 8, 9, 14)
are encoded as what they mean for the driver: whether the insurer must pay a
judgment against them, and what it can recover from them afterwards.

Decisions are phrased as that person asks: `Does section 3 require cover for my
use`, `Am I guilty of an offence by using the vehicle without cover`, `Am I presumed
to be using the vehicle on a road in Singapore`, `Must my policy cover my liability
for this death or injury`, `Is my agreement with my passenger of no effect`, `What
must my insurer do about the judgment against me`, `How much can my insurer recover
from me after paying under section 9`, `Do my rights against my insurer pass to the
person I injured`, `What must I tell the person claiming against me`, `Have I failed
to surrender my certificate in time`, `Am I guilty of failing to report the
accident`, `What is the most I can be fined in dollars`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, instruments brought in:** followed, with one input. The
  "relevant amount" is $5,000 unless another amount is prescribed under s 20. No
  such order is deposited, so the relevant amount is an input to the s 9 rule; the
  cases supply 5000 and, once, 20000 to show the figure is not in the rule. The
  Schedule is in the deposit (its territory, as laid out, reads West Malaysia; the
  page is garbled, so the encoding refers to "a territory in the Schedule").
- **The actors the text names:** followed. The user, the owner, the insurer, the
  giver of a security, the person injured (the third party), the Public Trustee, the
  police officer, the Deputy Commissioner of Police, the court.
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally:** followed. The
  court's "special reasons" (s 3(3)), the court's declaration that the insurer may
  avoid the policy (s 9(7)), and whether an accident was reported "as soon as
  possible" (s 16(4)) are inputs; the rules say what follows from each.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts, borrowed terms asked as
  facts:** followed. "Specified person" (s 2, para (b)) is worked out from the
  injured person's age and four facts; "liability covered by the terms of the
  policy" (s 9(14)) is used as defined. "Platform worker", "public service vehicle",
  "lacks capacity", "place of detention" and the Work Injury Compensation Act case
  are borrowed and asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit:** followed. One use of one vehicle, one
  injury, one judgment, one demand, one police request, one accident.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss:** followed, in comments quoting
  the text beside each type and rule.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out:** followed. The Public Trustee's
  powers, the power to amend sums, the Minister's power to exempt, rules and deposits
  are out. Departed from in one respect: ss 5A and 6 (settlement payments routed to
  the Public Trustee) are operative but left out, because they address the insurer
  and the claimant and decide nothing the driver must do or can be made to pay.

## Why this scope

The Act is 30-odd sections. The encoding takes every provision that tells the
driver or owner what they must do, what they risk, or whether their insurance will
stand between them and the person they injure, and keeps to 59 assertions by testing
each rule at its boundary rather than at every combination. It covers at least what
the first pass covered (ss 2 to 16, less s 6's machinery) and adds s 3(6) and (7)
exemptions, s 3A, s 4(9), (11) and (12), s 9(8), s 9(9), s 10(2) and (6), s 11's
reasonable excuse, s 16(3), (4), (6), (7) and the penalties. What is out is listed in
the module header.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **At or below the relevant amount, the insurer "may" pay; only above it "must".**
   s 9(1)(a) is permissive, s 9(1)(b) mandatory, and "does not exceed" puts the
   relevant amount itself on the permissive side. At $5,000 the insurer may pay; at
   $5,001 it must. The marginal note says "Duty". From the driver's seat, a small
   judgment may stay theirs to pay. Asserted.

2. **A policy without a certificate is no policy for the purposes of the Act.**
   s 4(9): a policy "is of no effect for the purposes of this Act unless" a
   certificate of insurance is issued. A driver with a policy but no certificate is
   using the vehicle without complying cover under s 3. The first pass did not read
   s 4(9) into compliance. Asserted.

3. **A security must be big enough for the vehicle.** s 4(11)(b): not less than
   $225,000 for a public service vehicle, $45,000 otherwise. A public service vehicle
   on a $45,000 security is uninsured for s 3. Asserted.

4. **The employed driver's defence needs all three limbs, and the driver must prove
   them.** s 3(4): not my vehicle and not on hire or loan; in the course of
   employment; and no knowledge or reason to believe there was no cover. An employee
   who suspected the truth has no defence. Asserted.

5. **Conviction carries a 12-month disqualification unless the court finds special
   reasons**, starting on conviction or, if imprisoned, on release (s 3(3), as
   amended by Act 2 of 2025). Asserted for the period.

6. **The registered owner is presumed to be the user; a lessor is not.** s 3A(1)
   presumes, until the contrary is proved, that the recorded owner uses the vehicle;
   for an unregistered vehicle, whoever has a legal right to possession, including a
   lessee or hirer, "but not the lessor while the vehicle is being leased". Asserted.

7. **The employment and platform-work exclusions are each two-part.** s 4(4)(a): the
   injured person's status AND that the injury arose "out of and in the course of"
   the employment or the platform service. An employee hurt off duty is inside the
   required cover. Asserted.

8. **A passenger cannot sign away the driver's liability, before the journey or by a
   mere understanding.** s 5(1) catches "any antecedent agreement or understanding
   ... (whether intended to be legally binding or not)", and s 5(2) excludes willing
   acceptance of the risk. An agreement made after the liability arose is not
   antecedent. Where s 3 did not require cover, s 5 does not apply and the encoding
   answers that the general law decides, rather than guessing. Asserted.

9. **Conditions and restrictions fail against the victim, and come back to the
   driver.** s 7(1) strikes only conditions about what is done "after the happening
   of the event"; s 8(1) strikes restrictions on a closed list of eight grounds. But
   s 7(2) keeps the insurer's repayment clause and s 8(3) lets the insurer recover
   what it paid only because of s 8. Asserted for ss 7(1) and 8(1).

10. **Four bars stop the insurer having to pay, and the driver's own paperwork is one
    of them.** s 9(6)(a) no notice within 7 days of the proceedings starting (day 7 in
    time, day 8 not); (b) enforcement stayed on appeal; (c) the policy cancelled before
    the accident AND the certificate surrendered or declared lost (before the
    accident, or within 14 days of the cancellation), or the insurer suing over its
    non-surrender within 14 days; s 9(7) a court declaration that the insurer may
    avoid. Cancellation alone does not bar. Read with s 15, which obliges the holder
    to surrender within 7 days: a driver who does their s 15 duty and then drives
    leaves the victim with no claim on the insurer and themselves exposed. Asserted.

11. **A declaration helps the insurer against earlier proceedings only if it warned
    the claimant.** s 9(8): within 7 days of starting its action, specifying the
    non-disclosure or misrepresentation. Without the warning the insurer must pay.
    Asserted.

12. **Whatever s 9 makes the insurer pay beyond what the policy owed, the insurer can
    recover from the driver.** s 9(9). The cover protects the victim, not the driver.
    Asserted.

13. **Bankruptcy passes the driver's rights against the insurer to the victim, and
    does not end the liability.** s 10(1) and (2) (including a deceased debtor's
    estate administered in bankruptcy), subject to s 10(6) (not where the Work Injury
    Compensation Act s 28 case applies). s 13(1) keeps the liability alive where a
    certificate was issued; where none was, s 13 is silent and the encoding answers
    `NOTHING`. Asserted for s 10(1), s 10(6)(b) and s 13(1); s 10(2) is encoded but
    not asserted.

14. **s 12 times an agreement differently from a payment.** On this reading of the
    text, an agreement with the insurer is struck down only if made after BOTH the
    liability and the bankruptcy; a waiver, assignment, disposition or payment to the
    insured after the bankruptcy alone. The first pass required both for all of them.
    A reading of the sentence's structure, not tested against case law. Asserted on
    that reading.

15. **The duty to say whether one was insured survives the insurer's avoidance.**
    s 11(1): "or would have been so insured if the insurer had not avoided or
    cancelled the policy", with the certificate particulars. s 11(6) makes failure an
    offence only "without reasonable excuse"; the first pass omitted the excuse.
    Asserted.

16. **The production duty has grace periods the first pass missed.** s 16(3): a
    driver who gives the details but cannot produce the certificate is not convicted
    for that alone if they produce it in person within 5 days at a police station they
    named. After an injury accident where the certificate was not produced at the
    scene, s 16(4) requires a report at a police station "as soon as possible and in
    any case within 24 hours", with the same 5-day grace for production (s 16(6)).
    Asserted.

17. **The penalties.** s 3: a fine up to $1,000 or 3 months or both. ss 11, 15 and 16:
    $500 on a first conviction; $1,000 or 6 months on a second or subsequent one, with
    no imprisonment the first time. Asserted for the s 3 fine and the s 15
    imprisonment limits.

18. **The deposit's arrangement of sections is out of step with the body.** The
    arrangement table lists "Short title" against 2, "Interpretation" against 3, and
    so on; the body numbers them 1, 2, 3, 3A. Following the body, s 17 is deposits,
    s 18 solicitation, s 18A the Public Trustee's power to obtain information, s 20
    the power to amend sums, s 21 securities, s 22 the general penalty, s 23 the power
    to exempt, s 24 rules. The first pass's not-encoded list used some of the table's
    numbers (it calls s 21 the power to amend sums). Not asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- No check was made for an order under s 20 changing the relevant amount, or the sums
  in ss 3(6)(b), 4(6) and 4(11)(b). The rules take the relevant amount as an input;
  the s 4(11)(b) security minimums and the s 3(6)(b) $125,000 are as printed.
- The s 12 reading (finding 14), the reading of "before the end of a period of 14
  days" as including day 14 for s 9(6)(c)(ii), and the treatment of s 16(4)'s "as soon
  as possible" as an input have not been checked against any decided case. No case law
  was searched and no rules under s 24 were retrieved.
- ss 5A and 6 (settlement payments to the Public Trustee), s 4(5)-(7) (hospital
  expenses), the information duties of trustees, liquidators and insurers in
  s 11(2)-(5), and the Minister's power to exempt under s 23 are not encoded.
- Whether the driver is liable to the injured person at all is the general law of
  negligence and is not decided here.
