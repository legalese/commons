## Why this Act, and why scoped

Requirement **REQ-0135**: Tier 2 of the remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by
everyday-life relevance. The requirement asks what the Act decides for a person it
applies to; no scenario has asked a sharper question yet. The people it touches are
schoolchildren who join the NCC, their parents, the teachers appointed as officers,
and volunteer instructors. This row takes eligibility (s 8), the Corps' separation
from the SAF (s 4(3)), leaving an appointment (s 9(4), (5)), discharge and dismissal
(s 11), summary discipline and its punishments (ss 12, 13), boards of inquiry (s 14),
compensation (s 15), the tort bar (s 16) and the weapons-Acts exemption (s 17).
The organisation of the Corps, the Council and Commandant (ss 3 to 7), secondment
(s 10) and the regulation-making power (s 18) are not encoded. The regulations (age,
enrolment period, the summary procedure, compensation amounts) were not retrieved.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. An injured cadet generally cannot sue — and the Minister need not pay

s 16(2) and (3) bar tort claims against a fellow member (including honorary officers
and instructors), the Government, or a Government officer for a member's death or
personal injury, if the injured member was on duty or on Corps land, premises, ship,
aircraft or vehicle, and the Minister certifies the harm "attributable to service for
the purposes of entitlement to compensation under section 15". But s 15 only makes it
"lawful for the Minister to compensate", in accordance with regulations; it is a power,
not a right. The Minister's certificate on whether someone was on duty or a place was in
Corps use is "conclusive" (s 16(4)). Asserted.

### 2. The member loses the shield when the act was unconnected with duty; the Government does not

The exception at the end of s 16(2) — the court "is satisfied that the act or omission
was not connected with the execution of his or her duties" — says only that the
subsection "does not exempt a member". On the text the Government stays shielded in
the same case. Asserted (the government-side result is a reading of the words, not a
decided point).

### 3. A commanding officer cannot dismiss a cadet; a school can veto a dismissal

s 13 limits summary punishment, after a recorded finding of guilt, to reprimand,
reduction in rank (only where the cadet holds one) and suspension for not more than
3 months. Dismissal is the Commandant's, on the commanding officer's recommendation
(s 11(2)), and for a school student needs "the concurrence of the principal" (s 11(3)).
Asserted.

### 4. Only wilful defiance of a lawful command, or a breach of a knowable rule, triggers discipline

s 12(a) requires disobedience of a "lawful" command "in such manner as to show wilful
defiance of authority"; (b) requires that the order or regulation be "known to the
cadet or which the cadet might reasonably be expected to know". Asserted.

### 5. Officers face a board of inquiry with an MOE nominee

s 14(2): a president not below captain, and at least two others, one nominated by the
Permanent Secretary to the Ministry of Education. The board can only recommend
reprimand, reduction in rank or dismissal (s 14(3)) — not suspension. The Act does not
say who imposes the recommended punishment. Separately the Minister may revoke any
officer's appointment "with or without assigning any reason" (s 9(5)), and an officer
may resign only "with the approval of the Minister" (s 9(4)). Asserted.

### 6. Citizens and PRs are eligible; others by the Minister's special approval

s 8. The Act sets no age and no student requirement; both are left to regulations
(s 18(2)(d)). Cadets are not SAF members (s 4(3)), and the Arms Offences Act and the
Corrosive and Explosive Substances and Offensive Weapons Act do not apply to members
"while on parade or in the course of their duties" (s 17). Asserted.

### 7. Drafting slips

s 16(3)(c) and (d) speak of "that other person" though the limb's subject is "a member
of the Corps"; the encoding reads it as the injured member. The printed arrangement of sections is
shifted by one against the body (it lists "Short title" against 2). Not asserted.
