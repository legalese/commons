# Income Tax Act 1947 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the Act is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No REFUSE: a case the encoding does not cover returns
`NOTHING`, with a comment saying why. No pipeline, no coverage table, no independent
test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

This encoding replaces the one moved to `solutions/Litigate.4/sg/income-tax-act-1947/`
on 2026-10-19. That encoding's NOTES were read for scope and pitfalls; none of its L4
was copied.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, version in force from
11/9/2026, as deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/ITA1947.txt`.

**Checks:** two modules (`ita-tax.l4`, `ita-duties.l4`) and two case files, checked
with the stable `l4` from VS Code extension 1.6.98:
`ita-cases-tax.l4` 35 assertions satisfied, `ita-cases-duties.l4` 24 satisfied,
59 in all, 0 errors, 0 warnings. The case files are generated by the scratchpad
script `gen_income-tax-act-1947_baseline.py`. Every expected value in it was worked
out by hand from the text, and the Table 3 sums are shown in its comments.

## Seat

**The individual taxpayer**: a person, resident or not, whose income for one year
of assessment is charged. The Act is mostly addressed to the Comptroller ("the
Comptroller may", "the Comptroller must"). The person who meets it is the one who
earns, files, objects, pays, or is penalised. So every decision is phrased as that
person's question: `Am I resident in Singapore`, `What is my tax at the resident
rates`, `By when must I tell the Comptroller I am chargeable`, `Can I be prevented
from leaving Singapore`, `What penalty must I pay on conviction for`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults, one by one:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The Act
  prints its own rate tables and relief figures, so no instrument was needed for
  those. One subsidiary instrument matters and is not in the deposit: rules under
  s 76(9) that may substitute a longer objection period. That period is a `MAYBE`
  input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the Comptroller, the Board of Review's
  Chairperson, the taxpayer. The appeal is also written as a regulative rule with
  one party, `the aggrieved taxpayer`, because the duty falls on that person.
- **A judgment handed to someone else is answered conditionally.** Followed: four
  are inputs and none is guessed. They are the Comptroller's satisfaction that the
  foreign-income exemption is beneficial (s 13(7A)(b)), the Comptroller's
  satisfaction about a late objection (s 76(4)), the Chairperson's permission to
  appeal late (s 79(11)), and the Comptroller's opinion that I may leave without
  paying (s 86(1)).
- **The Act's definitions worked out from facts, borrowed terms asked as facts.**
  Followed: "resident in Singapore" is worked out from residence and day counts.
  Some ideas the Act uses without defining are asked of the person as facts:
  a "reasonable" temporary absence, "above 55 years of age", and being "totally
  blind or ... permanently and severely" restricted in capacity for work.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one individual, one year of
  assessment (or one assessment, for the process rules).
- **The Act's own terms with a plain gloss.** Followed: every section has its
  statutory words quoted in a comment, followed by a `Gloss:`.
- **The operative sections in, the machinery out.** Followed. Out: the
  Comptroller's appointment and powers, the rule-making power, the constitution of
  the Board, and the exchange-of-information Parts.

## Why this scope

The Act runs to 1,200 pages. For an individual, the provisions met most often are:
whether I am resident, what rate I pay, the commonest reliefs and rebate, when I
must tell the Comptroller about my income, how I dispute an assessment, when I must
pay, and what happens if I pay late or get my return wrong. Those are encoded.

Not encoded: what income is charged and its source rules (ss 10-12, apart from
s 13(7A)); all other exemptions; deductions and capital allowances; how statutory
and assessable income are worked out (assessable income is an input); every s 39
relief except earned income and spouse; Tables 1 and 2 (years of assessment before
2024); companies, trustees and executors; ss 40A, 40C, 40D; withholding; tax
credits; the concessionary rates; additional assessments; appeals beyond the Board;
and the PIC bonus limbs of the offences. The moved encoding found that much of the
Act turns on Ministerial approvals and that the PIC regime is tied to years before
2024. Both are good reasons to leave those parts out of a baseline.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Residence has a day-count limb, and a director's working days do not count
   towards it.** Under s 2, an individual is resident if they resided in Singapore in
   the preceding year apart from reasonable absences, or were present for 183 days
   or more, or exercised an employment here for 183 days or more "other than as a
   director of a company". A visitor here 182 days is not resident; one here 183
   days is. Asserted.

2. **A resident's remitted foreign income is exempt only if the Comptroller is
   satisfied the exemption benefits them.** s 13(7A) exempts a non-resident's foreign
   income received in Singapore outright. For a resident, the exemption depends on
   the Comptroller being "satisfied that the tax exemption would be beneficial to the
   individual", and it never covers income received through a Singapore partnership.
   Asserted.

3. **The earned income relief does not say what happens when disability and age
   overlap.** s 39(1)(b) gives a disabled individual $4,000 "without affecting any
   deduction allowable under paragraph (c) or (d)" (the age-based $6,000 and $8,000).
   That can be read as both deductions together or as the larger of the two, and the
   text does not settle it. The encoding returns `NOTHING` for a disabled individual
   above 55. Asserted.

4. **The spouse relief's income limit doubled from YA 2025.** s 39(2)(a): $2,000 for
   a spouse whose income was not more than $4,000 (YA 2010-2024) or $8,000 (YA 2025
   on). A spouse earning $6,000 qualifies in YA 2025 but not in YA 2024. Asserted.

5. **All s 39 reliefs together are capped at $80,000** (s 39A, YA 2018 on).
   Asserted.

6. **Table 3 tops out at 24% above $1,000,000, the same rate as the flat
   non-resident rate.** The YA 2024 table: nil on the first $20,000, then bands
   rising to 22% and 23%, and 24% above $1,000,000. Tax on $40,000 is $550, on
   $320,000 it is $44,550, and on $1,000,000 it is $199,150. s 43(1)(b) taxes a
   non-resident individual at 24% on every dollar from YA 2024 (22% before).
   Asserted.

7. **A highly paid non-resident employee gets nothing from the 15% rate, because
   of the resident floor.** s 40B taxes a non-resident employee's employment share
   of chargeable income at 15%, with the rest at the non-resident rate. But s 40B(3)
   says the tax "must not be less than that which would be payable by a resident of
   Singapore in the same circumstances". On $100,000 the 15% rate gives $15,000,
   well above the resident $5,650. On $1,000,000 the 15% would be $150,000, but the
   resident figure is $199,150, and that is what is payable. A director is outside
   s 40B and pays 24% flat. Applying the floor to the whole tax, and taking the
   resident's chargeable income as an input, is an inference. Asserted.

8. **The parenthood rebate is larger for later children, and unused rebate carries
   forward.** s 42A: $5,000 for a first child born from 2008, $10,000 for a second
   child from 2004, and $20,000 for a third or fourth child from 2004 or a fifth or
   later child from 2008. It is allowed for the year of assessment after the birth.
   A fifth child born in 2006 gets nothing under the legitimate-birth limbs. Rebate
   that the tax payable cannot absorb carries forward (s 42A(3)). Asserted.

9. **If I am not asked for a return by the end of March, I must say I am
   chargeable.** s 62(4): a chargeable person not required to make a return within 3
   months of the start of the year of assessment must give notice "within 14 days
   after the end of that period". This encoding reads that date as 14 April
   (inference). A new arrival has one month from arrival (s 62(5)). Asserted.

10. **An individual has 30 days to object, and the Comptroller must extend for good
    cause.** s 76(3)(b) gives 30 days from service (a company gets 2 months), and the
    objection must "state precisely the grounds". Under s 76(4) the Comptroller
    "must extend" once satisfied that absence, sickness or other reasonable cause
    prevented the objection. Asserted.

11. **An appeal is two 30-day steps, and lateness is forgiven only at the
    Chairperson's discretion.** s 79(1): a notice of appeal within 30 days of the
    refusal to amend, then a petition within 30 days of the notice. Without the
    petition the notice "is deemed to be withdrawn". Under s 79(11) the Chairperson
    "may" let a late appeal proceed. Asserted. The same two steps are written as a
    regulative rule (`my appeal to the Board of Review`), which type-checks with the
    stable binary. It is not traced.

12. **Objecting does not delay payment.** s 85(1): tax is payable within one month
    of service "despite any objection or appeal". Asserted.

13. **Late payment costs 5% at once, then up to 12% more.** s 87(1): 5% if tax is not
    paid within the s 85 period. If it is still unpaid 60 days after that, a further
    1% of the tax outstanding is added for each completed month, capped at 12%. The
    Comptroller may remit for good cause (s 87(2)), so these are the most that can be
    added. On $10,000 left unpaid for 20 months, that is $500 + $1,200. The text
    does not say when the completed months start counting, so the count is an input.
    Asserted.

14. **Unpaid tax can keep me in Singapore.** s 86: if the Comptroller is of the
    opinion I am about to leave without paying, I can be prevented from leaving until
    I pay or give security. Asserted.

15. **The penalty multiple of the tax undercharged follows culpability: 1, 2, 3,
    4.** s 95(1) incorrect return, once the tax undercharged. s 95(2) without
    reasonable excuse or through negligence, double, plus a fine of up to $5,000 or
    3 years. s 96 wilful evasion, treble, plus up to $10,000 or 3 years. s 96A false
    books or fraud, 4 times, plus up to $50,000 or 5 years. Repeat evasion
    convictions carry a minimum of 6 months for any imprisonment (ss 96(2), 96A(2)).
    Under s 96(3), once a false entry in my return is proved, I am presumed to have
    intended to evade unless I prove otherwise. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The other s 39 reliefs and the Fifth Schedule (child relief, CPF, life insurance,
  course fees and the rest) are what most residents actually claim. Without them,
  chargeable income can only be worked out from a reliefs total given as an input.
- What income is charged at all (ss 10-12) and the common exemptions in s 13.
- The s 39(1)(b)/(c)-(d) overlap (finding 3), and when the s 87 completed months
  start counting (finding 13), need an authority: IRAS practice or a decided case.
  None was searched.
- The s 76(9) rules, if any have been made, should be deposited.
- The s 42A limbs for a later marriage and for adoption, and sharing the rebate
  between parents (s 42A(2C)).
- Coverage table, independent test pass and the human gates.
