# Misuse of Drugs Act 1973 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design
defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

This encoding replaces the one moved to `solutions` (Litigate.4) on 2026-10-19. It was
written fresh from the deposited text; that encoding's notes were read for scope and
pitfalls only.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, version in force from
1/6/2026, as deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/MDA1973.txt` (SSO, retrieved
2026-10-01).

**Checks:** two modules (`mda-offences.l4`, `mda-punishment.l4`) and two case files,
generated by a scratchpad script with expected values written by hand from the text.
`mda-cases-offences.l4` has 30 assertions and `mda-cases-punishment.l4` has 30: 60
satisfied, 0 errors and 0 warnings, run with the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98.
The offences case file also runs two `#TRACE`s of the urine-specimen duty (provided in
time: `FULFILLED`; not provided by the deadline: breach by the person asked). One
expected value was changed on purpose in a throwaway copy, and the runner reported it
as a failure.

## Seat

**The person suspected of, or charged with, a drug offence (the accused person).** The
Act is a criminal statute. The individual it is applied to is the person found with a
drug, tested for one, or charged. The decisions are that person's questions: `What
offence would my act be`, `Am I presumed to have had the drug for trafficking`, `Must I
give a urine specimen when asked`, `What can I be sentenced to for possession`, `Can I be
spared the death penalty`. Where the text speaks to an official (s 31(1), the officer
who may require a specimen; s 33B, the court and the Public Prosecutor), the encoding
gives what that means for the person: whether they must comply, and what sentence the
court may or must pass.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The numbers
  that matter (the s 17 thresholds, the Second Schedule's bands, the ranges in ss 33 and
  33A) are in the Act itself. No instrument was needed. The Act's own First, Third,
  Fourth and Fifth Schedules (lists of substances) are **not** encoded. Whether
  something is a controlled drug, its class and whether it is a "specified drug" are
  asked as facts. This departs from "definitions worked out from facts", because those
  lists are chemical names and cannot be worked out from facts a person could state.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: officers of the Bureau, immigration officers,
  police officers (with the rank of sergeant), the Public Prosecutor, the court.
- **A judgment the law hands to someone else, answered conditionally.** Followed. The
  officer's reasonable suspicion (s 31(1)) and the Public Prosecutor's certificate of
  substantive assistance (s 33B(2)(b), "at the sole discretion of the Public
  Prosecutor", s 33B(4)) are inputs. The court's discretion under s 33B(1)(a) is
  returned as "the court may", not guessed. Whether a fact is "proved" or a presumption
  is rebutted is also an input.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts, borrowed terms asked as facts.**
  Followed for "traffic" (s 2 with s 5), "opium" and "cannabis" (glossed only). One
  departure: "young person" (below 21) and "vulnerable person" (an impairment of mind
  that affects judgment, which is itself a judgment) are asked together as one fact
  about the intended recipient. The trafficker's own age is asked as `I am 21 or older`.
  "Citizen" and "permanent resident" are borrowed terms and are asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** One act, one charge, one conviction. Previous
  convictions and admissions are counts in a record.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed. The section and paragraph
  are named in the constructors (`possession, section 8(a)`).
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out.** Followed. The appointment of officers,
  advisory committees, regulations and the power to amend the Schedules are out. So are
  treatment and rehabilitation (Part 4) and committees of inquiry. A previous admission
  to an approved institution is an input to punishment.

## Why this scope

The Act is large: offences for controlled drugs and psychoactive substances, a dozen
presumptions, enforcement powers, rehabilitation, and an 86-row punishment schedule.
This encoding takes the provisions a person most often meets. They are which offence an
act is, the presumptions that shift the burden onto that person (ss 17, 18, 21, 22), the
duty to give urine (s 31) and consumption abroad (s 8A). On punishment it takes
trafficking, import and export (the class columns, the sixth column for the seven drugs
named by quantity, ss 33(4A), (4B), (4D)), the death-penalty alternative (s 33B),
possession (row 8(a), s 33(3B)) and consumption or refusing a urine test (ss 33(3A), (4),
(4AA), 33A). It returns `not covered by this encoding` for two things. One is opium of
800 g or more: those rows turn on the morphine content as well as the weight, and leave
gaps. The other is a consumption offence committed before 1 April 2019, because the
earlier Schedule rows are deleted from this text.

Left out: the psychoactive-substance offences (Part 2A), ss 9 to 14, manufacture,
ss 19 and 20, certificates, search and arrest, hair and oral fluid tests, s 31(1A),
(1B), (3), the relevant-date rules of s 33A(5), and s 31A(2) as the offence charged.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Possession alone, with no trafficking, can carry 20 to 30 years.** Row 8(a) gives
   possession of more than 15 g of diamorphine "Maximum 30 years and 15 strokes /
   Minimum 20 years and 10 strokes". Between 10 and 15 g the range is 10 to 20 years and
   5 to 10 strokes. The possession bands use the same figures as the trafficking bands.
   Asserted.
2. **The trafficking presumption begins far below the trafficking bands.** s 17 presumes
   trafficking from "more than" 2 g of diamorphine (exactly 2 g does not trigger it,
   2.01 g does), 15 g of cannabis, 25 g of methamphetamine and 113 g of ketamine. The
   sixth-column bands start at 10, 330 and 167 g. Asserted.
3. **s 17 runs from possession "proved", s 18(2) from possession "proved or presumed".**
   Inference, labelled: the encoding reads s 17 as not triggered by a possession that is
   only presumed under s 18(1). Asserted as the encoding's reading. No case law was
   searched.
4. **"I never held it" is no answer, and shared possession is everyone's.** s 18(3): the
   presumptions "are not to be rebutted by proof that the accused never had physical
   possession". s 18(4): where one of several people has the drug with the knowledge and
   consent of the rest, it "is deemed to be in the possession of each and all of them".
   That is a deeming, with no rebuttal provided. The owner of a vehicle and the person in
   charge of it are both presumed to possess what is found in it (s 21). Asserted.
5. **One hundredth of a gram separates life imprisonment from death.** For trafficking,
   10 to 15 g of diamorphine carries "Maximum 30 years or imprisonment for life and
   15 strokes / Minimum 20 years and 15 strokes". "More than 15 grammes" carries
   "Death". The same holds at 500 g against 501 g of cannabis. Asserted.
6. **Below the sixth column, a repeat conviction or a young recipient raises the
   minimum.** s 33(4A) (convicted again) and s 33(4B) (an offender of 21 or older with
   a young or vulnerable intended recipient) give Class A 10 to 30 years. The ordinary
   s 5(1) class column is 5 to 20. s 33(4D): they apply only where the sixth column does
   not. A 19-year-old selling to a young person stays in the class column. Asserted.
7. **Being only a courier is not enough to escape death.** s 33B needs proof that
   involvement was restricted to transporting, sending or delivering. On top of that it
   needs either the Public Prosecutor's certificate of substantive assistance (the court
   *may* substitute life imprisonment and at least 15 strokes) or an abnormality of mind
   that substantially impaired responsibility (the court *must* substitute life
   imprisonment). Inference, labelled: where both are proved, the mandatory limb is
   taken. Asserted.
8. **A second consumption conviction has a minimum of 3 years and no printed maximum.**
   s 33(4) and (4AA) say "not less than 3 years" and print no upper limit. The encoding
   returns no maximum. Asserted.
9. **Any two antecedents of five kinds trigger the long-term regime, but only for a
   specified drug.** s 33A(1)(a) to (f) and (1B)(a) to (i) between them list every pair
   drawn from admissions and convictions for specified-drug consumption, urine refusal,
   hair refusal and military specified-drug consumption. Any two lead to 5 to 7 years
   and 3 to 6 strokes, and a later conviction leads to 7 to 13 years and 6 to 12 strokes
   (s 33A(2)). The same record on a charge of consuming a drug that is not a specified
   drug leads only to s 33(4)'s minimum of 3 years. Asserted.
10. **Who may demand urine.** s 31(1): "Any officer of the Bureau, immigration officer or
    police officer not below the rank of sergeant". The encoding reads the rank as
    qualifying the police officer only (a fork, labelled). Refusing without reasonable
    excuse within the time the officer requires is an offence (s 31(2)), punished like
    consumption (s 33(3A)). Asserted.
11. **Abroad counts, for citizens and permanent residents.** s 8A: s 8(b) applies to
    them "outside as well as within Singapore" where both sealed-part urine tests under
    s 31(4)(b) find the drug. The same two tests found the s 22 presumption of
    consumption. A positive preliminary test alone founds neither. Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The First, Third, Fourth and Fifth Schedules (which substances, which class, which are
  specified drugs) are asked as facts. A wrong answer there makes every punishment wrong.
- The Second Schedule's layout is scrambled in the text extraction. The cells used here
  were read from the text, not from the printed PDF page, and no second person checked
  them.
- The opium rows, manufacture, the psychoactive-substance offences and the s 33A(5)
  relevant-date rules are not encoded.
- The readings in findings 3, 7 and 10 need a lawyer and the case law. No case law was
  searched.
- No domain expert has read the modules, and there was no independent test pass.
