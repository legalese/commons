## Seat

**The seat: a person with a claim or a debt, or who has contracted, asking on facts they can state what the Act provides.**

Why this seat: the Civil Law Act is private law.
It is applied by courts between people, and the people it helps are the ones who hold a claim, owe a debt or have made a contract: a lender asking whether a wager is enforceable, a tenant asking whether a landlord can charge double rent, a family asking what they can claim after a wrongful death, a person who made a contract at 18 asking whether it binds them.
No section of the Act is addressed to an official as its main reader, so there is no second seat.
Where a provision gives a power to the Minister or to the court, it is in layer 1 only (s 5B(8) regulations, s 21(6) the Minister's substituted sum, s 27(3) to (5) the Minister's warrants, s 36(2) the Minister's power to amend the Schedule), and the seat reaches it only as the number or fact the person is asked to state (for example, "if the Minister has substituted another sum by order, what is it? 0 if none").

**How the seat is built.**
`cla-seat.l4` imports the layer-1 modules and calls their functions; it restates no rule, and no layer-1 module was changed to suit it.
Each question has `@export` (the question as the person would ask it: "Must the court give me...?", "Am I entitled to...?", "May I...?"), `@desc` on every input (the wording the person is asked), and a `DECIDE` that is one call to a layer-1 function.
Questions are grouped under `§§` headings in the Act's order.
Inputs are plain: yes or no, a number, a date, or a choice from a short list.
A fact the person may not know (a fact the Minister or a regulation settles) is asked as `Yes / No / I am not sure` (`My answer`); "I am not sure" becomes `NOTHING`, and a rule that cannot answer without it answers `NOTHING`.
A number the person may not know is asked as 0 and becomes `NOTHING`.
Questions take no list of public holidays, so a last day that is a public holiday is not moved (a Sunday is).
The seat's header comment says all of this at the top of the file.

**Companions.**
For the two duties whose "if" can fail (QD), the seat exports the companion ("does the duty arise for me?") next to the compliance question: s 5B(3) and s 28(5).
For the two bars that a person would plainly ask about from the other side (QA), layer 1 has a twin: `s 6 the action is barred for want of a signed writing` and `s 18 the claimant is barred from costs in the action`.
The seat exports both of s 6's questions ("May I bring an action on this promise without a signed writing?" and "Is my action barred because there is no signed writing?") and only the permission question of s 18.

**Cases.**
`cla-cases-seat.l4` has one or more asserted cases for every question, including cases that end `NOTHING` (the Act does not answer) and `JUST FALSE` (the Act says no).
Every case file ends with assertions equal to assertions satisfied (see "What check.sh prints").

## Unstable dialect

No second, unstable encoding was written: the author looked at time, versions and workarounds and found nothing the unstable dialect would express better than main already does.
Where the author looked:

- **Time.** The provisions that turn on a date are s 8 ("contracts made on or after 1 January 1999"), s 19(4) ("before 1 January 1999"), s 31(1) and (2) (settlements made on or after, and before, 15 December 2004), s 35(1) and (6) ("as from 1 March 2009"), and the periods of s 20(4)(b), s 20(5) and s 28(4).
  Each is a scope or a period on a fact date (the date a contract or settlement was made, the date of a death or an eviction), and main expresses it with an ordinary date input and a comparison (`NOT (d AT LEAST (Date 1 3 2009))`), asserted on both sides of each date.
- **Versions.** The text carries amendment notes (for example "[Act 25 of 2021 wef 01/04/2022]", "[Act 15 of 2022 wef 01/08/2022]", "[Act 20 of 2022 wef 15/10/2024]") dating each amendment, but it is the single current version; no earlier version of any section is among the sources.
  A rule-version axis (`RULES EFFECTIVE DATE`) would have no second version to select, so it would encode nothing the current text says.
- **Workarounds.** Month counting is the main builtin `add months` (fork F-M1), so no workaround is needed there.
  The one workaround that main forces is the `MAY ... WITHIN` of s 20(3) to (5), which ends `FULFILLED` whether used or lapsed; the windows are tested through decision functions instead (see "What the main binary does not accept").
  The difference the unstable build is known to have for a `MAY` is with a `LEST` (lapse becomes `BREACH`), and the Act says nothing about what follows if the window lapses, so adding a `LEST` would invent a consequence.

## What is encoded and what is not

**Encoded: every operative provision of the Act, sections 2 to 37 and the Schedule.**
Each subsection, paragraph and sub-paragraph that makes a rule, a definition or a deeming has a layer-1 function with `@ref` and the quoted words, and a test on each side of every condition and every threshold.
The Act's own defined terms are decision functions that return the term's meaning as the Act states it (see the defined-term rows of the coverage table).
The Act's own interpretation section (s 2) is three definitions and is encoded as three decision functions.

**Not encoded, and why** (every row of the coverage table has exactly one disposition; there are no out-of-scope or deferred rows):

- Inert, reason 1 (not operative): the title page, the contents list, the long title, the date of enactment, the Schedule's heading, and the Legislative History, abbreviations and comparative table, which the text says "is not part of the Act".
- Inert, reason 2 (machinery): s 1 (the short title) and s 5B(8) with its three paragraphs (the Minister's power to make regulations; what the regulations prescribe is an input to ss 5B(1), (3) and (10), not a rule here).
- Repealed or blank in this text: the Schedule's paragraph 4(b), "[Deleted by Act 29 of 2014]".
- Inert reason 3 is not used: the one deleted paragraph has the disposition `repealed or blank in this text`.

**Taken as inputs, not encoded as rules.**
What a Minister's order, notification or regulations set is a plain input and never a number from memory: the proceedings and requirements prescribed under s 5B (`MAYBE BOOLEAN`, "I am not sure" is `NOTHING`), the activities the Minister has prescribed by notification under s 5(5)(i), the sum the Minister has substituted under s 21(6), the Minister's notification under s 27(1)(b), and an amendment of the Schedule under s 36(2).
What other Acts say (the exempted operators of the Gambling Control Act 2022, the registration of a business, a limited partnership or a healthcare professional) is a plain yes-or-no fact.
Public holidays are a list of dates the caller supplies.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING

Every layer-1 decision function returns `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (or `MAYBE NUMBER` or `MAYBE DATE`), and a caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.

**`JUST FALSE` is "the Act says no", and only that.**
In this row it means one of: a definition that says "means" and no limb holds ("`court` means the General Division of the High Court"); a condition the text opens with "if", "only if", "where", "when" or "upon" and which fails, or an "unless" or "except" that is met against a duty, a deeming or a permission; a period the text fixes in its own words ("within 3 years after the death", "as from 1 March 2009") when the date falls outside it; or a flat negative ("shall not", "does not", "is not", "Nothing in this section shall").
Every branch that answers it carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the quoted words>"` in the `.l4` file.

**`NOTHING` is "not covered": the Act does not answer on these facts.**
In this row it means one of the six reasons the brief names, and every such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`:

- *silent*: the Act says nothing on the point (a bare "must" that was not done: the Act says what follows only elsewhere, if at all);
- *list not closed*: a list the text does not close with "only";
- *includes*: a definition that says "includes" and the case is outside the listed limbs;
- *undefined term*: a term the Act uses and does not define;
- *points outside the text*: a power, regulation, notification or other Act that is not among the sources, whose answer the person must supply;
- *limited by its own wording*: a grant or prohibition limited by words in the same sentence ("without", "subject to", "against", a named complement, a scope noun phrase such as "contracts made on or after") and the case falls outside the limit; a bar lifted by an "unless" grants nothing (QA); a lifted permission grants nothing.

**Three uses of the answer, because a reader meets them first.**

1. A caller who gets `NOTHING` has not been told "no". The seat says so in its header, and every `NOTHING` case in `cla-cases-seat.l4` is named as a case the Act does not answer.
2. Where a number, a party or a date is the answer (`MAYBE NUMBER`, `MAYBE DATE`), there is no `JUST FALSE` to give; the "this rule applies" twin carries it (for example `s 28(5) the duty of the subtenant to give notice to his immediate landlord arises`, `s 21(4)` with its "damages awarded under section 21" input).
3. "An action may be brought" and "an action is barred" are different questions: the first answers `NOTHING` when the Act merely lifts a bar, and the second (the twin, where one was written) answers the bar. See the Judgment calls, QA.

Two readings are for a lawyer, not for the author: that a failed "if" counts as the text's no, and that a prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not.
Every branch that rests on one is listed under "Marginal readings for a lawyer", with the other reading.

## Coverage table

The table was built by script from the text file, one row per provision unit: each section, subsection, paragraph, sub-paragraph, Schedule paragraph and illustration, plus the front matter, the Schedule's heading, the Legislative History's parts and entries, each abbreviation, and the comparative table's rows.
A section's marginal heading belongs to that section's row.
The script checks that every line of the text, blank or not, falls in exactly one row; it stops if one does not.
Defined terms that the text defines inline are added as separate rows after the partition (the "defined-term rows" below), each pointing at the rule that encodes it.

Dispositions, as the brief defines them: `encoded` (a rule, definition or table in layer 1 with a test and `@ref`); `fork` (the text can be read two or more ways with different outcomes: the row names the fork id, which is in the fork register, and the rule that encodes the chosen reading); `inert` (only reasons 1, 2 and 3 of the brief); `repealed or blank in this text`.
A row's "rules" column lists the layer-1 functions whose quoted lines fall in the row, as `module:line`, where `p04` is `cla-p04-gaming-funding.l4` and so on (the module list above gives each code), with the function's name cut at 64 characters.
"Forks, marginal readings" lists the fork ids and the numbers of the marginal readings (M-n) that sit in the row.
A function that gathers several subsections by hand (ruling E1, option B) is listed in every row it quotes.

Each relation is stated once, from the holder's side.
A provision that creates no relation (a definition, a counting rule, a saving, a rule of construction) is listed once as `none`, with the reason in the note.
Where the text names no holder for a duty (s 5B(3)), the holder column says so.

## Marginal readings for a lawyer

Two readings are the status quo of this corpus and are for a lawyer, not for the author, to decide.
(a) A failed "if" counts as the text's no, clause by clause, within the provision that holds the "if" (an inference: "if A then B" does not itself say "if not A then not B"); flat negatives count as the text saying no.
(b) A prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not count as the text's no.
Every branch of the encoding that rests on one of them carries `-- MARGINAL (a)` or `-- MARGINAL (b)` in the `.l4` file, and is listed below with the other reading.
Flat negatives are not listed: they follow the N2 ruling, which is not marginal.
The ruling of 2026-11-05 that treats "where", "when" and "upon" like "if" is applied under (a).
A period the text fixes in its own words answers `JUST FALSE` outside the period under rule (a) of the same ruling and is not marginal.

Where a reading cannot be tied to a single clause-opening word the encoding records a fork instead (F-W1, F-W2, F-W3 in the register above), so a site can sit in both lists.

## What the text turns out to say

Numbered findings.
Each ends "Asserted." where a case in the deposit asserts it, naming the module and function; the exact case lines are in the cited file.

1. **The Act counts in months and years, and the Interpretation Act does not tell it how.**
   Section 20(4)(b) says "within 6 months after the death" and s 20(5) says "within 3 years after the death".
   The copied Interpretation Act module defines "month" and "year" and gives no counting rule, so this encoding counts calendar months with the main builtin `add months` (F-M1), which gives the last day of a month that is too short, and then applies the Interpretation Act's s 50(b) (a last day that is a Sunday or public holiday moves to the next day that is not).
   For a death on 15 July 2025 the persons for whose benefit the action could be brought may bring it from 16 January 2026.
   For a death on 29 February 2024 the third year ends on 28 February 2027, a Sunday, so the last day is Monday 1 March 2027.
   Asserted (`cla-p08-wrongful-death.l4`: `s 20(4)(b) the first day after the 6 months`, `s 20(5) the action is brought within 3 years after the death`).

2. **The day of the death is not "after" the death.**
   An action brought on the very day of the death is outside "within 3 years after the death" (`JUST FALSE`), the next day is inside, the same date three years later is the last day, and the day after that is outside (F-P2).
   The same reading puts the first day of double rent the day after the tenancy is determined (s 28(4)), and the last day charged is the day possession is given up (F-P3).
   Asserted (`cla-p08-wrongful-death.l4`: `s 20(5) the action is brought within 3 years after the death`; `cla-p10-property-rules.l4`: `s 28(4) the day falls within the period for which double rent or double value is chargeable`).

3. **Interest runs on both endpoints.**
   "For the whole or any part of the period between the date when the cause of action arose and the date of the judgment": both the day the cause of action arose and the day of the judgment are inside (F-P1).
   Asserted (`cla-p06-survival-interest-charges.l4`: `s 12(1) the day falls within the period for which interest may be ordered`).

4. **Four provisions turn on a date written in the text, and the Act is silent on the other side of two of them.**
   Section 8 applies to "contracts made on or after 1 January 1999" and is silent on an earlier contract (`NOTHING`, F-S8).
   Section 19(4) says "Nothing in sections 15, 16, 17 and 18 shall affect any case where the debt ... became due or ... the damage ... occurred before 1 January 1999": a case before that date gets `JUST FALSE` (the sections do not affect it) and a case on or after it gets `NOTHING`.
   Section 31 has two regimes: income from a settlement made on or after 15 December 2004 may be accumulated for its duration (subsection (1)), and one made before it only for the six periods of subsection (2).
   Section 35(1) is "as from 1 March 2009": a contract entered into on 28 February 2009 gets `JUST FALSE` and one on 1 March 2009 gets `JUST TRUE`; subsection (6) keeps the earlier law for contracts before it.
   Asserted (`cla-p05-writing-trusts-euro.l4`: `s 8 ...`; `cla-p07-contribution.l4`: `s 19(4) sections 15 to 18 affect the case`; `cla-p10-property-rules.l4`: `s 31(1)`, `s 31(2)`; `cla-p11-minors.l4`: `s 35(1)`, `s 35(6)`).
   These are the provisions an unstable-dialect rule version could express more directly (see "Unstable dialect").

5. **A minor "has attained the age of 18 years" on the 18th anniversary.**
   The anniversary day itself is the first day of being 18 (F-A2), and a minor born on 29 February attains 18 on 28 February in a year that is not a leap year (F-A1).
   A contract made on the day before the 18th birthday gets `NOTHING` from s 35(1): the Act is silent on a minor under 18.
   Asserted (`cla-p11-minors.l4`: `the minor has attained the age of 18 years on`, `s 35(1) ...`).

6. **Section 35(4) takes some contracts out, and a lease of 3 years or less is the odd one.**
   A contract "for a lease of land not exceeding 3 years" is taken out of paragraph (a)'s list of land contracts by the words "other than", and paragraph (b) separately lists leases of more than 3 years; the Act therefore says nothing of a short lease in either paragraph, and the encoding answers `NOTHING` for it (QE).
   A lease of more than 3 years answers `JUST FALSE` (it does not have full-age effect).
   Asserted (`cla-p11-minors.l4`: `s 35(4) ...`; `cla-cases-seat.l4`: the question "Does my contract have effect as if I were of full age").

7. **The perpetuity period is never longer than 100 years, and an instrument that refers to lives in being gets 100.**
   An instrument that specifies 80 years has 80; one that specifies 150, or none, has 100; one that refers to lives in being has 100 (s 32(1) and (2)).
   Asserted (`cla-p10-property-rules.l4`: `s 32 the perpetuity period in years`).

8. **Bereavement damages are a fixed sum unless the Minister has substituted another, and the classes of claimant cascade.**
   The sum is $15,000 (s 21(4)) unless the Minister has substituted another by order (s 21(6)); the surviving children are claimants only where there is no spouse by or for whom a claim can be made, the parents only where there is neither, and so on down to brothers and sisters (s 21(2)), so a child with a surviving spouse gets `JUST FALSE`.
   The right to claim does not survive for the benefit of the claimant's estate (s 21(3)).
   Asserted (`cla-p08-wrongful-death.l4`: `s 21(4) the sum to be awarded as damages for bereavement`, `s 21(2) ...`; `cla-cases-seat.l4`: the bereavement questions).

9. **The bars in ss 5(2), 6 and 18 are lifted by their own exceptions, and a lifted bar grants nothing.**
   Where the contract is within the exception of s 5(3), the exceptions of s 5(3A) or the saving of s 5(4), the question "may I sue to recover the sum?" answers `NOTHING`, not `JUST TRUE`: the Act lifts the bar and grants no right (QA).
   The same holds for s 6 when the promise is in signed writing, and for s 18 when the court is of the opinion that there was reasonable ground; for s 6 and s 18 the twin "am I barred?" question answers the bar itself.
   Asserted (`cla-p04-gaming-funding.l4`: `s 5(2) ...`; `cla-p05-writing-trusts-euro.l4`: `s 6 ...`; `cla-p07-contribution.l4`: `s 18 ...`).

10. **Section 5B depends on regulations that are not in the text.**
    Whether proceedings are "prescribed dispute resolution proceedings", what the "prescribed requirements" are, and whether relief has been granted all rest on the Minister's regulations (s 5B(8)) or on a court's order.
    The encoding takes them as inputs: with "I am not sure" the answer is `NOTHING`, and the duty companion ("does the duty arise for me?") answers `JUST FALSE` for anyone who is neither a qualifying Third-Party Funder nor the funded party.
    Asserted (`cla-p04-gaming-funding.l4`: `s 5B(2) ...`, `s 5B(3) ...`; `cla-cases-seat.l4`: the s 5B questions).

11. **Section 36 applies to a list the Act hands to the Minister to amend.**
    Section 36(1)(a) says "specified in the Schedule" and does not say the Schedule has effect for determining it, and s 36(2) lets the Minister amend it; a proceeding that matches no paragraph answers `NOTHING` (QC).
    Asserted (`cla-p11-minors.l4`: `The Schedule the proceeding or action is one to which section 36 applies`; `cla-cases-seat.l4`: the question "May I conduct this legal proceeding in my own name").

12. **Section 22(1) names three things that are not taken into account, and says nothing of any other item.**
    A sum paid under a contract of assurance or insurance, a sum under the Central Provident Fund Act 1953, and a pension or gratuity paid as a result of the death are not taken into account (`JUST FALSE`); any other item answers `NOTHING`.

## What the main binary does not accept

The binary governs: where a skill describes a construct that main rejects, this section says what was written instead.
Everything below was met while writing on 1.6.109 (main, 4c887e0, 2026-11-04) and re-read with 1.6.108 (main, 2ae7c9b, 2026-11-02) by `check.sh`; the two builds read every module the same way (every row of the `check.sh` output says `same`).

**Loud: the binary says no, with an error.**

- `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` and `BECAUSE` (except `LEST BREACH ... BECAUSE`, which is used in the regulative rules) do not exist on main. "Not covered" is `NOTHING` or a constructor, and it is asserted.
- `A` is a keyword, so it cannot be the name of a `DECLARE`d type.
- `BEFORE (Date ...)` is unusable; the encoding writes `NOT (x AT LEAST (Date ...))`.
- Precedence must be spelled with brackets: `IF NOT f args EQUALS JUST TRUE` and `IF f EQUALS JUST TRUE AND ...` do not parse as one reads them, and `JUST (a TIMES b / c)` must be `JUST ((a TIMES b) / c)`. Every `NOT` is parenthesised.
- A `GIVEN` typed `MAYBE BOOLEAN` is written `IS A MAYBE BOOLEAN`.
- A multi-line `IF ... ELSE CONSIDER` does not parse; the encoding uses `BRANCH ... OTHERWISE CONSIDER`.
- Not used, because the brief forbids them on main: a section `GIVEN`, `TYPICALLY`, an opaque `DECLARE`, sets, `EVERY`, and a `YMD` of the encoding's own (dates are `Date d m y`).

**Silent: the binary answers, exit code 0, and the answer is not what a reader expects.**

- **A `MAY ... WITHIN` cannot tell a used window from a lapsed one.**
  In the traces for s 20(3) to (5), a `MAY` that is used and a `MAY` whose window passes unused both end `FULFILLED`, on both builds.
  The windows are therefore tested through decision functions (`s 20(5) the action is brought within 3 years after the death`, `s 20(4)(b) no action was brought by an executor or administrator within 6 months after the death`, `s 28(4)`), and the regulative rules are kept as traces.
  A `MUST` with no `WITHIN` stays a residual obligation until the act is done (the trace for `s 20(6)` prints `PARTY ... MUST ... HENCE FULFILLED` after `WAITUNTIL 400`).
- **`check.sh` does not see traces.** A trace prints `FULFILLED`, `BREACH` or a residual obligation, never an assertion line, so no count moves. The 16 traces (`cla-p03-section-4.l4`: 3, `cla-p04-gaming-funding.l4`: 3, `cla-p08-wrongful-death.l4`: 7, `cla-p10-property-rules.l4`: 3) were run with both builds and the full `l4 run` output of each module compared: 0 differing lines in each of the four.
- **`check.sh` compares counts, not which assertion fails.** Every count here is also zero failures, so equal counts cannot hide a different assertion failing on each side.
- **A binary older than the prelude it reads fails in a way that does not look like a version mismatch.** `JL4_LIBRARY_PATH` was left unset throughout, so the standard library is the binary's own.

**What each check does and does not prove.**
It proves that every module in this folder parses, type-checks and evaluates on two main builds with zero errors and zero warnings, and that every asserted case comes out as the author wrote it from the text.
It does not prove that the author read the text correctly.

## What the text does not answer

These are the questions a reader might put to the Act that no function answers, or answers with `NOTHING`, because the text is silent, points outside itself, or says only when something does not apply.
Each line says what the encoding does about it.
Line numbers are lines of `inputs/CLA1909.txt`.

### Where the encoding answers `NOTHING` and the register says why

- **A fact date outside a date gate.** A contract made before 1 January 1999 (s 8), a settlement made before 15 December 2004 (s 31(1)) or on or after it (s 31(2)), and an instrument taking effect before 15 December 2004 (s 32(3)): fork F-DATE-GATE, with the other reading.
- **Day 1 January 1999 and later under s 19(4).** The subsection says what sections 15 to 18 do not affect (a case before that date) and says nothing of a later case.
- **A minor who has not attained 18** under s 35(1), and a contract for a lease of land not exceeding 3 years under s 35(4)(a): the paragraph takes the lease out of its own list and the subsection says nothing about it (QE).
- **A settlement whose terms provide to the contrary** under s 31(1), a proceeding outside the Schedule, and a sum or benefit not named in s 22(1)(a) to (c): the Act names what is left out of the damages and does not say that nothing else is.
- **Lifted bars (QA)**: the Act says when a bar does not apply and not what then follows.
- **A bare "must" that was not done (QB)**: the Act says the thing must be done and not what follows if it is not (s 7(1), s 7(2), s 37(3) and the others in the register).
- **What follows when a `MAY ... WITHIN` window of s 20(3) to (5) lapses**: the Act says nothing of a consequence, so the encoding adds none (no `LEST`).

### Questions with no function, one line each

- **The short title (s 1, l.85-87).** "This Act is the Civil Law Act 1909" is machinery (inert, reason 2) and has no function.
- **The date 1 January 1879 (s 3).** It appears only inside names and comments of the s 3 functions, never as a date value; whether the equity side would have given the relief before that date is one plain yes/no input, and the Act is not re-run through the law of 1879.
- **The legality of a champertous contract outside s 5B (s 5A(2), l.664-671).** The text says the abolition of civil liability "does not affect any rule ... as to the cases in which a contract is to be treated as contrary to public policy or otherwise illegal", so `s 5A(2) the abolition of civil liability affects the rule ...` answers `JUST FALSE` (and `NOTHING` where s 5B applies); whether a particular contract is illegal is the other rule's question and the Act does not answer it.
- **A funder that qualifies and complies (s 5B(4), l.697-707).** The disability arises where the funder ceases to qualify or fails to comply; for a funder that does neither, `s 5B(4) ...` answers `NOTHING` (tested), because the Act says when the right is not enforceable and not that it is enforceable otherwise.
- **Who makes the regulations (s 5B(8), l.739).** The Minister may make them; that power is machinery (inert, reason 2) and has no function, and what the regulations prescribe is a plain input to s 5B(1), (3) and (10).
- **The one-year test (s 6(e), l.820-821).** `s 6 an action may be brought on the promise or agreement` takes the kind of action as an input, and the caller decides whether an agreement was "not to be performed within the space of one year from the making thereof"; no dates are asked.
- **The rate of interest (s 12(1), l.1051).** The court may order interest "at such rate as it thinks fit"; the Act gives no rate and no function returns one.
- **The amount of contribution (s 16(1), l.1174-1178).** `s 16(1) the amount of the contribution is the amount the court finds just and equitable` answers whether an amount is the one the court finds just and equitable (QB); the standard is given and no figure is computed.
- **The date of the appointment (s 23(1), l.1491).** It is not an input: the words "has been or is after 23 July 1909 made" are read as reaching every appointment (F-23).
- **"Full age" and "minor" (s 35, l.1949-2025).** This Act defines neither word; s 35(1) speaks of "a minor who has attained the age of 18 years" and deems the contract to have effect "as if he were of full age", so the encoding takes the age of 18 from that sentence and nothing else.
- **A contract date at s 35(4) (l.1981-1984).** `s 35(4) the contract has effect as if the minor were of full age by virtue of subsection (1)` takes no contract date, so on its own it cannot tell whether s 35 applies at all (s 35(6): a contract made before 1 March 2009 is outside the section); the combined `s 35 the contract has effect as if the minor were of full age` and the seat do check the date, and answer `JUST TRUE` for a contract for a lease of land not exceeding 3 years made after 1 March 2009 by a minor who has attained 18 (tested in both).
- **Whether a profession "provides medical advice" (s 37(7), l.2205-2207).** `s 37(7) the individual is a healthcare professional` takes it as a plain yes/no input, so "not settled" cannot be entered as a third answer; the registered professions in (a) and (b) are separate inputs.

### Questions that now have a function, and what they leave open

- **Whether the legal right has passed on a day (s 4(8), l.333-334)**: `s 4(8) the legal right to the debt or chose in action has passed by the day` (fork F-S4-8).
- **The assignee's power to give a good discharge (s 4(8), l.335-336)**: `s 4(8) the assignee has the power to give a good discharge ... without the concurrence of the assignor`.
- **The 21-year term from a death (s 31(2)(b), l.1792-1793)**: `s 31(2)(b) the last day of the term of 21 years from the death` and `s 31(2)(b) the day falls within the term of 21 years from the death`, built on the Interpretation Act module's s 50(b); the Act itself gives no counting rule (fork F-S31-21), so a lawyer may prefer a different last day.

### Readings that stand, as they were

- **The bare "must" sites s 7(1), s 7(2) and s 37(3)** answer `NOTHING` when the thing was not done; that is the QB call, and the other reading (`JUST FALSE`) is in each QB row.
- **The Schedule** answers `NOTHING` when no paragraph holds (QC): section 36(1)(a) says "specified in the Schedule" and does not say that the Schedule has effect for determining the question, and section 36(2) lets the Minister amend the Schedule, so the list is not a closed hand-off.
- **A lifted bar asked as a grant** answers `NOTHING`, and the "bar applies" twin answers `JUST FALSE` (QA); the question "may the tenant commit waste" for an estate without impeachment of waste is asked of the grant, and the Act's exception is not a grant.
- **s 19(4)** answers whether sections 15 to 18 affect a case, `JUST FALSE` for a case before 1 January 1999; it does not answer whether contribution may be recovered, which is s 15(1).

## What would need doing before this encoding is worth relying on

1. **A lawyer reads the marginal readings and the judgment calls.**
   The marginal readings (list above) and the QA to QE lines of the fork register are the places where a reasonable reader could answer differently; the author took the default the brief gives unless the text pointed the other way.
2. **The inputs the Act delegates are filled from their own sources.**
   The regulations made under s 5B(8) (the proceedings and requirements s 5B takes as "prescribed"), the Minister's notification under s 5(5)(i), the Minister's order under s 21(6), the notification under s 27(1)(b), and any amendment of the Schedule under s 36(2) are not among the sources.
   Each is a plain input here; none is a number or a list from memory.
3. **The text is checked against the official version.**
   The source is a converter's plain-text rendering of an unofficial consolidation; page furniture and amendment notes were dropped from the quoted lines by script, and the quoted lines were not compared with the printed Act by a person.
4. **The seat is tried by someone who is not a lawyer.**
   The 55 questions and their `@desc` wording were written from the Act; none has been shown to a person in the seat.
5. **Public holidays.**
   The seat passes no public holidays to the Interpretation Act's s 50(b); a last day that falls on a public holiday is not moved.
6. **The fork rows of the coverage table are coarse.**
   A fork id is attached to every sub-unit of the sentence it sits in (for example the five classes of s 6 and the six periods of s 31(2)); a reviewer who prefers fork rows at the single clause can narrow them.
7. **The ones the brief marks "for a lawyer" stay open.**
   Whether a failed "if" is the text's no, and whether a prohibition or grant limited by its own wording is not.
