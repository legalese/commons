## Marginal readings for a lawyer (roc8)

Two readings are the status quo of the corpus and are for a lawyer, not for the encoder, to decide.
(a) A failed "if", "where", "unless" or "provided that" counts as the text's no, clause by clause, within the provision that holds it, and a flat negative counts as the text saying no.  Other reading: NOTHING, because "if A then B" does not itself say "if not A then not B".
(b) A prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not say no outside the limit.  Other reading: JUST FALSE outside the limit.
Periods, definitions that say "means", and the "until" bars are not listed: they are ruled (2026-11-05), not marginal.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (roc8)

Every decision function answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` unless it says otherwise.

- **JUST TRUE** is the Rules saying yes on these facts: the requirement attaches, the step was done as the paragraph says, the date is within the period.
- **JUST FALSE is the Rules saying no in their own words**, and in these Orders that means one of four things: (1) a period the text fixes that has run ("within 14 days after ..." and the act came later); (2) an "if", "where", "unless" or "provided that" that is not met, so the permission, power or requirement it conditions does not exist (for example Order 34 rule 10(1), where the arbitration is not to be, is not being and has not been held within Singapore, or where the Court has not given permission; Order 35 rule 3(2), where no consent in Form 39 can be obtained); (3) a flat negative or a bar ("Nothing in paragraph (6) affects ..."; the Court order "does not take effect while ..."; the award "must not be enforced until ..."); (4) a definition that says "means" and is not met.
- **NOTHING is "not covered": the Rules do not answer on these facts.**  It is a bare "must" that has not been done (no Order in this range says what follows; Order 3 rule 2(4), outside this range, lists what the Court may do on non-compliance); a scope limit ("Every application to a Judge under (a) to (f)", "An application under section 394"); a term the Act defines, which is not among the sources ("appellate court", "qualifying creditor", "Official Assignee"); a time the Court fixes and has not fixed ("within such time as the Court directs"); an open standard ("as soon as practicable"); and the answer after an "until" bar has ended (the rule says when a thing may not happen, not when it may).
- **A caller must never turn NOTHING into FALSE.**  A decision function about a period takes the date the act was done and answers JUST FALSE for a date after the last day; an act never done has no date, and is read from the regulative twin (no act by the day after the last day is a BREACH).
- **Order 3 rule 2(1) is a caveat on every answer in this encoding, and it is not an input.**  It says that, unless the context otherwise requires and subject to any other written law, all requirements in these Rules are subject to the Court's discretion to order otherwise in the interests of justice, even if they are expressed using imperative words such as "must".  A JUST TRUE that a step was required, or a JUST FALSE that an act was out of time, is therefore the Rules' answer and not a prediction of what the Court will do.
- The answers are decision functions over facts the person states.  They do not decide who wins, and they do not say what the Court will order on non-compliance.

## Seat (roc8)

The seat is a party to a civil action in the Singapore courts (a claimant, a defendant, a third party or an appellant) asking, on facts they can state, what they must file or serve, by when, how, at what cost, what the Court may order, and what follows if they do not.
`roc8-seat.l4` has 70 questions in 10 groups, in the text's order, each with `@export` and an `@desc` on every input; `roc8-cases-seat.l4` has 135 assertions, about two for each question (the facts a party would state when the Rule says yes or gives a figure, and the facts when it says no or does not answer), written from the text.

- **Names.**  Every question begins "Under Order N, rule M(x),", and the question's identifier in the module is that same sentence, so no two parts' questions can share a name.  Wiring functions are named for the record they build, ending "from my answers".
- **Plain inputs.**  A yes/no fact is a BOOLEAN; a date is a DATE; the kind of application, the way an application is made, the manner of service and where a sum was deposited are choices from the layer-1 lists; a document with required parts is asked as one yes/no question per part, and the wiring function builds the record the module expects.  A time the Court fixes ("such longer period as the Court may allow") is asked as "has the Court fixed or allowed a date" plus the date.
- **Dates and periods.**  The count of a period is Order 3 rule 3's, which is not in these Orders, so every question that turns on a period asks for the last day (or, for a bar, the first day), "counted as Order 3, rule 3 requires".  A party who wants the length asks the separate "how many days" question (6 of them).
- **Wording of the `@desc` text is partly mechanical.**  A table turns "the defendant wishes to ..." and similar into "Do you wish to ..."; every other yes/no input reads "Is it true in your case that <the fact>?", and every part of a document reads "Is this true of your document: <the part>?".  A later pass should reword these in the second person; the questions and inputs are right, the phrasing is not yet the person's.
- **What follows if they do not.**  The last of the seat's concerns cannot be answered by these Orders: none states a consequence of non-compliance (finding 1).  The seat answers whether the step was required, done as required and in time; what the Court will do about it is Order 3 rule 2(4), another part's.
- **Not in the seat.**  70 of the 294 layer-1 functions are asked; the rest are reachable through layer 1 and are the same rules at finer grain (the other content records of Orders 34, 36, 37 and 38; the definitions; the Order 8, 15 and 19 applications).  There is never a seat for an official: Order 38 rule 11 (the Registrar's certificate) and the Court's powers (Order 34 rule 10(2), Order 37 rules 2(4), 3(4), 7(4), 9, Order 38 rules 4(1) and (2)) are in layer 1 for what they decide and reach the party only through the questions about their own steps.
- **One seat**, so no mirror cases.

## Findings (roc8)

### What the text turns out to say

1. **No Order in this range states a consequence of non-compliance.**  In lines 5051 to 5510 the words "fail", "dismiss", "struck", "penalt" and "non-compliance" do not occur; "default" occurs once (line 5304) in the sense of a foreign judgment given by default.  Every "must" is therefore bare, and non-compliance is answered NOTHING (judgment call QB).  What the Court may do is Order 3 rule 2(4), outside this range.
2. **Four rules share one shape, a bar that runs "until": Order 34 rule 14(4), Order 36 rule 2(4), Order 37 rule 8, and Order 37 rule 6(3)(c) as the content the Court order must state.**  In each the text says when the thing may not happen and not when it may.  Each is a yes/no bar function (JUST TRUE before, JUST FALSE after) with a twin that never says the thing has happened (NOTHING after the bar, JUST FALSE during it), and, for the two with a named bearer, a `SHANT` trace.
3. **Order 34 rule 7(5) calls rule 6(2) "return date"; rule 6(2) itself says "hearing date" and fixes it at not earlier than 3 months after the filing.**  The functions say "hearing date".
4. **Order 34 rule 15 is a deleted rule** ("[ Deleted by S 91/2023 wef 01/03/2023 ]"), the only repealed or blank row in the range.
5. **Orders 37 rule 2 and rule 3 repeat one shape** (an originating application without notice, a supporting affidavit that must state nine matters, exhibits, and a power to require a supplementary affidavit) with different cross-references: rule 2(3)(d)(iii) and rule 3(3)(d)(ii) both point back to "paragraph (2)(a) and (d) to (i)".  The two are encoded separately, each from its own words.
6. **Order 38 rule 4(2) puts a "where" condition on a power of the Court** while rule 4(1) gives a general power, and says "without limiting paragraph (1)" (fork F12).
7. **Every stated period in Orders 34 to 38 runs "after" an event the party must know** (the filing, the service, the grant of permission, the order), except Order 38 rule 9 (a time the Court directs) and Order 38 rule 14(1) and (3)(a) (counted backwards from the hearing); none says how days are counted, so all depend on Order 3 rule 3.

### What the main binary does not accept, and traps found

Sorted by how they show up: items 1 and 7 are **silent** (a wrong answer, or a wrong field, with exit code 0); the others fail loudly or are differences in meaning.

1. **SILENT: `NOT a AND b` is `NOT (a AND b)`, and `NOT a OR b` is `NOT (a OR b)`.**  A scratch module (`work/probe6.l4`, not shipped; the three expressions are quoted here so they can be re-run) gives the same result on main 1.6.108 and 1.6.109: `NOT FALSE AND FALSE` is TRUE, `TRUE AND NOT (FALSE) AND FALSE` is TRUE, `NOT TRUE OR TRUE` is FALSE.  The Commons guide already says "Parenthesise every NOT"; I still wrote it unparenthesised at three sites (Order 34 rule 6(10), rule 7(7)(i), rule 14(4)) and the tests caught it with five failing assertions, because the three sites produced the wrong answer with no error.  `work/notscan.py` finds a `NOT` whose operand can swallow a later AND/OR; it reports none unparenthesised in the final sources.  Another part that wrote `NOT a AND b` has a rule that returns the wrong value with a green build unless a test happens to reach the branch.
2. **A DEONTIC value cannot be compared in `#ASSERT`**, and `check.sh` counts only assertion lines, so a regulative rule moves no count.  `work/trace-check.py` runs every `#TRACE` under both builds and compares its outcome (FULFILLED, a breach, or a residual obligation) with the `-- expect:` line above it; 72 traces, 0 problems.  Each duty is also encoded as a decision function over the facts, and that is what the assertions test.
3. **A MAY with no LEST lapses to FULFILLED on main**, whether or not the party acts inside the period.  Both builds, this module (`IMPORT prelude`, `IMPORT daydate`, a `party` and an `act` enumeration), `MAY apply WITHIN 14 HENCE FULFILLED` with no `LEST`: `#TRACE ... PARTY debtor DOES apply AT 14` is FULFILLED and `... AT 15` is also FULFILLED.  The same obligation with `SHANT enforce WITHIN 14 HENCE FULFILLED LEST BREACH BY creditor BECAUSE "enforced too early"` gives BREACH at day 10 and day 14 and FULFILLED at day 15 (see finding 4).  The traces for Order 34 rule 14(4), Order 36 rule 2(4) and Order 37 rule 7(3) read the same way.  The closing of the window is therefore carried by the decision function (JUST FALSE after the last day), not by the trace.
4. **`SHANT` inside `WITHIN`** reads the way a bar needs: the act inside the period is a BREACH, and the act after it, or no act, is FULFILLED (both builds; the traces for the two bars, and the example in finding 3).
5. **`NOT EQUALS` is not valid**: write `NOT (a EQUALS b)`.
6. **Positional BOOLEAN arguments are easy to transpose**, so every content record names its fields, and the tests and the seat build it through a constructor function; the generated tests set each field false in turn.
7. **SILENT: a Period constant's `period direction` is read by no function in this encoding**, because every "in time" function takes the last day as an input.  A wrong direction therefore changes no answer and no existing assertion: O 38 rule 14(1) and rule 14(3)(a) carried `After the anchor` for "at least N days before the date fixed for the hearing" until a test asserted the direction of every constant.  Anyone counting from the hearing date with the constant would have counted the wrong way.  Assert the direction (and the unit) of every Period constant.
