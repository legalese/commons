### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (roc2)

In this part a decision function answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` and the three values mean:

- **`JUST TRUE`**: on the facts given, the Rules allow the act, impose the duty, or give the entitlement the function is named for.
- **`JUST FALSE`**: the Rules say no in their own words on those facts.
  That is one of: a flat negative ("must not", "may not", "No ... may"); a failed "if", "where", "when" or "upon" inside the provision that holds it; a stated period (including "at any time after" and "after having filed") that has run out (every "in time" function); a requirement written with "must" that is not met (the thing is not as required).
  It does not say that the Court cannot order otherwise.
- **`NOTHING`**: the Rules do not answer on those facts.
  That is one of: a scope limit with no "only" (outside the persons, documents or stage the rule names); a list that is not closed ("may be by ..." with no "only"; 9 such lists carry the comment "list not closed"); a power or prohibition limited by its own wording whose limit is not met (a manner or qualifier such as "by summons in Form 23" or "as far as possible", or the scope of a prohibition, which no trigger word makes a no); an open definition ("includes"); a case the Rules leave to another Order that is not in this part.
  Never read `NOTHING` as no.

**Order 3, rule 2(1) applies to every answer.**
It says that, unless the context otherwise requires and subject to any other written law, all requirements in the Rules are subject to the Court's discretion to order otherwise in the interests of justice, even if they are expressed with imperative words such as "must".
No `JUST TRUE` or `JUST FALSE` here says that discretion is absent.
This rule is in another part's range (O 3 is roc1's) and is not encoded here; the caveat is in the header of the seat file.

## Unstable dialect

I looked at time (the 24 period values and the ten deadline rules, all counted from an event), versions (the Rules have eleven dated historical versions in `SCJA1969-S914-2021.meta.json`, and only the current text was supplied; nothing in Orders 9 to 14 turns on a commencement date) and workarounds (the one workaround main forces is that a DEONTIC value cannot be asserted: a probe with the same rule on `l4` 1.6.109 (main) and on `unstable-20261104-68498bc` gave the same "assertion could not be evaluated" on both, so unstable would not remove it) and found no shape that earns a second encoding.
No second, unstable encoding was written.


## What the main binary does not accept

Measured on 1.6.109 (and read the same on 1.6.108 by `check.sh`):

1. **`#ASSERT` on a DEONTIC value** (`#ASSERT \`the duty\` EQUALS \`the duty\``) is "assertion could not be evaluated" (probe run again on 2026-11-05 on both main 1.6.109 and unstable-20261104-68498bc). `#TRACE ... WITH PARTY x DOES act AT n` and `(WAITUNTIL n)` work and print `FULFILLED` or `DEONTIC BREACHED`.
   Workaround: each duty is also a decision function over the facts.
2. **`A` and `AN` are keywords**, so no type or constructor can be named `A`; the party and act types of each regulative rule are named with the provision label (for example `o 9 r 25(8) party` and `o 9 r 25(8) act`).
3. **`MAY` with `LEST`**: a `LEST` after `MAY` degrades on main (recorded in decision 4 of `PROGRESS.md`); for that reason no `MAY` regulative rule is written (all ten are `MUST` rules).
4. **One `#ASSERT` per line**; every `NOT` parenthesised; no `REFUSE`; no section-level `GIVEN`; dates as `Date d m y`; no `YMD` defined here (the brief's list; the binary governs and the files check on both builds).
5. **A `DECLARE` between a quote run and its `@ref` closes the quote block** (`srcrefs.py`), so each type is declared before its `-- src:` lines.


## What the text turns out to say (findings)

1. **The Rules count their own time.** Order 3, rule 3(1) disapplies the Interpretation Act for time (line 324), so none of this part's periods is governed by the Interpretation Act module that Commons stages.
2. **Three units of time.** 21 of the 24 period values are in days, two (O 9 r 1(1)(a), (b): 8 and 12 weeks) are in weeks and one (O 9 r 25(8)) is in hours; the case-conference date is a fixed day, not a deadline (F-O9-1).
3. **A period can bound a permission instead of a duty.** O 9 r 9(10) and r 14(3) are limits on when an application or an amendment may be made, not deadlines for doing something (F-O9-3).
4. **Two acceptance windows.** A claimant may accept a payment into Court within 14 days after receipt of the notice and before the trial or hearing begins (O 14 r 3(1)); where the payment is made or increased after the trial or hearing has begun, within 2 days after receipt and before the Judge begins to deliver judgment (r 3(2)); and Rule 1(5) (amendment of the notice of payment) does not apply once the trial has begun (r 3(3)).
5. **"Subject to" cross-references that may not bar anything.** O 11 r 4 is "Subject to Rules 5, 8 and 9", but Rules 8 and 9 are about reliance and about objection, not about the Court's power (F-O11-1).
6. **Silence about consequences.** 54 branches ([marginal-c]) are "must" requirements for which the Rules in these Orders say nothing about what follows; the general powers on non-compliance are in Order 3, rule 2(4), another part's range.
7. **Nine rules gather several routes to one effect by hand** (E1, option B); the limbs are tested one by one.
8. **N2 is only half applied.** No `SHANT` rule is written for the party-borne prohibitions (for example O 9 r 9(10), O 11 r 9(1), O 12 r 3(2)); the prohibition is the `JUST FALSE` branch of the permission function. Deriving the `SHANT` twin remains to do.
9. **Under the seat layer, the same facts reach the same answer as layer 1**: every seat case that re-asks a layer-1 test returns the layer-1 answer (289 satisfied, 0 failed).
10. **Positive controls.** The seat cases (above) and the O 11 tests (134 passed, 1 failed after one expected value was flipped) were run with a deliberately wrong expected value and failed as they should.


## What the text does not answer

- **Lists the text opens and does not close** (9 branches commented "list not closed"): for example the ways an application for an injunction may be made (O 13 r 1(3)), what else Order 11 affects (r 1(1)), who else may be an independent counsel (O 9 r 23(2)), which other provisions of Orders 6 to 8 apply to a third party notice (O 10 r 3(3), (4)), which other orders the third party or the person may apply for (O 10 r 4(2), r 8(4)), which other circumstances allow an amendment after limitation (O 9 r 14(4)), which other provisions apply to a counterclaim payer (O 14 r 6). The answer is `NOTHING` for each.
- **Gaps: matters on which the text is silent and for which no function is written** (each is also marked `GAP` in the coverage table): whether the case conference may be held by video link (O 9 r 1, line 731); who pays an independent counsel (O 9 r 23, line 947); whether the expert's report must state the expert's fee (O 12 r 5(2), line 1206); the rate of interest on an excess repaid (O 13 r 8(9), line 1299, which says only "with or without interest"); the rate of interest on money in Court (Order 14, which does not mention it); and the fee per page over the page limit (O 9 r 25(15)(b), line 1020, which the text leaves to the fees "prescribed"; it is taken as the input `sch 4 the fee for this document`).
- **What follows when a "must" is not met** in these Orders (F-must): the Rules here give the requirement, not the consequence.
- **Judgments handed to someone else**, taken as plain inputs and never guessed: whether it is "a special case" (O 9 r 9(10), r 14(3), O 11 r 4-5, O 12 r 3(2)), whether the Court "thinks" a number of conferences appropriate, whether a claimant is "embarrassed" by a payment (O 14 r 1(5)), whether the Court "is satisfied" that no grant has been made (O 14 r 11), whether it is in "the interests of justice" (O 11 r 1(4)).
- **Sums the Rules leave elsewhere**: the fee for pages over the limit comes from the Fourth Schedule (input `sch 4 the fee for this document`, O 9 r 25(15)); costs are not fixed in these Orders.
- **Other written law**: the content of Orders 6, 7, 8, 22 and 44, section 388 of the Companies Act, the Civil Law Act, the Exchange Control Act and the Legal Aid and Advice Act are not among the sources; each is an input or an "applies" Boolean.
- **The payee rule inside the entitlement question**: `Under Order 14, rule 3(6)` does not ask whether a particular payee is a proper one; that is the separate Order 14, rule 10 question.
- **The version question**: only the text labelled "Current version as at 05 Nov 2026" was supplied; what the Orders said on an earlier date is not answered.
