The text is a plain-text rendering made by a converter script from Singapore Statutes Online.
It is not the official text, and nothing here has been checked against the official text.

The names `work/cov.py`, `work/seatgen.py`, `work/seatspec.py`, `work/tracecheck.py`, `work/units.py`, `work/forks.py` and `work/notes.py` are this part's build scripts, kept beside the deposit and not shipped with it.
Each claim they back can be re-checked in the shipped folder without them: the traces by `l4 run roc9-tests-order-NN.l4`, reading each printed outcome against the `-- expect:` comment above its `#TRACE`; the coverage by the Coverage table and `srcrefs.py check --strict`; the forks and marginal readings by `grep -n 'FORK\|MARGINAL' roc9-order-*.l4`; the assertion counts by `check.sh`.

Three things to know before reading anything below.

- **The brief governs over PLAN.md on periods.**
  PLAN.md section 7, item 10, still says a stated period answers `NOTHING` outside it, while its own section 2 says the ruling of 2026-11-05 replaces that.
  This part follows the ruling: outside a period the text states in its own words the answer is `JUST FALSE` (ruling (a) in the fork register below).
  A cap that limits a grant (the 6-week cap on a term of committal, O 42 r 22) answers `NOTHING` beyond it, which is marginal reading (b) below; and the length of a renewal (O 42 r 25) answers `NOTHING` because the text gives none (F42.1).
- **Layer 1 and layer 2.**
  Layer 1 is `roc9-order-39.l4` to `roc9-order-45.l4`, one module per Order, with `roc9-tests-order-NN.l4` beside each.
  Layer 2 is `roc9-seat.l4` and `roc9-cases-seat.l4` (section `## Seat` below).
  Every top-level name in layer 1 begins with the label of its provision (`o 40 r 2(4) ...`); every name in layer 2 begins `Under Order N, rule M,` or `Under Orders 39 to 45,`.
- **S-T2 is folded, and that is a choice this part made, not a ruling.**
  PLAN.md section 5 lists a separate seam `o 3 r 4 the last day as extended or shortened` (a `MAYBE DATE`).
  This part does not use it.
  Every in-time function compares with the one input `o 3 r 3 the last day for doing the act` (and `o 3 r 3 the first day on which the act may be done` where a rule has a window), and the `@desc` in the seat asks for that day "counted as Order 3, Rule 3 requires".
  A person whose time was extended or shortened under Order 3, Rule 4 gives the extended or shortened day as that one input.
  The other reading is to thread S-T2 as a `MAYBE DATE` through every in-time function, which adds one input to each and, for a person whose time was never extended, changes no answer.
  Assumed, not ruled.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (roc9)

Every function of layer 1 that decides a question answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (or a `MAYBE NUMBER`, a date, or a named constructor), and layer 2 passes the answer through unchanged.

- `JUST TRUE` means the Rules say yes on these facts: the document is as the Rule requires, the party may, the period has not run, the bar applies.
- `JUST FALSE` means the Rules themselves say no in their own words, and the branch quotes them. In Orders 39 to 45 that is one of: a definition that says "means"; a condition introduced by "if", "where", "when", "upon", "whenever", "unless" or "until" that is not met; a period the text fixes ("within 14 days of the seizure", "at least 7 clear days before the date fixed for the hearing") that has been missed; a list the text closes with "only" ("may only be exercised by a District Judge"); a flat negative ("does not", "may not", "is not entitled", "is not to be taken to admit"). A "no" is the text's answer to this party's facts, not a prediction of what a Court would do.
- `NOTHING` means the Rules do not answer on these facts. It is never to be read as no. The reasons, each named on its branch: silent; list not closed; includes; undefined term; points outside the text; limited by its own wording. In this range the commonest are: a bare "must" that has not been done (the Rule does not say what follows: QB); a duty whose trigger has not occurred (the companion function "the duty arises" answers `JUST FALSE`: QD); a bar lifted by "unless" (the permission is not granted by the Rule: QA); and a scope limit ("an action for libel", "a person under disability") with no "only".
- A duty, an "until" bar and a deeming: for "must ... until X" the layer has its own yes/no function (`the step cannot yet be taken`: `JUST TRUE` before X, `JUST FALSE` after X, per the ruling on "until" bars); a function that would report that the thing has happened is not written. Where a result is a number, a date or a choice (the amount refunded, the day a reply would arrive, the event the 14 days run from), the yes/no twin is the "applies" or "arises" function beside it.
- **Order 3, Rule 2(1) is a caveat on every answer here, and it is not an input.** All requirements in the Rules are subject to the Court's discretion to order otherwise in the interests of justice, even where they are expressed with an imperative word such as "must". No function of Orders 39 to 45 asks whether the Court has ordered otherwise in the interests of justice; the only such inputs are where the Rule itself says "unless the Court otherwise orders" or "directs" (or "the Registrar otherwise orders"), which 27 functions take as an input named after the Rule's own words (for example O 39 r 7(1), O 40 r 10(2), O 42 r 13(3), O 45 r 13).
- The deadlines are counted by Order 3, Rule 3, which is another part's. Each in-time answer says whether the act was done on or before the day the asker gives as the last day; if that day is wrong the answer is wrong.

## Seat

**Seat:** a party to a civil action in the Singapore courts (a claimant, a defendant, a third party or an appellant), asking, on facts the party can state, what the party must file or serve, by when, how, at what cost, what the court may order, and what follows if the party does not.
There is no seat for an official; the Registrar, the Sheriff, the Accountant-General and the proper officer appear only as what a party may claim, must give, or may expect.

`roc9-seat.l4` exports 350 questions over 97 of the 98 rules, grouped under one `§` heading per Order and one `§§` heading per Rule, in the text's order. Per Order: Order 39: 35, Order 40: 86, Order 41: 25, Order 42: 86, Order 43: 26, Order 44: 57, Order 45: 35.
The rules with no question: O 44 r 1. Each is a rule whose only function is a definition or a helper (see below), not a question a party asks.
Each question's name is `Under Order N, rule M, is it so that <the layer-1 function's words>` (or `what is ...` for a date, number or choice), takes plain inputs, and calls exactly one layer-1 function.
The questions are generated from the layer-1 function signatures (`work/seatgen.py`), so a question exists for every decision function, every "the duty arises" companion (so the QD companions are exported next to the compliance question), every "may" and every "in time" function.
The cases are in `roc9-cases-seat.l4`: one case per question with the facts a party would state, and a second where the question has a companion or a failing side.
How the seat cases were made, and what they verify: the layer-1 tests (`roc9-tests-order-NN.l4`) are hand-written from the text and verify the meaning; the seat cases verify the wiring (that a party's plain answers reach the right layer-1 arguments and the answer comes back unchanged).
The first case of most questions is a template by the function's class (all facts in order: `JUST TRUE`; the first fact no: `NOTHING`; a "duty arises" companion with no trigger fact: `JUST FALSE`), generated by `work/seatgen.py`; the cases for the 81 questions whose answer is not that template (a flat negative, a bar lifted by "unless", a number or a choice as the answer) were read from the Rule and written by hand in `work/seatspec.py` before they were run.
The first run of the templates had 60 failures out of 473 assertions and the second 2; each was traced to a template or a default input that did not fit the Rule (a flat negative expected `JUST TRUE`, an exception input set the wrong way, a form number) and none to a wiring error; the questions whose template did not fit were then given hand-written cases read from the Rule (and a few default inputs were corrected in the generator), and the run after the seventh Order had 568 assertions, all satisfied.
The 27 questions added afterwards (the in-time functions, the "barred yet" twins and the functions that decide a requirement from the facts) had 15 template failures on their first run, each a template that did not fit the new function (a bar expected `JUST TRUE` with its exception input set, a requirement function whose default inputs made the exception apply, a count of months left at 0); each of those questions was given hand-written cases read from the Rule, and the final run has 660 assertions, all satisfied.
A template case that passed was not read against the Rule; the layer-1 test of the same function is what was.
The questions carry the layer-1 wording ("is it so that the supporting affidavit states what the Rule requires") and the inputs a plain "Is it true that ...? (yes or no)", not the party's own voice ("Must I ...?", "Did you ...?") that the Commons guide section 2 describes; rewording 350 questions by hand was not done, and the lead may want it.

What is asked, and how:

- Plain inputs only: yes/no, a number, a date, a time of day (hour and minute, turned into the minutes after midnight the modules count), or a choice from a list.
  A MAYBE input (the longer period the Court allowed) is asked as "can you state it?" and the date.
- A record of facts (what the affidavit states, who is served) is asked as one input per field, built inside the question in its `WHERE`; a record field no rule reads gets no placeholder because none exists.
- A choice is a type of the seat with its own constructors (`Under Order 40, rule 2(4): served`) and a wiring function `Under Orders 39 to 45, my choice for <layer-1 type>` that maps it to the layer-1 constructor. The seat's choice types: `Under Orders 39 to 45, who would decide it`; `Under Order 39, rule 2: kind of instrument`; `Under Order 39, rule 2: how the application is made`; `Under Order 40, rule 1(3): how the application is made`; `Under Order 40, rule 2(4): service on a person`; `Under Order 40, rule 2(1): a statement may be required`; `Under Order 40, rule 19(4): whom the copy is served on`; `Under Order 42, rule 19: to whom the debtor pays`; `Under Order 43, rule 3(4): a statement may be required`; `Under Order 44, rule 2(2): a statement may be required`; `Under Order 44, rule 4(5): the step`; `Under Order 44, rule 4(6): a document is required`; `Under Order 44, rule 4(6): a statement may be required`; `Under Order 44, rule 13(2): whom the document is served on`; `Under Order 45, rule 7(2): an item`; `Under Order 45, rule 11: service on a person`.
- No input offers "I am not sure"; a fact the party cannot state is a fact the party has to find before asking. (The `sssa` example in the guide offers it; it was not asked for here.)
- The dates the Rules compute (the last day, the day service is deemed effected, the moment a document is deemed filed) are asked as dates, with the Order that computes them named in the `@desc`.
- Left out of the seat, as definitions and helpers that are not questions: the definitions of "the Act" and "a reference to a section" (Orders 39, 40, 42, 44, 45), "the expression has the same meaning" (O 40 r 1(2)), "a person is a person lacking capacity" and "a person under disability" (O 44 r 1), "a person is a landlord / a tenant / the Sheriff" (O 45 r 1), "an agreement is a settlement" (O 44 r 10(3)), "the amount is a sum paid into Court for the purpose of paragraph (2)" (O 44 r 12(3)), the helper "the statement is required" / "the document is required" (O 40 r 2(1), O 43 r 3(4), O 44 r 2(2) and r 4(6)), the helper "the kind of instrument" (O 39 r 2) and the duplicate gathered `o 39 r 1 the person may exercise the power ...` (F39.1), whose two halves are the questions at O 39 r 1(1) and r 1(2).
- The DEONTIC cycles (O 42 r 5(3) and r 10(2), and the others checked by `#TRACE` in the tests) are not exported: a trace is not a question the Interact form can ask.

## Findings (What the text turns out to say)

1. **O 42 r 27 cites "Rules 6 and 11" for "any amount spent by the claimant"** (line 5936).
   Rule 6 is applications under section 13 or 17 for arrest and attachment before judgment (line 5866), and Rule 11 is release on payment (line 5881); the Rules in which a claimant puts money up are Rule 5 (conveyance and subsistence money, line 5859) and Rule 10 (deposit against costs of attachment, line 5874).
   The citation is followed as printed (`roc9-order-42.l4:1196`); a reader who thinks 5 and 10 were meant would change one input's wording and no branch.
2. **O 44 r 12(1) refers to directions "under Rule 13 (if that Rule applies)"** for dealing with money paid into Court (line 6058).
   Rule 11 of the same Order is the rule that gives directions for dealing with money in Court; Rule 13 is about serving a document on a person under disability.
   Followed as printed (`roc9-order-44.l4:935`); the input is `the apportionment is made when giving directions for dealing with it under Rule 13 (if that Rule applies) or when authorising its payment out of Court`.
3. **O 44 r 4(6)(b) and (c) say "the person proposing to be such litigation representative of the person under disability, being a person lacking capacity, is authorised under the Act"** (lines 5999 and 6001).
   "Being a person lacking capacity" can attach to the proposer or to the person under disability; the authorisation is to conduct proceedings "in the name of the person lacking capacity", which reads more naturally as the second.
   Fork F44.4 records the choice: `o 44 r 4(6)(b) a copy, sealed with the seal of the Court, of the order or other authorisation is required` and `o 44 r 4(6)(c) a certificate in Form 74 is required` take two facts, that the person under disability is a person lacking capacity and that the proposer is authorised under the Act; (b) needs both, (c) is needed unless both hold, so an authorised deputy of a person lacking capacity brings (b) in and takes (c) out, and a minor's representative needs (c) and not (b).
   The sub-paragraphs inside the certificate, (c)(i) and (c)(ii), ask about the same person under disability ("where the person under disability is a person lacking capacity").
4. **Registry duties in Orders 39 and 42 are read as what the party may claim.**
   O 42 r 5(4) and (5) and r 10(3), (4) and (5) put duties on the proper officer, the Registrar and the Sheriff (to give a receipt, apply the sum, return the balance, refund the deposit).
   They are encoded as the party's entitlements (`o 42 r 5(4) the judgment creditor is entitled to a receipt`, `o 42 r 5(5) the judgment creditor is entitled to the return of the balance`, `o 42 r 10(3) the claimant is entitled to a receipt`, `o 42 r 10(4) the claimant is entitled to the return of the balance`, `o 42 r 10(5) the refund of the claimant's deposit arises`), O 42 r 11 as `o 42 r 11 the receipt is given and the moneys are kept as the Rule requires`, and O 39 r 6 as `o 39 r 6 the instrument is to be entered in the register kept in the Registry` (the text names no one who may claim the entry, and the Jural table says so); the Jural table records each of the others as the party's claim against that official, and no seat question is addressed to the official.
5. **O 42 r 5(3) and r 10(2): a deposit repeated every 14 days.**
   "the judgment creditor must before or at the end of the first 14 days of the custody, deposit in the Registry, if the Registrar so requests, a further sum of money ... for the next ensuing 14 days and must continue to make such deposits in advance before or at the end of each successive period of 14 days so long as the debtor remains in custody" (line 5861).
   The cadence ends only with the custody ("so long as the debtor remains in custody"), and the text does not say what happens if a deposit is not made.
   Each period is the same rule again with a fresh start (`roc9-order-42.l4:239`): the function asks about the further sum due in the period the caller names, with the last day of that period as the seam input, and the DEONTIC rule is one cycle.
6. **O 42 r 25: an order continues in force "for one year from and including such date and no longer, but it may be renewed"** (line 5932).
   The year is `Period 12 Months`, with its last day the seam input (Order 3, Rule 3 counts it, "from and including").
   After the year the answer is `JUST FALSE` unless the order was renewed; a renewed order answers `NOTHING`, because how long a renewal lasts is Order 22, Rule 2(6)'s (F42.1).
7. **O 42 r 11 does not say to whom the receipt is given** (line 5881): "the proper officer must give a receipt and keep the moneys in such place as directed by the Sheriff".
   The compliance function asks whether a receipt has been given and whether the moneys are kept where the Sheriff directed (`o 42 r 11 the receipt is given and the moneys are kept as the Rule requires`), and names no recipient because the text names none.
   An earlier build of this function asked only about the moneys and dropped the receipt limb; it was found while writing these notes and fixed, with its tests, before the final run.
8. **Rule 3(2) of Order 45 reads "whereupon the writ is deemed to be issued"** (line 6078).
   Read as "where" (all four steps done: serial number, signature, seal, date), so that the deeming is `JUST TRUE` when all four are done and `JUST FALSE` otherwise; "whereupon" is not one of the words the delegated-definitions ruling names (its (c) says "when", "upon" and "whenever" count as "where"), so this is a reading, not a ruling.
9. **Several rules in Orders 40 and 44 set their own flat negative and then lift it**: O 40 r 10(2) and r 16(2) (no affidavit of fitness or security "unless the Court otherwise orders"), O 44 r 4(1) ("Except as provided by paragraph (3) or (4) or by Rule 5"), O 44 r 9, O 44 r 11(4).
   The flat negative is `JUST FALSE`; once the exception is met the answer is `NOTHING` (QA), not `JUST TRUE`; the layer-1 comment names the exception on each branch.
   For the bars a party asks about directly, a second yes/no function says whether the bar still bites, and answers `JUST FALSE` once the exception is met: `o 39 r 8(2) the bar on applying for an enforcement order applies`, `o 40 r 10(2)` and `o 40 r 16(2) the exemption from an affidavit of fitness and security applies`, `o 40 r 19(6)` and `o 40 r 20(2) the bar on inspecting or taking a copy of the document applies`, `o 41 r 6(1) the Accountant-General is barred from making the payment`, `o 43 r 4(1)(b) the bar on the claimant's costs against the other defendant applies` and `o 43 r 6 the bar on the defendant's evidence applies`.
   The first function of each rule is kept as it was; O 44 r 3(1), r 4(1), r 4(3), r 11(4) and O 41 r 3(2) have the same shape and no second function; one can be added the same way if a user plainly asks.
10. **A window the text states for a bare "must" is its own question.**
   Where a "must" is hedged by a time or an event the text states in its own words ("before any arrest is made", "before proceeding further with the action", "before delivery to the Sheriff"), the compliance function stays `NOTHING` when the thing is not done as required (QB), and a separate function says whether it was done inside the window: `JUST TRUE` inside, `JUST FALSE` outside (ruling (a)), `NOTHING` when it has not been done at all.
   The nine such functions are `o 44 r 5(1)` and `o 44 r 5(2) the application is made within the window the Rule fixes`, `o 42 r 5(1) the deposit is made before the arrest`, `o 42 r 5(2) the deposit is made before the order of commitment is executed`, `o 42 r 7(1) the endorsement is made before delivery to the Sheriff`, `o 42 r 10(1) the deposit is made before the order of attachment is executed`, `o 43 r 2 the endorsement is made before the originating claim is issued`, `o 43 r 5(2) the statement is submitted before the date fixed for the trial` and `o 45 r 9 the notice is given before the officer gives up possession`.
   "As soon as possible" (O 42 r 6(2)), "as soon as the amount recoverable ... has been realised" (O 45 r 7(1)) and "at such time ... as the Court may direct" (O 42 r 21(1)) state no period or window of their own, so they have no such function.

## What the text does not answer (roc9)

Every place where a function answers `NOTHING` because the text is silent carries `NOTHING-kind (silent)` on its branch: 148 branches (`grep -n 'NOTHING-kind (silent)' roc9-order-*.l4`).
The questions below are ones a party might ask and the Rules do not answer, or answer only through a figure or a form the asker has to supply; none is asked of the encoding, and each is listed so that its absence is not read as an oversight.

- **What follows when a bare "must" is not done** (QB, 123 sites): no sentence in Orders 39 to 45 states a consequence, so every compliance function answers `NOTHING`.
- **The figures the Rules leave to the asker**: the rent due and the number of months for which it is due (O 45 r 8(1)); the amount of all fees and costs due in respect of the issue and execution of the writ of distress (O 45 r 8(1), the second amount); the sum that is "sufficient" to defray the cost of bringing the debtor before the Court or to provide for subsistence "at such rate as may be prescribed by the Minister" (O 42 r 5(1), r 5(2)); the days of post (O 41 r 2(3)). The Rules fix which period or what the figure is for; the figure is an input.
- **Form numbers and form contents**: the Rules name Forms (40 and 60 to 78 in this range) and the encoding asks "is it in Form NN" as a yes/no input; what a Form contains is outside the text, and a wrong form number given by the asker is not detected.
- **Whether the Form 73 consent is required** (O 44 r 4(6)(a)): the text lists it without a condition, so there is no function; it is always required.
- **Who may apply under O 40 r 8(1) where the case is one of urgency**: see fork F40.1; the function does not take the applicant.
- **Whether the order made without notice was continued at the hearing** (O 40 r 6(2)): the paragraph says the order has effect "until a day which is to be fixed for the hearing with notice" and says nothing of what the Court does on that day; after it, the paragraph answers `JUST FALSE` and the Court's later order is not modelled.
- **Who receives the receipt** (O 42 r 11) and **the address on an envelope or the form of a notice** where the Rule names only its content: the text does not say.
- **The Court's discretion under Order 3, Rule 2(1)**: not an input (see the reading-a-result notes above).

## What the main binary does not accept

Measured on 1.6.109 (the binary used) and 1.6.108 (the second column of `check.sh`); both give the same counts for every module.

- `NOT` is always bracketed; every `#ASSERT` is one line; no `YMD`; `Date d m y` throughout.
