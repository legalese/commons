## Why this Act, and why scoped

Requirement **REQ-0204** in `subjects/sg/requirements.jsonl`: Tier 3 of the
remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by everyday-life relevance. The requirement asks
what the Act decides for a person or business it applies to; no scenario has asked
a sharper question yet.

Most of the Act constitutes the Corporation and gives it powers. This row takes
what a resident, property owner, builder or boat user meets: permits for facade and
marine-facility works in the resort area and the cessation order (ss 20-22), the
maintenance contribution, who pays it and how it is recovered (ss 24, 25), the
navigation offences (ss 27, 28, 33), oral contracts with the Corporation (s 37(3)),
composition (s 38) and the regulation penalty ceiling (s 40(2)). Not encoded: the
Corporation's constitution, functions, powers and finances (ss 3-18), vehicle
permits and charges (s 11, a pure discretion), the content of a cessation order and
the self-help power (s 22(2), (4), (6)), the Marina Superintendent's powers
(ss 26, 29-32, 34), the Registrar's documents on sale (s 25(4)) and the 1972
transfer (s 41). The prescribed contribution and interest rates are in subsidiary
legislation that was not retrieved.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. A buyer of resort-area property answers for the seller's unpaid contributions

s 24(4) makes "the owner of the immovable property" liable, jointly and severally
with "any person who was liable to pay the same when the contributions became due".
The present owner is therefore liable for arrears that fell due under a predecessor,
while s 24(5) keeps the former owner liable only for what was unpaid when it ceased
to own (and interest). A mortgagee in possession is jointly liable too (s 24(6)).
Asserted.

### 2. Unpaid contributions become a charge, and the Corporation can sell the property

90 days after a written demand, unpaid contributions and interest become a charge on
registration (s 25(1)); the Corporation then has a mortgagee's power of sale
(s 25(2)(a)), exercisable only after a resolution, a Registrar-approved newspaper
notice, six weeks without payment, and with no restraining action pending (s 25(3)).
Paying in full before the sale entitles the owner to a discharge (s 25(5)). Further
contributions falling due after registration rank as if owed at the date of the
charge, "Despite" s 74 of the Land Titles Act 1993 (s 25(8)) (not encoded). "At the
end of 90 days" and "6 weeks" are read as 90 and 42 days having run. Asserted.

### 3. The contribution is on top of property tax, payable without demand

s 24(2), (3): quarterly in advance, "without demand", "in addition to any property
tax", with interest if not paid "within 30 days" of falling due. Only the 30-day
interest trigger is encoded; the rates are prescribed elsewhere. Asserted.

### 4. Changing a facade needs a permit; interior works do not, under this Act

s 20(1) is limited to works "affecting or changing the facade or external
appearance" of a building in the resort area; s 21(1) covers any construction or
alteration of a marine facility there. Breach, and ignoring a cessation order
(s 22(5)), carry up to $5,000 or 6 months, plus $100 a day after conviction. The
Corporation may refuse a permit outright (s 20(2)) with no stated criteria or appeal.
Asserted.

### 5. Boat users carry the burden of their defences

The owner (including a charterer, s 2) or master of a vessel adrift commits an
offence unless they prove it was not their neglect or default (s 28); a master who
ignores a direction must prove peril to the vessel or impracticability (s 33(2)).
Careless or dangerous navigation is $5,000 (s 27), ignoring a direction $2,000
(s 33(1)); none of these carries imprisonment. Asserted.

### 6. Every offence can be compounded for at most $500

s 38 sets one ceiling, $500, for compounding any offence under the Act or its
regulations, including the $5,000 ones. The same $500 caps any oral contract
involving payment by the Corporation (s 37(3)). Asserted.
