### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ea3)

This Act is read from the employee's side.  In these answers:

- **JUST TRUE** means the Act, on the facts given, gives the thing the question names (the Commissioner may decide the dispute; the appeal is in time; the fee is the registration fee).
- **JUST FALSE**, "no", means the Act says no in its own words.  Examples: "No fees other than a registration fee ... are to be charged" (s 118(1)); "No court fees are chargeable in the first instance on any proceedings commenced by an employee ..." (s 126); "The Commissioner must not inquire into or decide a dispute ... unless" (s 115(1A)); "is not bound to act in a formal manner or in accordance with the Evidence Act 1893" (s 119(2)(a)); a party "must not be represented by an advocate or solicitor or a paid agent" (s 120); an appeal made after the 14 days the text fixes (s 117(1)); a "where" or "if" that is not met (s 121(2), s 125(1)).
- **NOTHING**, "not covered", means the Act does not answer on these facts, and it is never a no.  Examples: the Act names who may represent a party and says nothing of anyone else (s 120); it names the courts and the complainants of s 129(1) without saying that no other will do; it labels five officials "relevant authorities" without saying no other is one (s 124(1)(a)); it names three ways of serving a summons and does not say that no other will do (s 137(1)); it points to another Act for who is an "approved mediator" or a "conciliation officer" (s 115(2A)).
- A caller must never turn NOTHING into FALSE.  In the seat, a question answered NOTHING means "the Act does not say"; it does not mean "you may not".

## Findings (what the text turns out to say)

1. The one year of s 115(2) is counted "from the date of lodging a claim under section 119 or the termination of the contract of service": the text does not say which, and for a matter that arose between the two dates the readings give different answers (F1).
2. s 115(3A) sets a $20,000 ceiling on an order for payment for an employee in a managerial or executive position and says nothing of others, for whom s 115(1) says "without limitation of the amount thereof" (line 4627); no section of this range defines "managerial or executive".
3. s 116(3) is drafted as a double negative ("nothing in this subsection relieves ... of ... liability for the balance of the salary up to the amount for which that other person is liable under that section"); the amount is s 65's (F26).
4. The Second Schedule states a registration fee for an employee ($3) and for an employer ($20) and none for a subcontractor for labour, to whom s 115(3) gives a claim (F17).
5. The 24-day limit on the periods of absence of s 76(1)(c)(ii) is in s 76, not in the Fifth Schedule: the Schedule gives the number of days (rounded down to the nearest half day) and s 76 takes "or 24 days, whichever is the lower".
6. The Third Schedule speaks only of an employee on a monthly rate or on piece rates (s 107A); for an hourly, daily or weekly rate it is silent, and layer 1 answers NOTHING.
7. First Schedule item (8) names workmen, so it adds no one who is not already a workman by another limb (F16).
8. s 139(2A) leaves a second conviction more than a year after the first with no ceiling (F12).
9. The offence in s 124(2) states no penalty in this range; PLAN.md names s 112 as the section that supplies one (seam S15), and the caller picks the first or the subsequent conviction.
10. s 126D(2) states its own penalty: a fine not exceeding $5,000 or imprisonment not exceeding 6 months or both.
11. Several "must" duties with a day count are stated as what a party must do (s 119(1)(d) "not less than 3 days before"; s 126B(4) "within the time specified"); the encoding answers whether the party complied and nothing about a consequence the text does not state (the exceptions are the regulative rules, whose `LEST` is the text's own).

## What the main binary does not accept

- `#TRACE` results cannot be asserted: a trace prints FULFILLED or BREACH and no assertion line.  The twelve traces in `ea3-claims.l4` (7) and `ea3-penalties.l4` (5) were read by eye, and their outcomes are identical on 1.6.109 and 1.6.108 (checked by running both and comparing the outcome lines; `check.sh` does not see traces).  Every regulative rule has a decision-function twin that the tests assert.
- A `MAY` with `LEST FULFILLED` (`s 115(3C) rule`, `s 117(1) rule`, `s 126C(1) rule`) ends FULFILLED whether the act is done in time or late; the trace says "no breach", not "the late act is valid".  The question whether it is valid is the decision function's, which for a late power answers JUST FALSE (ruling (a), 2026-11-05; fork F7 overruled).
- `DEONTIC` values are not comparable with `EQUALS` on this build, so no test compares a contract.
- `REFUSE` and `BECAUSE` are not used; "not covered" is NOTHING.
- An empty list written bare before the next field (`... IS LIST` then a new line) is read as a list holding the next word; it is written `(LIST)`.  A `WITH` record inside another `WITH` record needs a helper function.  Arithmetic operands and `JUST` operands are parenthesised when they are field accesses.  Each is a loud failure (a parse error), found and fixed while writing.
