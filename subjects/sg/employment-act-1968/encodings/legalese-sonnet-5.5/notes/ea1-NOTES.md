# NOTES: Employment Act 1968 (Singapore), ea1 (Parts 2 to 6, ss 8 to 66, lines 785-2596 of `EmA1968.txt`)

## What this is, and how it was checked

This is the ea1 part of a three-part encoding of the Employment Act 1968 (Singapore), 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation "version in force from 5/12/2025".
ea1 owns lines 785-2596 of `EmA1968.txt`: Part 2 (contracts of service, ss 8-19), Part 3 (payment of salary, ss 20-34), Part 4 (rest days, hours of work and other conditions of service, ss 35-53), Part 5 (restrictions on the payment of salary to a workman, ss 54-64) and Part 6 (contractors and subcontractors, ss 65-66).
The keel (`ea-core.l4`, `ea-definitions.l4`, lines 1-784, s 2(1) and Part 1) is read-only here and is described in `KEEL-NOTES.md`.
The source text is a plain-text rendering made by a converter script from Singapore Statutes Online; it is not the official text, and nothing here is verified against the official text.
Every `-- src:` line copies a line of that file with `srcrefs.py quote`; every quoted phrase in a `-- FALSE-kind` or `-- NOTHING-kind` comment was checked mechanically against the converted text by `lint.py` (a script of mine in `work/tools/`, which reads the file).

Checked on 2026-11-05 with `l4` 1.6.109 (main, 4c887e0) and the second main build 1.6.108 (main, 2ae7c9b):
- `check.sh` printed 0 errors, 1366 assertions satisfied, 0 failed, 0 refused, on 21 modules (the 18 files below, the two keel modules and the Interpretation Act copy), with the same counts on the 1.6.108 column ("same" on every line). The full output is under "check.sh output" below.
- The 1366 are 860 in the eight test modules and 506 in the seat's case file.
- 29 `#TRACE` queries over the 18 regulative rules were read with `work/tools/traces.py` (which prints FULFILLED or BREACH for each). All 29 give the outcome the rule's own words call for: a duty met within its time is FULFILLED, a duty missed is BREACH, a prohibition done is BREACH (eleven duties have a FULFILLED and a BREACH trace, s 41A(3) has one FULFILLED trace because it has no time, and the six prohibitions have one BREACH trace each). Eleven of the 29 carry a comment beside them naming the expected outcome; the other 18 do not, and for them this is the only check.
- `l4 run` of each of the 18 files shows no `DiagnosticSeverity_Error` and no `DiagnosticSeverity_Warning` on either build (a grep over the output, since `check.sh` counts errors and not warnings).
- `python3 -I srcrefs.py check --refs` and `check --refs --strict`, over the 18 files of mine (not the Interpretation Act copy), report 1113 `src:` lines, 184 runs, 268 locators, 0 problems.
- `lint.py` checked 349 quoted phrases in FALSE-kind and NOTHING-kind comments and found 0 not in the text.
- A positive control: changing one expected value in a copy of the case file (the months of s 31(2), 12 to 13) made exactly one assertion fail and 505 of the 506 pass, so the assertions are evaluated.
- No assertion is meant to fail. Expected values are hand-written from the quoted words of the Act, beside each group; none was copied from the encoding's own output. Where a function disagreed with an expectation, the function was changed: the one case found is the month-end rule of ss 11(1) and 20(2), after `Date 31 2 2026` was seen to normalise to 3 March (see finding 1). The seat's case file restates, at seat level, expectations written from the text; it does not take them from the seat's output. After the rulings of 2026-11-05 every expectation that changed from `NOTHING` to `JUST FALSE` (or the other way) was re-read against the quoted words first and carries a comment saying why.

The seat is an employee, or a workman, working in Singapore: a person who works under a contract of service for an employer, asking how the job can end, what they are owed and by when, what the employer may take off their salary, what their rest days and hours are, what they get on retrenchment or retirement, and what the employer may not do with their pay.
It has no seat for an official: where a provision addresses only an official (the Commissioner's powers, the Minister's recommendations and notifications, the Comptroller of Income Tax), the rule is in layer 1 and the seat asks only what it means to the person (for example whether an order exempts them).

### Forks decided by ea1 (each lists the reading taken and the other reading)

- **F-10(2). The length of notice.** Text: the length "is to be determined by any provision made for the notice in the terms of the contract of service, or, in the absence of such provision, must be in accordance with subsection (3)". Taken: the contract's length governs where it gives one, and s 10(3)'s least applies where it does not (`s 10(2) the length of the notice, in days`). The reading in which a contract term shorter than s 10(3) is void under s 8 is its own function (`s 8 the number of days by which the contract's term on the length of notice is void as shorter than section 10(3)`), so a caller can ask it without changing s 10(2). Other reading: s 10(3) is a floor that overrides a shorter contract term everywhere, so the length of notice is never less than s 10(3)'s.
- **F-10(3). How the length of employment is counted.** Text: "so employed for less than 26 weeks", "2 years or more", "5 years or more". Taken: the length is measured at the day the notice is given; 26 weeks is 182 days after the day employment began; a year is the same day of the month in a later year. A person who began on 1 January 2024 has 26 weeks complete on 1 July 2024 (182 days after 1 January), so a notice on 30 June 2024 is for one day and on 1 July 2024 for one week (tests in `ea1-tests-part2a.l4`). Other reading: the length is measured to the last day of the notice, or 26 weeks is 26 times seven days counting both ends.
- **F-11(1). "the period of the notice" and "less than a month".** Taken: "the period of the notice" is the whole period; a period of N days starting on the day of notice is less than a month if it ends before the same day of the next month; where the next month has no such day (a notice on the 31st), the month ends on the next month's last day. For a monthly-rated employee and a period under a month the sum is the gross rate of pay for one day (the Third Schedule's answer, an input: seam S6) for each day. Other reading: "the period of the notice" is only the part of the notice that is not served; a month is 30 days.

Taken: limb (a) reads as a managerial or executive employee dismissed with notice, or without notice but with payment of salary in lieu, after at least 6 months' service. Other reading: the line begins a separate limb. Whoever has the official text should compare it.

Other reading: for insufficient notice the sum is the s 11(1) sum for the missing days only.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (ea1)

A decision function is asked of one case, for example "is this deduction one that may be made?".
`JUST TRUE` and `JUST FALSE` are answers the Act gives in its own words.
`JUST FALSE` appears only where the Act itself says no: a flat negative ("No contract of service as an employee is enforceable against a person below 18 years of age", "must not", "does not apply"), a definition that says "means" and the case is outside it, or an "unless"/"only if"/"until" clause that the case fails, within the provision that carries it, or (rulings of 2026-11-05) a "where", "when" or "upon" condition the case does not meet, or a period the text fixes in its own words that the case misses.
It is never the answer to "the Act does not say".
`NOTHING` means the Act does not answer on these facts: a list the Act does not close ("includes"), a term the Act does not define, a case the Act is silent on, a fact or an answer that lies outside the sources (another Act, the Gazette, the Minister's substitution, section 112, the Holidays Act), or a grant or prohibition limited by its own wording.
A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`: it means "the Act does not say", and a person who needs a yes or no from it has to ask a lawyer.

Six examples from ea1's range, each a pair that differs in the answer:
- s 12(2). A person aged 17 whose contract of service is not for their benefit: `JUST FALSE` ("No contract of service as an employee is enforceable against a person below 18 years of age"). A person aged 18: `NOTHING`, because the section says nothing about someone who is not below 18.
- s 45. An employee dismissed for redundancy with service of under two years: `JUST FALSE` ("No employee who has been in continuous service ... for less than 2 years is entitled to any retrenchment benefit"). An employee with two years or more: `NOTHING`, because the section says who is not entitled and does not say that anyone else is.
- s 26(1). A deduction that nothing authorises: `JUST FALSE` ("No deduction is to be made ... unless the deduction is authorised by or under any provision of this Act"). A deduction that is authorised: `NOTHING`, because "unless" says when a deduction is not to be made and does not itself say it is allowed; whether it may be made also depends on s 27.
- s 20A(1). A monthly-rated employee who did not complete a whole month because they started after the first day: `JUST TRUE` for the formula. One who did not complete it for none of the listed reasons: `JUST FALSE`, because the "If" is not met. A person who is not paid by the month does not meet the "If" either and is `JUST FALSE`.
- s 66(1). The bar on acting as a contractor or subcontractor without registration, as its own question: before the Minister's notification, `JUST FALSE` ("thereafter" has not begun; ruling (a)); after it, while the person is not registered, `JUST TRUE`; once registered, `JUST FALSE`. "May the person act?" is never answered yes on the strength of the bar alone.
- s 35. A workman on a salary above $4,500 a month: `JUST FALSE` for s 35(a). In the seat, every Part 4 question for such a person answers `NOTHING`, since Part 4 gives no answer about someone it does not reach.

Every branch that answers no carries `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<quote>"` and every branch that answers "not covered" carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)`, with <why> one of silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.


## Seat (ea1)

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (ea1)

The brief's two readings are taken as the status quo: (a) a failed "if", "unless", "only" or "until" is the text's no, clause by clause, and flat negatives are noes; (b) a prohibition or grant limited by its own wording ("who ...", "in the case of ...", "subject to ...") is not a no.
Two rulings of 2026-11-05 took lines out of this list: a "where ..." condition that is not met, and a period the text fixes in its own words that is missed, are noes (`JUST FALSE`) and are no longer tagged marginal; they carry `ruled 2026-11-05` in their comments (59 branches) and are described in the fork register. A scope limit with no "only" stays `NOTHING` (ruling (c)) and, where the branch was tagged, it stays in the table.
A branch that rests on (a) answers `JUST FALSE` and the other reading is `NOTHING`; a branch that rests on (b) answers `NOTHING` and the other reading is `JUST FALSE`.

## Findings

1. **`Date` rolls an impossible day forward, silently.** `Date 31 2 2026` is 3 March 2026 (probed on 1.6.109: `#EVAL Date 31 2 2026` prints `DATE OF 3, 3, 2026`). A month-end rule written as "the same day of the next month" is therefore wrong, with no error, for a period that starts on the 29th, 30th or 31st. ss 11(1) and 20(2) take the earlier of "the same day of the next month" and "the first of the month after the next", and the tests include 31 January to 28 February (within one month) and 31 January to 1 March (not). Silent.
2. **A `MUST` with no `WITHIN` cannot be breached by lapse.** s 41A(3) (display the Commissioner's order) is the only one of the 12 duties written without `WITHIN`, because the text gives it no time; a `#TRACE` of it can be FULFILLED, and there is no deadline to miss, so it has one trace and not a FULFILLED and BREACH pair. The other eleven duties carry a time in the text (s 14(9) "immediately" is `WITHIN 0`) and each has both traces. Silent.
3. **Four offences state no penalty** (ss 19, 34(1) outside ss 21, 22 and 23, 48(4), 66(7)): each says the person "shall be guilty of an offence", and the penalty is in s 112, which is outside this range. Each takes s 112's answers as inputs (seam S15), and the seat's penalty questions for them answer `NOTHING` for everyone. Sections 34(2), 53 and 61 state their own penalties and are answered. Loud in the seat (the answer is `NOTHING`), silent if a reader assumes there is no penalty.
4. **The converter breaks sentences at page footers.** A running footer ("Informal Consolidation - version in force from 5/12/2025" and the page line) sits inside the text of s 48(1) and others, and a quotation that crosses it is not a phrase of the converted text; `lint.py` reported such quotations as "not in the text" until each was split with `...` or reworded. The same converter garbled the lettering of s 14(2A) (fork F-14(2A)) and, per PLAN.md, s 87A(8).
5. **Blank sections.** ss 14(4)-(7A), 27(1)(c) and (g), 30(1), 33(5) and (6), 42, 43, 44, 51, 62 and 65(1C) read "[Deleted ...]" or are blank in this text; they carry the disposition `repealed or blank in this text` and no rule.
6. **Part 4 reaches only the employees s 35 names, and the seat says so by answering `NOTHING`.** The case assertions include a workman above the ceiling, whose Part 4 questions are `NOTHING`. If a lawyer reads Part 4 as reaching everyone, the seat's gate is the one place to change (`if Part 4 applies to me, the yes or no` and `if Part 4 applies to me, the number`).
7. **The month, the week and the day are the text's own.** "Week" in ss 36, 38 and 40 is s 41's seven days from midnight on Sunday (`s 41 the week that holds the day`), not the Interpretation Act's.
