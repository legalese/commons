## Why this Act, and why scoped

This is requirement **REQ-0192** in `subjects/sg/requirements.jsonl`: Tier 3 of the
remaining Singapore Acts, ordered by everyday-life relevance. The requirement asks what
the Act decides for a person or business it applies to; no scenario has asked a sharper
question yet.

Most of the Act constitutes and finances a Board. What reaches a business or person is
narrower: what the Fund may pay for (ss 3, 4), what the Board may demand of an applicant
or grantee (s 24), the offences (ss 13, 17, 25, 26, Third Schedule para 10), the
liability of officers and partners (s 27), composition (s 28), and the member rules that
govern who decides on an application (First Schedule paras 7, 8, 10). Incorporation, the
seal, functions and general powers, staff, immunity, accounts, audit, reports,
dissolution and regulations are not encoded.

## What the Act turns out to say

### 1. The Fund cannot lend

s 4(1)(a) allows "financing (but not loans) or incentives (including grants and
scholarships)"; s 4(1)(aa) allows only grants. A loan fits neither limb, so a business
cannot borrow from the Fund however good the programme. Asserted.

### 2. Two doors, with different locks

Limb (a) admits financing or any incentive, to "any public authority, enterprise,
educational institution or other person (whether in Singapore or elsewhere)", but only
for a programme on productivity enhancement and continuing education. Limb (aa), added
by Act 29 of 2023, needs no such programme, but admits only **grants**, only to an
**enterprise**, and only for investment or economic activity **in Singapore** to develop
the Singapore economy. A scholarship or non-loan financing for investment, with no
productivity programme, gets through neither door; a grant to an individual for a
productivity programme gets through (a). Every payment also needs the Board's
authorisation (s 4(2)). Asserted.

### 3. Obtaining a grant by a misleading statement needs no knowledge, on the face of it

s 26(b) punishes giving information "knowing it to be false in any material particular".
s 26(c), added in 2023, punishes obtaining financing, incentive or grant "by means of any
false or misleading statement" — with no word of knowledge. The encoding follows the
text; whether a court would read in a mental element is not answered by it. $5,000 or
6 months or both. Asserted.

### 4. The Board can question anyone, and money recovered goes back into the Fund

s 24(1) lets the Board require "any person" — not only the applicant — to produce
information to test an applicant's statements or whether a grant "has been properly
applied". s 3(1)(ba) sends money "paid to or recovered by the Board" in connection with
financing or a grant back into the Fund. Possession of papers may be taken on any one of
three grounds (s 24(2)). Asserted.

### 5. Composition is capped at the lower of half the fine or $2,000

s 28(1): so $2,000 for the $5,000 offences (ss 13, 25, 26), $1,000 for secrecy (s 17),
$500 for obstructing the auditor (Third Schedule para 10) — but only for offences
"prescribed as a compoundable offence", and no regulations were retrieved. Asserted.

### 6. A conflicted member's silence does not undo the decision

First Schedule para 8: a member must disclose an interest and stand out of the decision
and the quorum; the interests of a "spouse, parent, son, adopted son, daughter or
adopted daughter" are deemed the member's — a sibling's or grandchild's are not deemed
(they may still be an indirect interest). But para 8(3): "No act or proceedings of the
Board may be questioned" because a member contravened it. An applicant cannot attack a
refusal on that ground. Asserted.

### 7. The continuing-offence fines

Copying the Board's symbol (s 13): $5,000 plus $250 a day after conviction.
Obstructing the auditor: $1,000 plus $250 a day, and no imprisonment. Asserted.
