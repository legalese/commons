# Building (Strata Management) Act 2004 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

It replaces two earlier encodings:

- the encoding moved to `solutions/Litigate.4` on 2026-10-19. Its notes were read
  for scope and pitfalls, among them its forks on "before the 14th day" and on
  counting "30 days after". Its L4 was not copied.
- the first-pass (naive) encoding. Everything that encoding covered is covered
  here. Its hand-checked figures were re-derived from the text: the 75% reduction
  and the s 44 guarantee give the same answers on the same arithmetic.

The Act was enacted as the Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act and
has since been renamed. The module prefix is `bsma`.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, version in force from
1 October 2026, with amendments to Act 15 of 2026 shown (some in force 1 July and
some 1 October 2026). Deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/BSMA2004.txt`.
The deposit's arrangement of sections does not match the body, so the encoding
follows the body's section numbers.

**Checks:**

- One module (`bsma-owner.l4`) and one case file (`bsma-cases-owner.l4`).
- 60 assertions written, 60 satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings.
- A negative control was caught: one expected figure was altered, from 300 to 299.
- The case file is generated by a scratchpad script. Its expected values were
  written by hand from the text, and the script's comments show the arithmetic.

## Seat

**The subsidiary proprietor: the owner of a lot (a flat, shop or other unit) in
a strata development.** For s 63, the seat widens to anyone who occupies the lot,
because that duty binds a lessee or occupier directly.

The owner is the person the Act is applied to. The owner is levied, charged,
sold up, fined, made to undo works, allowed or barred from voting and from the
council, and entered upon. The management corporation, the Commissioner and the
Boards are the other side. Where a provision addresses only them, it is encoded
as what it means for the owner:

- s 31 says when they may enter.
- s 43(3) says when they may sell.
- s 47(2) says by when the owner must be allowed to inspect.
- s 37(4A) says when the owner can be made to undo works.

Decisions are phrased as the owner asks them, for example:

- `What do I need before I make this improvement`
- `Can the management corporation sell my lot`
- `Have I committed the offence of not paying after a written demand`
- `As the buyer, am I liable with the seller for the arrears`
- `Will my vote at the general meeting count`
- `Can I stand for election to the council`
- `When may the management corporation enter my lot`
- `What is the most I can be punished with`

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers or dates:**
  followed as far as the deposit allows. The Act itself fixes every number used
  (30 days, 14th day, 6 weeks, 75%, 3 months, 21 years, 49%, 10 days and the
  fines). No regulations or prescribed by-laws were deposited. The prescribed
  fees are not needed. "Floor area" takes its meaning from the Planning
  (Development) Rules 2008 (s 37(5)), which were not deposited, so it is asked as
  a fact.
- **The actors the text names:** followed. The subsidiary proprietor, mortgagee
  in possession, lessee, occupier, management corporation, Board, first
  mortgagee and Registrar of Titles.
- **A judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally:** followed. These
  are inputs, never guessed:
  - the management corporation's satisfaction under s 37(4) and s 37(4A);
  - a Board's view that a refusal was unreasonable (s 111(b));
  - a general meeting's decision that arrears bear no interest (s 40(6)(b)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts, borrowed terms asked as
  facts:** followed.
  - "Safety equipment" (s 37A(3)) is worked out from what is installed.
  - The council's "threshold number" (s 53(12)) is computed.
  - "Floor area", "nuisance or hazard", "immediate family" and "occupier" are
    asked.
- **One case is the text's natural unit:** followed. One lot, one levy, one
  improvement, one election, one demand.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss:** followed. "Subsidiary
  proprietor", "management corporation" and "share value" are glossed at the head
  of the module. The other terms are glossed beside their types.
- **Operative sections in scope, machinery out:** followed. The following are
  out:
  - the Commissioner;
  - the Boards' constitution and procedure;
  - regulation-making;
  - commencement and transitional provisions.

  For size, many operative provisions are also left out; see below.

## Why this scope

The Act runs to 139 sections and two Schedules, so this encoding keeps to the
provisions an owner most often meets:

- renovating, and the safety-equipment override;
- what by-laws can and cannot control;
- contributions, interest, the written demand and the offence;
- buying or selling a lot that has arrears on it;
- the statutory charge and the power of sale;
- the owner's guarantee of the management corporation's debts;
- voting and the council;
- duties to neighbours;
- entry into the lot;
- notifying a sale;
- the fines an owner can face.

These are not encoded:

- the developer's duties (Parts 4 and 5 Division 1);
- the management corporation's internal duties, funds and accounts;
- meeting procedure and resolution thresholds (s 2(2)-(8), First Schedule
  beyond para 2);
- exclusive-use by-laws and common-property leases (ss 33-34A);
- insurance;
- subsidiary management corporations and managing agents;
- the Boards' orders other than s 111(b);
- additional or varied contributions (ss 41(3)-(8), 108(3)).

Where one of those variations applies, the owner's share answers `NOTHING`, not
a figure.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **Unpaid contributions can cost you the lot.** Under s 43(1), contributions
   still unpaid on the expiry of 30 days after a written demand become, once
   registered, a charge on the lot. The management corporation then has a power
   of sale as if it were a mortgagee. Before selling it needs all of:
   - a special resolution;
   - a notice in an approved daily newspaper;
   - 6 weeks after publication with no payment of the debt, interest, the cost of
     publication and charges;
   - no court action pending to restrain the sale.

   Paying everything, legal costs included, before the sale entitles the owner to
   a discharge (s 43(5)). The encoding counts 30 days and 42 days as complete at
   the end of their last day. A charge is possible on day 31 and a sale on day 43
   after publication. Asserted.

2. **Not paying after a demand is a crime, and "before the 14th day" means by
   the 13th.** Under s 40(10) the fine is up to $10,000, plus up to $100 for every
   day or part of a day still unpaid after conviction. 30 days unpaid after
   conviction gives at most $13,000. The court may also order the arrears paid as
   a fine (s 40(11)). Payment on the 14th day after service is too late.
   Asserted.

3. **Interest runs, and a demand can be served, only after the 30th day.** Under
   s 40(6)(b) and (9), payment on the 30th day is in time. A general meeting may
   determine that arrears bear no interest. Asserted.

4. **A buyer takes the seller's arrears, unless a s 47 certificate says
   otherwise.**
   - s 40(3) makes a new owner jointly and severally liable for contributions
     unpaid when they became owner, "subject to section 47(3)".
   - s 47(3) makes the management corporation's certificate conclusive, in favour
     of a buyer, of what it states.
   - The encoding reads this as protecting a buyer whose certificate stated
     nothing unpaid. That reading is an inference.
   - The seller remains liable only for what was unpaid when they sold (s 40(4)).

   Asserted.

5. **Window grilles and cat mesh override the by-laws; a balcony enclosure needs
   90%.**
   - s 37A lets an owner install safety equipment "despite ... any by-law" that
     prohibits it. That covers grilles and railings against falls, restrictors,
     animal and insect screens, alarms and locks. The owner must install it
     competently, keep it in keeping with the building and repair any damage.
   - Adding floor area needs a 90% resolution, plus planning permission where
     the Planning Act requires it (s 37(1), (2), (2A)).
   - A change to the appearance needs the management corporation's authority,
     which it may give only if satisfied on appearance and structural integrity
     (s 37(4)).
   - The encoding reads s 37A ("despite any other provision of this Act") as
     overriding s 37(1) too. That is an inference.

   Asserted.

6. **Only an appearance refusal can go to a Board under s 111.** s 111(b)
   reaches an unreasonable refusal "to authorise under section 37(4)", so a
   refused floor-area improvement is outside it. Asserted.

7. **The current owner can be made to undo someone else's renovation.**
   - s 37(4A) applies to the owner "whether or not the subsidiary proprietor is
     responsible for the contravention".
   - If the work is not done, the management corporation may do it and recover
     the cost as a debt (s 30(2)(a), (d), (3)).
   - Under s 30(3)(b)(ii) the cost can also be recovered from a later owner of
     the lot.
   - s 43(1)(a) makes a s 30 amount chargeable on the lot like a contribution.

   Asserted.

8. **No by-law can stop you letting, selling or mortgaging.** s 32(9): no by-law
   "is capable of operating" to prohibit or restrict a transfer, lease (which
   includes a tenancy), mortgage or devolution of a lot. By-laws may control the
   keeping of pets, parking and floor coverings (s 32(3)). Asserted.

9. **Owners guarantee the management corporation's lawful debts.** Under s 44,
   each owner's share is what a contribution levied to raise the sum would cost
   them. On a $500,000 debt, 10 of 1,000 shares gives $5,000. Asserted.

10. **Arrears cost you your vote and your council seat.**
    - The voting rule is in the First Schedule (para 2(1)(a)), not the Second. An
      owner may vote only if they have paid all contributions and other moneys.
      A first mortgagee who votes displaces the owner's vote on that proposal
      (para 2(2)). An owner who has ignored a s 65(8) notice cannot vote
      (s 65(9)).
    - A candidate is ineligible for the council if any arrears stand on the lot
      on the 3rd day before the election (s 53(7)).
    - A member can be removed without a general meeting once arrears exceed
      3 months (s 54(2)(a)).

    Asserted.

11. **Other council rules.**
    - A council candidate must be at least 21.
    - Joint owners cannot both stand. This bars the joint owner, not a family
      member they nominate.
    - An undischarged bankrupt may stand only if the bankruptcy is declared in
      writing at nomination.
    - Nobody may serve a third consecutive term as treasurer.
    - Missing 3 consecutive meetings without permission vacates the seat.
    - An owner of several lots can seat nominees only up to the lower of their
      proportional number and 49% of the council, fractions ignored. With 300 of
      1,000 shares and a council of 14 that is 4. With 600 shares it is 6.

    Asserted.

12. **Contributions are cut by 75% until a TOP issues for any lot in the
    building** (s 41(1), (2)). Asserted.

13. **The sale notice is due within 10 days, with no penalty attached.** s 65(2)
    gives the seller 10 days after registration of the transfer to notify the
    management corporation. No penalty is expressly attached. The consequence the
    Act gives is a s 65(8) notice and the loss of the vote (s 65(9)). Asserted.

14. **Entry is at any time in an emergency, otherwise at a reasonable time on
    notice** (s 31(1)), and only for the listed purposes. Obstruction is an
    offence (s 31(2)) with no penalty of its own, so s 128's $10,000 applies.
    Disobeying a Board order can bring up to 5 years' imprisonment
    (s 120(2)). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- **Readings to test.** The s 37A over s 37(1) reading (finding 5) and the
  s 47(3) reading (finding 4) are inferences and have not been tested against
  any decided case or Board decision. No case law was searched.
- **Day counting.** The counting of "30 days after", "6 weeks after" and "before
  the 14th day" is the encoding's reading of the text.
- **Missing law.** The Planning (Development) Rules 2008 ("floor area") and the
  prescribed by-laws were not deposited.
- **Unencoded provisions:**
  - resolution thresholds and meeting procedure;
  - exclusive-use by-laws;
  - the Board's general jurisdiction (s 101) and its other orders;
  - additional or varied contributions;
  - the liability of a mortgagee in possession (s 40(5)).
- **Untested duties.** The regulative rules for the s 40(10) and s 65(2) duties
  type-check but are not exercised by a `#TRACE`.
