# Prevention of Corruption Act 1960 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the `writing-l4-rules`
skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is applied, with the design defaults
ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE` (the stable binary rejects it). No pipeline, no coverage
table, no independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition (amendments up to and including 1 December 2021, in
operation 31 December 2021), the current version as at 01 Oct 2026 on SSO, as deposited at
`../../registers/source-bundle/PCA1960.txt`.

**Checks:** one module (`pca-offences.l4`) and one case file (`pca-cases-offences.l4`):
60 assertions written, 60 satisfied, 0 errors, 0 warnings, with the stable `l4` of VS Code
extension 1.6.98. Expected values were written by hand from the text; the fixtures were
generated by a script in the session scratchpad.

This replaces the encoding moved to `solutions/Litigate.4` on 2026-10-19. That encoding's
notes were read for scope and pitfalls (its readings of s 8, s 7 and s 13 especially); none
of its L4 was copied, and it was written from the prosecutor's seat.

## Seat

**The accused person**: an individual whose dealings with a gratification are in question,
whether as the giver, the receiver, an agent, a public servant or a Member of Parliament, or
who is arrested by a Corrupt Practices Investigation Bureau officer or served with the Public
Prosecutor's notice. The Act is a criminal statute, and the person it is applied to is the one
who did, or is said to have done, the act. Every decision is asked as that person would ask it
(`Have I committed the section 5 offence`, `What is the most I can be punished with`,
`What can the Public Prosecutor make me declare on oath`, `May the officer search me`), and the
facts are ones that person could state, or a finding the court or an officer makes, named as such.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The Act sets all its
  own figures; no instrument is needed. The Penal Code and the Criminal Procedure Code, which
  the Act points at, are not brought in.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the accused, the agent and the principal, the
  Director and special investigators, the Public Prosecutor, the court, a public officer.
- **A judgment handed to someone else, answered conditionally.** Followed. Whether I acted
  "corruptly" (undefined by the Act), whether I proved the contrary under s 8, whether I can
  "satisfactorily account" for wealth (s 24), the officer's reasonable suspicion (s 15), the
  Public Prosecutor's reasonable grounds (s 21(1)(c)), and the court's opinion under ss 35(3)
  and 36(3) are inputs named as such.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts; borrowed terms asked as facts.** Followed
  for "gratification" and "agent". "Public body" is defined but is asked as part of a fact
  (whether the receiver worked for one). "Abets" and "criminal conspiracy" (the Penal Code's)
  are asked as the part I played.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one dealing, one conviction, one notice.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in comments above each rule.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed: Part 2 (appointments and the officers'
  superannuation scheme), s 15A (arms) and s 34 (District Court jurisdiction) are out.

One departure in form, not substance: `gratification` is defined by "includes", so it is not a
closed list. A thing the list does not name gets `NOTHING` from `Is it a gratification`, and an
offence that turns on it gets `not covered by this encoding`, instead of a guess. Section 37
likewise answers only for citizens; a non-citizen abroad gets `not covered by this encoding`.

## Why this scope

The person most meets the corruption offences (ss 5, 6), the presumption that turns a payment
into a bribe (s 8), what a conviction can cost including the penalty on top (ss 5 to 7, 10 to
13, 29 to 31), what the employer can sue for (s 14), the evidence rules that bite on the accused
(ss 23, 24), arrest and search (s 15), the Public Prosecutor's demand for a sworn statement of
property (s 21), and the procedural protections (ss 33, 35, 36, 37). The public officer's duty
to arrest a briber (s 32(2)) is included because it is laid on an individual.

Left out: the elements of ss 10, 12, 26 and 28 (their punishments are encoded); s 16 (bail,
handed to the Criminal Procedure Code); ss 17 to 20 and 22 (investigation, bank books, search
warrants: powers whose effect on the person is mostly the Code's); s 25 (accomplice credit);
s 27 (the duty to give information); ss 35(2), 36(1), (2) and 37(2). The one duty with a deadline
(s 21(2): comply within the time the notice specifies) is written as a regulative rule.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A promise is itself a gratification.** s 2 "gratification" (e) includes "any offer,
   undertaking or promise" of the things in (a) to (d), and it also covers a favour, a job, a
   released debt and an official's forbearance. Offering a job for a favour is a s 5 offence
   if the court finds it corrupt. Asserted.

2. **The s 8 presumption needs a payment, not an offer.** It operates where a gratification
   "has been paid or given to or received by" a government or public-body employee from
   someone who "has or seeks to have any dealing" with that body. It then deems the payment
   corrupt and an inducement "unless the contrary is proved". A contractor who only offered
   money does not face it; a civil servant who took money from one does, and is guilty under
   s 5 without any finding of corruption unless they rebut it. Reading the presumption as
   supplying the purpose limb as well as "corruptly" is an inference. Asserted.

3. **An agent who takes a bribe is guilty even if they could not deliver.** s 9(1): having
   reason to believe or suspect it was offered for an act concerning the principal's affairs is
   enough "notwithstanding that he did not have the power, right or opportunity", did not
   intend to, or did not do it. s 9(2) does the same for the giver. Asserted.

4. **Some offences need no corruption at all.** s 6(c) (a false receipt or account given to,
   or used by, an agent to mislead the principal) needs neither a gratification nor
   "corruptly". s 11 (offering to a Member of Parliament, or a Member accepting) does not say
   "corruptly" either, and neither do ss 10 and 12. Asserted for ss 6(c) and 11.

5. **A government contract raises the prison ceiling, not the fine.** s 7 lifts ss 5 and 6
   (including s 6(c)) from 5 to 7 years where the matter was a contract, a proposed contract
   or a subcontract with the Government or a public body; the fine stays at $100,000. ss 10, 11
   and 12 are at 7 years anyway. The Act prints no minimum. Asserted.

6. **Abetting, attempting and conspiring carry the full punishment.** ss 29 to 31 deem the
   person "to have committed the offence". Asserted.

7. **Taking a bribe costs its value on top of the sentence, and the court has no choice.**
   s 13(1): the court "shall" order a penalty equal to the gratification where it is money or
   can be valued; s 13(2) lets it add up to the total in charges taken into consideration. A
   favour that cannot be valued, or a conviction for giving, attracts none. Treating a
   conviction for accepting as the trigger (not soliciting or agreeing) follows the words
   "committed by the acceptance". Asserted.

8. **An acquittal does not stop the employer suing.** s 14(1): the principal may recover the
   gratification as a civil debt from the agent or the giver, and "no conviction or acquittal"
   bars it. Asserted.

9. **I cannot say it was customary, and unexplained wealth can count against me.** s 23 shuts
   out evidence that such gratification is customary in a trade. s 24 lets wealth out of
   proportion to known income, or an unexplained accretion around the time, corroborate a
   witness, including wealth someone else holds for me on trust or as my gift (s 24(2)).
   Asserted.

10. **Only a woman may search a woman.** s 15(2). Asserted.

11. **A public servant can be made to list the family's property too.** s 21(1)(a) reaches the
    property of "the spouse, sons and daughters" and when each item was acquired; anyone else
    (s 21(1)(c)) lists only their own, and only where the Public Prosecutor has reasonable
    grounds to believe it can assist. An oath of secrecy is no excuse for not complying
    (s 21(2)). Asserted.

12. **A public officer offered a bribe must arrest the giver.** s 32(2): failing to arrest and
    hand the person to the nearest police station, without reasonable excuse, is an offence
    ($5,000 or 6 months). Asserted.

13. **A co-accused who testifies fully is entitled to indemnity.** s 35(3): a certificate that
    bars "all legal proceedings" for the matters examined, if the court thinks the discovery
    true and full. No prosecution without the Public Prosecutor's consent (s 33). The informer
    is protected unless the court finds a wilfully false complaint at trial, or justice requires
    it in another proceeding (s 36(3)). A citizen can be dealt with for an offence abroad as if
    it were done in Singapore (s 37(1)). Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- No case law was read. "Corruptly", which the Act does not define, is an input here, and the
  scope of the s 8 presumption and of s 13 "committed by the acceptance" are readings of the
  words alone.
- The elements of ss 10, 12, 26 and 28, and the investigation powers of ss 17 to 22, are not
  encoded.
- The Penal Code (abetment, conspiracy, ss 161 to 165 and 213 to 215) and the Criminal
  Procedure Code (arrest by police, bail, taking offences into consideration) are not brought
  in; where the Act relies on them, the answer is an input.
- No independent test pass and no review by a lawyer.
