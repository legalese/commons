# Insurance Act 1966 — baseline encoding

**Method: baseline.** Written straight from the deposited text with the
`writing-l4-rules` skill, from the seat of the individual to whom the law is
applied, with the design defaults ruled on 2026-10-18. No `REFUSE`. Checked with
the stable `l4` of VS Code extension 1.6.98. No pipeline, no coverage table, no
independent test pass, no human gate. Not for public use as advice.

This encoding replaces the one moved to `solutions` (Litigate.4) on 2026-10-19.
That encoding's notes were read for scope and pitfalls; none of its L4 was copied.

**Edition:** 2020 Revised Edition, informal consolidation, version in force from
9 March 2025, as deposited at `../../registers/source-bundle/IA1966.txt`.

**Checks:** two modules, two case files; 58 assertions satisfied (20 in
`ia-cases-life-policy.l4`, 38 in `ia-cases-nominations.l4`), 0 errors, 0 warnings.
The case files are generated by a script (scratchpad
`gen_insurance-act-1966_baseline.py`), and the expected values in it were written
by hand from the text.

## Seat

**The policy owner**: the individual who takes out and owns a life policy, or an
accident and health policy on his or her own life, and decides who benefits from it.
For s 147 the seat is the young person who wants to take out insurance.

Why: most of the Act is addressed to insurers, brokers, the Authority and the
courts. The individual meets it at the policy: whether the policy is valid, what it
can pay, what can be deducted, what they can do after 3 years, and whom their money
goes to when they die. The questions are phrased as that person would ask them:
`Is my life policy valid`, `May I revoke my trust nomination`,
`Whom may my insurer pay without probate when I die`.

The 2026-10-18 defaults, one by one:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. The
  Insurance Regulations are not deposited. So the prescribed amount under s 150(2)(b),
  the prescribed manner of nominating, and the surrender-value and paid-up rules are
  inputs (yes/no facts or a number), and the encoding says so where they are used.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the policy owner, nominees, trustees, the
  licensed insurer, executors, proper claimants, creditors, the court and the Authority.
- **A judgment handed to someone else, answered conditionally.** Followed. Proof of
  intent to defraud creditors (s 132(5)) is an input,
  `a court finds the policy was taken and paid for to defraud my creditors`. A court
  order on the order of deaths (s 133(6)) returns `NOTHING`, not a guess.
- **The Act's own definitions worked out, borrowed terms asked.** Followed. "Relevant
  policy" (s 131) and "proper claimant" (s 150(12)-(13)) are computed. "Insurable
  interest", "life policy", "Singapore policy" and "will" are asked as facts. "Life
  policy" and "Singapore policy" are defined in the Act's First Schedule but turn on
  facts about the insurer and residence that this encoding does not reach.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one policy, one nomination, one
  death.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed, in comments beside each
  type and field.
- **Operative sections in, machinery out.** Followed, and narrowed further for the
  seat. See the next section.

## Why this scope

The Act runs to 155 sections and two Schedules. The great bulk (Parts 2 to 4:
licensing, funds, control of insurers, intermediaries, brokers, inspection, failing
insurers, transfers, winding up, offences, administration) governs the regulated
person, not the policy owner. From the owner's seat the provisions met in life are
Part 3C (ss 131-136, nominations) and ss 146-150 (insurable interest, capacity,
deductions, surrender and payment on death). Those are the ones encoded.

Left out within that range: trustee appointment and the variation of terms under a
trust nomination (s 132(9)-(15)), the insurer's register (s 134), co-operative society
policies (s 135), living benefits (s 150(1)), estate duty (s 150(3)-(6)), insurance
on events other than lives (s 151) and fire (s 152). s 133(8)-(9) are subject to s 57
of the Probate and Administration Act 1934, which is not deposited.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **The insurable-interest rule bites only on a licensed insurer's policy.** s 146(1)
   voids "a life policy ... issued by a licensed insurer" without an insurable interest.
   A policy on my own life, my spouse's, my child or ward under 18, or a person I depend
   on, needs no interest. A policy on a stranger is void. The encoding returns
   `s 146 does not reach this policy` for a policy from an unlicensed insurer, or one
   issued before 1967 (s 146(8)). Asserted.
2. **A trustee's policy on a settlor is saved only from 1 March 2009.** s 146(1)(c) and
   (3)-(4) "do not affect policies issued before 1 March 2009" (s 146(9)). The same
   trust policy is valid if issued in 2015 and void if issued in 2005. Asserted.
3. **What the policy pays is capped at the insurable interest, but only where the
   interest is what saves it.** s 146(2) caps policy moneys "where subsection (1)(a)
   applies". *Reading (inference):* the cap applies where the policy stands on
   insurable interest alone, so a 500,000 policy on a business partner worth 200,000 to
   me pays no more than 200,000, and a policy on my own life is not capped. Asserted.
4. **Under 10, consent does not give capacity.** s 147 gives capacity to a person "over
   the age of 10 years" and withholds it from anyone under 16 without a parent's or
   guardian's written consent. So a 9-year-old without consent lacks capacity, but a
   9-year-old with consent is not given capacity by s 147, and the general law decides.
   The encoding says `s 147 does not decide it`. Age is in completed years, and
   reading 10 completed years as "over the age of 10" is an inference. Asserted.
5. **No deduction from my payout without my consent**, except for sums due under the
   policy or a charge on it (s 148(1)). This applies to all Singapore policies and to
   other policies issued from 1967 (s 148(2)). Asserted.
6. **After 3 years I may surrender, stop paying without lapse, or go paid-up**
   (s 149(1)-(3)), counting time under any policy it replaced (s 149(4)). This does not
   apply to an annuity for a term dependent on human life (s 149(5)(a)). The amounts are
   prescribed and not computed here. Asserted for surrender.
7. **A trust nomination is for spouse and children only, and for everything.**
   s 132(2)(a) limits it to "his or her spouse, his or her children ... or any of them".
   s 132(3) and s 133(3) make a nomination invalid unless it disposes of all the moneys.
   A revocable nomination may name anyone (s 133(2)), but not while a trust nomination
   stands unrevoked (s 133(1)). Asserted.
8. **The trust keeps the money from my creditors, except the premiums paid to defraud
   them.** s 132(4) takes trust moneys out of my estate and away from my debts. Under
   s 132(5), creditors who prove intent to defraud recover "a sum equal to the premiums
   so paid". Under a revocable nomination s 132(4) gives no shield, and the encoding
   returns `NOTHING` rather than guess at the general law. Asserted.
9. **Once a nominee has died, revoking a trust nomination may need nobody's
   consent.** s 132(7) requires nominees' consent only "so long as no nominee has died
   before the revocation". With no outside trustee and a nominee dead, (b) names no one,
   and only the prescribed requirements in (c) remain. With an outside trustee, that
   trustee's consent is always enough. Asserted.
10. **A nominee who dies before me drops out and the others share the portion pro
    rata.** s 133(5), A / B × C. Wife 60, son 20, daughter 20: if the son dies first the
    wife takes 75 and the daughter 25. A sole survivor takes the whole of the dead
    nominee's portion. If none survives, the nomination is deemed revoked. The text layer
    prints the formula as a fraction, and A over B was read from its layout. Asserted.
11. **Where the order of deaths is uncertain, the elder is taken to have died first**,
    unless a court orders otherwise (s 133(6)). Asserted.
12. **A nomination beats my will, and my will beats intestacy.** This holds "despite
    anything in the Wills Act 1838, the Intestate Succession Act 1967" (s 133(8)-(9)).
    But a later will that disposes of all death benefits and gives the prescribed
    particulars itself revokes the nomination (s 133(7)(b)). Asserted.
13. **My insurer can pay without probate in four ways, and sometimes in none.** The
    four are s 150(7)-(10): to an outside trustee or the nominees under a notified
    trust nomination, to the nominees under a notified revocable nomination, to the
    executor under a notified will, or to any proper claimant within a prescribed cap.
    *Reading (inference):* s 150(10)(a)-(b) mean "neither a nomination nor such a will"
    and "notice of neither". On that reading, an owner who notified a nomination that
    was later deemed revoked, and made no qualifying will, leaves no one who can be paid
    without probate (unless prescribed circumstances exist). Also an inference: s 150(9)
    is said to be subject to (7) only, and the encoding puts (8) before (9) as well.
    Asserted.
14. **Grandchildren are not proper claimants.** s 150(12) lists widower, widow, parent,
    child, brother, sister, nephew, niece and executor. A child born outside marriage
    counts (s 150(13)). Asserted.
15. **The sharing formula sets no "lesser of".** Read literally, s 150(11) gives each of
    two or more claimants A / B × C of the prescribed amount C. Where the claims total
    less than C, a claim is paid more than its full amount: with C at 150,000, a 20,000
    claim out of 50,000 gets 60,000. The encoding applies the text as written and flags
    it; the prescribed amount used in the cases is illustrative. Asserted.

  First Schedule definitions of "life policy" and "Singapore policy" are not encoded.
