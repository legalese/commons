## Seat

**The accused person**: the individual whose act is measured against an offence in the Code
and who may be convicted and punished under it. The Code is a criminal statute, and the
person it is applied to is the one who did, or is said to have done, the act. Every decision
is asked as that person would ask it (`Have I committed theft`, `Which section punishes the
hurt I caused`, `Is what I did no offence because it was private defence`, `Must the court
imprison me if I am convicted under`), and the facts are ones that person could state about
their own act and state of mind.

The 2026-10-18 defaults:

- **The Act alone, with the instruments that set its numbers.** Followed. No instrument
  sets a number used here. The one Ministerial order the module meets (s 416B(2), a method
  declared not to be remote communication) is not deposited, so it is an input.
- **The actors the text names.** Followed: the accused, the person deceived, threatened or
  hurt, the person in possession, the court, a public servant (s 106A), the Minister
  (s 416B).
- **A judgment handed to someone else, answered conditionally.** Followed. The court's
  findings are inputs named as such: whether a child of 10 or 11 had sufficient maturity of
  understanding (s 83); whether an act is dishonest by the ordinary standards of reasonable
  and honest persons (s 24(b)); whether an advantage is too slight to complain of (s 25
  Explanation 1); grave and sudden provocation within the provisos to Exception 1 of s 300
  (ss 334 to 335); a reasonable opportunity to seek a public authority's protection and more
  harm than was reasonably necessary (s 98); special reasons not to disqualify (s 379A(2)).
- **The Act's own definitions worked out from facts; borrowed terms asked as facts.**
  Followed for "wrongful gain" and "wrongful loss" (s 23), "dishonestly" (s 24),
  "fraudulently" (s 25), "movable property" for theft (s 378 Explanations 1 and 2), "hurt"
  (s 319), "grievous" (s 320), "voluntarily causes hurt" (s 321), "voluntarily causes grievous
  hurt" (s 322), "cheat" (s 415), "cheat by remote communication" (s 416B) and "criminal
  intimidation" (s 503). "Legally entitled", "possession", "valuable security" and "public
  servant" are borrowed or defined elsewhere and asked as facts.
- **One case is the text's natural unit.** Followed: one taking, one dealing, one blow, one
  threat, one act of defence.
- **The Act's own terms, each with a plain gloss.** Followed in the comments above each rule,
  with the statutory words quoted.
- **Operative sections in; machinery out.** Followed, and further narrowed for size (below).

Three readings the text does not settle were taken and are labelled in the module:
"during the space of 20 days" (s 320(h)) is read as 20 days or more; provocation displaces
s 324 as well as ss 323 and 325; and a weapon used to cause grievous hurt that was not
intended falls under s 324 rather than s 323A.

## Why this scope

The Code has over 500 sections, so this baseline keeps to what the individual most meets:
whether age saves them, the mental elements most offences turn on, theft, cheating, hurt,
criminal intimidation, the right of private defence of the body, and what a conviction (or
an attempt) can cost. Left out: every other offence (robbery, extortion, criminal breach of
trust, misappropriation, homicide, sexual offences and the rest), the other general
exceptions (mistake, accident, unsoundness of mind, intoxication, necessity, consent),
private defence of property, abetment, conspiracy and common intention, the enhanced
penalties of ss 73 to 74D, ss 418, 419 and 420A, and jurisdiction (ss 2 to 4B). The Code
lays prohibitions on the individual, not duties with deadlines, so no regulative rule is
written.

## What the Act turns out to say

1. **A child under 10 commits no offence; a child of 10 or 11 commits none unless the court
   finds sufficient maturity of understanding.** ss 82, 83. A 12-year-old has no such
   protection under these sections. Asserted.

2. **"Dishonestly" has two limbs, and the second is the court's standard.** s 24: an intended
   wrongful gain or loss (temporary or permanent), or an act dishonest by the ordinary
   standards of reasonable and honest persons that the person knows is dishonest by those
   standards. Taking back what one is entitled to is not dishonest (s 378 Illustration (i)).
   Asserted.

3. **Theft is about possession, not ownership, and about movable things.** One's own pawned
   watch can be stolen from the pawnee (Illustration (k)); a ring on the high road cannot be
   stolen because no one possesses it (Illustration (g)); a rooted tree becomes movable
   property only once severed (Explanation 1, Illustration (a)). Asserted.

4. **Several theft sections can reach one theft.** s 379 punishes any theft; s 379A (a motor
   vehicle or its part), s 380 (a dwelling or building for custody of property) and s 381 (a
   clerk or servant stealing the employer's property) each carry 7 years with mandatory
   imprisonment, and s 379A adds disqualification from driving unless the court for special
   reasons orders otherwise. Asserted.

5. **Cheating can be fraudulent without being dishonest, and then s 420 does not apply.**
   s 415 accepts "fraudulently or dishonestly"; s 420 requires that the cheat "dishonestly"
   induce the delivery. Cheating by remote communication with a dishonest delivery
   (s 420(2), amended by Act 21 of 2025) carries mandatory caning of at least 6 strokes.
   A promise meant when made and later broken is not cheating (Explanation 2, Illustration
   (g)). Asserted.

6. **Intending grievous hurt is not enough for s 325; causing it is not enough either.**
   s 322 requires both. Meaning to break a jaw but leaving bruises is s 323; a punch meant to
   hurt that causes paralysis is s 323A (5 years, $10,000), not s 325. Hurt that keeps a
   person from ordinary pursuits for 20 days is grievous (s 320(h)). Asserted.

7. **Grave and sudden provocation brings the maxima down sharply.** s 334: 6 months or
   $2,500; s 334A: one year or $7,500; s 335: 6 years or $10,000, against 3, 5 and 10 years
   without it, provided the person meant no hurt to anyone but the provoker. Asserted.

8. **Criminal intimidation is 2 years, or 10 for a threat of death, grievous hurt, fire or a
   serious offence, and anonymity adds 2 more.** ss 506, 507. Asserted.

9. **Private defence of the body can extend to killing only against the assaults s 102
   lists**, never where there was a reasonable chance to seek a public authority's protection
   or where more harm than necessary was done (s 98), and not against a public servant acting
   in good faith whom the person knew to be one, unless death or grievous hurt was feared
   (s 106A). Asserted.

10. **An attempt is punished like the offence, but the court is not bound by a minimum.**
    s 512(2), (3): an attempt at s 420(2) keeps the 10 years but the 6 strokes become
    discretionary; an attempt at an offence punishable with life imprisonment (s 326) is
    20 years with liability to fine or caning (s 512(1)). Whether mandatory imprisonment with
    no stated minimum term is relaxed too is not settled by the text; this encoding keeps it.
    Asserted.

## What would need doing before this is worth anything

- The provisos to Exception 1 of s 300 (which ss 334 to 335 borrow) were not encoded.
- The enhanced penalties (ss 73 to 74D) were not encoded; s 74A alone, which was read, lets
  the court go to twice the maximum against a vulnerable person, so the maxima here are not
  always the most a person can face.
- Where the Code says only "with fine", no maximum is stated in the deposited text; any
  general limit elsewhere in Singapore law was not read.
- The three readings above (20 days, provocation over s 324, s 324 over s 323A) and the
  attempt reading need a lawyer's view.
- No case law on "dishonestly", "grave and sudden provocation" or private defence was
  searched.
- No independent test pass, coverage table or human review.
