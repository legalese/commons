**Design choices that are mine (assumed, not ruled).**
- **A right is the data subject's claim against the controller, and one rule states the relation.**
  A right function takes the two role facts `the person is the data subject` and `the body is the controller` and answers `NOTHING` when either is false (the grant is to the data subject, against the controller, and says nothing of anyone else).
  The controller's duty is the correlative: it is the same function, asked from the controller's seat, and the regulative rule (a `MUST` or a `SHANT`) is traced where the text names the controller and the act.
- **Duty outcome.**
  A duty with a gate ("where", "if", "unless", "where possible") answers `MAYBE Duty outcome` of the core: `the duty does not arise` is the text's own no to a failed gate (periods ruling (b)), `the duty arises and is not met` is the provision's own question.
  A duty with no gate answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN`.
- **Lists of items.**
  The text lists the information item by item (Art 13(1)(a) to (f), Art 13(2)(a) to (f), Art 14(1)(a) to (f), Art 14(2)(a) to (g), Art 15(1)(a) to (h), Art 23(1)(a) to (j), Art 23(2)(a) to (h)).
  Each item is a constructor, and the caller states the items the controller provided and the items the data subject already has; an item with its own "where applicable" or "if any" is required only where the caller says it applies.
- **The points of Art 17(1), 17(3), 18(1) and 22(2)** are one function each, in the pattern of P1's Art 6(1) and Art 9(2): a point answers `JUST TRUE` when it holds and `JUST FALSE` when not, by an inference from the chapeau (marginal reading (a)); the combining function takes the points' answers as MAYBE BOOLEANs.
- **Arguments the text declares irrelevant are kept so the clause has a test, and are not read:** Art 12(1) `the information is addressed specifically to a child` ("in particular"), Art 12(1) second sentence `electronic means are appropriate` ("including, where appropriate"), Art 14(5)(b) `the processing is for archiving ... subject to ... Article 89(1)` ("in particular"), Art 16 `the data subject provides a supplementary statement` ("including"), Art 22(1) `the automated processing includes profiling` ("including").
- **Article 23 is not wired into the rights of Articles 12 to 22** (finding F-3).
- **Names.**
  Every function names the provision it implements and what the provision says, never "is the processing lawful" and never "may I"; a seat question says "Under Article N, as a data subject, am I entitled to ..." or "..., as a controller, must I ...", and its body answers in the same direction.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING

Read in this subject's terms.

- `JUST TRUE` means the Regulation says yes in its own words: the data subject has the right (or the controller the duty), the exception applies, the controller's duty is met in a MAYBE BOOLEAN function, a permission is given.
- `JUST FALSE` means the Regulation itself says no, and only where it does, on the brief's list: a condition the text introduces with "if", "only if", "unless", "except", "provided that" and which fails; a list closed with "only" (Art 18(2)); a flat negative ("shall not apply", "shall not refuse", "shall not be", "shall no longer be processed").
  Three further inferences are used and are listed for a lawyer: a failed "where", "when" or "upon" gate gives `JUST FALSE` (periods ruling (b) and the delegated ruling (c) of 2026-11-05); a failed "if" or "where one of the following applies" within the provision that holds it counts as the text's no, point by point (marginal rule (a)); an unmet "shall" in the provision's own question answers `JUST FALSE` or `the duty arises and is not met` as split site (i).
- `NOTHING` means the Regulation does not answer on these facts: it is silent; the list is not closed; a term is left undefined; the answer lies in another provision (P1's Art 11(2), 6(1)(e) and (f), 9(2); the Article 89(1) conditions); or the provision limits a grant or a duty in its own wording and the case falls outside the limit ("The data subject shall have the right to obtain from the controller" says nothing of another person; "inaccurate personal data" says nothing of accurate data).
- A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`: "the Regulation does not say" is not "the Regulation says no".
- A `Duty outcome` says one of three things: the duty does not arise (a gate failed); the duty arises and is met; the duty arises and is not met.
  `NOTHING` beside it is "not covered".
- **Where a function reads P1's answer** (`Art 11(2) Articles 15 to 20 are disapplied`, `Art 6(1)(f) point (f) applies ...`), `JUST TRUE` and `JUST FALSE` are P1's own answers and `NOTHING` is P1's "not covered"; P2 does not turn a P1 `NOTHING` into either, and answers `NOTHING` unless the other facts already decide the case (for example, a duty that is met whatever the answer).
- A bar is stated as a question about the bar: `Art 21(1) the controller shall no longer process the personal data unless ...` answers `JUST TRUE` when the bar stands and `JUST FALSE` when the "unless" holds.
  An exception is stated the same way: `Art 20(3) the right to data portability does not apply to ...` answers `JUST TRUE` when the right does not apply.
- Where the brief and the Guide differ, the Guide governs.
  The brief's list of markers on which the text says no does not contain "where", "when" or "upon"; the Guide's periods ruling (b) and the delegated ruling (c) of 2026-11-05 add them.
  I followed the Guide, so a failed "Where" or "When" gate answers `JUST FALSE` (or `the duty does not arise` for a duty); that is the one difference I found between the two documents.
  On a function named for a grant the brief and the Guide agree (grant ruling of 2026-11-12): limit 1 gives `NOTHING` for a grant whose "if" gate failed, limit 2 keeps `JUST FALSE` where the text closes the gate itself, limit 3 says to list a "may" gated by "where", "when" or "upon" and not to change its answer; the nine sites are under "Split sites".
  `REFUSE` does not exist on the stable binary: "not covered" is `NOTHING`, asserted.

## Seats

- **A data subject** `[subject]` (required): `P2-seat-subject.l4`, 95 questions, `P2-cases-seat-subject.l4` (432 cases); every function that takes P1's `Art 11(2) Articles 15 to 20 are disapplied` asks two plain facts about Article 11(2) instead of P1's yes, no or not sure, so that input remains nowhere in the seat (finding F-12).
  The data subject is the natural person whose personal data are processed.
  The seat fixes `the person is the data subject` TRUE and asks `the body is the controller`.
  Chapter III is the data subject's main part: every right is asked as "Under Article 15(1), as a data subject, am I entitled to ...".
- **A controller** `[controller]`: `P2-seat-controller.l4`, 95 questions, `P2-cases-seat-controller.l4` (436 cases).
  It asks every layer-1 rule that addresses a controller, grouped by article, as "Under Article 15(1), as a controller, must I ...", "have I ...", "may I ...".
  The seat fixes `the body is the controller` TRUE and asks `the person is the data subject`.
- **A processor** `[processor]`: no seat module and no processor question; the only provision of the range that speaks of a processor is Art 23(1) ("the data controller or processor is subject"), asked from the controller's seat.
- **Never** a seat for a supervisory authority, the Board, the Commission or a lawmaker.
  Article 12(8) (the Commission's power) is `inert` machinery; Article 23 (a lawmaker's power) is asked from the data subject's and the controller's seats for what it means to them.
- Mirror cases: `P2-cases-mirror.l4` puts the same facts to both seats and asserts that the answers are equal (265 assertions over the 95 questions both seats ask); each case is a layer-1 case in which the role facts are TRUE.
- **Direction audit** (the brief asks for it): I listed the 190 seat questions against the result type of the function each calls (a script, then by eye), and read each name against the direction of its function's `JUST TRUE`.
  The audit found and fixed, in both seats, three "must" questions whose body answers a `Duty outcome` (Art 12(3) electronic means, Art 12(7) machine-readable icons, Art 15(3) electronic form: now "has the information been provided ...", "are the icons ... machine-readable") and the Art 21(3) question "may ... no longer be processed", which read as a permission to stop (now "are the personal data barred from being processed ... any longer"); and, in the data subject's seat, the grammar of Art 17(1)(b), (c) and Art 18(1)(a) to (d) ("I withdraws" is now "I withdraw").
  After the fixes a script checks that in each seat no question whose function answers a `Duty outcome` begins with "must", "may", "do", "does" or "is ... barred", that the four NUMBER questions begin "within how many months", and that no other question does; the counts are 19 `Duty outcome`, 4 NUMBER and 72 BOOLEAN questions in each seat (95 in all; re-run after the fix round of 2026-11-26).
  What the direction of each kind is: a "may" question answers `JUST TRUE` for "may"; a "must I" question answers `JUST TRUE` for "must" and is never a `Duty outcome` question; "is the controller barred from refusing" and "am I barred from basing a decision" answer `JUST TRUE` for "barred" (Art 12(2), Art 22(4)); "do paragraphs 1 to 4 not apply because" answers `JUST TRUE` for "do not apply" (Art 14(5), Art 17(3), Art 20(3)); "is my right limited because" answers `JUST TRUE` for "limited" (Art 15(4), Art 20(4)); "does my exercise prejudice my right to erasure" answers `JUST FALSE` for "without prejudice" (Art 20(3)); a `Duty outcome` question asks "have I ..." or "has the controller ..." and its three outcomes are the three answers (`the duty does not arise` is "this question does not arise for you").
  The audit is by name and result type: it does not prove that the body of each question's function answers in that direction, which the layer-1 tests do.
- **Words in a question against its inputs (R4, 2026-11-26).**
  A script printed every seat question whose name contains a time word ("at the latest", "within one month", "before", "prior to", "without undue delay", "at the time", "in any event", "after", "any longer", "at any time") with the names of its inputs, and I read the list.
  One gap: the Art 13(1) and Art 13(2) questions said "at the time I obtain them" and no input carried time, so a controller that provided the information late got `the duty arises and is met`; the decision functions now take `the information was provided at the time when the personal data were obtained` (a controller that provided nothing, because the data subject already has everything, has nothing late), and the questions read "at the time it obtained my personal data from me" with the input behind them.
  Every other hit has its input: Art 12(3) provide ("without undue delay" and "within one month": the two dates and the BOOLEAN), Art 12(3) notice ("within one month": the two dates), Art 13(3) and 14(4) ("before", "prior to": `the information was provided prior to that further processing`), Art 14(3) (the months), Art 18(3) ("before the restriction ... is lifted": `the controller informed the data subject before ...`), Art 21(1) and 21(3) ("after I objected": the fact that the data subject objected, and "any longer" is the bar itself), Art 21(4) ("at the latest at the time of the first communication": `this is done at the latest at the time of the first communication with the data subject`); "at any time" in Art 21(2) describes the right and is not a condition.
- **New questions of the fix round (R10).**
  Per item, in both seats: Art 13(1), Art 13(2), Art 14(1), Art 14(2) ("am I entitled to be given the item of information I name", "must I give the data subject the item of information he or she names"), answered by the new functions `Art 13(1) the controller owes the data subject the item of information` and its three siblings: JUST TRUE the item is owed, JUST FALSE the text says it is not (a failed "where", a condition that fails, "already has", paragraph 5), NOTHING P1's answer is missing.
  Months allowed, in both seats: the Art 12(3) notice of the extension and the Art 12(4) reasons ("within how many months"), each a function with no input that answers 1.
  The seat questions are 95 per seat: the 89 of before, these six, and no question was removed.

## Jural relations

One row per provision that creates a relation, re-read against the text on 2026-11-26 (R5); each row quotes the words that carry the relation.
The terms are Hohfeld's: a **claim** has a **duty** as its correlative, a **privilege** a **no-right**, a **power** a **liability**, an **immunity** a **disability**.
Three kinds of provision recur.
A prohibition ("shall not ...", "shall no longer be processed") is a claim of the person protected against the person bound, with a duty as correlative; the encoded rule states that duty (a decision function, or a SHANT where a party is named).
An exception that lifts a duty ("shall not apply where ...", "unless ...", "may ...") is the privilege of the person who is freed, with the other person's no-right as correlative; it is not an immunity.
An immunity is read only where the text keeps a position out of reach of another's act ("without prejudice to Article 17").
Chapter III is mostly claims: the right of each of Articles 15 to 22 that the text calls a "right to obtain" is the data subject's claim against the controller, whose correlative duty is the controller's, and one layer-1 function states each such claim once.
The data subject's seat asks the claim as "am I entitled to ..." and a duty already due as "has the controller ..."; the controller's seat asks "must I ..." for the claims of Art 15(1), 15(2), 16, 17(1), 18(1), 20(1), 20(2) and 22(1) and for the per-item questions of Art 13 and 14, and "have I ..." for the duties already due in Art 12(1), 12(2), 12(3) to (5), 13(1) to (3), 14(1) to (4), 14(5)(b), 15(3), 17(2), 18(3), 19, 21(4) and 22(3) ("am I processing ... only" for 18(2), "are the icons ... machine-readable" for 12(7)); powers, privileges and limits are asked as "may ...", "is ... barred" and "do paragraphs ... not apply".
A definition, a counting rule and a heading are `none`.

## Findings

- **F-1 The two role facts are plain inputs, not calls of Art 4(1) and Art 4(7).**
  PLAN.md 4.2 says "call `Art 4(1) the information is personal data` (keel)" for Articles 14, 15 and 17.
  Calling the keel in every right would add the nine inputs of `Art 4(1) the person is a data subject` and `Art 4(7) the body is a controller` to every right function (fifteen functions: the three of Art 15(1), Art 15(2), the two of Art 16, Art 17(1), Art 18(1), the two of Art 20(1), Art 20(2), the three of Art 21 and Art 22(1)), and a data subject's seat would ask them each time.
  P2 asks two BOOLEANs, `the person is the data subject` and `the body is the controller`; a caller derives them with the keel (`... EQUALS JUST TRUE`).
  The one function that does call the keel is `Art 19 ...` (`Art 4(9) the body is a recipient`), where who is a recipient is the heart of the rule.
  PLAN.md 4.2's other call in this range, `Art 4(1) the information is personal data`, is neither called nor taken as an input by any `P2-*.l4` module (grep, 0 hits, 2026-11-26): the rights of Articles 15 to 22 are asked about "personal data concerning the data subject" as a fact the caller states (`personal data concerning the data subject are being processed`, `the personal data concerning the data subject are inaccurate`), and whether information is personal data is the caller's earlier question to P1's `Art 4(1)`; the two role facts above are all that F-1 answers (R14).
- **F-2 Art 4(3) and Art 4(4) are not called.**
  PLAN-K2.md 5.1 lists Article 18 and 19 as askers of `Art 4(3) the act is a restriction of processing` and Article 22 as asker of `Art 4(4) the processing is profiling`.
  Article 18 gives a right to "obtain restriction of processing", the act Article 4(3) defines, and asks no question that its definition answers; Article 22(1) says "including profiling", a list that includes, so profiling is an input that no answer reads.
- **F-3 Article 23 is not wired into the rights of Articles 12 to 22.**
  Article 23(1) lets Union or Member State law restrict "the scope of the obligations and rights provided for in Articles 12 to 22 and Article 34".
  A restricting law would have to be an input of every function of Articles 12 to 22; P2 states Article 23 as a rule on its own (`Art 23(1) ...` answers whether a law may restrict, and whether it is necessary and proportionate), and a caller with a restricting law treats the restricted right or duty as not covered.
  A lawyer may prefer one input `a Union or Member State law restricts the right under Article 23(1)` in each right; adding it changes no existing answer when it is `JUST FALSE`.
- **F-4 The "unless" of Art 12(2) second sentence.**
  Read literally, in the cases of Article 11(2) the controller is by definition "able to demonstrate that it is not in a position to identify", so "unless the controller demonstrates that it is not in a position to identify the data subject" would always hold and the bar would never bite.
  P2 reads the demonstration as a fresh one after the data subject's additional information (Art 11(2) second sentence, Art 12(6)): the input `the controller demonstrates ...` is stated for the request in hand (F12-2).
- **F-5 The right to confirmation does not depend on processing; the right to access does.**
  Art 15(1) gives "confirmation as to whether or not personal data ... are being processed" with no condition, and access "where that is the case": `Art 15(1) ... confirmation` is JUST TRUE for a data subject against a controller whether or not data are processed; `Art 15(1) ... access` and the information are JUST FALSE where none are.
- **F-6 Art 17(1)(c) has two limbs with different tests.**
  An objection under Article 21(1) opens erasure only if there are no overriding legitimate grounds; an objection under Article 21(2) (direct marketing) opens it with no such test.
  `Art 17(1)(c)` answers JUST TRUE for an Article 21(2) objection although overriding grounds exist, and says so in its tests.
- **F-7 Art 14(5)(b) attaches two limbs to different conditions.**
  "proves impossible or would involve a disproportionate effort, in particular for processing for archiving ... subject to the conditions and safeguards referred to in Article 89(1) or in so far as the obligation referred to in paragraph 1 ... is likely to render impossible or seriously impair": the text does not say whether the Article 89(1) conditions qualify the second limb; P2 reads the second limb as a ground of its own and the Article 89(1) clause as an example (F14-3).
- **F-8 The text says "within one month" from receipt in Art 12(3) and (4) and "at the latest" from obtaining in Art 14(3).**
  A month is counted with `add months`; a reply on the same day of the next month is timely.
