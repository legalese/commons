## A Directive binds Member States: what an answer means

Article 21 says "This Directive is addressed to the Member States", and the text says "Member States shall ..." throughout.
So every answer in this encoding says what the DIRECTIVE requires or allows, as printed.
It does not say what any Member State's transposing law provides, because no national law is among the sources.
Whether a person may rely on a provision of the Directive directly against a provider, a sender or an authority is not answered by the text, and this encoding does not answer it.
Where the text asks national law to choose (Article 13(3), the choice of options), the choice is an input, and where it is not stated the answer is `NOTHING`.
This is not legal advice.

## Seat

The brief rules that the individual's seat comes first and is required, that further seats are welcome for the other parties the text puts duties on, and that there is never a seat for an official.
Three seats are encoded, each its own module pair over the same layer-1 modules, so the seats cannot disagree about the law.

Article 13 is fully encoded on the sender's seat and the recipient's (the individual's) seat, and `epd-cases-seat-mirror.l4` holds the mirror cases: 50 scenarios, each asserted on the recipient's seat and on the sender's seat with the same expected value (written from the text), and a third assertion that the two seats' answers are equal (150 assertions; 17 question pairs).
Court, authority and Member State powers are asked in the person's own terms and as yes/no questions about what the Directive requires: "Does the Directive require my Member State to give the authority the power to order me to stop?", "May the Member State penalise me for negligently contributing to unsolicited-message infringements?", "Must national law make an unsolicited marketing call or message that is not an automatic call, fax or e-mail not allowed?".
No seat exists for a court, an authority, a Member State or the Commission.

### How the layers are built

Layer 1 (nine modules, `epd-nouns.l4` and `epd-art*.l4`) restates the Directive provision by provision, citing each with `@ref` and quoting the text mechanically with `-- src:N | <text>` lines that `srcrefs.py` checks against the source file.
Each seat module IMPORTs every layer-1 module and defines questions with `@export` and `@desc`; every question calls one layer-1 function with every argument named (`WITH name IS input`), from plain inputs worded as the person is asked.
A seat restates no rule.
Each case module asserts, for each question, answers whose expected value was decided from the text and written beside the assertion with the reason.
The layer-1 modules and the seat modules were generated from templates by scripts that read the source file (those scripts and the templates are in `../work/`, beside the deposit, not in it): the generator copies every quotation out of the file and fails if a quoted slice does not occur on the cited line.

## Seat polarity audit

The defect class: a seat question whose name says one thing and whose `JUST TRUE` / `JUST FALSE` mean the opposite, so that a lay reader takes a wrong answer at face value (the run exits 0).
Method: all 158 seat questions in the three seat modules were compared by the encoder, question by question, against the layer-1 function each calls and what `JUST TRUE` and `JUST FALSE` mean in it (the function name and its branches); every `JUST FALSE` case in the three case modules was then read under its question title; and a scan listed every question that begins "Must" or "Do I need" and is bound to a function named as a check of a fact ("is met", "has ensured", "is free of charge", "is as required"), every "excused" question and every "stop" question not bound to a "prevents", "affects" or "cessation" function.
This is the encoder's own comparison, not an independent reader's; the independent reader's pass that the lead reports found I40 and P11.
Seat questions that say "stop" or "reach" are consistent: their layer-1 functions are "prevents", "affects" and "applies" (`JUST FALSE` = the rule does not stop it, does not apply), so `JUST FALSE` reads as "no".
Mismatches found: 8 (two reported by the lead, 6 found in the audit). Fixed: 3 by swapping the two definite answers in the seat, as the lead says the BGG chapter 3 row does for its Article 83 question, and 5 by rewording the question to the layer-1 polarity (a swap would have turned a statement about a fact into a statement about a duty, or an answer the text does not give).
In the three swapped questions the layer-1 function answers only `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING` (see `epd-tests-art04.l4` and `epd-tests-art05-06.l4`), so the swapped `JUST FALSE` cannot arise on any facts and no case asserts it; each swapped question carries a `POLARITY SWAP` comment above it.
Other mismatches: none found.

## Scope: what is encoded and what is not

Encoded: Articles 1 to 21 as printed in the file, including Articles 14a and 15a, every paragraph, subparagraph, sentence and list limb, every defined term of Article 2, and the footnotes (as inert rows; see the coverage table).
Article 15(1a), added by Directive 2006/24/EC, is encoded as printed (fork F4).
Not encoded: the recitals (not in the file); Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (not among the sources, fork F5); Directive 95/46/EC, Directive 2002/21/EC and Directive 2000/31/EC (the terms they define are inputs, fork F1); Directive (EU) 2018/1972 (fork F2); any Member State's transposing law.
Machinery rows (committee procedure, transposition, review, repeal of Directive 97/66/EC, entry into force, the Commission's implementing measures) are inert for reason 2; see the coverage table.
Nothing is out of scope or deferred: every non-blank line of the file is in a coverage row.

## Jural relations

Hohfeld's correlatives: a claim-right is the other party's duty; a privilege is the other party's no-right; a power is the other party's liability; an immunity is the other party's disability.
Each rule is stated once in layer 1 as a relation between two parties and named neutrally; the seats ask it from each side without restating it.
One row per operative coverage row; a provision that creates no relation (a definition, a scope rule, a rule of construction) is listed as `none`.
Inert rows (headings, banner, footnotes, machinery) create no relation and are listed once at the end.
Hohfeld kinds are the encoder's reading and are not typed in any answer (ruling N2).

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING

Every decision function in layer 1 and every question in a seat answers `JUST TRUE`, `JUST FALSE` or `NOTHING` (a `MAYBE BOOLEAN`).
`JUST TRUE` means the Directive, as printed, says yes to the question asked.
`JUST FALSE` is a "no" in this row: the Directive says no in its own words.
For this Directive that means: a definition in Article 2 that says "means" and is not met; Article 1(3) excluding an activity ("shall not apply"); a condition introduced with "if", "unless", "provided that" or "except" that is not met; Article 13(1)'s "only ... prior consent" where there is no consent and no soft opt-in; a flat negative ("shall not", "must not", "does not apply"); and, by the periods ruling, a period the text fixes ("up to the end of the period", "by 25 May 2011") that has passed and a failed "where", "when", "in case of", "on demand" condition on a duty.
`NOTHING` is "not covered": the Directive does not answer the question asked.
For this Directive that means: the text is silent (a message that is not direct marketing, in Article 13); a limit in the text's own wording is not met (Article 13(5) applies paragraphs 1 and 3 to subscribers who are natural persons, and says nothing of the others except that Member States must protect their legitimate interests; Article 13(2)'s soft opt-in is for electronic mail details); a term is defined elsewhere (the data subject's consent, a service's being "publicly available", the terms Article 2 borrows from Directives 95/46/EC and 2002/21/EC); or the text hands the choice to national law (the options of Article 13(3)).
`NOTHING` is not "no" and not "yes": for example `NOTHING` on whether a company may be sent marketing e-mail does not mean it may be, it means the Directive leaves it to the Member State's protection of the company's legitimate interests.
A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.

## What the text turns out to say

Numbered findings.
A finding ends "Asserted." where an assertion in this folder states it; the file is named.

1. **Direct marketing by electronic mail needs prior consent or the soft opt-in, and nothing else.** Article 13(1) says such use "may be allowed only in respect of subscribers or users who have given their prior consent"; Article 13(2) lets a person who obtained a customer's electronic contact details for electronic mail in the context of a sale use them for direct marketing of its own similar products, provided the customer was clearly and distinctly given a free, easy opportunity to object when the details were collected and on each message; with neither, the whole-message answer is `JUST FALSE`. Asserted (`epd-tests-art13.l4`, the block "no consent, no soft opt-in: not allowed (\"only\")"; the sender seat and the recipient seat give the same answer in `epd-cases-seat-mirror.l4`).
2. **The soft opt-in is for electronic mail only.** The details are "electronic contact details for electronic mail", so with the same facts a fax or an automated call gets no consent-free route; the sender needs consent (fork F15). Asserted (`epd-tests-art13.l4`, "fax and automated calling: paragraph 1 alone").
3. **Article 13(4) applies "in any event".** A direct marketing electronic mail that disguises or conceals the sender, contravenes Article 6 of Directive 2000/31/EC, has no valid address for stop requests, or encourages visits to websites that contravene that Article is prohibited whatever the consent or the soft opt-in. Paragraph 4 is about electronic mail only, so a fax with the same feature is not caught by it. Asserted (`epd-tests-art13.l4`, "paragraph 4 \"in any event\"" and "paragraph 4 is about electronic mail only").
4. **For every other means the Directive hands the choice to national law.** Paragraph 3 says unsolicited communications "are not allowed either without the consent of the subscribers or users concerned or in respect of subscribers or users who do not wish to receive these communications, the choice between these options to be determined by national legislation". The national option is an input. Where it is not stated, the answer is `NOTHING`, except in the overlap case: where the recipient has not consented and does not wish to receive the communication, both options prohibit it in the text's own words, so the answer is `JUST TRUE` (the Directive requires it not to be allowed, and the message may not be sent) whichever option national legislation chose (fork F19; added after review on 2026-11-18, before the option is asked). Where the options differ on the facts (no consent and no objection; consent and an objection) the answer stays `NOTHING`, and consent given with no objection stays `NOTHING` (a limit of a prohibition in its own wording). Asserted (`epd-tests-art13.l4`, "other means (a call with a person): paragraph 3 and the national option" and "national legislation's choice is outside the sources, but the overlap case is decided whichever option was chosen"; `epd-cases-seat-mirror.l4`, mirror scenarios 8, 48, 49 and 50).
5. **Recipients who are not natural persons are left to the Member State.** Paragraph 5 applies paragraphs 1 and 3 to subscribers who are natural persons and asks Member States to protect the legitimate interests of the others sufficiently; for them the whole-message answer is `NOTHING`, except that paragraph 4 still applies. Asserted (`epd-tests-art13.l4`, "a recipient that is not a natural person" and "... but paragraph 4 applies \"in any event\"").
6. **A text message is electronic mail.** Article 2(h) defines electronic mail as "any text, voice, sound or image message sent over a public communications network which can be stored in the network or in the recipient's terminal equipment until it is collected by the recipient"; a short message stored until collected is within it, so Article 13 reaches it. Asserted (`epd-tests-art01-03.l4`, `Art 2(h)`; `epd-cases-seat.l4`, the question "Is this message \"electronic mail\" under the Directive (Art 2(h))?").
7. **Storing or reading information on a device needs consent.** Article 5(3) says the storing of information, or the gaining of access to information already stored, in the terminal equipment of a subscriber or user "is only allowed on condition that the subscriber or user concerned has given his or her consent, having been provided with clear and comprehensive information, in accordance with Directive 95/46/EC"; where the condition is not met the answer is `JUST FALSE`; the exception for the sole purpose of carrying out the transmission, or what is strictly necessary for a service the user explicitly requested, is a separate sentence. Asserted (`epd-tests-art05-06.l4`, `Art 5(3)`).
8. **"Without undue delay" has no number.** Article 4(3) requires notification of a personal data breach "without undue delay" and states no period; the decision function asks the person for the judgment, and the regulative twin takes the deadline in hours as an input (fork F9). Asserted (`epd-tests-art04.l4`, the `#TRACE` outcomes `FULFILLED` and `BREACH`, checked against `-- TRACE-EXPECT:` comments by `check-traces.py`).
9. **The last day of a period named in the text is inside it.** Article 6(2) lets billing processing continue "only up to the end of the period during which the bill may lawfully be challenged or payment pursued"; the end day is permissible and the next day is `JUST FALSE`; Article 15a(1)'s "by 25 May 2011" is read the same way (fork F23). Asserted (`epd-tests-art05-06.l4`, `Art 6(2)`; `epd-tests-art14-21.l4`, `Art 15a(1) third sentence`).
10. **Article 1(3) takes out six things, and the "when" limb limits only the economic well-being of the State.** Activities outside the scope of the Treaty, public security, defence, State security and the State's activities in areas of criminal law are excluded without a further condition; the economic well-being of the State is excluded only "when the activities relate to State security matters" (fork F22). Asserted (`epd-tests-art01-03.l4`, `Art 1(3)`).
11. **Article 15(1a) excludes data required by Directive 2006/24/EC from paragraph 1.** As printed, for that data paragraph 1 "shall not apply"; for other data the paragraph does not say paragraph 1 applies. Whether the reference still has effect is not in the file (fork F4). Asserted (`epd-tests-art14-21.l4`, `Art 15(1a)`).
12. **Most duties in Articles 8 and 9 are conditioned on the service being offered or consent having been obtained.** "Where presentation of calling line identification is offered", "where consent ... has been obtained": where the condition fails, the duty does not bind and the answer is `JUST FALSE` by the periods ruling (b); a lawyer may prefer `NOTHING` (fork F6, marginal readings above). Asserted (`epd-tests-art07-08.l4`, `epd-tests-art09-11.l4`; `epd-cases-seat-provider.l4`).
13. **The Directive gives a person adversely affected a power to sue, and a provider is one.** Article 13(6) gives "any natural or legal person adversely affected by infringements of national provisions adopted pursuant to this Article and therefore having a legitimate interest in the cessation or prohibition of such infringements, including an electronic communications service provider protecting its legitimate business interests" the power to bring legal proceedings; it does not say what the court may order, and Article 15a(2) and (3) give the authority powers to order cessation and to investigate (fork F18). Asserted (`epd-tests-art13.l4`; `epd-cases-seat.l4`, `epd-cases-seat-provider.l4`, `epd-cases-seat-sender.l4`, the questions on Article 13(6)).
14. **Penalties are for Member States to lay down.** Article 15a(1) requires "rules on penalties" that are "effective, proportionate and dissuasive", and the penalties "may be applied to cover the period of any breach, even where the breach has subsequently been rectified"; no amount, no maximum and no period is in the text, so none is encoded. Asserted (`epd-tests-art14-21.l4`, `Art 15a(1)`; `epd-cases-seat-sender.l4`, the question on a penalty for a breach since put right).
15. **The only figures in the text are dates and one review period.** The text states no amount, rate or number of days, hours or months; its only figures are "25 May 2011" (Article 15a(1), encoded), "31 October 2003" (Article 17(1), inert machinery) and "three years after the date referred to in Article 17(1)" (Article 18, inert machinery); every period or amount the text leaves to Member States is an input or `NOTHING`.

## What would need doing before this encoding is worth relying on

1. A lawyer who knows EU electronic communications law should read the marginal-readings table and the fork register and overrule any reading; the two readings the brief marks "for a lawyer" are applied throughout, and the rest are the encoder's.

3. The Directive's terms must be tied to the instruments it borrows from (Directive 95/46/EC, and the later Regulation (EU) 2016/679 that the brief says not to encode; Directive 2002/21/EC and what replaced it, fork F2) and to the national law of the Member State concerned; none is among the sources.

6. The seat questions have not been put to real people; their wording has been checked only against the brief's rule that a question is phrased as the person asks it.
