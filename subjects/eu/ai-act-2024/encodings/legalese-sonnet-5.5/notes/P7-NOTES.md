How each kind of claim in this file was checked:
- Quotations and locators: `python3 -I srcrefs.py check --refs` and `--strict` on all six layer-1 modules, 0 problems (output recorded below).
- Behaviour: `l4 run` on every file with the supplied `check.sh`, on 1.6.110 and, as the second stable build, 1.6.109; the numbers are in the section "What check.sh prints", copied from its output.
- Traces: `#TRACE` outcomes are not counted by `check.sh`, so each one carries a `-- EXPECT` line written from the text, and `traces.py` (in the work folder, which is not shipped) compared the outcome with it on both builds (section "Traces").
- Counts in the coverage, marginal-readings and seat sections were computed by a script from the `.l4` files, not typed.
- Readings of the law (the forks, the marginal readings, the split sites): not verified by a lawyer; each is stated with the other reading.
- Expected values in the tests were written from the text before the run, and none was edited to match what the code computed.

BRIEF.md governs where it differs from inputs/PLAN.md.
The differences met in this part:
- The brief carries the ruling of 2026-11-12 (a function named for a grant answers `NOTHING` when an "if" gate fails, with three limits) and split-site question (iii) (a system saved by Article 111(2)); PLAN section 9 names only split sites (i) and (ii).
  This part follows the brief.
- PLAN seam S7 names `all the conditions laid down in Article 57 or 60 are fulfilled`.
  Article 57 lays down no list of conditions for testing in real world conditions (Article 57(5) says only that sandboxes "may include testing in real world conditions supervised therein"), so this part provides one function, `Art 60(4) all the conditions laid down in Article 60 are fulfilled`.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (P7)

In this part a `JUST FALSE` is the text saying no in its own words, and nothing else.
Four kinds of no occur here.
First, a duty unmet: a function named for what a party has done under a "shall" (for example `Art 65(4)(a) the Member State has ensured that its representative has the relevant competences and powers`) answers `JUST FALSE` when the stated act is not done, with the quoted words of the "shall" in a `FALSE-kind` comment.
Second, a flat negative: "shall not affect", "shall not be interpreted as", "renewable once" (a second renewal is `JUST FALSE`).
Third, a limit the text closes with "only": Article 60(4) "only where all of the following conditions are met", Article 71(4) "accessible only to market surveillance authorities and the Commission, unless", and Article 2(2) "only Article 6(1), Article 60a and Articles 102 to 112 shall apply" (the seam S14).
Fourth, a periods-style gate, read by the periods ruling of 2026-11-05 (rule (b), a "where" condition, with "when" counted as "where") as `JUST FALSE`: "when all of the following conditions are met" on Article 59(1) (split site ii, fork F-59-1-when) and "Where appropriate" on Article 65(6) second sentence (split site ii, fork F-65-6-appropriate).
A `NOTHING` is "not covered": the text does not answer the case.
It is the answer before a provision applies (Articles 57 to 63 and 71 before 2 August 2026; Articles 64 to 70 before 2 August 2025), where a grant or a power is limited by its own wording and the case falls outside it ("may", "shall be authorised", "has the power", with a gate that fails), where a list is open ("in particular", "other actors", "including"), where the text is silent, and where the case needs a thing outside the sources (an implementing act under Article 58(1) or 60(1), an Article 49 registration).
A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`: that a grant's conditions are not met does not say the act is forbidden.
Before a provision applies every function answers `NOTHING`, and the tests assert that on the day before the date of application and on the day itself.
In the seat layer the same values are returned unchanged, and every question says in its `@desc` what the three answers mean.

## What the text turns out to say (numbered findings)

1. Chapter VI (sandboxes, real-world testing, SME measures) and Chapter VIII (the EU database) are in the group General and apply from 2 August 2026, which is after the Omnibus text is in force on 27 July 2026; Chapter VII (governance) is in the group that applies from 2 August 2025.
   So Chapter VI never has an in-force as-enacted reading, and the as-enacted branches of its functions are reached only by calling them directly (tested), while Articles 64(3), 69(2) and 70(8) have a real as-enacted period, 2 August 2025 to 26 July 2026.
2. The deadline for a national sandbox moved from 2 August 2026 (as enacted, base line 1401) to 2 August 2027 (as amended, line 848); a Member State with no sandbox on 3 August 2026 is in breach under the first text and not yet under the second.
   The tests assert both, and the dispatcher gives the amended answer from 2 August 2026.
3. Article 2(2) takes Article 60 away from a high-risk system of Section B products and keeps Article 60a, so a Section B product is tested under Article 60a (a Member State's framework) and not under Article 60(1); the seam is a call to the keel's `Art 2(2)` (S14), and the seat layer asks the route and the two Section B facts.
4. Article 60(4) says the testing may be conducted "only where all of the following conditions are met"; this part reads that as the text's own no (`JUST FALSE`) for the lead and keeps each point (a) to (k) as its own function, with forks on (b) (30 days, tacit approval, authorisation), (c) (three registration routes), (d) (legal representative), (f) (six months and one extension) and (g), (h), (j) (classes of persons).
5. Article 60(4)(b) gives tacit approval only "where the market surveillance authority has not provided an answer within 30 days"; the function takes the 30 days from the date of submission and treats an express approval and an authorisation as the same yes (fork F-60-4-b).
6. Article 63(1) as enacted lets only microenterprises comply with parts of the quality management system in a simplified manner; as amended, "SMEs, including start-ups" may, in both texts "provided that they do not have partner enterprises or linked enterprises" (fork F-63-1).
