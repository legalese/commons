The text is the Publications Office consolidated version of 27.7.2026, which says of itself that it is "meant purely as a documentation tool and has no legal effect": it is a rendering of the Regulation, not the official text, and nothing here is "verified against the official text".
The as-enacted wording of the two replaced passages is quoted from `eu-ai-act-2024-1689.txt`, a mechanical extraction of the Official Journal text.
Where `PLAN.md` and the brief differ, the brief governs.
Two places mattered: the plan's S4 is an input and here it is also a function (Seams); and the plan's section 8 example scopes the applicability question "Under Article 113", while here each applicability question is scoped by the Article of the provision the seat asks about ("Under Article 50, as <role>, does General apply on the date asked", "Under Article 51, as <role>, does Notified bodies, GPAI, governance, penalties and Art 78 apply on the date asked"), because the brief says no two parts may share a name and another part may also ask about those groups.

Commencement IN (assumed, not ruled): the brief says so, the Guide's section 4 would put it out; Articles 111 and 113 and the Omnibus's Article 4 are the keel's, and every function of this part that states a duty, a power or a consequence takes `the date asked` first.

The seat layer: five seats, one pair of files each (`P6-seat-<slug>.l4`, `P6-cases-seat-<slug>.l4`): person (a person affected by an AI system), provider (of an AI system), deployer, rep (an authorised representative of a provider of a general-purpose AI model), gpai (a provider of a general-purpose AI model).
The other four seats (importer, distributor, product manufacturer, notified body) are not addressed by the text of these ranges (see Seats).
A seat question is a thin wrapper: it takes plain inputs with an `@desc`, builds the keel's records in a `WHERE`, calls one layer-1 function and restates no rule.
Its name begins `Under Article N, as <role>,` (or `Under Annex N point M, as <role>,`).
Every seat module and case file is generated from the layer-1 signatures and the layer-1 test assertions by `work/gen_seats.py` (outside the deposit): the expected values of the cases are the ones decided from the text for the layer-1 tests.
There is never a seat for the Commission, the AI Office, the Board or any other official.

Layer 1 is five modules, `P6-art-50`, `P6-art-51-52`, `P6-annex-xi-xiii`, `P6-art-53-54`, `P6-art-55-56`, and their tests `P6-tests-*`.
Each function is named for the provision it implements (`Art 53(1)(b) ...`, `Annex XI Section 1 point 1 ...`), carries the provision's quotation and an `@ref`, and answers `JUST TRUE`, `JUST FALSE` (FALSE-kind) or `NOTHING` (NOTHING-kind); the regulative rules (`PARTY ... MUST ...`) call the decision function and are traced.

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (P6)

The two readings the brief leaves to a lawyer, taken here as the status quo:
(a) a failed "if" (or "where", "unless", "until", "upon", "when") counts as the text's no, clause by clause, within the provision that holds the "if"; flat negatives count as the text saying no.
The other reading of (a): `NOTHING`, because "if A then B" does not itself say "if not A then not B".
(b) a prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not make the text say no outside the limit: `NOTHING`.
The other reading of (b): `JUST FALSE`, because a limit written into the wording is itself the text's no outside it.
Every branch of every layer-1 function that rests on one of them is listed below (52 branches answering `JUST FALSE` on (a), 67 answering `NOTHING` on (b)); the time-axis gates (the group has not begun to apply: 91 branches) are the keel's and are not listed; the bare-"shall" sites are the Guide-95 class F-bare-shall (listed in the fork register), not a lawyer reading.
The readings that cut the other way from a ruling or from the plain words are the forks F-53-4-or (the "or" read as "and"), F-53-4-presumption (reading (b)), F-50-7-if, F-52-1-if and F-56-9-ii (the grant ruling), F-111-3-scope and F-52-5-upon, F-XI-energy-where (split site (ii)).

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (P6)

A `JUST TRUE` is a "yes": the duty, power, presumption or description of the paragraph holds on these facts and this date.
A `JUST FALSE` is the text's own "no", in these terms:
- an exception the text states and the facts meet ("This obligation shall not apply to ..." in Articles 50(1) to (4), 53(2), 54(6)); a condition the text introduces with "where", "if", "unless", "until", "upon" that is not met (Articles 52(1), 52(3), 52(5), 53(4), 56(7), 56(8), Annex XI point 2(e)); a period the text fixes that is missed (the two weeks of Article 52(1), "at the latest at the time of the first interaction" of Article 50(5), 2 May 2025 in Article 56(9), six months in Article 52(5)); an "until" bar that has been reached (Article 53(4), Article 55(2));
- content that the paragraph says a document "shall contain" and the document lacks (the "... is what the point requires" functions, and the Annex functions): the `JUST FALSE` there says "this is not what the point requires", and is the Guide-95 site F-bare-shall.
A `NOTHING` is "not covered":
- the provision does not yet apply on the date asked: Article 50 before 2 August 2026 (group General), Chapter V before 2 August 2025, a provider of a model placed on the market before 2 August 2025 before 2 August 2027 (Article 111(3)), a provider of a synthetic-content system placed before 2 August 2026 before 2 December 2026 as to Article 50(2) (Article 111(4), as amended only); or a legacy high-risk system saved by Article 111(2) (the keel's fork F-111-2-nothing);
- the text limits its own paragraph and the case falls outside the limit: an AI system not intended to interact directly with natural persons (Article 50(1)), a model that is not a general-purpose AI model, a provider established in the Union (Article 54(1): "providers established in third countries"), a presumption outside "to the extent that those standards cover those obligations" (Articles 53(4), 55(2));
- a power the text gives only on an "if" and the "if" fails (Articles 50(7), 52(1), 56(9)): the grant ruling;
- the text does not say (what follows when the Commission accepts the arguments of Article 52(2): finding 4).
A caller never turns `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.
Functions that return a plain `BOOLEAN` (a `FALSE` from them is not the text's no): `Art 50 the group General applies to the AI system on the date asked`, `Art 50 the group General applies on the date asked`, `Art 50 Article 2(2) leaves the paragraph out for the AI system`, `Art 50(2) the date for compliance under Article 111(4) has come`, `Art 50 the AI system falls under Article 50`, `Art 51 the group applies on the date asked`, `Art 51 the time to comply has come for the provider of the model on the date asked`, `Art 51 the general-purpose AI model is classified as a general-purpose AI model with systemic risk under Article 51`.
`Art 50 the AI system falls under Article 50` (the seam S4) answers `FALSE` for "none of the four descriptions fits these facts".
A regulative rule that reads `FULFILLED` on the branch "nothing is owed on these facts" says that no duty arises on these facts; it is not a finding that the party complied.
On the seat side: `JUST TRUE` is "yes", `JUST FALSE` is "no, in the text's own words", `NOTHING` is "not covered", and the applicability question of a seat ("does <group> apply on the date asked?") answers `JUST TRUE` or `JUST FALSE` from the keel's function, so that "not yet applicable" can be asked on its own; every other question of the seat answers `NOTHING` before the group applies.

## Findings: what the text turns out to say

1. **Article 56(9) dates the codes before the Chapter begins to apply.**
   "Codes of practice shall be ready at the latest by 2 May 2025", and Chapter V applies from 2 August 2025 (Article 113, third paragraph, point (b)).
   The function (`Art 56(9) the codes of practice were ready at the latest by 2 May 2025`) is gated by the group and answers `NOTHING` for any date asked before 2 August 2025; from then it judges the date the codes were ready against 2 May 2025 (F-56-9-date).
   The text names no party who must have the codes ready.
2. **Article 56(9), second subparagraph, limb (ii) speaks of an assessment that, as amended, the AI Office no longer makes.**
   "if the AI Office deems it is not adequate following its assessment under paragraph 6": as enacted, paragraph 6 gave the assessment to the AI Office and the Board; as amended it gives it to the Commission.
   The limb is kept as the plain fact it names ("the AI Office deems the code not adequate") in both versions (F-56-9-ii).
3. **"Approved code of practice" has no approval route as amended.**
   Articles 53(4) and 55(2) (the third sentence of each) and Article 89(1) (line 1373, outside these ranges) speak of an "approved code of practice".
   As enacted, Article 56(6) second subparagraph let the Commission approve a code by implementing act; the replacing paragraph 6 does not, and Article 50(7) as amended replaces approval by assessment.
   A search of the whole consolidated text for "general validity", "approve a code" and "approve those codes" finds no line.
   The encoding takes "the provider adheres to an approved code of practice" as a plain input in both versions, and the approval power exists only in its as-enacted function.
4. **Article 52(2) and (3) say what follows a rejection and not what follows an acceptance.**
   Where the Commission rejects the arguments, the model "shall be considered to be a general-purpose AI model with systemic risk" (`Art 52(3) the model is considered ...`); where it accepts them the text does not say that the model is not classified, nor how a model that meets Article 51(1), point (a) and was presumed under Article 51(2) is then treated.
   The encoding has no input for an acceptance: `Art 51(1) the model is classified ...` answers on the facts it is given, so a model above the threshold whose arguments the Commission accepted is still answered `JUST TRUE` (classified).
   That is the reading the text's silence allows, not one it states (F-51-1-a-presumption); a caller that knows of an acceptance must not use the answer.
5. **Article 111(3) delays "the obligations laid down in this Regulation" without saying which are the obligations of a provider of a model.**
   Read as the duties of the provider of the model (F-111-3-scope).
   The Article 54(1) duty to appoint a representative "prior to placing" cannot be met by a model already on the market on 2 August 2025: the encoding gates it by Article 111(3) (`NOTHING` until 2 August 2027) and then reads a model placed on or before the date asked with no representative as a breach.
6. **Articles 53(4) and 55(2), third sentence: the literal "or" contradicts the second sentence.**
   Read literally, a provider "who do[es] not adhere to an approved code of practice or do[es] not comply with a European harmonised standard" must show alternative means; a provider that complies with a harmonised standard but follows no code would be bound, although the second sentence gives it a presumption of conformity.
   The encoding reads the duty as falling on a provider that follows neither route (F-53-4-or; a marginal reading).
7. **Article 50(7) as enacted never governs a date on which Article 50 applies.**
   The group General begins on 2 August 2026, after the Omnibus (27 July 2026); the as-enacted functions answer the counterfactual and are tested directly; the dispatchers answer `NOTHING` before 2 August 2026 on both sides of 27 July 2026.
8. **Article 50(2) has two dates.**
   The group General begins on 2 August 2026 and Article 111(4) (inserted by the Omnibus) gives a provider of a system placed before that day until 2 December 2026; the keel's function carries the second date, and a system placed before 2 August 2026 is `NOTHING` as to Article 50(2) from 2 August to 1 December 2026.
9. **Article 50 puts no time on its duties except in paragraph 5.**
   "At the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure" is read as the time of the duties of paragraphs 1, 3 and 4 (the rules there are `WITHIN 0`, F-50-5-party); paragraph 2 has no time of its own, and its rule has no breach path.
10. **Several duties are duties to aim.**
    Articles 56(2), (4) and (5) say the AI Office (and the Board) "shall aim to ensure"; the encoding states the duty to aim and, separately, whether the codes cover what the paragraph aims at; neither has a consequence in the text.
11. **A threshold is strict and a floor is inclusive.**
    Article 51(2) is "greater than 10^25" (a model at exactly 10^25 floating point operations is not presumed); Annex XIII point (f) is "at least 10 000 registered business users" (10 000 is enough).
    The consolidated text prints `10^25`; the as-enacted extraction flattens it to `1025`, and the literal written here is 10 to the power 25 (`10_000_000_000_000_000_000_000_000`), tested at 10^24 (not presumed), 10^25 (not presumed) and 10^25 + 1 (presumed).

## What the text does not answer

- What follows when the Commission accepts the arguments of Article 52(2) (finding 4).
- Who must have the codes of practice ready by 2 May 2025 (Article 56(9), finding 1), and how an "approved" code is approved as amended (finding 3).
- Who bears the duty of Article 50(5), Article 53(7) and Article 55(3) (forks F-50-5-party, F-53-7-party, F-55-3-party).
- Which provisions are "the obligations laid down in this Regulation" that Article 111(3) delays for a provider of a model (F-111-3-scope).
- What "immediately" means in days (Article 54(5), F-54-5-immediately) and what "without delay" means as a period (Article 52(1), F-52-1-without-delay); both are read, neither is measured by the text.
- The consequence of any breach: the text of these ranges states none (Article 99 is another part's); no rule here has a `LEST` consequence beyond the one the text gives, and no deadline was invented.
