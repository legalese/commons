# Source license

The quoted text in this folder's `.l4` files, and the tables built from it, is taken from `../../registers/source-bundle/eu-ai-act-2024-1689-consolidated-20260727.txt`, a plain-text rendering, made by `xhtml_to_text.py`, of the Publications Office consolidated version of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (CELEX `02024R1689-20260727`, in force from 27 July 2026), and from the Official Journal texts `eu-ai-act-2024-1689.txt` and `eu-omnibus-2026-1744.txt` in the same folder.
It is not the Official Journal text. Only the Official Journal of the European Union is authentic; a consolidated version is a documentation tool with no legal effect.

**Re-use terms, read the way the TFEU row of this repository reads them** (`subjects/eu/tfeu-2007/SOURCE-LICENSE.md`): Commission Decision 2011/833/EU of 12 December 2011 on the reuse of Commission documents makes documents available for re-use for commercial or non-commercial purposes, free of charge and without individual application (Article 4), subject to acknowledging the source and not distorting the original meaning (Article 6(2)).
EUR-Lex's own notice, which says how the Decision applies to Official Journal content, was not read: `eur-lex.europa.eu` answers automated requests with a bot challenge, as that row records. That EUR-Lex's notice applies the Decision to the Official Journal and asks for the acknowledgement below is therefore unverified here.

**Required attribution** (as in the TFEU row): (c) European Union, https://eur-lex.europa.eu

**The subject-level note reads differently and the two are not reconciled.** `../../SOURCE-LICENSE.md` records the Official Journal texts of this subject as UNDETERMINED, because no reuse grant for Official Journal legal texts had been read when they were deposited. This file reads the Commission Decision as covering them, the way the TFEU row does; which reading governs is an open question for the maintainer (listed in NOTES.md, section 6 of the findings), and the subject-level file was not changed.

What this encoding adds:

- Every quotation is a line copied out of a text by script, carrying its line number, so a reader who holds the text beside the code can check it. `srcrefs.py` re-checks each quotation against the text.
- Nothing in this folder claims to be verified against the Official Journal.
- The encoding itself (the code, the tests, the notes) is Apache-2.0 like the rest of this repository; that does not widen the terms of the quoted text.
