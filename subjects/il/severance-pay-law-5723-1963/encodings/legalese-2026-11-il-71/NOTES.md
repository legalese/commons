## 1. What this row is

The row records two items of the Law as typed values and tests them.
Item I1 is paragraph (4) of subsection (ג) of section 11, inserted by Amendment 27 (SH 2338, issue date 27.2.2012).
Its words refer to "the definition ילד referred to in paragraph (2)", and paragraph (2) was replaced by section 90 of the Civil Service Law (SH 2632, issue date 5.4.2017) with words that do not contain ילד.
Item I2 is subsection (ה) of section 11, inserted by Amendment 10 (SH 856, issue date 25.3.1977).
It says "כמשמעותה בסעיף 47 לפקודת מס הכנסה" for a provident fund, and section 67 of the Supervision of Financial Services (Provident Funds) Law (SH 2024, issue date 10.8.2005) replaces exactly those words in section 21 of the same Law, and no instruction on any page deposited here replaces them at subsection 11(ה).
Beside each item the row keeps controls: a test that finds the word or phrase where it is, and the place where the same instruction did its work.
Each item has two limbs: limb (a), the target is repealed, missing or says something else, read from an official instrument; limb (b), the citing words still read so in the latest official instrument read.
Subsection letters are written in Latin letters in identifiers: (ג) is Gimel, (ד) Dalet, (ה) He, (ו) Vav, (א) Alef.
The phrase "כמשמעותה בסעיף 47 לפקודת מס הכנסה" is called P in the modules and below.
English titles of instruments are working translations.

## 2. Seat

The seat is the employee whose resignation subsection 11(ג) or 11(ה) treats as dismissal, in the places where those subsections apply.
The row asks that individual no question, because it records references: there is no input to ask for and no `@export`.

Quotation lines in the modules are of two kinds.
A `src:N` line is expanded by `tools/srcquote.py` from the excerpt; `python3 -I tools/srcquote.py --check <excerpt> *.l4` exits 0.
An `ext:[shNNN-pPPP]` line quotes the Hebrew read from the deposited image of issue NNN, printed page PPP.
`python3 -I tools/hebcheck.py <excerpt> *.l4 NOTES.md` exits 0: every run of Hebrew outside a quotation line is in the excerpt or on an `ext:` line of the row.
That is one change from the il-67 copy of the tool: the `ext:` lines are added to the text a run is looked up in, because a typed string that carries words printed only on an official page is not in the excerpt, and the `ext:` line above it is the quotation it was copied from.
The tool therefore checks that a typed string equals a stretch of a quotation line; it does not check the quotation line against the page.
The quotation lines were compared with the deposited crops by eye.
They were also compared by script with a second typing of the same page text: 48 of the 59 `ext:` lines occur in it.
The other 11 were compared by eye only: pairs (2) and (3) of SH 2459, which were read from the image because the text layer of that page does not find the words the image shows, and the nine footer lines, of which only the Hebrew date and the Gregorian date match the second typing (and the Hebrew dates were converted, section 6).
Three of the 48 lines are of two words or fewer (the Schedule heading and two margin notes), so their match by script is weak and they too were compared by eye.
No text layer of any page was used to check a line of this row.
Where a term is printed between quotation marks, the `ext:` line types the Hebrew gershayim; the consolidation prints a different pair of marks (fork F3).
