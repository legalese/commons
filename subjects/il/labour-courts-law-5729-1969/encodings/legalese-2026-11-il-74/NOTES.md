Status on 2026-11-11: draft, version 0.1.0, target compiler main.

## 1. What this row is

The row records one item of the Law (the Labour Courts Law, 5729-1969, "the Law") as typed values and tests it.
The item is labelled I1: item 1, paragraph (e), of the Third Schedule of the Law.
Its words, as Sefer HaChukim (SH) 2350 prints them on printed page 309, are a decision of a committee for appeals on functional status, under section 230B of the National Insurance Law [Consolidated Version], 5755-1995.
SH 2036 (issue dated 28.11.2005) added section 230B and prints, in its section 2, the period the section covers, in words: decisions given from the commencement day (the end of ten months from publication) to the end of three years from the commencement day.
SH 2232 (issue dated 3.3.2010) adds to the end of that section 2 a period that starts on a printed day, 29 September 2009 (printed with its Hebrew date, 11 Tishrei 5770), and runs to the end of 30 months from that day.
That is the last period the instruments read print for section 230B, and its end day is not printed.
By computation the end is 28.3.2012 if the start day is day 1 of the 30 months, and 29.3.2012 if the months are counted from the day after the start day; the row types both and chooses neither.
SH 2350 prints the item in its section 16, and its issue is dated 1.4.2012, which is 4 days after 28.3.2012 and 3 days after 29.3.2012 (computations).
Section 18(a) of SH 2350 prints "60 days from its publication" and no calendar date; 1.4.2012 plus 60 days is 31.5.2012 by computation, the publication day not counted (30.5.2012 and 1.6.2012 are the same count with the publication day counted, and with one day more).
The pages print the period of section 230B in terms of decisions of the Institute given in the period, and item 1(e) in terms of decisions of the committee; the row compares dates only and does not compare those two kinds of decision.
The row types each instrument as Printed, and every date it takes from a footer as an issue date; it does not say what follows from the dates, and it says nothing about any other status of an instrument or a provision.
Each item has two limbs.
Limb (a): the target is repealed, missing or says something else, read from an official instrument; here, the last period the instruments read print for the cited section ends, by computation, before the day the citing instrument was published.
Limb (b): the citing words still read so in the latest official instrument read; here, no later instrument read that prints words of the Third Schedule names item 1.

## 2. Seat

The seat is the person aggrieved by a decision of the National Insurance Institute on their functional status, the person to whom paragraph (a) of section 230B is written.
The row asks that person no question, because it records a reference: there is no input to ask for and no `@export`.

Quotation lines in the modules are of three kinds.
A `src:N` line is expanded by `tools/srcquote.py` from the excerpt, and N is the line number in the whole page, which the excerpt's header lists; `python3 -I tools/srcquote.py --check <excerpt> *.l4` exits 0.
An `ext:[shNNNN-pPPP]` line quotes the Hebrew read from the deposited image of issue NNNN, printed page PPP, and may be skipped by `tools/hebcheck.py` only because it is a quotation line.
A `tr:[tag] | LATIN` line gives the Latin form of words quoted on the lines above it, and `tools/translit.py` checks it: the Latin must occur in the transliteration of the quoted Hebrew (by the rules in that file: the letters A to D after a digit, the letters (a) to (e) in brackets), and must occur as a string literal in the code; `python3 -I tools/translit.py *.l4` exits 0 (4 tr lines checked, 0 problems).
`translit.py` was copied from row il-70 with one change: the letter he, alone in brackets, is written (e), because paragraph (e) is the paragraph the item is.
Hebrew is typed in code in two strings only, the citing words and the title of the National Insurance Law, and only because the same words are in the deposited excerpt; `python3 -I tools/hebcheck.py <excerpt> *.l4 NOTES.md` exits 0.
This file carries Hebrew only on the quotation lines above.

The Hebrew of every `ext:` line was read from the deposited image, and then compared with the text layer of the PDF page as a bag of words (the word order of these layers is reversed or scrambled, so only the words and the numbers could be compared).
36 `ext:` lines were compared; 33 matched word for word and number for number.
The three that did not match fully, each for a reason seen in the layer: the footer of SH 2647 (the layer holds the footer line with its letters reversed, so it was read from the image only), the number 1995 in section 1 of SH 2036 (the layer glues the footnote number to it, as 11995), and the word for "as to" in paragraph (e) on printed page 309 (the layer glues it to the next word, as one token; the double-yod spelling is in the glued token).
The footer of SH 2036 and the footers of the other issues matched the layer, except that of SH 2647.
Words of one or no letter (the labels (a) to (e) and the single letters after a number) were not compared.

Hebrew calendar: each Hebrew date printed beside a Gregorian date on a footer, and the one printed in the period of SH 2232, was compared with a Hebrew-calendar routine on 2026-11-11, after the control 30 Kislev 5785 = 31.12.2024 passed: 26 Cheshvan 5766 = 28.11.2005 (SH 2036); 11 Tishrei 5770 = 29.9.2009 (SH 2232, the period); 17 Adar 5770 = 3.3.2010 (SH 2232); 9 Nisan 5772 = 1.4.2012 (SH 2350); 1 Kislev 5773 = 15.11.2012 (SH 2387); 9 Tammuz 5777 = 3.7.2017 (SH 2647).
All six pairs agree with the Gregorian date the page prints.
The day, month and year of each Hebrew date were converted by hand from the Hebrew letters and words the page prints (the letters of the year on the footer of SH 2036 give 5766, and so on) before the routine was run, so the comparison checks the page's own pair of dates and does not check that conversion.

## 9. Expected red

Four assertions in `lb74-tests-red.l4` fail on purpose, and `check.sh` and `encoding.json` declare four.
R1, first convention: the last period printed ends on or after 1.4.2012, the issue date of SH 2350, when the start day is day 1 of the months; the last day is 28.3.2012, which is 4 days before.
R1, second convention: the same comparison when the months are counted from the day after the start day; the last day is 29.3.2012, which is 3 days before.
R2, first convention: the last period printed ends on or after 31.5.2012, the day the commencement counts to (computed from the printed 60 days); the last day is 28.3.2012, which is 64 days before.
R2, second convention: the same comparison; the last day is 29.3.2012, which is 63 days before.
Each is false, because the printed start day and the printed number of months give a last day before the day it is compared with, and the readings module pins each comparison negated.
If a later instrument is read that prints a longer period for section 230B, the assertions it changes turn green and the declared count must go down with it.
