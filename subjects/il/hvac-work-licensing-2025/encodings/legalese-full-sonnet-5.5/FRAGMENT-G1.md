### F1. Whether "up to 18" and "up to 70" kilowatts include the limit

- Readings: (a) taken: the limit is inside, so grade 1 covers a system of exactly 18 kW and grade 2 one of exactly 70 kW; (b) the limit is outside.
- Licensing text: "up to" in the definitions of grade 1 and grade 2 (src:59, src:60) in its ordinary sense includes the limit; s.9(a)(1) (src:104) speaks of systems "over 70 kW" as those beyond grade 2, so 70 kW is not "over" and is a grade 2 system; IL s.8 settles "until" for time and quotations only, so it does not settle a capacity.
- Callable alternative: `s 2 — the licence kind covers the cooling system, "up to" read as excluding the limit` and `licence kinds that cover a cooling system, "up to" read as excluding the limit`.
- Tests: six assertions on the alternative in `g1-tests-interpretation`; both sides of both limits (17.99, 18, 18.01, 69.99, 70, 70.01) on the reading taken in `g1-tests-interpretation` and in s.3(a) cases s02, s03, s04, s05 of `g1-tests-licensing`; red: two assertions.
- Changes an answer only for a system of exactly 18 kW or exactly 70 kW.

### F2. A cooling capacity that is not above zero (*assumed, not ruled*)

- Readings: (a) taken: a named refusal, `a cooling capacity that is not above zero cannot be matched to a licence kind`, wherever a capacity limit is read (grade 1 and grade 2, and so the list function); (b) read the limit literally, so that 0 and a negative number are "up to 18" and grade 1 covers them.
- Licensing text: none; s.2 says only "up to 18" and "up to 70" (src:59, src:60); a system whose purpose is to remove heat (src:48) has a positive capacity, and a recorded 0 is most likely "not recorded".
- No callable alternative: reading (b) would be a default for a case the source does not answer.
- Grade 3 reads no limit ("any system", src:61) and does not refuse.
- Tests: `#ASSERT REFUSED` for 0 and minus 5 on the list function and on the per-kind function (`g1-tests-interpretation`), and in the s.3(a) outcome (cases s34, s35 of `g1-tests-licensing`).

### F3. Oil work, installation or maintenance (trap T8)

- A.1(5) (src:505) lists "replacing or adding oil" among the installation works under grade 1 (and so 2 and 3); B.3(3) (src:559) lists "adding or replacing oil in a cooling system" among the maintenance works under grade 3 only.
- The same physical act is licensed differently by the Part it falls under, and the Law defines neither "installation" nor "maintenance" nor where one ends (commissioning, A.1(7), is installation).
- Taken: the Part is part of the classification the caller supplies, so the decision takes the activity, and the activity carries its Part (`the job kind of the activity`).
- Discriminating case: a grade 1 licensee adds oil on a 10 kW system while installing it (A.1(5)): permitted (case s10); the same act on a service visit (B.3(3)): forbidden, no licence of the appropriate kind, Third Schedule item (2) (case s09); a grade 2 licensee on 50 kW: forbidden (s11); a grade 3 licensee: permitted (s12).
- Callable: both activities; `licence kinds that may do an act described by any of the activities` / `all of the activities` for an act that is both (F4).

### F4. B.1(3) and B.3(2) both reach components (trap T9)

- B.1(3) (src:536): grade 1 (and so 2 and 3) may check, replace, change, dismantle or repair "mechanical and electrical components" of a cooling system; B.3(2) (src:558): only grade 3 may do the same to "a refrigerant system and its electrical and mechanical components".
- Whether "a refrigerant system" is the whole refrigerant circuit, and so whether a compressor replacement is B.1(3) or B.3(2), is not stated.
- A textual oddity: "its" is masculine in the Hebrew, which agrees with "refrigerant" (masculine) and not with "system" (feminine); the gazette prints it so (p. 201); read as a drafting slip, not as a different term.
- Note: grade 3 has row 1's list, so B.3(2) gives grade 3 nothing that B.1(3) does not already give it for components; the item can only be meant to reach something B.1(3) does not, the refrigerant system as a whole, which is why the classification is left to the caller.
- Taken: the decision takes ONE activity; an act that both items describe is read, with Column A ("the kind appropriate ... as specified in column A", src:71), as permitted to a kind the Schedule lists the act under in at least one of the items (the union): `licence kinds that may do an act described by any of the activities`.
- Alternative, callable: the act needs a kind listed under EVERY item that describes it: `licence kinds that may do an act described by all of the activities`.
- Tests: compressor replacement as `[B.1(3), B.3(2)]`: any gives all three kinds, all gives grade 3 (`g1-tests-first-schedule`); grade 1 doing B.1(3) is permitted and doing B.3(2) is forbidden (cases s14, s16, s15 of `g1-tests-licensing`); red: one assertion that a grade 1 compressor replacement, classed B.1(3), is forbidden.

### F5. "Integral", and items A.1(11), A.1(12), B.1(14) (trap T10)

- The defined term is spelled with one letter (src:486); items A.1(12) (src:512) and B.1(14) (src:547) spell the word with an extra letter; the gazette prints the extra letter too (pp. 199 and 200), so the misspelling is official.
- A lookup by string finds nothing; the encoding keys the three items on a FACT of the act: `Facts about an act for the limits of First Schedule items` has `the parts connected are integral control and monitoring accessories only` and `the electrical work is done in the integral electrical system only`.
- Taken: an act that does not stay inside the "only" limit of the item it is classified under is not work under that item (`s 2 — the act is work on a cooling system` is false; s.3(a) does not apply): electrical work outside the integral system belongs to the Electricity Law (ss.58 and 61, G7), and accessories that are not integral are not what A.1(11) lists.
- Whether the same act is then work under ANOTHER item (A.1(6) lists installing or connecting "control components" without the word integral) is the classification the caller makes (open question 3).
- Tests: every activity against the four fact combinations (`g1-tests-first-schedule`); s.2 and s.3(a) cases s21 to s26.

### F6. "Monitoring, warning and control": any one function, or all three

- The definition of integral control and monitoring accessories (src:472) lists three functions after "serve": monitoring, warning, control.
- Readings: (a) taken: a permanently connected part is an accessory if it serves at least one of the three (a sensor that only monitors qualifies); (b) it must serve all three.
- Callable: `the part is an integral control and monitoring accessory, serving all three functions`.
- Tests: six fixed-part fixtures on both readings (`g1-tests-first-schedule`); red: one assertion that a monitoring-only sensor is not an accessory.

### F7. The parenthesis of "integral electrical system"

- The definition (src:486) is followed by a parenthesis: part of the dedicated panel, supplied by the manufacturer or to its requirements, or intended for it.
- Readings: (a) taken: the parenthesis is part of the definition: a dedicated system that is part of the cooling system, part of the dedicated panel, and supplied by the manufacturer or supplied to its requirements or intended for it; (b) it only illustrates: a dedicated system that is part of a cooling system is enough.
- Callable: `the electrical system is the integral electrical system of the cooling system, the parenthesis read as an illustration`.
- Tests: six fixtures on both readings (`g1-tests-first-schedule`); red: one assertion that a dedicated system outside the dedicated panel is integral.

### F8. A licence that is not in force: "no licence at all" or "not of the appropriate kind"

- A person whose only licence is suspended, revoked or lapsed (not in `licence kinds in force on the day`) does the work: Third Schedule item (1) says "no licence at all", item (2) "no licence of the appropriate kind".
- Readings: (a) taken: a licence that is not in force is no licence: item (1); (b) the person holds a licence, but not one that is in force and appropriate: item (2).
- Licensing text: s.3(a) says "unless a licence ... is in his hand" (src:71); the Law does not say whether a suspended licence is "in his hand".
- Callable: `the outcome of section 3(a) for the work, a licence that is not in force counting as a licence held` and, for s.4, `the outcome of section 4 for the self-description, a licence that is not in force counting as a licence held`; the input flag `a licence of the person is not in force`.
- Tests: cases s07 and s08 on both functions (`g1-tests-licensing`); self-descriptions f01 and f09; red: one assertion.

### F9. Work outside the conditions of a licence of the right kind (trap T12)

- s.3(a) (src:71) forbids work except "in accordance with the conditions of his licence and the provisions under this Law"; the Third Schedule prices only no licence (item 1) and no licence of the appropriate kind (item 2).
- Readings: (a) taken: the work is forbidden and no item prices it: `the listed violation committed under section 3(a)` is NOTHING; (b) it is counted as work without the appropriate kind: item (2).
- Callable: `the listed violation committed under section 3(a), work outside the conditions counted as work without the appropriate kind`.
- Tests: cases s29 to s31 and s33 on both (`g1-tests-licensing`); red: one assertion.
- A restriction under s.10(a) or a direction under s.10(c) reaches this rule as the flag `the licensee is restricted from the work`, so it falls here and not under item (2).

### F10. Whether the duties or conditions of an exemption order are conditions of the exemption

- s.3(b) (src:72): "if the Minister fixed an exemption, he may fix in the order duties or conditions that apply to a person who does work in the classes of systems for which the exemption was given".
- Readings: (a) taken: the exemption lifts the s.3(a) licence requirement and stands; the order's duties bind as the order says; (b) the duties or conditions are conditions of the exemption: a person who does not meet them is not exempt.
- Callable: `the outcome of section 3(a) for the work, the exemption subject to the conditions of the order`, with the input `the duties or conditions of the exemption order were met`.
- Tests: cases s28, s01, s36 on it (`g1-tests-licensing`); red: one assertion.
- No order is in the sources (IP14), so the content of any order is an input.

### F11. A consultation of the Council whose advice was not validly given (IP7)

- s.3(b) and s.10(a) to (d) say "after consulting the Council".
- Readings: (a) taken: a consultation counts if the Council was consulted AND its advice was validly given (quorum, majority, subcommittee delegation: ss.24 to 26, G4); (b) it counts once the Council was asked.
- Callable: `the Minister may exempt classes of cooling systems by order, a consultation counting once held`; the two flags are separate fields everywhere, so the other functions read (b) by ignoring the second flag.
- Tests: `g1-tests-licensing` (an order after a consultation whose advice was not valid); red: one assertion.

### F12. The reach of an exemption order

- s.3(b) exempts a person "from obtaining a licence under that subsection" (src:72).
- Taken: only the s.3(a) licence requirement is lifted; the person does not become a licensee, so s.4 still forbids advertising as one (the s.4 function has no exemption input); s.18(a) (an operator may not engage an unlicensed person) is G3's, and the INTEGRATOR decides whether it reads the exemption.
- No callable alternative: the only other reading (an exempt person may advertise as licensed) has no support in the text; it is recorded so that a reviewer sees it was considered.

### F13. "A licence of that kind" in s.4: the same kind, or a higher grade

- s.4 (src:76): no person shall advertise himself as a licensee "of a certain kind" if he has no licence "of that kind".
- Readings: (a) taken: the same kind: a grade 3 holder who advertises as a grade 2 licensee holds no grade 2 licence (though he may lawfully do all grade 2 work, which s.3(a) allows); (b) a licence of a higher grade counts.
- Callable: `the outcome of section 4 for the self-description, a licence of a higher grade counting as the kind advertised`.
- Tests: self-descriptions f02 to f06, f11, f12 on both readings (`g1-tests-licensing`); red: one assertion.

### F14. When s.4 commences for an advertisement (*assumed, not ruled*)

- s.63(a) states the commencement of the Law for work on a system with a listed flammable refrigerant ((a)(1)) and for work on any other system ((a)(2)); an advertisement is not work on a system, and the text does not say which limb governs it.
- The input `the Law has commenced for the advertisement` takes the answer; two helpers state the two readings: `s 4 commences for an advertisement as soon as the Law has commenced for any work` and `s 4 commences for an advertisement only when the Law has commenced for work on every kind of system`.
- Tests: all four combinations on both helpers (`g1-tests-licensing`); self-description f08.

### F15. s.5(a): a duty to apply, or only the definitions of "applicant" and "licence application"

- s.5(a) (src:79): "whoever seeks to obtain or to renew a licence ... shall submit an application to the Registrar".
- Readings: (a) taken: a duty in form, `s 5(a) — the applicant must submit a licence application`, a MUST with no period and no consequence the Law names (no Third Schedule item prices it); (b) a procedural step that only defines "applicant" and "licence application".
- Callable: the two predicates `s 5(a) — the person is an applicant` and `s 5(a) — the request is a licence application` carry reading (b) without the duty.
- No period is stated in s.5 for the application, the demand or the publication, so no rule there carries a `WITHIN`; a regulation under s.60(2)(a) may fix the time for a renewal application and none is in the sources (IP14).
- Tests: `g1-tests-application` (the duty stands however long the clock runs).

### F16. The order of the reasons s.3(a) does not apply (*assumed, not ruled*)

- The outcome function asks, in order: is the act work on a cooling system, has the Law commenced for it, is the system exempted by order; the first yes names the reason.
- The text does not rank the three reasons, and in every case they agree that nothing is forbidden; only "not forbidden" is source-decided, so a case with two reasons (the Law not commenced and the class exempted, case s36) asserts only that s.3(a) forbids nothing and that no listed violation arises.
- Change the order and only the NAME of the outcome changes.

### F17. Whether the three-year cap of s.10(a) reaches a restriction as well as a suspension (kind V)

- s.10(a) (src:110): "revoke a licence, restrict it or suspend it for a period not exceeding three years".
- Readings: (a) taken: "for a period not exceeding three years" follows "restrict it or suspend it" (one item, the comma after "revoke a licence" closing the earlier items), so it reaches both; a restriction with no end is then outside the power; (b) it reaches the suspension only (the survey's reading).
- Callable: `the Registrar may take the measure under section 10(a), the cap reaching suspensions only`, and the record `Reading of the three-year cap of section 10(a)`.
- Tests: restriction for four years, suspension for four years, restriction for exactly three years (`g1-tests-adverse`); red: one assertion.

### F18. Whether the day a suspension begins is its first day (kind V)

- IL s.10(b): a period of years after an event ends on the same-numbered day; the first day of the period is the day after the event.
- Readings: (a) taken: the day the suspension begins is the event, so the last day allowed is the third anniversary (a suspension from 2026-03-01 may run to 2029-03-01); (b) the day it begins counts as its first day, so the last day allowed is the day before (2029-02-28).
- Callable: `the Registrar may take the measure under section 10(a), the cap counting the beginning day` (a strict comparison, no date arithmetic).
- Tests: begin 2026-03-01 with ends 2029-03-01 (in on (a), out on (b)), 2029-03-02 (out on both), 2029-02-28 (in on both); begin 2024-02-29 with ends 2027-02-28 (in) and 2027-03-01 (out), the month lacking the day (`g1-tests-adverse`); red: one assertion.

### F19. IL s.10(c) applied to the three-year cap (kind V, *assumed, not ruled*)

- The brief's default for a period of validity is not to apply IL s.10(c): a validity period is not a time for doing something, and applying it would lengthen a suspension by a day of rest.
- Callable: `the Registrar may take the measure under section 10(a), the cap moving past days of rest`, taking the domain `Calendar of rest days`.
- Tests: begin 2026-03-01, ends 2029-03-02, with 2029-03-01 a day of rest (in on the alternative, out on the reading taken); two rest days in a row (1 and 2 March 2029, ends 2029-03-03: in); a rest day elsewhere (no effect) (`g1-tests-adverse`); red: one assertion.

### F20. Ground (2) of s.10(a) and the renewal conditions of s.8 (see contradiction C4)

- s.8 (src:100) renews a licence on the conditions of s.6 EXCEPT the condition of s.6(a)(4); ground (2) (src:112) says "a condition for the grant under s.6 or 9 ceased to hold" without that exception.
- Readings: (a) taken, the words: any condition ceasing grounds every measure, including a refusal to renew; (b) s.8 is carried into ground (2): the loss of the s.6(a)(4) condition alone cannot ground a REFUSAL TO RENEW (it can still ground a revocation).
- Callable: `the Registrar may take the measure under section 10(a), the renewal conditions of section 8 carried into ground (2)`, with the field `the only condition that ceased to hold is a condition of section 6(a)(4)`.
- Discriminating case: a licensee struck off the register of technicians (s.6(a)(4)(b)) applies to renew.
- Tests: three fixtures (`g1-tests-adverse`); red: one assertion.

### F21. "Until final judgment" in s.10(b): the day of the judgment (kind V, trap T27)

- s.10(b) (src:117) suspends the licence "until final judgment"; IL s.8 says "until" in relation to time includes the day named.
- Readings: (a) taken: the suspension is in force through the day of the judgment; (b) it ends the day before.
- No number and no cap: the end is the day of the judgment, an input; with no judgment yet there is no end (NOTHING), never a number (P11).
- IL s.10(c) is not applied (kind V).
- Callable: `a suspension under section 10(b) is in force on the day, ending before the day of the judgment`.
- Tests: the day before, the day of and the day after a judgment on 2027-06-10, and no judgment (`g1-tests-adverse`); red: one assertion.

### F22. s.10(c), first limb: whether a written hearing is needed

- The first limb (src:118) names the consultation of the Council and not the hearing; (a) and the second limb name both.
- Readings: (a) taken, the words: no hearing is required for the direction, so the record has no field for one; (b) the hearing is required here too.
- Callable: `the Registrar may direct that the licensee not do the specified work until the defect is cured, a written hearing also required`.
- Tests: `g1-tests-adverse`.

### F23. s.10(c), second limb: whether the Council is consulted again

- The second limb (src:118) names the written hearing and not the Council; the measures are "as stated in (a)", whose power begins "after consulting the Council".
- Readings: (a) taken, the words: the preconditions are the limb's own (the period passed, the defect not cured to the Registrar's satisfaction, the hearing) and (a)'s ground, cap and hearing; the Council's consultation made for the direction suffices; (b) (a)'s Council consultation is carried in.
- Callable: `the Registrar may take the measure after the cure period, the Council consulted again`.
- Tests: `g1-tests-adverse`; red: one assertion.

### F24. IL s.10(c) applied to the period the Registrar sets for the cure (kind D, P12, *assumed, not ruled*)

- The brief's default for a deadline for doing something is to apply IL s.10(c): a last day that is a day of rest moves to the next day that is not one.
- The Registrar fixes the period in days, weeks, months or years from the day of his direction: the length and unit are inputs (`Period fixed by the Registrar under section 10`), counted by IL s.10(a) and (b) through the vendored time module.
- Callable alternative: `the last day of the cure period under section 10(c), without IL s 10(c)` and `the Registrar may take the measure after the cure period, without IL s 10(c)`.
- Discriminating case: 30 days from 2026-01-01 end on 2026-01-31; with that day a day of rest, IL s.10(c) moves the end to 2026-02-01, so the measure may be taken on 2026-02-02 and not on 2026-02-01 (on the alternative, on 2026-02-01).
- Tests: units (days, weeks, months, years) with and without rest days and the cases above (`g1-tests-adverse`); red: one assertion.

### F25. s.10(d): whether the power to add conditions to a new licence needs the Council

- (d) (src:119): the Registrar "shall direct", after consulting the Council, the period before a new application; he "may" make the grant of a new licence conditional on added conditions.
- Readings: (a) taken: the consultation is named in the first clause only, so the power needs only the revocation; (b) the power needs the consultation too.
- Callable: `the Registrar may add conditions to the grant of a new licence, the Council consulted`.
- Tests: `g1-tests-adverse`; red: one assertion.

### F26. IL s.10(c) applied to the bar after a revocation (kind W, P13, *assumed, not ruled*)

- The period before which the person may not apply again is the complement of a window in which an application may be made; the brief's default for a window is to apply IL s.10(c).
- The length and unit are the Registrar's: an input, with no default.
- The person may apply only AFTER the last day of the bar: a comparison between dates, not an addition to one.
- Callable alternative: `the last day of the bar under section 10(d), without IL s 10(c)` and `a new application may be made on the day after the bar, without IL s 10(c)`.
- Discriminating case: 2 years from 2026-03-15 end on 2028-03-15; with that day a day of rest, IL s.10(c) moves the end to 2028-03-16 and the first day for a new application is 2028-03-17 (on the alternative, 2028-03-16).
- Tests: `g1-tests-adverse`; red: one assertion.

### F27. The day a revocation takes effect (*assumed, not ruled*)

- The Law names no effective day for a revocation; the register records one (`revoked on`).
- Readings: (a) taken: the licence is out of force from that day on; (b) from the day after.
- Callable: `s 10 — the licence is revoked on the day, the revocation taking effect the day after the day recorded`.
- A suspension runs from its first to its last day, "until" including the day (IL s.8), which is what the domain's `suspended until` says ("the last day of a suspension"); no fork there.
- Tests: `g1-tests-adverse`.

### F28. "Final certificate": what the domain certificate says

- s.2 (src:63-65) defines a final certificate by what it attests (a passed exam with a theoretical and a practical part, for the Division's certificate; completed studies in the climate-control stream, for the Ministry of Education's); the domain `Certificate` names the certificate and not what it attests.
- Taken: `s 2 — the certificate is a final certificate` tests the facts in a record of its own; `the Second Schedule calls the certificate a final certificate` states, for the seven constructors of the domain, that the Second Schedule's column A calls each of them a "final certificate".
- G2 (s.6(a)(4)(a) and the Second Schedule) chooses which of the two it wires; see the nouns request.
- Tests: eight fixtures on the facts and seven constructors (`g1-tests-interpretation`).

### F29. s.11(b): a passive sentence, a constitutive rule or a duty of the Registrar

- s.11(b) (src:125): "the register shall be open to public inspection by way of a query that states identifying details of the person".
- Readings: (a) taken: a passive sentence with no actor is not a duty (the phrasebook's entry 5.2): `s 11(b) — the register shows, on a query` (an EITHER: a query without identifying details is a named problem); (b) it implies a duty of the Registrar, who keeps the register.
- Callable: `s 11(b) — the Registrar must open the register to public inspection by query, on the reading that the passive sentence implies a duty` (a MUST with no period).
- Tests: `g1-tests-register`.

### F30. What is an opinion and what is a fact (*assumed, not ruled*)

- Where the Law says "in the opinion of", "if he saw", "holds that", "to his satisfaction" or "needed by him" the fact is the Registrar's (or the Minister's) and is an input named for the opinion: `in the Registrar's opinion ...`, `the Registrar saw that ...`, `the Registrar holds that ...`, `the defect was cured to the satisfaction of the Registrar`, `the Minister was satisfied that ...`.
- The six grounds of s.10(a) carry no such words and are stated as facts; ground (6) ("lack of professionalism or negligence that may endanger life or property") is an evaluative finding all the same, and who makes it is not said; it is an input like the others.
- "An offence as stated in s.6(a)(3)" (s.10(b)) is an offence that, in the Registrar's opinion, makes the person unfit: two inputs, the indictment and the opinion.
- The conditions of a licence and the provisions under the Law (s.3(a)) are not listed anywhere in the sources (s.17(d), G3, makes the training duty a condition); a breach is a boolean input.

## (4) Nouns requests for the INTEGRATOR

1. **`First Schedule activity`** is declared in `g1-first-schedule.l4`, not in `domain.l4`, because the survey keeps the activity enumeration in G1.
   G3 (s.13, s.18(b)), G5 (s.31(3)), G6 (Third Schedule item (4)) and G7 (s.61) all speak of "work" and of installation or maintenance.
   If the INTEGRATOR moves the declaration to `domain.l4`, the modules need only an `IMPORT` of domain and the constructors keep their names; nothing else changes.
2. **`Work item`** (domain) carries `the kind of job` and `the system` but no activity.
   The s.3(a) rule therefore takes the system and the activity as fields of its own record, `Circumstances of work under section 3(a)`, and derives the job kind from the activity: `the job kind of the activity` and `the job kind agrees with the First Schedule part of the activity` let the INTEGRATOR check a recorded job kind against the activity.
   Request: add `the activity the act amounts to` (a `MAYBE First Schedule activity`) to `Work item`, once the activity type is in the domain.
3. **`Certificate`** (domain) names a certificate and not what it attests, while s.2 "final certificate" is defined by what the certificate attests (exam, theoretical and practical parts; the climate-control stream).
   G1 defines the record `Facts about a certificate under section 2` for the test, and `the Second Schedule calls the certificate a final certificate` for the seven constructors; G2 chooses which to wire.
   Request: either a field on the domain for what each constructor attests, or a ruling that the seven constructors are final certificates by the Second Schedule's own wording.
4. **`Person`** (domain) has a reference and a kind only; s.11(a)(1) says the register shows "the licensee's details".
   `Entry of the register of licensees` uses `Person` as it stands; if the register must show a name or an address, add the fields to `Person`.
5. **`Licence`** (domain) is used as it stands for s.10's effect (`revoked on`, `suspended from`, `suspended until`) and for the register entry.
   A restriction under s.10(a) or a direction under s.10(c) is NOT a field of `Licence`, by the survey's design: G1 holds it as `Period of a restriction or suspension under section 10` and gives it to s.3(a) as the flag `the licensee is restricted from the work`.
6. **Local stand-ins** that another group may want the INTEGRATOR to share: `Period fixed by the Registrar under section 10` and `Unit of a period fixed under section 10` (G5, G6 and G3 also have periods an official fixes, with a length and a unit as inputs); `Grounds under section 10(a)` (G2 needs the loss of a condition, IP18); `Unmet precondition of section 10`.
7. **No `ASSUME` and no section `GIVEN`** is used in any g1 module, so no input is reachable only through a section scope.

## (6) Contradictions found in the source

This is the result of a separate pass made AFTER the modules and their tests were written, reading the Hebrew of ss.1-5, 10-11 and the First Schedule again for two provisions (or two limbs of one) that give different answers to the same facts, and for provisions that overlap.
The pass looked at: every pair of items of the First Schedule that describe the same physical act; the lists of the three licence kinds against the capacity limits of s.2; s.3(a) against s.4 and against the Third Schedule; s.10(a) to (d) against each other and against ss.6 and 8; and the wiki against the gazette pages 182-185 and 198-201 (no difference of number, list or wording was found; the one oddity, the extra letter of A.1(12) and B.1(14), is in both).
Each entry gives the provisions, the case that discriminates, and the callable function for each reading.

**C1. Oil work: A.1(5) against B.3(3) (trap T8).**
Provisions: A.1(5) (src:505) lists replacing or adding oil as installation work under grade 1; B.3(3) (src:559) lists adding or replacing oil as maintenance work under grade 3 only; the grade 1 maintenance list, which grade 2 and 3 repeat, has no oil item.
Discriminating case: a grade 1 licensee adds oil on a 10 kW system.
While installing (A.1(5)): permitted.
On a service visit (B.3(3)): forbidden, no licence of the appropriate kind.
The Law defines neither "installation" nor "maintenance".
Callable: the two activities are two constructors; cases s10 and s09 of `g1-tests-licensing`.

**C2. Components: B.1(3) against B.3(2) (trap T9).**
Provisions: B.1(3) (src:536) lists, under grade 1 and so under all three, the check, replacement, change, dismantling and repair of "mechanical and electrical components"; B.3(2) (src:558) lists, under grade 3 only, the same acts on "a refrigerant system and its electrical and mechanical components".
Discriminating case: a grade 1 licensee replaces a compressor.
If the act is B.1(3): permitted. If it is B.3(2): forbidden.
Grade 3 has the row 1 list, so B.3(2) adds nothing for grade 3 over B.1(3) for components, and an item that adds nothing was presumably meant to reach the refrigerant system as a whole.
Callable: `licence kinds that may do an act described by any of the activities` (the union, taken) and `... all of the activities` (the intersection); cases s14 to s16; the red assertion.

**C3. Controls: A.1(6) against A.1(11).**
Provisions: A.1(6) (src:506) lists installing, connecting or checking the working of all mechanical and electrical components of the refrigerant-carrying system, "the control components" and the piping; A.1(11) (src:511) lists connecting "integral control and monitoring accessories only".
Discriminating case: a grade 1 licensee connects a control accessory that is not integral (a thermostat bought separately).
Under A.1(11): not work (the "only" limit fails), so no licence is needed.
Under A.1(6): work, and every grade may do it.
Either the "only" of A.1(11) is idle, or the control components of A.1(6) are the integral ones.
Callable: the caller classes the act; `A.1(11)` with the limit false answers "not work" (cases s21, s22), `A.1(6)` answers "work".

**C4. Section 8 against ground (2) of section 10(a) (trap T26, in part).**
Provisions: s.8 (src:100) renews a licence on the conditions of s.6 EXCEPT s.6(a)(4); ground (2) (src:112) is "a condition for the grant under s.6 or 9 ceased to hold", with no such exception.
Discriminating case: a licensee struck off the register of technicians (the s.6(a)(4)(b) condition) applies to renew.
Under s.8 the renewal does not require that condition; under ground (2) its loss lets the Registrar refuse to renew.
Callable: `the Registrar may take the measure under section 10(a)` (the words, taken) and `... the renewal conditions of section 8 carried into ground (2)` (fork F20); the red assertion.

**C5. Section 10(b) against ground (2) and the cap of section 10(a).**
Provisions: s.6(a)(3) (src:87) makes it a condition of a licence that no indictment is pending for a disqualifying offence, so a pending indictment is the loss of that condition, which is ground (2) of s.10(a); s.10(a) then lets the Registrar revoke, restrict or suspend for at most three years; s.10(b) (src:117) separately lets him suspend "until final judgment", with no cap.
Discriminating case: a licensee is indicted for an offence the Registrar holds disqualifying and the trial takes five years.
By (a)(2): a suspension of at most three years, or revocation.
By (b): a suspension for the five years.
So how long a suspension on an indictment may last depends on the route the Registrar takes: the cap of (a) is avoidable through (b).
Callable: `the Registrar may take the measure under section 10(a)` with the field `a condition for the grant ceased to hold` true, and `the Registrar may suspend the licence until final judgment`; both are tested, the cap in `g1-tests-adverse`.

**C6. The Council in the second limb of section 10(c).**
Provisions: the second limb (src:118) lets the Registrar, "after giving the licensee an opportunity to make his claims in writing", take "measures as stated in (a)"; (a) (src:110) lets him take them "after consulting the Council".
The limb names the hearing and not the Council, and the other three subsections each name the Council expressly.
Discriminating case: the Registrar revokes after the cure period without asking the Council again.
Callable: `the Registrar may take the measure after the cure period` (the words, taken) and `... the Council consulted again`; the red assertion.

**C7. Section 3(a) against section 4 on a higher grade.**
Provisions: s.3(a) lets a grade 3 holder do all the work grade 2 covers (the kind appropriate to a system up to 70 kW is any kind that covers it); s.4 (src:76) forbids advertising oneself as a licensee "of a certain kind" without a licence "of that kind".
Discriminating case: a grade 3 licensee advertises as a grade 2 licensee.
By s.4 as written: forbidden (item (3)).
By s.3(a): he may lawfully do every grade 2 job.
Callable: `the outcome of section 4 for the self-description` and `..., a licence of a higher grade counting as the kind advertised`; cases f03 and f05; the red assertion.

**C8. A.1(6), A.1(11) and B.1(8) against B.1(2): measuring.**
Provisions: A.1(6), A.1(11) and B.1(8) (src:506, 511, 541) each end "except measuring energy consumption"; B.1(2) (src:535) lists "measuring pressures, currents, voltages, resistances and temperatures of components and piping of the refrigerant circuit".
Measuring energy consumption is excluded from three items and listed in none, so by the definition of "work" (an act of a kind the Schedule lists) it is not work and needs no licence; yet measuring currents and voltages, from which energy is computed, is B.1(2) work.
This is a boundary the text leaves to the caller's classification, not a conflict of answers; it is recorded for the reviewer (open question 4).

**C9. Wording: "work" in s.3(a) and "an act" in Third Schedule item (2).**
s.3(a) speaks of a licence appropriate "for work in that system"; item (2) of the Third Schedule speaks of a licence appropriate "for performing an act on that system" (a different Hebrew word).
The two words are taken to be the same thing, since s.2 defines "work" as the performing of an act of the listed kinds; no answer depends on it.

**Considered and found consistent.**
Rows 2 and 3 of Part A and row 2 of Part B ("all the works listed in this column for grade 1") against the capacity limits: the grades differ only through s.2 and through B.3(2) and B.3(3), as the survey's trap T11 says; the test `g1-tests-first-schedule` asserts all 28 activities against all three kinds.
s.2's "up to 70 kW" against s.9(a)(1)'s "over 70 kW": complementary, which is the evidence for the inclusive reading (F1).
s.6(a) "shall give" a licence against s.10(a) "may refuse to renew" (trap T26): s.6(a) governs the grant, s.8 says a licence "can be renewed" on the conditions (permissive), and s.10(a) is the special rule for refusing a renewal; no mandatory-against-discretionary clash arises unless s.8 is read "shall renew", which its words (src:100) do not say.
