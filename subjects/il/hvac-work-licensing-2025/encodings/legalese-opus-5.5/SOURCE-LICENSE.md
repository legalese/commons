# SOURCE-LICENSE — the terms the quoted source text carries

This file is about the **source text** this encoding quotes, not about the encoding itself.
The encoding (the `.l4` modules, `check.sh`, `NOTES.md`) is offered under Apache-2.0, as `encoding.json` says.

The modules quote Hebrew source text verbatim, in comments and in string literals: the Law, the Fees Regulations, and the SimpLEX figure.
So the question of what terms that text carries is real.

**Status: undetermined.**
None of the input files states a licence or copyright term for the text it carries.
A rule that would settle the status of Israeli legislation is not among the sources, and this file does not assert one.
A reviewer should establish the terms before the encoding is published.

What would settle it, as an assumption to check and not a finding:

- **Legislation.** Whether Israeli statutes and regulations are excluded from copyright, and so free to quote, is a question about Israeli copyright law. That law is not among the sources.
- **Wikisource.** Hebrew Wikisource's site-wide licence terms are published on the site, not in the downloaded wikitext.
- **The paper's figure.** The terms are the publisher's or the authors'. The quotation here is a transcription of a few lines, made for analysis.
