# Row il-77: Population Registry Law, 5725-1965, a targeted record of two references

Status on 2026-11-10: draft, version 0.1.0, target compiler main.
No domain expert has read this against the source.
Every claim below was checked against a deposited page image or the deposited excerpt named beside it; where it was not, it says so.
This row does not encode the Law.

## 1. What this row is

The row records two items of the Law as typed values and tests them.
Item I1 is section 3 (its margin note is "המרשם ראיה לכאורה"), whose words point at the particulars listed in paragraphs (1) to (4) and (9) to (13) of section 2.
The row types the second group, written "(9) עד (13)", as a range with two end labels, as the Law was enacted (the issue of 1.8.1965, printed page 270).
Three later instructions insert paragraphs of subsection 2(א) between those labels: SH 2029 (issue date 6.9.2005) inserts (11א), SH 2333 (issue date 23.1.2012) inserts (11ב), and SH 2980 (issue date 27.6.2022) inserts (11ג).
The red assertion for I1 is about (11א) and (11ב).
The instruction that inserts (11ג) is typed with its commencement as not established from a page, and it is left out of the assertion.
Item I2 is paragraph (2) of subsection (ג) of section 6, written by SH 2042 (issue date 26.12.2005).
Its words name "סעיף 3(ד)" of the Genetic Information Law, 5761-2000, and use the term "בדיקה גנטית להורות".
SH 2173 (issue date 30.7.2008) prints the word "בטל" against subsection (ד) of section 3 of that law, and prints in its section 1(1) a definition of "בדיקה גנטית לקשרי משפחה" in place of the definition of the term "בדיקה גנטית להורות".
Beside each item the row keeps controls: a test that finds the range, the label, the pointer or the subsection where it is, and the places where the instructions did their work.
Each item has two limbs: limb (a), the target is repealed, missing or says something else, read from an official instrument; limb (b), the citing words still read so in the latest official instrument read.
Subsection letters and the letters of paragraph labels are written in Latin letters in identifiers: (א) is Alef, (ב) Bet, (ג) Gimel, (ד) Dalet.
The Law is "the Law" in the modules and below; English titles of instruments are working translations.
The commencements are typed as the pages print them, a calendar date where a page prints one and a period where it prints a period; no calendar date is computed from any period.

## 2. Seat

The seat is a reader who relies on a registry copy or certificate (item I1), and the person who gives the notice under section 6 (item I2; the heading of section 6 in the consolidation is "הודעה על לידה בישראל", CONSOLIDATION-ONLY).
The row asks that individual no question, because it records references: there is no input to ask for and no `@export`.

Quotation lines in the modules are of two kinds.

`python3 -I tools/hebcheck.py <excerpt> *.l4 NOTES.md` exits 0: every run of Hebrew outside a quotation line is in the excerpt or on an `ext:` line of the row.
The tool therefore checks that a typed string equals a stretch of a quotation line; it does not check the quotation line against the page.
The quotation lines were compared with the deposited crops by eye.
They were also compared by script in two ways.
First, 78 `ext:` lines were looked up word by word (words of two letters or more) in the text layer of the PDF page they quote; for 67 lines every word was found.

The text layers of the 1965 issue, SH 1766 and SH 1030 are OCR of old printed pages; the image decided wherever the two differed, and the comparison covers only the words that layer has.
Digits, paragraph labels and punctuation were compared by eye only.
Second, 64 of the 78 `ext:` lines occur verbatim in a second, independent typing of the same page text; that comparison shows that two typings agree and is not a comparison with the page.
Of the other 14 lines, 11 are the footer lines above, and three differ in form from the second typing and were compared by eye: the line of subsection (ד) of section 3 in SH 1766, the opening line of section 1 in SH 2173, and section 1 of SH 1030, which is typed with the space before the semicolon that the page prints.
