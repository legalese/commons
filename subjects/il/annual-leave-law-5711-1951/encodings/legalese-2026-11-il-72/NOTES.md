# Row il-72: Annual Leave Law, 5711-1951, a targeted record of two cross-references

Status on 2026-11-10: draft, version 0.1.0, target compiler main.
No domain expert has read this against the source.
Every claim below was checked against a deposited page image or the deposited excerpt named beside it; where it was not, it says so.
This row does not encode the Law.

## 1. What this row is

The row records two citations in section 27 of the Annual Leave Law, 5711-1951 (the Law), as typed values and tests them.
Item I1 is subsection (c) of section 27, which names section 2 of the Criminal Procedure Ordinance (Evidence) and, as "that same Ordinance", sections 3 and 4.
Section 12(2) of the law that amends the Criminal Law Ordinance (No. 24), issue date 21.1.1965, names section 4 of that Ordinance in a list of repeals.
Item I2 is subsection (a) of section 27, which points at section 10 (1) of the Labour Department Ordinance, 1943.
Section 41(1) of the Labour Inspection (Organization) Law, issue date 3.9.1954, names section 10 of that Ordinance in a list of repeals.
Beside each item the row keeps the sound references nearby: the other provisions the same instruction names, and which of them the citing words do not name.
Each item has two limbs: limb (a), the target is repealed, missing or says something else, read from an official instrument; limb (b), the citing words still read so in the latest official instrument.
The row says what the pages print.
It does not say what a repealed section provided, what replaced it, whether a reference was valid when made, how a citation should now read, whether section 25 of the Interpretation Law reaches either citation, or what a court would do.
The English titles are working translations, not enacted titles.
Numbers and letters of sections are typed in Latin letters (subsection (d) is dalet; "and-" is the prefix vav with maqaf); the Hebrew is on the quotation lines.

## 2. Seat

The seat is the individual to whom the Law applies, an employee or an employer.
The row asks that individual no question, because it records references: there is no input to ask for and no `@export`.

Quotation lines in the modules are of three kinds.

The Hebrew of the 41 `ext:` lines was also compared, by script, with an independently typed reading of the same pages: 39 lines match word for word, and the other two (section 39 of the Law and section 31 of the Interpretation Law) differ only by the section number printed before the words, which the crops show.
