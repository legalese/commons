## Seat

The seat is a person who founds, directs or holds shares in a company under the Companies Law.
That person is a founder, a director or officer, or a shareholder, a minority shareholder included.
The questions are the ones such a person asks of Part 6 chapters 2 to 5 and Part 7: what the Law lets me do, what it requires of me, what I may require of the company, and what can be done to me.
There is no seat for an official. The Registrar, the court and the Minister appear only as what the person is owed or exposed to.
Where the person is the other party to the company (a creditor, a buyer of securities, "another person" who dealt with the company), the question is still asked in the first person ("may the company cancel the transaction against me?").
Every question is in `ic3-seat.l4`, has `@export` and `@desc`, takes plain inputs (yes or no, a number, a date, a choice from a list) and builds the layer-1 records inside.
The questions are grouped under `§` headings in the text's order; a group the person may not be in says so in a `Situation` comment under its heading.
Each question calls rules of the layer-1 modules and restates none.
A record field that no rule reads gets a fixed placeholder, with a comment where it is built (the statuses of a company that no rule of this piece reads: public-benefit company, government company, reporting corporation).
A company is described by the four facts about its shares (listed, offered by prospectus, offered abroad, held by the public) and, where the question reads them, the same four about its bonds. The core's rules work out from them whether it is public or a bond company.
The figures the Minister may vary are the Law's own: the 2% of 267א(ב)(2) and 275(א)(3)(ב). A regulation that varies them would change the answers.
The questions about approvals answer with a record (`האישורים שהעסקה שלי טעונה`): the organs whose approval is needed, in the order the text gives them, whether the general meeting's approval needs the majority of 275(א)(3) or of 267א(ב), and whether the articles lay down another way of approval.
The question about a private offer answers with the record `התשובה על הצעה פרטית שאני צד לה`: whether the offer is a material private offer and, if so, its approvals.
Rights and duties come in pairs. The questions of the person and of the other party over the same relation are in the Jural relations table, and the cases end with seven mirror cases that ask the same facts from both sides.
The case file is `ic3-cases-seat.l4`: at least one case for every question, and the expected values are written from the text.

## Jural relations

One row per provision that creates a relation; a provision that creates none is listed once as `none`.
The columns follow Hohfeld: a right (claim) corresponds to a duty, a privilege to a no-right, a power to a liability, an immunity to a disability.
Where the Law takes a power away from a party and names no one who gains the matching immunity (for example the company may not exempt an office-holder from liability for breach of loyalty), both cells say `none` and the first cell says what the Law takes away; where it does name the party protected, the row gives the immunity and the disability.
The court, the Registrar and the Minister are named only where the Law makes them the bearer or the holder of a relation; no question is asked from their seat.
The rule named is the layer-1 rule that encodes the relation, stated once; the seat questions of the same relation are in the coverage table.

### F1: section 269(א), what "in a public company or in a private bond company" qualifies
- Text (src:1824): "נושא משרה בחברה או בעל שליטה בחברה ציבורית או בחברה פרטית שהיא חברת איגרות חוב".
- Taken: the qualifier attaches to the controlling shareholder only. An office-holder in any company must disclose his personal interest.
- Not taken: it attaches to both, so an office-holder of a private company that is not a bond company is outside the duty.
- Where: `סעיף 269(א) - האדם חייב לגלות לחברה את ענינו האישי` in `ic3-part6-ch5-interested-transactions`.

### F2: section 270, the proviso "ובלבד שהעסקה היא לטובת החברה"
- Text (src:1829): "עסקאות של חברה המפורטות להלן, טעונות אישורים כאמור בפרק זה, ובלבד שהעסקה היא לטובת החברה".
- Taken: the proviso limits what may be approved. A transaction that is not for the company's benefit may not be approved; whether it is for the company's benefit is a plain input.
- Not taken: it is a condition of the transaction needing approval at all, so a transaction not for the company's benefit would fall outside the chapter.
- Where: `סעיף 270 - אפשר לאשר את העסקה`.

### F3: section 270(4), a private offer in which the controlling shareholder has an interest
- Text (src:1835): "עסקה חריגה של חברה ציבורית עם בעל השליטה בה או עסקה חריגה של חברה ציבורית עם אדם אחר שלבעל השליטה יש בה ענין אישי, לרבות הצעה פרטית שלבעל השליטה יש בה ענין אישי".
- Taken: a private offer in which the controlling shareholder has a personal interest is within limb (4) even if the offer is not an extraordinary transaction.
- Not taken: "לרבות" places the private offer inside "עסקה חריגה", so it counts only if it is extraordinary.
- Where: `סעיף 270(4) - מתקיים בעסקה של בעל שליטה האמור בפסקה, בלי סוג החברה`.

### F4: sections 270(4) and 270(4א), the terms-of-service and employment limbs
- Text (src:1835, 1836): the limb on services names "בעל השליטה בה או עם קרובו"; the limbs on terms of service ("אם הוא גם נושא משרה בה") and on employment ("אם הוא עובד החברה") speak of "הוא".
- Taken: the services limb covers the controlling shareholder and his relative; the two other limbs cover the controlling shareholder only.
- Not taken: all three limbs reach his relative.
- Where: the same rule as F3.

### F5: sections 267א(ב)(1) and 275(א)(3)(א), what "majority" counts
- Text (src:1799, 1877): "רוב מכלל קולות בעלי המניות שאינם ... המשתתפים בהצבעה; במניין כלל הקולות ... לא יובאו בחשבון קולות הנמנעים".
- Taken: the ordinary majority of those who vote, abstentions set aside: votes for exceed half of votes for plus votes against, in the whole meeting and in the group.
- Not taken: a majority of all the voting rights of the group, voting or not.
- Where: `סעיף 267א(ב)(1) - מבחן רוב הקולות`; `סעיף 275(א)(3)(א) - מבחן רוב הקולות` calls it. The core's `מנין קולות` counts for plus against.

### F6: section 267א(ג), what "controlled by a controlling shareholder" qualifies in "public grandchild company"

### F7: section 272(ב), the reach of the private company without an audit committee

### F9: section 255(א), "an act among those listed in section 254(א)"

### F11: section 263(2), "intentionally or recklessly, except if done only negligently"

### F17: section 301, additional limits the company undertook
- Text (src:2003-2004): "חברה רשאית לבצע חלוקה לפי הוראות פרק זה בלבד; ואולם חברה רשאית להתחייב בתקנונה או בחוזה שלא לבצע חלוקה במגבלות נוספות על הוראות פרק זה"; "חלוקה בניגוד להוראות פרק זה היא חלוקה אסורה".

## What the text turns out to say

Each finding is something the formalisation made visible that a reader of the text could miss.
"Asserted" names the case file that holds an assertion of it; a finding with no assertion says "Not asserted" and why.

1. Accepting a void undertaking is itself a breach. Under 264(ב) an indemnification undertaking or insurance for a breach of loyalty (other than the good-faith breach of 261(2)) is void, and an office-holder who receives one is regarded as having breached the duty of loyalty. The provision therefore bites twice: the cover fails and the receiving is a wrong. Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, the cases under 264(ב) ("receiving is a breach").
2. A private company without an audit committee sends a director's extraordinary transaction to the general meeting. Under 272(ב) a transaction that would need the audit committee and the board needs, where the company is a private company that is not a bond company and has no audit committee, the board alone for an office-holder who is not a director, and the board and the general meeting for a director. Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, the 255(ב) case "a material act of a director in a private company without an audit committee".
3. The second majority test can pass while the whole meeting votes against. Under 275(א)(3) and 267א(ב) the opponents are counted among the shareholders with no personal interest only, and compared with 2% of all the voting rights; with 300 for and 600 against in the meeting, and 100 for and 10 against among the disinterested out of 1,000, the test is met. Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, 262(ב), 267א(ב) and 275(א)(3), the cases with 300 600 100 10 1000.
4. The company has no remedy under 281 against a counterparty to a transaction of the kind 271 describes. Section 281 reaches transactions that need approval "other than" those of 271 (a transaction with an office-holder that is not extraordinary). Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, 281, the case whose second argument is TRUE.
5. Without recent statements a company has no profits for the profit test; it is not zero. Under 302(ב) "profits" are drawn from the last adjusted statements, audited or reviewed, made up not more than six months before the distribution; with none, the text gives no figure and the profit test cannot be met, so the only route the chapter leaves for a distribution is the court's approval under 303 (leaving aside the redemption of redeemable securities under 312). Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, 302(ב), the cases whose answer is NOTHING.
6. The presumption of 310(ב) is for a shareholder in a public company who is not an insider, and it can be rebutted. A shareholder in a private company has no presumption; a director, general manager or controlling shareholder has none; and proof that the shareholder knew or should have known defeats it. Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, 310(ב) and 310(א).
7. A purchase of the parent's shares by a subsidiary that the parent does not wholly own is a distribution only to the extent of the parent's share of the subsidiary (309(ד)); for a wholly owned subsidiary the subsection does not speak. Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, 309(ד).
8. The three-year clock of 275(א1) runs from the day the company became public, if it did. A five-year transaction of a company that became public two years ago needs no fresh approval yet. Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, 275(א1).
9. An office-holder with an interest may attend the discussion to present the transaction if the chair says he is needed, but may not vote (278(א)); a director may do both when most of the body is interested (278(ב)). Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, 278, the two question groups on the same facts.
10. A shareholder who does not state his interest before the vote cannot vote, and his vote is not counted (276). The duty and its consequence are asserted together. Asserted: `ic3-cases-seat.l4`, 276 and mirror case 4.
11. The word "עבירות" in the headings and text of 293 and 294 is transferability ("הגבלת עבירות" is restriction of transferability), not offences. The rules are named for transferability. Not asserted: it is a reading of a word.
12. Section 290(א) gives no rule of rounding. A shareholder's entitlement to a share of a new issue is computed as his proportion times the number of new shares, and may be a fraction of a share. Asserted only for a whole number (1,000 new shares at a quarter: 250); the fractional case is not asserted, because the text is silent.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Written for the stable build the brief names; every file was run with the binary at `L4_BIN` and nothing here was run on any other build.
What this build did not accept while the files were written, and what was written instead:
1. `NOT a EQUALS b` gave a parse error. Written `NOT (a EQUALS b)`.
2. A bare `NOT a AND b` gave an error in one line of the seat. Every `NOT` is parenthesised, and a conjunction that starts with one is written `((NOT a) AND b)`.
3. A positional record construction (`record OF a, b, c`) failed when it crossed a line. A record built on several lines is written with `WITH`, one field to a line; `OF` appears only where the whole construction is on one line.
4. Arguments written as bare names over several lines failed. They are parenthesised, or the call is put on one line.
5. The Encoding Guide says a continuation line of an `#ASSERT` is rejected; every assertion here is on one line.
6. The constructs the brief lists as unavailable (`REFUSE`, `BECAUSE`, a section-level `GIVEN`, `TYPICALLY`, `SET OF`, `EVERY`, `@lang`, `@nlg`) are not used.
Accepted, and used: `WHERE` after a record built with `WITH`; a field of a named constant read as `` `name`'s `field` ``; `LIST OF` with `append` and `sum`; a rule with no `GIVEN` called with no argument.
Slow, not rejected: `l4 run` on `ic3-seat.l4` and on `ic3-cases-seat.l4` takes minutes on this machine (2 minutes 43 seconds for a file that only imports the seat, 3 minutes 58 seconds for the cases), because they import every layer-1 module and the core.

## Before this is worth relying on

1. The source is an unofficial consolidation (Hebrew Wikisource), not the official text. The range contains no enactment or commencement date; section 377 (piece ic6) holds the commencement the two transitional rules (290(ב), 313) depend on.
2. One author wrote the rules, the tests and the cases, reading the same text. The brief allows no independent test pass, so the expected values are one reading's; an independent reading may disagree, and the fork register (F1 to F17) lists the places where I know it could.
3. The Minister's regulations are not in the text, and five sections only say they may be made (267ג, 275(ב), 284, 302(ג), 305). The rules take their outcomes as inputs or use the Law's own figure; where a regulation changes a figure the answers change.
4. Judgments the Law hands to someone are inputs and are not guessed: whether a transaction is for the company's benefit (270), whether a case is "special" (267א(ג), 272(ג)), whether a concern about meeting liabilities is reasonable (302(א)), whether a person should have known (282), whether a plan has detailed criteria (288(ב)).
5. The seat answers a question from the facts the person gives; it does not check that the facts are true or that the person's company is incorporated under this Law (the company is always taken to be).
6. `check.sh` was run with `L4_TARGET=unstable`, as the brief asks, so it did not compare the build against a `main` build; no other build was tried.
7. A passing run says that every assertion written is satisfied; it does not say the assertions are the right ones. The twelve findings and the seventeen forks mark where reading mattered.
