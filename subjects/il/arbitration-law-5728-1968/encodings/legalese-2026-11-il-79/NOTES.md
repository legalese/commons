# Row il-79: Arbitration Law, 5728-1968, a targeted record of two references

Status on 2026-11-11: draft, version 0.1.0, target compiler main.
No domain expert has read this against the source.
Every claim below was checked against a deposited page image or the deposited excerpt named beside it; where it was not, it says so.
This row does not encode the Law.
The English title "Arbitration Law, 5728-1968" is a working translation; it is not an enacted title.
The Hebrew title is the one the first page of Sefer HaChukim (SH) 535 prints, and the later amending instruments spell the year with the letter ה.

## 1. What this row is

The row records two items of the Law (the Arbitration Law, 5728-1968, "the Law") as typed values and tests them.
Each item has two limbs.
Limb (a): the target is repealed, missing or says something else, read from an official instrument.
Limb (b): the citing words still read so in the latest official instrument read.

I1: section 38 of the Law, as SH 535 prints it on printed page 189 (issue dated 9.8.1968), cites "section 19(b)" of the Courts Law, 5717-1957, and "section 8(b) and (d)" of the Registrars Ordinance, 1936, by name and number.
The row types the citing words, the two footnotes of the page, and what later official pages print beside those two numbers:
the 1957 print of section 19 (SH 233, issue dated 1.8.1957, printed page 151) with its subsections (a) and (b);
the section 8 that SH 400 (issue dated 18.7.1963, printed page 123) prints in its section 5, with its subsections (a) to (d);
the Courts Law [Consolidated Version], 5744-1984 (SH 1123, issue dated 31.8.1984): section 41 with the bracketed reference printed beside it, section 96 with the bracketed reference printed beside it, and the comparison table of printed page 218;
the Registrars Ordinance [New Version], 5731-1971 (Dinei Medinat Yisrael, New Version 19, whose footer prints no issue date): section 12 and the comparison table of printed page 439;
and the instruction of section 3 of SH 1110 (issue dated 8.3.1984) on four sections of the Courts Law, 5717-1957.
Three tests that fail on purpose ask where a number sits in a printed table.

I2: SH 2760 (issue dated 28.11.2018) prints, in its section 1, a definition to come in place of the definition of "the court" in section 1 of the Law; it prints three sections, and none names section 38.
The row types the 1968 definition, the three sections of SH 2760, section 38, and the appeal provisions the Courts Law [Consolidated Version] prints for a court of each kind: section 41(b) and section 52(b), with the instructions SH 2169 (issue dated 27.7.2008) and SH 2253 (issue dated 27.7.2010) print on sections 41 and 52.
One test that fails on purpose asks whether an instruction of SH 2760 names section 38.

The row says what the pages print.
It types each table as the rows the deposited bands print, each row as the number printed under the heading for the previous section and the number printed under the heading for the new section, and it says nothing about what a table means.
It types the marks "[ב/19]", "[ר/12]" and "[ב/29]" as printed, and says nothing about what their letters stand for.
It quotes "בטלים" from SH 1110 section 3 for the four sections that section names, and the note of the New Version, "נוסח חדש זה בא במקום פקודת הרשמים, 1936", for the Ordinance; it says nothing of that kind in its own voice.
Every date it types from a footer is an issue date, and no page deposited prints a day of effect.
It types sections 1 and 25 of the Interpretation Law, 5741-1981, as SH 1030 prints them (issue dated 15.6.1981); the row does not say whether any of these provisions bears on the citing words.
It does not say how section 38 reads or should read, which appeal lies from a decision of a magistrates' court under the Law, or what a court would do.

## 2. Seat

The seat is a party to a proceeding in which the court makes a decision under the Law, or in which a registrar does, who wants to appeal it.
The row asks that person no question, because it records references: there is no input to ask for and no `@export`.

Quotation lines in the modules are of three kinds.
A `src:N` line is expanded by `tools/srcquote.py` from the excerpt, and N is the line number in the whole page, which the excerpt's header lists; `python3 -I tools/srcquote.py --check <excerpt> *.l4` exits 0.
An `ext:[tag]` line quotes the Hebrew read from the deposited image of the page the tag names (instrument and printed page), and may be skipped by `tools/hebcheck.py` only because it is a quotation line.
A `tr:[tag] | LATIN` line gives the Latin form of words quoted on the lines above it, and `tools/translit.py` checks it: the Latin must occur in the transliteration of the quoted Hebrew (the letters alef to dalet after a digit are A to D, the letters alef to he in brackets are (a) to (e), and a letter in brackets followed by a digit is a letter and the digit, so the bracket bet 1 is (b1)), and must occur as a string literal in the code; `python3 -I tools/translit.py *.l4` exits 0 (29 tr lines checked, 0 problems).
`translit.py` was copied from row il-74 with one rule added, for the bracket letter and digit.
Hebrew is typed in code only where the same words are on a quotation line; `python3 -I tools/hebcheck.py <excerpt> *.l4 NOTES.md` exits 0.
The Hebrew of the `ext:` lines was copied mechanically from the written reading of the pages, except the lines of the two table sub-headings, the cells of the tables, the first line of section 1 of the Law and the margin note of section 19, which were typed from the images; each was compared with the image on the crop, and the code strings were checked against the `ext:` lines by `tools/hebcheck.py`.

The Hebrew of every `ext:` line was read from the deposited image, and then compared with the text layer of the PDF page as a bag of words (word order in these layers is reversed or scrambled, so only the words were compared).
1086 words of two or more letters on the `ext:` lines of the 22 pages were compared; 1065 were found in the layers.
The 21 not found are by page: SH 535 printed page 184, two (words of the footer line); printed page 189, one (the layer glues a word to the year); SH 1123 printed page 198, two (footer); printed page 218, one (one word; the layer of that page has no usable section numbers); SH 233 printed page 148, two (footer); printed page 151, two (words of the lettered subsections); SH 400 printed page 122, two (footer); New Version 19 printed page 436, four (footer and note); SH 1110 printed page 82, three (footer and a word of the title glued to the year); SH 1030 printed page 302, two (footer).

The text layers of the 1957, 1963, 1968, 1971, 1974 and 1984 prints are partly wrong, and the image decides.

Hebrew calendar: each Hebrew date printed beside a Gregorian date on a footer was compared with a Hebrew-calendar routine (`hebcal.py`, not deposited) on 2026-11-11, after the control 30 Kislev 5785 = 31.12.2024 passed: 4 Av 5717 = 1.8.1957 (SH 233); 26 Tammuz 5723 = 18.7.1963 (SH 400); 15 Av 5728 = 9.8.1968 (SH 535); 13 Sivan 5741 = 15.6.1981 (SH 1030); 4 Adar II 5744 = 8.3.1984 (SH 1110); 3 Elul 5744 = 31.8.1984 (SH 1123); 24 Tammuz 5768 = 27.7.2008 (SH 2169); 16 Av 5770 = 27.7.2010 (SH 2253); 20 Kislev 5779 = 28.11.2018 (SH 2760).
