## 1. What this row is

The row records two items of the Law (the Purchase Tax (Goods and Services) Law, 5712-1952, "the Law") as typed values and tests them.
Item I1 is section 29C, which lists sections of the Customs Ordinance (sections 128, 170 and 171 among them).
SH 1195 (24.8.1986) added section 29C, and item 23 of SH 1533 (19.7.1995) replaces the last words of its list; the list still names sections 128, 170 and 171.
SH 1533 repeals section 128 in item 17 of the Customs Ordinance (Amendment No. 12) Law, and sections 170 and 171 in item 21(2) of the Customs Agents (Amendment) Law, both printed in the same issue.
Item I2 is section 29D, which lists section 144A of the Value Added Tax Law (and other sections).
SH 1195 added section 29D too; SH 1497 (28.12.1994) repeals section 144A in paragraph (15) of item 11; instructions of 1987, 2002 and 2014 edit the list of section 29D, and none of them removes section 144A.
Each item has two limbs: limb (a), the target is repealed, missing or says something else, read from an official instrument; limb (b), the citing words still read so in the latest official instrument read.
Section letters are written in Latin letters for the Hebrew letters the Law uses (29C is 29 gimel; 144A is 144 alef; 239B is 239 bet; (b) and (c) are the bracketed bet and gimel).
In the typed lists, `and-` stands for the prefix vav with maqaf and `to` for the word between two numbers that means "up to" (section 3 says how this was checked).

## 2. Seat

The seat is the dealer or importer to whom the list of Customs Ordinance provisions (I1) or of Value Added Tax Law provisions (I2) is applied for the purchase tax.
The row asks that individual no question, because it records references: there is no input to ask for and no `@export`.

## 7. The literal replay

The replay applies each instruction literally to the words of the list: the quoted words are looked for, nothing is added or removed but the quoted words, and no separator is supplied.
The modules use `CONCAT` and equality of strings only; the builtins listed in the skill's reference include no search of one string in another, so whether quoted words are in a text is typed (`the quoted words are in the text before the step`) and read, and where they are, the test joins the three parts and compares.

I1.
The list as SH 1195 prints it ends `..., 198, 204 and-231C`; the 1995 instruction replaces `and-231C` with `231C, 239A and-239B`; the literal result ends `..., 198, 204 231C, 239A and-239B`.
The literal result has a space and no comma between 204 and 231C; the consolidation's tail has a comma there (`204, 231C, 239A and-239B`, CO).
The numbers (tokens) of the list: twenty before, twenty-two after; 128, 170 and 171 are in both.

I2.
The list as SH 1195 prints it is `1A, 105 (c) and-144A`.
The 1987 instruction replaces the whole of it with `1A and-144A`; literal result `1A and-144A`.
The 2002 instruction inserts `106 and-112A` after `1A`; literal result `1A106 and-112A and-144A`.
The 2014 instruction, as the page prints it, inserts `102A,` after `1A,`, with a comma inside each pair of quotation marks; the literal text after the 2002 instruction contains no comma, so the quoted words `1A,` are not in it, and applied literally the instruction changes nothing: the literal result after the three later instructions is `1A106 and-112A and-144A`.
With punctuation ignored (the tokens), the instructions give `1A, 144A` after 1987, `1A, 106, 112A, 144A` after 2002 and `1A, 102A, 106, 112A, 144A` after 2014, which are the numbers the consolidation prints (`1A, 102A, 106, 112A and-144A`, CO).
144A is in the list in every one of these readings: no instruction read removes it.
The row does not say how a reader supplies separators or what the Law's text now is; it records what each instruction prints and what the literal application gives.
