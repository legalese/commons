In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.

### Statute text deposited 2026-10-20

Deposited from the Iowa Code 2026 edition, published by the Legislative Services Agency. It was the current edition on 2026-10-19; its official PDFs are stamped 9 December 2025. Each file is plain text converted from the chapter XML that the LSA publishes beside the PDF. Kept: chapter and subchapter headings, section numbers and headnotes, subsection and paragraph identifiers, the text, tables (rows as `| a | b |`), and the Code editor's history lines and footnotes. Acts of the 2026 legislative session are **not** incorporated; they will appear in the Iowa Code 2027. Each `.meta.json` gives the XML URL, the official PDF's URL, sha256 and edition stamp, the retrieval time (UTC), the sha256 of the text, and the character count.

- `registers/source-bundle/ia-code-ch-422-2026.txt` -- Iowa Code ch. 422, whole chapter. Subchapter I: definitions, including "Internal Revenue Code" (422.3). Subchapter II, personal net income tax: the flat 3.8% rate (422.5(1)(a); section 422.5A, "Tax rates", is repealed for tax years from 2025), net income computed from federal taxable income (422.7, including the subtraction of Social Security benefits taxable under IRC s86), the earned income tax credit at 15% of the federal credit (422.12B), and other credits. Subchapters III-X: corporate tax, financial institutions, administration, withholding (422.16) and others. 125 sections. 430,645 characters; sha256 of the download `9c74891e0545011275933069392a0f47d590709438c1cc2a90097e8c9a02c352`.
