## Seat (snap4)

**The seat is a member of a household that applies for, or receives, SNAP benefits.**
`snap4-seat.l4` asks 25 questions, each named "Under section N, ..." with `@export` and a plain `@desc` for every input, in six `§` groups in the text's order: who runs the program (2020(a)-(d)), applying and being certified (2020(e)(1)-(5)), acting for you and keeping your information private (2020(e)(7)-(8)), getting benefits quickly, hearings and lost benefits (2020(e)(9)-(11)), transitional benefits (2020(s)), and stores (2021).
Groups that only arise in a situation the person may not be in (the reservation question, recertification, hearings and lost benefits, transitional benefits) say so in a comment, so a form can ask "Has this happened to you?" first.
It calls the layer-1 rules and restates none: each question builds the module's inputs from plain answers (the modules' own choices, yes/no, numbers, dates) and calls the module's rule.
One input is fixed: for the question about the State agency's responsibility (2020(a)(2)) the module's seam input "the body is a State agency" is answered `JUST TRUE`, because the question is about the State agency; the seat comments say so.
Nothing is fixed that a rule reads except that one and the sanction in the stores question (disqualification, the first of the three the module names).
There is no seat for the State agency, the Secretary or a store: their duties appear in layer 1, and in layer 2 only for what they mean to the person.
Sections 2032, 2036b, 2036c and 2036d, and 2020(e)(12)-(26), (f)-(r) and (t)-(x), have no seat question: they bind the State agencies, the Secretary or grantees and say nothing a household member can ask about their own case.
`snap4-cases-seat.l4` has 65 assertions: a case for every question, with a "not covered" (`NOTHING`) case wherever the module can return one.

## Fork register (snap4)

A fork is a place where the text can be read two or more ways with different outcomes.
Each entry gives every reading, the words that license it, the outcome that differs, and the rule that encodes the reading taken.
Only the reading taken is a rule.
"Assumed, not ruled" applies to all three: no lawyer has ruled on any of them.
Numbered within snap4; the keel's register (KEEL-NOTES.md) has its own F-1 to F-5.
"Fork 5" in the coverage table is these three forks over five units (F-1 one unit, F-2 three units, F-3 one unit).

### F-1. s 2020(s)(2), lines 1609-1610: where does a period "of not more than 5 months after the date" end?

The words are: "a household may receive transitional supplemental nutrition assistance program benefits for a period of not more than 5 months after the date on which cash assistance is terminated".

| reading | licensed by | outcome that differs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 (taken): the period runs to the same day of the month five months on, and that last day is counted | "5 months after the date": counted from the date, the fifth month ends on the date's day-of-month, and a period "of not more than 5 months" is not over until that day is | termination on 15 January 2026: 15 June 2026 is inside the period (`JUST TRUE`) |
| R2: the period ends the day before | the five months are a span that includes the first day after termination, so the 15th of June would be a day over | the same day is outside the period (`JUST FALSE`) |

R1 is `s 2020(s)(2) the day falls within the transitional benefits period` and `s 2020(s)(2) the household may receive transitional benefits on the day` (tested on both sides of 15 June 2026).
R2 is not a rule: a caller who wants it moves the termination date back by one day.
The day of termination itself is not "after" it: the rule answers `NOTHING` for it (limited by its own wording).

### F-2. s 2021(b)(1), (2) and (4), lines 1713, 1714 and 1723: do the 5- and 10-year tiers cap a disqualification for a false application?

The words are: "(b) ... a disqualification under subsection (a) shall be— (1) for a reasonable period of time, not to exceed 5 years, upon the first occasion of disqualification; (2) ... not to exceed 10 years, upon the second occasion ...; (3) permanent upon— (A) the third occasion ...; (4) for a reasonable period of time to be determined by the Secretary, including permanent disqualification, on the knowing submission of an application ... that contains false information about a substantive matter".

| reading | licensed by | outcome that differs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 (taken): ground (4) governs a disqualification for a false application on the first or second occasion: the Secretary determines the period, permanent included | (4) names its own ground and says "including permanent disqualification"; a rule that capped it at 5 or 10 years would leave those words nothing to do on the first two occasions | a first disqualification, for a false application, imposed permanently: allowed (`JUST TRUE`) |
| R2: (1) and (2) cap it | the list is one sentence ("shall be—"), each limb is a requirement, and (1) and (2) speak of "the first occasion of disqualification" without exception | the same disqualification: not allowed (`JUST FALSE`) |

On either reading a third or later occasion is permanent under (3)(A).
R1 is `s 2021(b) the disqualification the text requires` and `s 2021(b) the period imposed is allowed`.

### F-3. s 2036c(a), lines 3286-3287: which day ends "the 180-day period beginning on February 7, 2014"?

The words are: "Not later than 1 year after the date specified by the Secretary during the 180-day period beginning on February 7, 2014".

| reading | licensed by | outcome that differs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 (taken): February 7 is the first day, so the last is August 5, 2014 | "beginning on February 7": a period that begins on a day includes it | a date specified on 6 August 2014 is outside the period |
| R2: the count starts on February 8, so the last day is August 6, 2014 | the usual rule of counting days "after" a date, excluding the date itself | the same date is inside the period |

R1 is `s 2036c(a) the date specified falls within the 180-day period beginning on February 7, 2014`.
The period is spent; the fork matters only for a reader asking what the Secretary could lawfully specify.

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (snap4)

The rule of 2026-10-29 leaves two readings to a lawyer, and this section does not decide them; it takes them as the status quo and lists every branch that rests on one, with the other reading.
- **Reading (a):** a failed "if" (or "unless", "until", "while", "except") counts as the text's own no, clause by clause, within the provision that holds it; and a flat negative ("shall not", "may not", "there shall be no") counts as the text saying no. The other reading: "if A then B" does not itself say "if not A then not B", and a negative is limited to the case it describes, so the answer outside is `NOTHING`.
- **Reading (b):** a grant or prohibition limited by its own wording ("on application of eligible entities", "for a period of not more than 5 months", "to any eligible household") does not say no outside the limit; the answer is `NOTHING`. The other reading: outside the limit the grant does not apply, so the answer is `JUST FALSE`.
- **Added by snap4, a third kind of (a):** a ceiling or floor ("up to $100,000", "not to exceed 5 years", "not less than $15,000,000", "limited to 2 years") is read as the text saying no beyond the limit. The other reading answers `NOTHING` beyond it. This is the largest group of `JUST FALSE` branches in the ranges, and the brief's list of "if" variants does not mention it.
- Branches that say no because of a definition ("means"), or a list closed with "only", are not marginal and are not listed.

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (snap4)

(The rule of 2026-10-29, assumed, not ruled by a lawyer.)
Nearly every decision function in this folder answers a `MAYBE BOOLEAN`.
- **`JUST TRUE`** is the Act saying yes: "the State agency must promptly restore the benefits", "a household may apply on the day it first goes to an office".
- **`JUST FALSE`** is the Act saying no in its own words, and only that. Here that is:
  a flat negative ("the State agency's responsibility shall not be affected" by State or county operation, 2020(a)(2); "there shall be no discrimination by reason of race, ..." 2020(c)(1); "shall include no additional requirements" 2020(e)(5); "allotments shall not be restored for any period of time more than one year prior", 2020(e)(11); "shall not be subject to judicial or administrative review", 2021(e)(2), 2021(g)(2)(C); "shall not be liable for the value of any interest", 2021(h)(3));
  a ceiling or floor ("up to $100,000", "not to exceed 5 years", "not less than $15,000,000", "not more than 10 pilot projects");
  a condition the text introduces with "if", "unless", "until" or "except", when the condition fails ("if the program disqualification is upheld, may be subject to forfeiture", 2021(h)(2)(B)(i); "unless a State documents that ...", 2032(a)(1)(C));
  a list the text closes with "only" ("Only funds appropriated under paragraph (1) in advance ... shall be available", 2036d(e)(2)); and a definition that says "means" ("eligible entity", "public agency", "fee agent", "indication of multiple issuance").
  Every such branch carries a `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<the words>"` comment, and the words are quoted from the text.
- **`NOTHING`** is "not covered": the Act does not say, or the case is outside what the sentence grants or prohibits.
  Here that is: a State that takes no part in SNAP (2020(a)(1)); a case outside the wording of a grant or duty ("on application of eligible entities", "On implementation of the approved system", "a household that ceases to receive cash assistance"); a matter the text leaves to the Secretary's determination, standards or regulations, which are not in these sources (the reservation determinations in 2020(d), the Secretary's restrictions on representation in 2020(e)(7), the period of a disqualification imposed under 2021(b)(4)); a list the text does not close ("shall include"); and silence about what follows from a requirement not met (2036d(c)(2)).
  Every such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<why>)` where `<why>` is one of: silent, list not closed, includes, undefined term, points outside the text, limited by its own wording.
- **A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.** In the seat layer an answer of "not covered" means the Act does not answer the question as asked, not that the household has no right.
- A few functions answer a plain `BOOLEAN` or `NUMBER` instead: a date test (a spent deadline), a period test (days against a period the Secretary determines), or a sum the text fixes (the largest 2036c(b) reduction, 50 percent of the amount otherwise payable). They carry no "no" because they decide nothing the text could be silent about.
- The two readings "for a lawyer" are flagged, not decided, in the next section.
