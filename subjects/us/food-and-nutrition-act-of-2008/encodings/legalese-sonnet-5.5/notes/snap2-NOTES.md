### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (snap2)

Every layer-1 rule answers one of three things (a `MAYBE BOOLEAN`, or an outcome type of its own whose last constructor is "not covered").

- **JUST TRUE**: the text says yes.
- **JUST FALSE**: the text says no. It is used in two cases only, and each branch carries a comment `-- FALSE-kind (the text says no in its own words): "<quoted words>"`. (i) The text says no in its own words ("shall not", "no household shall"). (ii) Reading (a), below: a failed "if", "only if", "unless", "until" or "in the event", or a flat negative ("No X that ..."), counts as the text's no, within the provision it appears in.
- **NOTHING**: the text does not decide. Each such branch carries `-- NOTHING-kind (<kind>)`, where the kind is one of: `silent`, `list not closed`, `includes`, `undefined term`, `points outside the text`, `limited by its own wording`.

**A caller must never turn NOTHING into FALSE.** "The text does not say" is not "the text says no": a household that falls outside a rule's class has not been told it is eligible or ineligible, it has been told nothing.
The seat layer keeps this: it wraps each plain answer in JUST, so NOTHING can come back only from a place where the rendering does not decide.

The two readings every marginal branch rests on:

- **Reading (a)**: a failed "if / only if / unless / until / in the event" and a flat negative count as the text's no, within the provision. Alternative: the failed condition is silent about the opposite case, and the answer is NOTHING.
- **Reading (b)**: a grant or prohibition limited by its own wording (an affirmative relative-clause class such as "Any person who has been found ...", "A household required to report less often than once each 3 months ...", a "when", a "upon", a stated period or complement) gives NOTHING outside the limit. Alternative: the limit is itself the text's no outside it.
  **How the wording of a bound decides (rule of 2026-11-04, from the triage).** The brief's list of FALSE grounds is made of words, so a bound is read by its words. A bound worded with a negative ("not less often than ... but not more often than" in s 2015(c)(1)(D)(i); "does not exceed" in s 2015(d)(1)(B); "not more than 3 years" in s 2015(d)(2); "No operating agency shall require" in s 2029(c)) answers JUST FALSE outside it. A bound with no negative in its wording ("of up to thirty days" in s 2029(e); "for a period of up to one year" in s 2015(h)) is a stated period of a grant, which the brief names as a NOTHING ground, and answers NOTHING outside it. In plain English "up to" and "not more than" mean the same, so this is a split by words and not by meaning: a lawyer who reads them as one shape moves s 2029(e), the "up to one year" branch of s 2015(h) and (for a date type, not a boolean) the (III) cap of s 2015(d)(1)(C) together with the negative-worded bounds, in whichever direction the ruling goes.
  Taken on 2026-11-04 for the two paragraphs where I had first answered the other way, (b)(1) and (c)(1)(D)(ii): outside such a class the answer is NOTHING, as everywhere else in the part.

Counts in layer 1 (comments in the nine modules): 122 FALSE-kind branches, all quoted; 176 NOTHING-kind branches: 130 `limited by its own wording`, 22 `points outside the text`, 18 `silent`, 3 `undefined term`, 2 `includes`, 1 `list not closed`.
`work/tools/verifyquotes.py` found every quoted fragment in the rendering (363 fragments, 0 not found); `work/tools/lintkinds.py` found a kind comment near each of 256 FALSE or NOTHING answers, 0 without.

Duties are encoded as "X is required to ..." (JUST TRUE when the conditions hold, NOTHING otherwise, because the text creates no duty outside them), with a regulative rule (`PARTY / MUST / WITHIN / HENCE / LEST`) where the text gives a deadline.
There are four: `s 2015(b)(2) the State agency must proceed against the individual`, the thirty-day rule of `s 2015(c)(2)(A)`, and the two in s 2027(d) (sixty days, seven days).
The stable binary cannot assert on a `#TRACE`, so each such rule is run as a trace (three in `snap2-tests-2015a-c`, four in `snap2-tests-2027`) and its outcome read by eye; each also has an ordinary decision function with assertions.
