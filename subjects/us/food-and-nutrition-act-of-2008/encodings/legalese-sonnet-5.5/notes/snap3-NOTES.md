## How to read the L4

- Every top-level name, type and constructor begins with its section label (`s 2016(h)(12)(B)(ii)(II) the State agency made the stored benefits available in time`), so no two parts can collide.
  Helpers begin `snap3 ` (`snap3-logic.l4`).
- A rule answers `MAYBE BOOLEAN` (or `MAYBE NUMBER`, `MAYBE DATE`).
  `JUST TRUE` means the text says yes, `JUST FALSE` means the text says no in its own words, and `NOTHING` means the text does not say.
  A caller must never turn `NOTHING` into `FALSE`.
  The reading rule behind this is under "Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING" below.
- A judgment the Act hands to the Secretary or to a State agency is a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` input.
  `NOTHING` there means no determination was supplied.
- A duty with a stated time is a function that answers `JUST FALSE` when the time was missed; a duty with no stated time answers `JUST TRUE` once done and `NOTHING` otherwise.
  Two duties of a State agency, each with a stated time limit and a stated consequence, are also written as regulative rules and traced: `s 2016(h)(12)(B)(ii)(II) duty to make the stored benefits available within 48 hours of the request` and `s 2016(h)(12)(C)(ii)(I) duty to give sufficient notice before expunging` (four `#TRACE` directives in `snap3-tests-s2016-h.l4`: fulfilled at the limit, breach one tick later).

## Marginal readings for a lawyer (snap3)

The brief fixes two readings as the status quo and leaves them to a lawyer: (a) a failed "if" counts as the text's no, clause by clause, within the provision that holds the "if" (an inference: "if A then B" does not itself say "if not A then not B"), and a flat negative counts as the text saying no; (b) a prohibition or grant limited by its own wording does not.
Every branch of mine that rests on one of them is listed below, with the other reading stated: 256 branches, of which 115 rest on (a) (the answer is `JUST FALSE`; the other reading is `NOTHING`) and 141 rest on (b) (the answer is `NOTHING`; the other reading is `JUST TRUE` or `JUST FALSE`, as the next paragraph says).
Ten more `JUST FALSE` branches answer a definition that says "means" or "has the meaning"; they are firm and are not listed.
The other reading of a (b) branch is not always `JUST FALSE`.
For a prohibition, an exemption, a ceiling or a necessary condition ("only"), the case that conforms to the wording is answered `NOTHING` here and `JUST TRUE` on the other reading: section 2016(c)(2)'s bar on a public official's name, section 2016(f)(4)(A)'s exemption, section 2016a(b)(2)(B)'s ceiling, section 2016(a)'s "only to households which have been duly certified".
For a grant or duty limited to a class or a scope, the case outside the class or scope is answered `NOTHING` here and `JUST FALSE` on the other reading: section 2016(i)(1), section 2017(c)(3), the eighteen months of section 2024(b)(1).
The table's last column says "`JUST TRUE` or `JUST FALSE`" for that reason, and the quoted words show which.
The table is generated from the `-- FALSE-kind` and `-- NOTHING-kind (limited by its own wording)` comments in the layer-1 files by `work/marginal.py` (not shipped), so it is as current as the files; the words are the text's, copied into those comments.
The "rests on" label separates a flat negative ("shall not", "may not", "nothing ... shall") from a failed condition ("only", "unless", "until", "if", "not later than") by a keyword match, so treat that label as a first sort and the quoted words as the evidence.
Of the 201 `NOTHING` branches in layer 1 that carry a kind comment (a bare pass-through, `WHEN NOTHING THEN NOTHING`, carries none), 141 are limited by their own wording, 39 are silent, 18 point outside the text and 3 are lists that are not closed; only the 141 are marginal.

Judgments beyond the mechanical table, each assumed, not ruled:

1. **A missed time limit is the text's no (reading a).**
   "Not later than October 1, 2002", "within ten days", "not later than 48 hours after request": a duty whose time is stated and missed answers `JUST FALSE` (the table's "failed condition" rows quoting "not later than" or "within").
   The other reading: the text names a time but does not say that a late act is a nullity or that the duty is gone, so `NOTHING`.
2. **A duty with no stated time never answers `FALSE`.**
   "The Secretary shall periodically inform State agencies" (section 2016(h)(5)) has no time, so the function answers `JUST TRUE` once it has been done and `NOTHING` otherwise.
   The other reading would count "not done yet" as the text's no; the text does not say that.
3. **"Only" limits and does not grant (reading b).**
   Section 2016(a) says EBT cards "shall be issued only to households which have been duly certified": an uncertified household gets `JUST FALSE`, and a certified household gets `NOTHING`, not `JUST TRUE`, because the limit is necessary and not sufficient.
   The other reading: the sentence also commands ("shall be issued"), so a certified household gets `JUST TRUE`.
4. **The one exception to item 3 is section 2016(b).**
   "Benefits issued to eligible households shall be used by them only to purchase food ..." both commands the use and limits it, so a conforming use answers `JUST TRUE`.
   The other reading: apply item 3 here too and answer `NOTHING`.
5. **Two hortatory clauses are encoded as always-`NOTHING` functions.**
   Section 2016(h)(1)(B) ("is encouraged") and (D) ("should take into account") create no duty, permission or power, so `s 2016(h)(1)(B) the State agency is required to implement the system as soon as practicable` and `s 2016(h)(1)(D) the electronic benefit transfer system is required to take into account the standard operating rules` answer `NOTHING` (silent), asserted.
   The other reading: they are not operative (reason 1) and need no rule; or "encouraged" and "should" are read as the text's no to "required" (`JUST FALSE`).
6. **Guidance is filed with rule-making (inert 2).**
   Section 2016a(a)(1) orders the Secretary to "issue guidance"; I filed it with the regulation-making powers.
   The other reading: it is a duty with no stated time, to be encoded as item 2.
7. **A Secretary's or State agency's determination is an input, never guessed.**
   Where the Act says "as determined by the Secretary" or "if the Secretary determines", the input is a `MAYBE BOOLEAN` and `NOTHING` means no determination was supplied.
   This is a design choice, not a reading of the text; the other design makes the input a `BOOLEAN`, which forces the caller to turn a missing determination into `FALSE`.
8. **The three one-year limits of section 2023(b) are alternatives (fork F-2023-1).**
   The other reading is cumulative.
9. **Section 2017(c)(1)'s $10 floor is a flat negative.**
   "No allotment may be issued ... if the value ... is less than $10" is `JUST FALSE` below $10 (reading a); the other reading is `NOTHING`.
10. **A cap on what someone may do is answered by the clause's wording, and the split is the weak point.**

### Reading a result: FALSE and NOTHING (snap3)

Every rule here answers one of three things, and a caller must keep them apart.

- **`JUST TRUE`** means the text says yes: the household may do the thing, the State agency must, the court shall.
- **`JUST FALSE`** is the text saying no in its own words.
  In this Act that is: the card may bear no public official's name; benefits are not income or resources for any purpose; a coupon not redeemed within a year is not redeemable; the State agency shall not reduce the monthly allotment by more than the greater of 10 percent or $10; the Secretary may not limit the authority of the judges; a forfeiture does not reach an owner who did not know; a duty with a stated time that was missed; a condition the text introduces with "if", "only", "unless" or "until" that the facts fail.
  A "no" always names a rule: it is not the absence of a rule.
- **`NOTHING`** is the text not saying.
  In this Act that is: a certified household and the question whether it must be given a card (section 2016(a) limits who may receive one and does not grant one); a Secretary's determination that has not been supplied; a time the text does not state; a rate, cost or standard the text leaves to the Secretary or to regulations (the cost of the thrifty food plan, the rate of interest, the extent of State liability for mailed authorizations); a term the text leaves undefined ("excessive requests", "sufficient notice", "reasonable period"); a case outside a limit the text wrote into the permission (the court may suspend a person "for an additional period of up to eighteen months": a longer suspension is not covered, it is not forbidden by that sentence either).

`NOTHING` is never `FALSE`.
A household that gets `NOTHING` to "may an EBT card be issued to my household?" has not been told no: this subsection does not answer, and the household asks the section that does (section 2020(e) for certification).
A caller who needs a yes-or-no must say which side bears the risk of the silence; this deposit does not decide it.
The seat questions that return a `MAYBE` pass the layer-1 answer through unchanged: `NOTHING` stays `NOTHING`, and the header comment of `snap3-seat.l4` says what each of the three answers means.

## What the text turns out to say (snap3)

Numbered findings from encoding and testing; the first four are about the converter's rendering and the Act's own words, the rest are about the figures and the stable binary.

1. **Section 2016(f)(3)(B) as rendered names subsection (i).**
   It says "only an EBT card issued under subsection (i) shall be eligible for exchange at any retail food store" (line 943).
   Subsection (i) is the State option to issue benefits to individuals made ineligible by welfare reform (line 1106); subsection (h) is the electronic benefit transfer subsection (line 976).
   I encoded the paragraph as written (fork F-2016-2); a reader who thinks (h) was meant changes one rule.
   The converter omits the note that might explain the letter.
2. **8 percent of the published $306 is $24.48, but USDA publishes $25 as the minimum allotment.**
   `s 2017(a) the minimum allotment, 8 percent of the cost for 1 member rounded to the nearest whole dollar` gives 24 for a cost of 306 and gives 25 only for a cost of at least $306.25 (half up: 0.08 times 306.25 is 24.5).
   The memo prints $306 as the maximum allotment for one person (line 61) and $25 as the minimum (line 151), and prints no thrifty food plan cost.
   The Act and the memo can both hold: section 2017(a) rounds an allotment down to the nearest lower whole dollar (line 1263), so if $306 is the allotment of a one-person household with no net income, the cost is at least $306 and under $307, and 8 percent of a cost from $306.25 to just under $307 rounds to 25.
   The text does not define "maximum allotment" and the memo does not print the cost, so this is a condition under which the two agree and not a finding of what USDA did.
   The module takes the USDA figure as the minimum (`snap3-s2017-cola.l4:58`) and takes the Act's formula as the rule (`snap3-s2017.l4:45`); the tests record both (`snap3-tests-s2017-cola.l4:93` and `snap3-tests-s2017.l4:29-31`).
3. **The USDA "9-17" row is a per-additional-person figure and "18+" is a total cap.**

   The rows are written out in `snap3-s2017-cola.l4` and tested at sizes 1 to 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 17 and 18 in `snap3-tests-s2017-cola.l4`.
4. **Section 2024(b)(1) says "processed" where it must mean "possessed".**

## What the stable binary does not accept (snap3)

Where the skills describe a construct the binary rejects, the binary governs.
Loud failures (the binary prints an error) first, then the silent one.

Loud:

- **`WAIT UNTIL` (two words) is not defined; it is spelled `WAITUNTIL`.**
  I used `WAITUNTIL` in the traces.
- **`... NOT EQUALS JUST TRUE` is a parse error.**
  Write `NOT (x EQUALS JUST TRUE)`.
- **`NOT a OR NOT b` parses as `NOT (a OR NOT b)`.**
  One assertion went 154 of 155 until I parenthesised each `NOT` (the brief warns of this).
- **No `REFUSE`, `REFUSED` or `BECAUSE` except in `LEST BREACH ... BECAUSE`.**
  A case outside the encoding is `NOTHING` or a "not covered" constructor, asserted.
- **A `DEONTIC` cannot be compared with `EQUALS` in an `#ASSERT`.**
  A regulative rule is run with `#TRACE`, and the same duty is also written as a decision function that the assertions test.
  `check.sh` counts assertions only, so the four trace outcomes in `snap3-tests-s2016-h.l4` (lines 166-175) are not in the 1049: they print as `FULFILLED` at the limit (48 hours; 12 days) and `DEONTIC BREACHED: BREACH BY the State agency BECAUSE "..."` one tick later.
  I compared those four outcomes by hand between 1.6.98 and 1.6.108: identical.
- **A `MAY` rule with a `WITHIN` deadline that has passed reports `FULFILLED` on `#TRACE`.**
  I measured it with a probe (not shipped): a `MAY` rule `WITHIN 10 HENCE FULFILLED`, traced with the act at tick 10 (in time), at tick 11 (late) and with `WAITUNTIL 11` and no act, reports `FULFILLED` in all three.
  So a trace cannot show that a permission has lapsed, and only `MUST` rules are traced.
  This is a loud-looking result that is easy to misread as success: it is not an error, it is an uninformative `FULFILLED`.
- **A name defined in two modules that are imported together cannot be used at all.**
  Every name carries its section label for that reason.
- **A constructor shared by two types, or a record field named like a constructor.**
  PLAN.md rule 6 states that this compiles and errors only where it is used; I did not test that.
  I declared each constructor once, under its type's label, and ended each test file with a section that touches every constructor and field I declared (PLAN.md rule 6).

Silent (the binary accepts it and returns something unintended):

- **Positional record construction (`Type OF a, b`) inside a larger expression: I parenthesise it every time**, as in `( \`Table row\` OF 1, ( ... ) )`, and the tests read each cell of each row.
  I did not measure what happens without the parentheses, so I make no claim about how it fails.
- **A quote with an elision (`...`) in a `-- FALSE-kind` comment is not found by `verifyq.py`.**
  This is my checker, not the binary: three occurrences were caught as "NOT FOUND" and rewritten as one contiguous piece of the text.
- **`add years` clamps 29 February to 28 February in the following year** (probe: `add years (Date 29 2 2024) 1` is `DATE OF 28, 2, 2025`), with no warning (fork F-2022-1).
