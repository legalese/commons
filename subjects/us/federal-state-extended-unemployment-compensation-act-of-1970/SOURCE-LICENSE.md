**Why a note, and what that means.** The Act was never classified to a section of the Code. The OLRC reproduces it, as amended, in a note, and this note is the only Code text of it. It is kept here as the operative text, unlike the other notes, which are dropped from the federal deposits. The note's opening paragraph lists the amending laws reflected, the last being Pub. L. 112-240 (Jan. 2, 2013). The text is **unofficial**: the Statutes at Large text, as amended, is the law, and the note is an editorial reproduction of it. Bracketed matter (catch-lines such as "[Payment of Extended Compensation]", "[formerly I.R.C. 1954]", "[probably means subpar. (C)]") is OLRC editorial matter, kept as printed. After the Act, the note sets out effective-date and related provisions of the amending laws in brackets. These are kept as printed.

**Not deposited.** The other notes under 26 U.S.C. 3304 are not deposited. They include several expired temporary programmes ("Temporary Extended Unemployment Compensation", "Full Federal Funding of Extended Unemployment Compensation for a Limited Period" and others). Later temporary changes to extended benefits that are not reflected in this note are also not deposited, for example the 2020 pandemic-era provisions. Those provisions are unverified (general knowledge), and they were not read in any deposited text.

**Connected text.** 26 U.S.C. 3304(a)(11) makes an extended benefit programme under this Act a condition of approving a State law. It is deposited in `../internal-revenue-code-of-1986/registers/source-bundle/usc26-ch23.txt`.
