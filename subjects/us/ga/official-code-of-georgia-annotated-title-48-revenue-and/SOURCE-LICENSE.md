# Source license

The text of **Official Code of Georgia Annotated, Title 48 (Revenue and Taxation), Chapter 7 — Income Taxes** is published on the [Official Code of Georgia Annotated, public access edition — published for the Georgia Code Revision Commission by LexisNexis (linked from the Georgia General Assembly site as 'Georgia Code')](http://www.lexisnexis.com/hottopics/gacode).

- **Terms**: This is the code that was at issue in Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 590 U.S. 255 (2020), where the Supreme Court held that the whole O.C.G.A. — the statutory text and the annotations prepared by LexisNexis under the Code Revision Commission's supervision — is uncopyrightable under the government edicts doctrine, because the Commission acts as an arm of the legislature in its legislative capacity. What the site asserts today does not reflect that. The public access edition still carries a blanket footer 'Copyright © 2026 LexisNexis.' linking to LexisNexis's own copyright page, which states that materials on the site may not be copied 'in whole or in part, for any reason', and its general terms forbid commercial redistribution and removing copyright notices. The site's own banner confirms the arrangement: the resources are made available by the Code Revision Commission through a contract with LexisNexis, which prepares and maintains the site. So: no copyright subsists in the O.C.G.A. itself, and reuse and adaptation of the Code are lawful, but the restriction on this particular delivery platform is contractual and still bites on anyone taking the text from that site. A copy of the Code should be sourced elsewhere rather than scraped from advance.lexis.com.

In the United States the text of a statute is not copyrightable: it is an edict of government (*Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org*, 590 U.S. 255 (2020)). A register's copyright notice can still cover a publisher's annotations, headnotes and numbering, which this corpus does not reproduce.
