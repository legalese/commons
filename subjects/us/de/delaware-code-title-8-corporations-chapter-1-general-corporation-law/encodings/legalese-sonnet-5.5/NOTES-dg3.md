## Seat

The seat is **a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock** in a Delaware corporation: an incorporator, a director or officer, or a stockholder, including a minority stockholder; and, for sections 162 to 166, a subscriber for stock.
Sections 151 to 174 and 201 to 203 speak to the corporation and to the people who deal in its stock: they say what a class or series may be, how stock is issued and for what, what is capital and surplus, when the corporation may buy back or redeem its shares, what a holder or subscriber still owes, how a lost certificate is replaced, when a dividend may be paid and what a director risks by paying it unlawfully, how stock may be restricted, and when a business combination with a 15% holder is barred. Those are the questions such a person asks.
Questions are phrased as the person asks them ("Must I ...?", "Am I ...?", "May our directors ...?"); a question about stock "like mine" speaks for the class or series, and a question about "our corporation" speaks for the corporation of which the person is a director or officer.
This is the individual's seat required by the Commons ruling of 2026-10-18. No further seat layer (for example the corporation itself) was written: the questions asked by a director or officer already speak for the corporation ("our corporation"), and the stockholder's side of each relation is in the questions about stock "like mine".
There is no seat for an official. The Court of Chancery (sections 168 and 203(e)), the Secretary of State (the filing of a certificate, section 103) and the State (section 159, the tax exemption) appear only as what their acts mean for the person: what the owner of a lost certificate may apply for, what must be served and by when, what bond must be given, what limits the corporation's liability, in which court a section 203 matter lies, and whether the State may tax the stock.
Every question takes plain inputs (yes or no, a number, a date, or a choice from the Law's own list), each with an `@desc` worded as the person would be asked. A percentage is entered on a 0 to 100 scale and the one question that returns a percentage returns it on the same scale. A vote is counted in votes, not shares (section 203(c)(8)).
Three kinds of input are the outcome of something outside this range or of someone else's judgment, and are asked as facts: whether a certificate has been executed, acknowledged, filed and become effective under section 103 (dg1); a court's satisfaction, a board's judgment, "good faith", "a reasonable time", "as soon as practicable", "not manifestly unreasonable"; and the Law's references to other statutes (Article 8 of Title 6, the Investment Company Act of 1940, the Internal Revenue Code, the Securities Exchange Act).
Records that layer 1 expects are built inside each question, in its `WHERE`, from those plain answers, and the same layer-1 rule is then called; no rule is restated in layer 2. A record field that the called rule never reads is given a fixed placeholder, with a comment saying so.
The questions follow the order of the text, in `§` groups: section 151; 152 and 153; 154 to 158; 159 to 169; 170 to 174 and 201 and 202; 203(a); 203(b); 203(c); 203(d) and (e); and section 203 as a whole.
One deliberate departure from the keel: its instruction 15 recommends `inert` for the "facts" sentence ("no case fact answers it"), but the brief's list of reasons for `inert` does not include that reason, so the three "facts" sentences in this range (sections 151(a), 152(c), 157(d)) are encoded as inclusive definitions (finding 1) and asked as seat questions.

## Jural relations

Hohfeld's correlatives: a claim is matched by a duty, a privilege by a no-right, a power by a liability, an immunity by a disability. Each relation is stated once in layer 1, as a relation between two parties, and named neutrally; the seat asks it from the person's side.
`none` marks a provision that creates no relation (a definition, a counting rule, a rule of form).

## Coverage table

A section heading and a History line each get their own `inert` row (a pure title, a pure citation); the text of each section is covered by one row per section, and by one row per subsection or paragraph wherever the subsections differ in what they state.

There is no `repealed or blank in this text` row: nothing in this range is struck, repealed or printed as a run of dots.
A row whose provision carries a reading the text leaves open has the disposition `fork`: the rule encodes the reading taken (labelled F1 to F21 in the module and in the fork register below), and the note names the fork. No heading in this range carries a bracketed application note (`[For application of this section, see ...]`), and no section is printed `[Reserved.]`.

## Needs from the core

Nothing was missing from the core that blocked this piece; the core's `Where a provision is made`, `Kind of corporation`, `Kind of entity`, `Actor`, `Vote requirement` and `Vote base`, `Period` and the date arithmetic, and `YMD` were used as they are.
Three things the lead may want to promote from this piece, none of them needed to merge:

1. **The "facts ascertainable outside the instrument" device.** Sections 151(a), 152(c) and 157(d) each carry the same two-part device (an instrument may make a term depend on facts outside it if the instrument says clearly how the facts operate; "facts" includes an event, a determination or an action by any person or body). This piece defines three local rules, one per section (`s 151(a) the matter is a fact ...`, `s 152(c) ...`, `s 157(d) ...`), each returning `JUST TRUE` for an event or determination and `NOTHING` otherwise. The keel's table lists the same device in other pieces' sections (102(d), 251 to 257, 263 to 267, 388, 390); a single shared rule and a single shared rule for "the instrument says how the facts operate" would replace the copies. I did not read those sections.
2. **A rule for "a percentage of voting stock is a percentage of the votes"** (`s 203(c)(8)`), which is local to section 203 ("as used in this section only") and stays local.
3. **A way to name a vote base that is a group of directors** ("the members of the board then in office who were directors prior to ..."). Section 203(b)(6)(iii) needs it; this piece uses the core's `another base named in the provision` with a string, which works but names nothing a checker can compare.

## What the text turns out to say

Each finding is something the text says, or does not say, that a reader might not expect. Where a worked case in `dg3-cases-seat.l4` (or an assertion in a layer-1 test file) pins it, the finding ends "Asserted."; the case is named by the number of the seat question it answers (the questions are in `dg3-seat.l4` in the order of the text).

1. **The "facts" sentences are inclusive definitions.** Sections 151(a), 152(c) and 157(d) say that "facts" "includes, but is not limited to, the occurrence of any event, including a determination or action by any person or body". An event, a determination or an action is a fact (`JUST TRUE`); whether anything else is one the text does not say, so that case is `NOTHING`, never `FALSE`. (Seat questions 2, 26 and 53.) Asserted.
2. **The registered-investment-company exception in 151(b)(1) names two triggers only.** Stock of a registered investment company may be made redeemable "by the corporation at its option or at the option of the holders", which lifts the full-voting proviso; a redemption "upon the happening of a specified event" is not mentioned in paragraph (1), so the proviso still applies to it. (Seat question 4.) Asserted.
3. **The number of shares of a series cannot be changed by certificate without the resolution's leave, and the text does not say what happens if the resolution forbids it.** Section 151(g) lets the number be raised (not above the authorised class total) or lowered (not below the number outstanding) "unless otherwise provided in any such resolution"; where the resolution does otherwise provide, the answer is `NOTHING`. The class total and the number outstanding are inclusive limits ("not above", "not below"). (Seat question 15.) Asserted.
4. **Section 153 requires no consideration at all for shares without par value.** `s 153 the consideration required for the shares, if any` is `NOTHING` for them, not zero: the section says the consideration "is determined" under section 152, and gives no floor. (Seat question 31.) Asserted.
5. **A board determination of capital that is made in time but is below the permitted floor leaves the capital undetermined.** Section 154 requires an amount "in excess of" the aggregate par value for an issue that includes shares without par value, and "only equal to" it where all the shares have par value; it does not say what the capital is if the board falls short, so the answer is `NOTHING`. A late determination, by contrast, is replaced by the default (aggregate par plus the consideration for shares without par). (Seat question 36.) Asserted.
6. **A transfer from net assets to capital cannot exceed the net assets in excess of capital.** Section 154 speaks of "a portion of the net assets of the corporation in excess of the amount so determined to be capital"; a larger transfer is not provided for and is `NOTHING`. (Seat question 38.) Asserted.
7. **Section 159's tax exemption is silent about everyone else.** "No stock or bonds ... shall be taxed by this State when the same shall be owned by nonresidents of this State, or by foreign corporations": for a resident the sentence says nothing, and the answer is `NOTHING`, not `FALSE`. (Seat question 62.) Asserted.
8. **The saving of notes in 160(a)(1) is disjunctive as printed.** A note given for an acquisition survives "if at the time such note ... was delivered by the corporation its capital was not then impaired **or** did not thereby become impaired": a note delivered when the capital was already impaired, but which did not make it worse, is saved; so is a note delivered when the capital was sound even if the note then impaired it. Only a note delivered when the capital was impaired and which impaired it further is not saved. (Seat question 65.) Asserted.
9. **Subrogation under 174(c) reaches only stockholders who received with knowledge.** For a stockholder who did not, and where no claim has been successfully asserted against the director, the section gives no amount (`NOTHING`); where the total received by knowing stockholders is zero there is no proportion (`NOTHING`). (Seat question 113.) Asserted.
10. **"Control" in 203(c)(4) is the power to direct, presumed at 20%.** With no power to direct and no presumption (below 20%, or the presumption rebutted, or a good-faith nominee holding), the person does not have control: `JUST FALSE`, not `NOTHING`. (Seat question 147.) Asserted.
11. **Section 170(a)(2) is open only when there is no surplus at all.** A corporation with some surplus, but less than the dividend, may not pay the excess out of net profits: the net-profits route is reached "in case there shall be no such surplus" (F16). For a nonstock corporation the capital is deemed zero, so the whole of the net assets is surplus. Net profits for the current and the preceding fiscal year may be added ("and/or"). (Seat question 100.) Asserted.
12. **The 12-month delay and its exclusion apply only to a corporation that has had a listed or widely held class or has elected to be governed.** Under 203(b)(3), a corporation that never had such a class and never elected in is freed from the day the amendment takes effect, and persons who became interested stockholders earlier are not excluded; for every other corporation the amendment takes effect 12 months later and does not reach anyone who became an interested stockholder on or before the amendment's effective day (F18). (Seat question 129 and the whole-section scenarios of question 156.) Asserted.
13. **A listing or holder count caused by an interested stockholder does not take the corporation out of the (b)(4) exemption.** The "unless" clause in 203(b)(4) is read as disregarding it (F17). Exactly 2,000 holders of record is not "more than 2,000". (Seat question 131 and the whole-section scenarios.) Asserted.
14. **The three years in 203(a) include their last day.** A business combination on the third anniversary of the day the person became an interested stockholder is still barred; the day after is not (F10). (Seat question 123 and the whole-section scenarios.) Asserted.
15. **The 20 days' notice in 203(b)(6) is owed for a merger needing a vote and for a disposition of 50% or more of the assets or stock value; not for a tender or exchange offer.** The notice sentence names "clause (x) or (y)" only. (Seat question 134.) Asserted.
16. **Section 203(a)(3) requires a meeting and forbids written consent.** The vote of at least 66 2/3% of the outstanding voting stock not owned by the interested stockholder must be given at an annual or special meeting "and not by written consent". (Seat question 122 and the whole-section scenarios.) Asserted.
17. **A revocable proxy solicited from exactly 10 persons does not count toward ownership.** Section 203(c)(9)(ii) says "10 or more persons". Stock tendered to the person's own offer counts only once accepted. (Seat question 148.) Asserted.
18. **For the 15% test the owner's unissued stock counts in the denominator too.** Section 203(c)(5): the voting stock deemed outstanding includes stock deemed owned through paragraph (9) but no other unissued stock. A holder of 100 votes with a right to 60 unissued votes owns 160 of 1,060 (15.09%) and is an interested stockholder; with a right to 50, 150 of 1,050 (14.29%), is not. (Seat question 149.) Asserted.
19. **The 1987 carve-outs are strict on one side and inclusive on the other.** A tender offer must have commenced "prior to" 23 December 1987 (the 23rd itself is too late); an exchange offer announced before that date must have commenced "within 90 days thereafter", and the 90th day (22 March 1988) is inside. (Seat question 149.) Asserted.
20. **Section 160(c) says nothing about holders other than the corporation and what it controls.** For them voting and quorum are `NOTHING`; shares held by the corporation as a fiduciary keep the right to vote, and for quorum the text neither restores them nor confirms their exclusion, so the answer is `NOTHING` (F19: the reading that they stay excluded, `JUST FALSE`, is the other defensible one). (Seat questions 69 and 70.) Asserted.
21. **Section 162(c) says who stays liable only when the transferee is protected.** Where the transferee is not protected, the text does not say whether the transferor is liable: `NOTHING`. (Seat question 77.) Asserted.
22. **Section 165 says nothing of a subscription for a corporation already formed.** `NOTHING`. Inside the six months the subscription is irrevocable unless its terms provide otherwise or all the other subscribers or the corporation consent. (Seat question 88.) Asserted.
23. **The bond caps the corporation's liability only for stock issued under a court order.** Otherwise `NOTHING`. (Seat question 98.) Asserted.
24. **Section 169's situs rule excludes taxation, and says nothing of any purpose it does not list.** Taxation is `JUST FALSE`; another purpose, or a corporation not existing under Delaware law, is `NOTHING`. (Seat question 99.) Asserted.
25. **Section 173 lists three forms of dividend.** A dividend in any other form is `NOTHING`. (Seat question 106.) Asserted.
26. **Sections 202(b), (b) second sentence and (d) are silent outside what they list.** A restriction imposed in any way other than the certificate, the bylaws or an agreement is `NOTHING`; a restriction adopted before securities were issued to the person says nothing about binding that person; a restriction with none of the listed purposes is `NOTHING` (the subsection says what is conclusively presumed reasonable, not what is unreasonable). (Seat questions 116, 117 and 119.) Asserted.
27. **The 90 days in 203(b)(2) include the 90th day.** From 2 February 1988 that is 2 May 1988, 1988 being a leap year (F12). (Seat question 125.) Asserted.
28. **The year in section 164 runs through its last day.** Forfeiture follows only when no bidder is had and the amount is not collected by the action "within 1 year from the date of the bringing of such action"; on the anniversary itself the year is still running. (Seat question 87.) Asserted.
29. **Scrip exchanged on the specified date itself is too late; the board may attach any condition.** (Seat questions 44 and 45.) Asserted.
30. **The 85% test keeps the interested stockholder's own shares in the base.** Section 203(a)(2) excludes shares owned by director-officers and by employee plans without a confidential tender decision "but not the outstanding voting stock owned by the interested stockholder": votes of those groups that the interested stockholder itself owns stay in the denominator. (Seat question 121.) Asserted.
31. **When no votes are outstanding the percentage tests have no answer.** With no votes outstanding and none outside the interested stockholder's holding, sections 203(a)(2) and (a)(3) have no percentage, and if nothing else frees the combination the whole-section question is `NOTHING`. (Seat questions 121, 122 and 156.) Asserted.

A test that fails on purpose: none. Every assertion in the tests and the cases is written equal to satisfied.

## What the stable binary does not accept

What I met while writing against the stable `l4` 1.6.98, each with what I wrote instead. Each of these is a loud failure: the binary prints a parse or type error and the file does not run. The quiet ones are listed last.

1. **A genitive (`x's y`) as the last argument of a mixfix call** is rejected ("unexpected AT"). Bracket it: `` `rule` a (x's y) ``.
2. **A positional constructor pattern with `WITH`** (``WHEN `ctor` WITH a b``) is a parse error. Write ``WHEN `ctor` a b THEN``.
3. **A constructor with a payload cannot be built with `WITH`.** Build it with `OF`: `` (`a corporation` OF `a stock corporation`) ``.
4. **A bare number is not a percentage.** `at least (200 DIVIDED BY 3)` compares with the wrong quantity; write `` `at least` ((200 DIVIDED BY 3)%) ``. In the seat, percentages are entered on a 0 to 100 scale and divided by 100 inside the question.
5. **`YMD` is not in the stable prelude.** The core carries the shim (`YMD y m d MEANS Date d m y`); this piece uses it from `dg-core.l4`.
6. **No section-level `GIVEN`, no `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` or `BECAUSE`, no `TYPICALLY`; every `NOT` is parenthesised; each `#ASSERT` is on one line.** Not-covered cases are `NOTHING` in a `MAYBE`, asserted `EQUALS NOTHING`.
7. **A `MAYBE BOOLEAN` is compared with `EQUALS JUST TRUE`; a `BOOLEAN` with `EQUALS TRUE`.** Writing `EQUALS JUST TRUE` against a rule that returns a plain `BOOLEAN` is a type error ("expected BOOLEAN ... but is here MAYBE OF BOOLEAN"); one seat question (the effect of the stockholders' election in a given combination) returns a `BOOLEAN` and is asserted accordingly.
8. **A name defined in two imported modules cannot be used at all** (the brief). Every top-level name here begins with its section locator (`s 151(a) ...`) or with `dg3`; field names are lower-case noun phrases and may repeat across record types.

The quiet ones (the binary says nothing; the result is wrong or the count is misleading):

9. **`l4 run` logs each satisfied assertion twice** (the diagnostic and the evaluation line). `grep -c "assertion satisfied"` on the raw output gives twice the number of assertions; `check.sh` counts the diagnostic once and prints the right number (see the next section).
10. **A failed assertion exits 0** (checked on 1.6.98 with `#ASSERT 1 EQUALS 2`: exit status 0, and "assertion failed" printed twice). Only the `DiagnosticSeverity_Error` line and `check.sh`'s table show it; the numbers were read, not the exit code.
