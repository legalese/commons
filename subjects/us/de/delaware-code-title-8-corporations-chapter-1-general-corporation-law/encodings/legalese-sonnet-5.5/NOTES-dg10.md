## Seat

The seat is the individual to whom the General Corporation Law is applied: **a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock in a Delaware corporation** (an incorporator, a director or officer, a stockholder including a minority stockholder), asking what the Law lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.
This is the seat the brief fixes for the piece; it was not chosen by me.
Layer 2 (`dg10-seat.l4`) has 165 questions, each with `@export` and `@desc`, grouped under four `§§` headings in the text's order: Suits against my corporation, its directors, officers or stockholders (ss 321 to 330; 31 questions); My close corporation (ss 341 to 356; 52); My public benefit corporation (ss 361 to 368; 26); A foreign corporation doing business in Delaware (ss 371 to 385; 56).
`dg10-cases-seat.l4` holds 665 worked cases, at least one per question and 53 `NOTHING` cases for questions the text does not answer.

How the seat is read in each subchapter (these readings are mine, not the brief's, and are labelled **assumed, not ruled**):

- **Subchapter XIII (suits).**
  The person is a director, officer or stockholder of a Delaware corporation, or someone who has a claim against it.
  The questions ask what can be done to the corporation or to the person (service of process, receivership, attachment of shares, liability for corporate debts), and what the person may do (recover a debt they paid, sue derivatively).
  Where the text gives a power to a plaintiff or creditor (ss 321(b), 323, 324, 325), the question is asked from the other side of the same relation and takes the facts about that other party as inputs, as the brief's Hohfeld rule requires; a few questions are therefore in the plaintiff's voice ("As plaintiff, have I done what I must ...?", "As a party in interest, may I file a statement ...?").
- **Subchapter XIV (close corporations).**
  The person is an incorporator, stockholder, director, transferee of close-corporation stock, or a provisional director.
  The classes' votes (ss 344, 346) are asked as one question about the class with the least approval: if that class carries, every class carries; the layer-1 rule takes a list of classes.
- **Subchapter XV (public benefit corporations).**
  The person is a director, stockholder, or acquirer of stock of a corporation that is or says it is a public benefit corporation.
- **Subchapter XVI (foreign corporations).**
  The person is a director, officer, incorporator or stockholder of the foreign corporation concerned, or its agent, or someone dealing with it or suing it.

There is no seat for an official.
What the Secretary of State, the Court of Chancery and the Attorney General must or may do is encoded in layer 1 and reaches layer 2 only as what is owed to the person or what may be done to them: the Secretary of State's letter (s 321(b)), the Court's power to appoint a receiver (s 322), a provisional director (s 353) or an injunction (ss 348(b), 384), the Attorney General's duty to proceed (ss 323, 384).
Proportions in the seat are percentages (75 for three quarters); the layer-1 rules take fractions and the seat divides by 100.

## What the stable binary does not accept

The binary is the stable `l4` 1.6.98 named in the brief (sha256 `82ea44addf093ea4dd84c4e4416184920857ca9c762c5b31feed20541c20263e`, as the task gave it).
I did not re-run the keel's probes; the table "What the stable binary does not accept" in `KEEL-NOTES.md` (probes 01 to 58, all rejected by this build) is the authority, and I wrote around it: no section `GIVEN`, no `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` or `BECAUSE`, no `TYPICALLY`, no body-less `DECLARE`, no `SET OF`, no `EVERY` or `WHOSE`, no `RULES EFFECTIVE DATE`, every `NOT` bracketed, one `#ASSERT` per line, no `IMPORT` of another piece.
What I met myself:

- **A mixfix call whose last argument is a genitive expression must have that argument bracketed.**
  `` `the date` p `before` (r's `x`) `` parses; the same without the brackets gives `unexpected AT` at a later comparison.
  A call result used as a record needs brackets too: `(rule arg)'s `field``.
- **`l4 check` and `l4 run` print one parse error at a time**, so a file with several mistakes needs several runs.
- **Positional arguments of a constructor that carries a field** are written as a bracketed call, for example `` (`a corporation` `a stock corporation`) `` (the core's `Kind of entity`).
- **A `MAYBE` result is asserted with `EQUALS JUST x` or `EQUALS NOTHING`** and a `MAYBE DATE` with `EQUALS JUST (YMD 2029 3 1)`; both are accepted.
- **A `DATE PLUS 1`** is accepted (used for the first day after a 30-day wait in s 355(a)).
