## Seat

The seat is the one the brief fixes: a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock in a Delaware corporation under the General Corporation Law.
That is an incorporator, a director or officer, or a stockholder including a minority stockholder.
They ask what the Law lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.

Why this seat.
Subchapters II to IV state what the corporation may do, how it is housed and represented, and how it is governed; each of those provisions is a power, duty or immunity of someone who sits inside the corporation or owns a share of it.
The individual to whom the law is applied is therefore the insider, never the Secretary of State, the Court of Chancery, the Attorney General or the Department of Education.

Officials get no seat.
The Secretary's power to refuse to file, the Court's power to enjoin, the Attorney General's power to sue and the Department's power to approve all appear in the seat as what the person is exposed to or entitled to (for example, "May the Secretary of State refuse to file a document my registered agent submitted?").
Their own procedure and rule-making are `inert` machinery in the coverage table.

Other parties.
Where a right of the person is the correlative of someone's duty, the seat takes as plain inputs the facts about that party on which the text makes the right depend (the agent's conduct, the board's vote, the corporation's undertaking, the court's finding).
Where the text also puts a duty or a liability on the person, a paired question asks it from the person's end (for example, section 145(c)(1) is asked both as "Must my corporation indemnify me?" and "How much must my corporation indemnify me?", and section 145(c)(2) as what the corporation may do for a person who is neither a director nor an officer).
Counts of directors are counts of votes where the certificate gives a director more or less than one vote (section 141(d), last sentence): the questions say so in their `@desc`s.

One case is the text's natural unit: one corporation, one board, one transaction, one proceeding, one director.

How the seat is built.
Every question has `@export` (a question ending in `?`, with the section it comes from) and every input has `@desc` worded as the person would be asked.
Inputs are plain (yes/no, numbers, dates, strings, or the modules' own enumerations without fields).
The records the layer-1 rules take are built in each question's `WHERE`, so no record or `MAYBE` input reaches the form.
Where the text does not say, the question answers `NOTHING` (a `MAYBE BOOLEAN`), and the question says so; it never answers `FALSE` for a case the text does not answer.
Questions are grouped under `§§` headings in the text's order: powers (ss 121-127), registered office and agent (ss 131-136), the board (s 141(a)-(b), (j)), committees (s 141(c)), classes, reliance, consent, meetings and removal (s 141(d)-(k)), officers and loans (ss 142-143), interested transactions (s 144), indemnification (s 145), and matters put to stockholders and the approval of instruments (ss 146-147).

## Jural relations

One row per provision that creates a relation.
The holder has the right (claim), privilege (liberty), power or immunity; the bearer has the correlative duty, no-right, liability (being subject to the power) or disability.
Each relation is stated once in layer 1 under a neutral name; the seat asks it from the person's end.
A provision that creates no such relation (a definition, a counting rule, a rule that fixes a date) is listed once as `none` at the end.
"No named claimant" means the text states the duty without saying whose claim it is; the table does not invent one.

## What the text turns out to say

Each finding says what a careful reader would not guess from the headings; "Asserted." means a case in `dg2-cases-seat.l4` (named by the question it answers) or an assertion in a `dg2-tests-*.l4` file states it.

1. Section 145(b) caps indemnity in a suit by or in the right of the corporation, after an adjudication of liability to the corporation, at what the court deems proper, and never above the expenses actually and reasonably incurred (F-145-3).
   With 1,000 of expenses and 300 deemed proper the corporation may indemnify 300; with 1,500 deemed proper, 1,000.
   Asserted (question "What is the most my corporation may indemnify me against in an action or suit by or in the right of the corporation").
2. Section 145(c) changes who is an "officer" for acts or omissions after December 31, 2020: only an officer deemed to have consented to service under 10 Del. C. § 3114(b).
   An officer who is not so deemed falls into paragraph (c)(2), where the corporation may, but need not, indemnify; the same officer for an act on December 31, 2020 or earlier falls in (c)(1), where the corporation must (F-145-4).
   Asserted (the (c)(1), amount and (c)(2) questions, with December 31, 2020 and January 1, 2021).
3. Section 145(f) protects a right arising under the certificate or a bylaw from a later amendment, repeal or elimination only for an amendment dated after the act or omission, and only if the provision in effect did not explicitly authorize it.
   A same-day amendment is not after; a right given only by a board resolution is not protected by this sentence (F-145-1).
   Asserted (question "Is my right to indemnification or advancement protected from later elimination or impairment").
4. Section 144(b) protects a controlling stockholder transaction that is not a going private transaction if the committee approved it, or the disinterested stockholders approved it as the terms conditioned, or it is fair; section 144(c) protects a going private transaction only if the committee approval AND the stockholder approval were both obtained, or it is fair (F-144-3).
   A going private transaction with only one of the two approvals and not fair is not protected.
   Asserted (the (b) and (c) questions).
5. Section 144(a)(1) adds a condition when the directors who are not disinterested outnumber half the directors: a committee of two or more directors, each determined by the board to be disinterested, must have approved or recommended the act (F-144-2).
   Asserted (question "Did the disinterested directors authorize the act or transaction as the Law requires").
6. Section 144(e)(2): one third of the voting power does not make a controlling stockholder without managerial authority.
   Thirty-four of 100 votes with managerial authority does; thirty-three does not; a majority alone does (F-144-5).
   Asserted (question "May I be deemed a controlling stockholder").
7. Section 144(d)(3): a nomination or vote by a person with a material interest is never, of itself, evidence that the director is not disinterested.
   Section 144(d)(2) lets a listed corporation's director be presumed disinterested only if the director is not a party and the board found the director independent under the exchange's rules, and the presumption is rebutted only by substantial and particularized facts of a material interest or a material relationship.
   Asserted.
8. Section 141(b): a quorum fixed by the certificate itself below a majority is not covered by the text (it speaks of the bylaws); the bylaws may fix a smaller quorum unless the certificate provides otherwise, and never below one third; a number fixed in a board resolution is ignored (F-141-3).
   Asserted (question "Is there a quorum of our board of directors").
9. Section 141(k): the class or series vote replaces the whole vote only for removal without cause (F-141-6).
   On a classified board the stockholders may remove only for cause unless the certificate otherwise provides, and, with cumulative voting and less than the entire board, no director may be removed without cause if the votes against would elect the director.
   Asserted (question "May the stockholders remove this director from office").
10. Section 141(c) picks the governing paragraph by a date and a vote: a corporation incorporated on or after July 1, 1996 is governed by (c)(2); an older one by (c)(1) unless a majority of the whole board resolved to elect (c)(2).
    Under (c)(1) the text names only "amending" a bylaw as withheld from a committee, so adopting or repealing one is not covered (F-141-7).
    Asserted.
11. Section 125 draws a gap: a corporation organized exactly on April 18, 1945 is neither "after" nor "before" it, and the text does not say what its power is (F-125-1).
    Asserted (question "May my corporation confer academic or honorary degrees").
12. Section 132(a) is a closed list: a foreign general partnership that is not a limited liability partnership, a business trust, a real estate investment trust, a common-law trust and a joint-stock association may not be a registered agent (F-132-1).
    Asserted.
13. Section 132(c) turns on "more than 50": an agent serving exactly 50 entities at one time is not a commercial registered agent; 51 is.
    Agents with an officer, director or managing agent in common are counted together at the same threshold.
    Asserted.
14. Sections 132(f)(4) and 136(b) both give the corporation thirty days to designate a new agent, from different events: the Secretary's notice of the court's order, and the agent's filing of the certificate of resignation (F-136-3).
    A designation on the thirtieth day is in time; a day later, the charter of a domestic corporation (or the qualification of a foreign one) is forfeited.
    Under section 136 the resignation itself takes effect on that thirtieth day, and the notice of resignation had to be given at least thirty days before the filing.
    Asserted.
15. Section 124 forbids treating an act as invalid for want of capacity or power, but lets the want be asserted in three proceedings only; where a court sets aside a contract, it never awards anticipated profits as a loss (F-124-1).
    Asserted.
16. Section 122(18): a provision of a contract with stockholders is not enforceable against the corporation to the extent it is contrary to the certificate or would be contrary to the laws if put in the certificate; but a covenant on a specified action is not contrary merely because a provision authorizes the board to take that action (F-122-5).
    Asserted.
17. Section 122(11): the condition "which the participating corporation would have power to conduct by itself" attaches only to participating in a transaction, undertaking or arrangement, not to joining an association (F-122-2).
    Asserted.
18. Section 141(d): a director's greater or lesser voting power applies in committees unless the certificate or the bylaws provide otherwise; a board resolution cannot (F-141-9).
    Asserted.
19. Section 141(f): a consent effective at a future time must take effect no later than 60 days after the instruction or provision, the last day being inside the period; a person who is not then a director, or who revoked, gives no consent.
    Asserted.
20. Section 147: the board may ratify an instrument it has approved only before the filing is effective, and the ratification is effective as of the original approval; it is not the exclusive means (sections 204 and 205 and the common law remain).
    Asserted.
21. Section 146: a corporation may agree to submit a matter to a vote of its stockholders whether or not the board later recommends against it.
    Asserted.
22. Section 142: a statement of the officers' titles and duties only in the certificate is not one of the two places the text names (F-142-1); a failure to elect officers does not dissolve or otherwise affect the corporation.
    Asserted.
23. Sections 133 and 134(a) leave the effective moment of a change of registered office open: section 133 says nothing, and section 134(a) fixes it only from "a given day".
    Before that day the text does not say where the office is (F-134-1).
    Asserted for s 134(a); s 133 has no rule on timing to assert.
24. The range contains no date of enactment or commencement the encoding could carry; the dates it does carry as defined values are those the rules use: April 18, 1945 (s 125), January 1, 2007 (s 132(f)(3)), July 1, 1996 (s 141(c)), December 31, 2020 (s 145(c)).
    Asserted (the boundary cases of each).

## Before this is worth relying on

1. **The source is a converter's rendering, not the official text.**
   Nothing here has been checked against the official Delaware Code, and `srcrefs.py` checks the quotations only against the same rendering.
   The headings of ss 122, 144 and 147 carry bracketed notes ("For application of this section, see 84 Del. Laws, c. 309, § 6" for ss 122 and 147; "see 85 Del. Laws, c. 6, § 3" for s 144); the session-law clauses are not in the text, so which provisions apply to which acts is not encoded.
2. **One agent wrote the rules, the tests and the cases.**
   The expected values were written by hand from the text before each run, and no assertion failed, but no independent test pass was made (the brief provides none).
   A reader who disagrees with a fork should change the rule and the assertions it affects together.
3. **The forks in the register are readings, not findings.**
   Each is listed there with the text that licenses it, and several are decided in favour of `NOTHING` or a narrower reading; a reviewer should read F-121-1, F-122-2, F-122-3, F-141-3, F-144-2, F-144-3 and F-145-4 first, because each changes the answer for ordinary facts.
4. **Judgments the text gives to someone else are inputs, never guessed.**
   They are: the directors' judgment that a loan will benefit the corporation (s 143); whether a transaction is fair (s 144); the board's determination that a director is disinterested or independent (s 144); whether a material interest or relationship would impair a director's objectivity or be material to a stockholder (s 144(e)(7), (8)); the court's finding that a person is fairly and reasonably entitled to indemnity (s 145(b)); the Department of Education's satisfaction (s 125); the dean's report (s 125); whether the Secretary's refusal to file is reasonable and necessary (s 132(e)); whether the Court finds a contract set-aside equitable (s 124(1)).
5. **What it needs from the other pieces.**
   The inputs listed under "Cross-references out of my ranges" are answered by sections not read here: ss 102, 103, 109, 115, 116 (dg1); 151, 158, 203 (dg3); 204, 205 (dg4); 232 (dg5); 251 to 264 (dg6 to dg8); 321 (dg10); and by law that is not among the inputs (Titles 10, 14 and 18, chapter 5 of Title 8, the federal securities and tax law).
   A caller who gets one of these wrong gets a wrong answer from a correct rule.
6. **The count of directors.**
   Section 141(b) speaks of "the total number of directors" and, elsewhere, of "the whole board"; the rules take the number as given and do not decide whether vacancies count (F-141-1).
   Where the certificate gives a director more or less than one vote, every count of directors is a count of votes; the questions say so, and a person who forgets gets a wrong quorum.
7. **Where the text does not say, the answer is `NOTHING`.**
   The questions and cases list them; a form that shows `NOTHING` as "no" misreads the Law.
8. **The guidance was not read in full.**
   I did not read pages 01, 02, 03, 04, 06, 09, 10 and 11 of the source-patterns in full.
   A convention those pages state and I did not apply could be missing from the modules.
