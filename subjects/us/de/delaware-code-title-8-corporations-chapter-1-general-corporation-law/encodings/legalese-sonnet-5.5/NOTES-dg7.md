## Seat

The seat is the individual to whom the Law is applied: **a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock in a Delaware corporation under the General Corporation Law** (an incorporator, a director or officer, a stockholder including a minority stockholder), asking what the Law lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.
In this Subchapter that person is:

- a director or member of a nonstock corporation that is merging or consolidating with another nonstock corporation, with a foreign nonstock corporation, with a stock corporation or with a foreign corporation (ss 255 to 258), asking whether the merger is allowed, what the agreement must state, how the members are told and vote, and what the charitable-status limit does;
- a director, officer or stockholder of a corporation that has merged (ss 259 to 261), asking what the merger did to the corporation's existence, property and debts, what the surviving corporation may issue, whether the agreement may carry penalties or a stockholder representative, and what happens to pending actions;
- a stockholder (or a beneficial owner) who may demand an appraisal of the fair value of the shares (s 262), asking whether the shares carry appraisal rights, how to perfect them, when to petition or withdraw, whether the stockholder is on the list, and what the Court may value, award, charge and bar.

The reason is the commons rule (ruled 2026-10-18): the person to whom the law is applied is the one who asks.
The Court of Chancery, the Register in Chancery, the Secretary of State and the Division of Corporations have no seat; what each does is asked as what the person is owed or exposed to ("May the Court dismiss the proceedings as to me for not submitting my stock certificates?", "Must my name and address be on the verified list?").
A judgment the Law leaves to the Court (fair value, equity of costs, good cause for different interest, discretion over expenses) is a plain input, never guessed.
Further seats (the corporation, the surviving entity) are not written; the questions that put the correlative duty on the other party are the paired ones listed in `## Jural relations`.

## What the text turns out to say

"Asserted." ends a finding when a test in a `dg7-tests-*.l4` file or a case in `dg7-cases-seat.l4` asserts it; every such expected value was written from the text, not copied from the rule's output.
A finding that the text does not answer is asserted as `NOTHING`.

1. **The 2,000-holder limit is "more than 2,000".** Paragraph (b)(1) takes appraisal rights away from a class or series that was listed on a national securities exchange or "held of record by more than 2,000 holders". A class with exactly 2,000 holders of record and no listing is not caught, so (b)(1) leaves its appraisal rights alone; with 2,001 holders it takes them away (src:1254). Asserted.
2. **Paragraph (b)(2) overrides all of (b)(1), including the proviso for a surviving corporation whose stockholders need not vote under s 251(f).** "Notwithstanding paragraph (b)(1)" is the whole paragraph. The two readings differ only where holders of such a surviving corporation's shares are required to accept something outside items a. to d.; the encoded answer is that they then keep appraisal rights (src:1254, src:1255; fork F-b1). Asserted: shares of a surviving corporation paid in cash carry appraisal rights, shares paid in its own stock do not.
3. **A beneficial owner is not a "stockholder" under subsection (a).** "Stockholder" is "a holder of record of stock"; a beneficial owner holding through a nominee or a voting trust reaches appraisal only through paragraph (d)(3), by a demand in the beneficial owner's own name with the documentary evidence, the statement of true copy and an address (src:1252, src:1266). Asserted.
4. **A proxy or a vote against is not a demand.** Under paragraph (d)(1) the demand must be a separate written demand delivered before the vote is taken (src:1264). Asserted.
5. **A first notice given on the effective date itself is late if a constituent corporation gives it and in time if the surviving entity does.** Under paragraph (d)(2) a constituent corporation gives the first notice "before the effective date" and the surviving entity "within 10 days after" it; day 0 is read as within the 10 days (src:1265; fork F-d2). The same split holds for the second notice, which the surviving entity sends "on or within 10 days after" the effective date. Asserted at both edges: for an effective date of 25 June, a constituent's notice on 24 June is in time and on 25 June is not; the surviving entity's is in time from 25 June to 5 July and late on 6 July or on 24 June.
6. **A late second notice goes only to those who demanded.** If the second notice is sent more than 20 days after the first (for a s 251(h) merger, later than the later of the offer's consummation and 20 days after the first notice), it need go only to holders entitled to appraisal rights who have demanded, and to beneficial owners who demanded under (d)(3) (src:1265). Asserted at the 20th and 21st day.
7. **The time at which a class is tested for listing is not named for a merger approved under s 253 or s 267.** Paragraph (b)(1) names the record date for notice, the record date for consent under s 228 and, for s 251(h), immediately before the agreement is signed. A s 253 or s 267 merger has none of them; those mergers are not on the list in the chapeau of subsection (b), and paragraph (b)(3) governs them (src:1254, src:1260; fork F-b4). Asserted (NOTHING).
8. **Paragraph (b)(3) turns on a double negative.** "All of the stock of a subsidiary ... is not owned by the parent" is read as "the parent does not own all of it", because a s 253 parent must own at least 90% of each class, so a parent owning none could not use the section (src:1260, src:1137; fork F-b3). Asserted.
9. **The mandatory dismissal in subsection (g) needs the shares to have been listed immediately before the transaction, and "exceeds" is strict.** Exactly 1% of the outstanding shares, or exactly $1,000,000 of consideration, does not exceed; the Court must then dismiss, unless the merger was approved under s 253 or s 267, in which case it need not (src:1269; fork F-g1). Asserted.
10. **Interest under subsection (h) is not covered for a part of a quarter.** The text says interest is "compounded quarterly" and accrues "at 5% over the Federal Reserve discount rate ... as established from time to time", but not that the rate is per year and not how a part of a quarter accrues; the rules answer for whole quarters only and answer NOTHING for a part-quarter (src:1270; fork F-h1). Asserted.
11. **Paragraph (i) speaks of an entity "of this State or of any state".** An entity formed under the law of a foreign country is not within the words; the rule answers NOTHING for it, and answers TRUE for this State and for another jurisdiction of the United States (src:1271; fork F-i1). Asserted.
12. **Subsection (k) bars voting, and dividends other than those payable to holders of record before the effective date, from the effective date, but does not say what happens when the right to appraisal has ceased.** A person who demanded may not vote the shares, or receive a dividend or distribution on them other than one payable to stockholders of record at a date before the effective date; whether the bar lifts on withdrawal or lapse is not stated (src:1273; fork F-k1). Asserted (NOTHING for the ceased case).
13. **The time limits of subsection (e) are 120 days (petition), 60 days (withdrawal without the Court), and the statement is due the later of 10 days after the request is received and 10 days after the period for demands expired.** Counted with the last day inside (fork F-d1): for an effective date of 25 June 2026 the last day for a petition is 23 October and for withdrawal 24 August. Asserted at both edges.
14. **The vote of members under s 255(c) is a majority of all the members entitled to vote, not of those present.** 50 of 100 is not a majority; 51 is; 30 of 60 is half, not a majority (src:1182; fork F-255-1). Asserted.
15. **The charitable-status limits speak only of a merger "into" another corporation, so the encoded rules do not bar a consolidation.** Sections 255(g) and 256(g) speak of a merger of a charitable nonstock corporation "into a nonstock corporation", ss 257(f) and 258(d) of one "into a stock corporation"; none mentions a consolidation, and the four rules answer FALSE for one (src:1186, src:1204, src:1220, src:1227; fork F-255-2). Asserted for ss 255(g) and 257(f).
16. **The lead-in of s 261(a) is contested, and on the reading taken it opens the provisions to the nonstock mergers of ss 255 to 258.** The lead-in reads "Any agreement of merger or consolidation governed by § 251 of this title, other than a merger effected pursuant to § 251(g), § 252, § 254, § 255, § 256, § 257, § 258, § 263 or § 264". Read as one list of the sections whose mergers proceed by an agreement of merger or consolidation with a single carve-out for s 251(g), it lets the agreements of ss 251 (other than (g)), 252, 254 to 258, 263 and 264 contain the penalties and consequences of (a)(1) and the stockholder representative of (a)(2), and keeps out a s 251(g) merger, a s 253 or s 267 merger, a conversion (s 266) and a transfer (s 390). Read as an exclusion list, it leaves only s 251 and gives the opposite answer for every nonstock merger. The encoded rule takes the first reading (src:1244; fork F-261-1, with the reasons and the lawyer's question). Asserted.
17. **Section 261(a)(2)(iii) says nothing about amendment of the representative provision before the merger is effective.** It speaks of a provision that "may not be amended after the merger or consolidation has become effective or may be amended only with the consent or approval of persons specified"; the rule answers NOTHING before the effective time (src:1246). Asserted.
18. **The fee in s 256(d) is taxed as costs only if the plaintiff prevails, and the Secretary of State need not retain the record of a service for more than 5 years from receipt.** Received 1 March 2026, the record need not be kept on 2 March 2031 and must still be kept on 1 March 2031 (src:1201). Asserted.
19. **The surviving constituent corporation does not cease to exist at the effective time; only the others do.** Section 259(a) ends the separate existence of "all such constituent corporations except the one into which the other or others of such constituent corporations have been merged" (src:1231). Asserted.
20. **The bonds a surviving corporation may issue under s 260(a) are capped by what, with its capital stock, covers the payments and obligations the transaction requires.** Payments and obligations of 1,000,000 with capital stock of 400,000 leave 600,000; capital stock at or above the figure leaves nothing to issue under the subsection (src:1236; fork F-260-1). Asserted.
21. **Expenses under s 262(j) are charged pro rata against the value of the shares, not by head.** The Court may charge "all or a portion" of a participant's expenses pro rata against the value of all the shares entitled to appraisal that were not dismissed under subsection (k) or are subject to an award under a reservation of jurisdiction, so $90,000 ordered against 2,000,000 of 18,000,000 in value is $10,000 for those shares; whether to charge any of it is the Court's discretion, an input (src:1272). Asserted.
22. **The sentence defining "facts" leaves the list open.** In ss 255(b), 256(b) and 257(b) the term "facts" "includes, but is not limited to, the occurrence of any event, including a determination or action by any person or body, including the corporation". An event, a determination and an action are facts, the corporation's own included; whether anything else is a fact the sentence does not say, and the rule answers NOTHING for it (src:1181, src:1199, src:1216; fork F-facts-1). Asserted.

## What the stable binary does not accept

The target is the stable build `main`, which `l4 --version` printed as `1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`, sha256 `82ea44addf093ea4dd84c4e4416184920857ca9c762c5b31feed20541c20263e`.
It is also the only binary that ran every check here, so no second compiler was compared.
The files were written in the dialect `writing-l4-rules/references/dialects.md` says `main` reads, using its "write instead" column; none of its rejected-construct probes was knowingly reproduced (no section `GIVEN`, no `REFUSE`, no `TYPICALLY`, no `SET OF`, no local `YMD`; `YMD` is the core's).
Measured on this binary while writing, beyond the dialects page:

- **A constructor that carries a field is matched with the field named after it, not with `WITH`.** `` WHEN `a corporation` k THEN ... `` is accepted; `` WHEN `a k` WITH m THEN m `` stops with `unexpected WITH` at the `WITH` (re-probed on 2026-10-25 with a two-constructor type).
- **A list of records cannot be written with `WITH` inside the list.** ``LIST `R` WITH `a` IS 1, `R` WITH `a` IS 2`` stops with `unexpected WITH`. Name each record first (``r1 MEANS `R` WITH `a` IS 1``), then ``LIST `r1`, `r2` ``, which evaluates to ``LIST R OF 1, R OF 2``.
- **`all`, `any` and `map` take a named rule or a `GIVEN r YIELD ...` function**, so ``all `s 262(b)(2) the item is described in a., b. or c.` items`` is accepted and no multi-line lambda is needed.
- **Compounding is exact.** ``product (map (GIVEN r YIELD 1 PLUS ((r PLUS 5%) DIVIDED BY 4)) rates)`` with two quarters at a 3% discount rate (8% a year, 2% a quarter) gives 1.0404, so interest of 4040 on 100,000, with no rounding.
- **A date made with the core's `YMD` compares with `AT MOST`, `AT LEAST`, `GREATER THAN` and `LESS THAN`** and is added to with the core's `the date p after d`; every limit here is tested at both edges.
- **`replicate` exists in the prelude on this build** (`replicate 3 1` is `LIST 1, 1, 1`; with a record as the element it gives `LIST R OF 1, R OF 1`; probed on 2026-10-25). The seat layer nevertheless builds its list of n identical corporations by a three-line recursion of its own (`dg7 seat n corporations that are all`), written before `replicate` was probed; the helper is redundant and could be replaced by one call.
- **A parameter named like a record field can turn a wrong-arity application into a "multiple definitions" error** that names the parameter, not the arity; once the application was bracketed correctly the error went away, so read the other error first.
- **Exports return `MAYBE BOOLEAN`, `MAYBE NUMBER` and `MAYBE DATE`** where the text does not answer. They type-check and run under `l4 run`, and the cases in `dg7-cases-seat.l4` assert `NOTHING` for them. They were not deployed (no Legalese Cloud access here), so how the Cloud serializes `NOTHING` was not tested.
- **A quote line must start and end at word boundaries of the raw line unless it ends in `…` or `...`** (`srcrefs.py`, its docstring); the template builder `tools/build.py` (kept beside the piece, outside this folder) adds ` …` whenever a slice stops before the end of the raw line.

## Before this is worth relying on

1. **A lawyer has not read it against the official text.** The source is a converter's plain-text rendering of the Delaware Code Online; nothing here is verified against the official text, and the History lines, the bracketed application notes (ss 261, 262) and the session-law clauses they point to (for example 84 Del. Laws, c. 309, § 6) are not in the inputs.
2. **The cross-references are inputs, not run.** The effect of ss 103, 114, 155, 228, 251(c) to (f), 251(h), 252(c), (d), 253, 263 to 267, 388, 390 and 391(a)(29) is taken as a plain input or as an "applies" rule (see `## Cross-references out of my ranges`, which names the pieces dg1, dg3, dg5, dg6, dg8 and dg11). The lead has to wire those pieces' rules to my inputs; until it does, the seat asks the person for the outcome (for example "Did you consent to the transaction in writing under section 228?").
3. **The shared nouns need promoting.** `dg7 Provision under which a transaction is effected`, `s 262 How the transaction was approved`, `dg7 Constituent corporation` and `dg7 Merger or consolidation` will be asked again by dg6, dg8 and dg11 (`## Needs from the core`); a seat that spans pieces needs one copy.
4. **Twenty-six forks are taken, none ruled.** The ones that change an answer a person would act on are F-d1 (counting the last day), F-d2 (a notice on the effective date), F-h1 (interest), F-k1 (the bar on voting after the right ceases), F-255-1 (one body of voters or two), F-b1 and F-b5 (what paragraph (b)(2) overrides and where it stops) and F-261-1 (which agreements s 261(a) reaches: the two readings of its lead-in give opposite answers for the nonstock mergers of ss 255 to 258, so this one wants a lawyer's ruling first).
5. **Foreign law and the Court are inputs.** Whether another jurisdiction prohibits a merger (ss 256(a), 258(a)), what the Federal Reserve discount rate was in each quarter (s 262(h)), what the Court found as fair value, what it taxed, and whether it exercised its discretion are facts the person (or the Court) supplies; no rule predicts them.
6. **The `MAYBE`-valued exports are untested on Legalese Cloud.** The seat has 121 questions; 18 return `MAYBE` (the text does not answer some inputs). Deployment under a name, the Interact form's rendering of an enumeration choice, and of the `LIST OF NUMBER` input for the quarterly discount rates (two questions), are untested.
7. **Counting.** All periods are calendar days (months and years by the core's date arithmetic); the text gives no rule for weekends or holidays and the core finds none (KEEL fork F3 records only that inclusive counting is recommended). The Court of Chancery Rules and Title 10 (not among the inputs) may add rules for filing on a day the court is closed.
8. **Commencement and dates.** The range contains no date of enactment, commencement or coming into force that a rule depends on: the History lines list session laws and the bracketed notes cite application clauses, and neither fixes a date. No date constant is carried and nothing here is "the law as at" a date; the text is the one vintage of 1 October 2026 (the file name).
9. **Some layer-1 rules are reached by no seat question.** Seven rules are encoded and tested but named in no seat question and used by no other rule: `s 262(a) stock or a share`, `s 262(a) a person`, `s 262(b)(2)d. the items are a combination of what a., b. and c. describe`, `s 262(c) the provisions of this section apply as nearly as is practicable`, `s 262(d) one of the corporations is a nonstock corporation`, `s 262(g) the person is one of the persons the Court has determined to be entitled to appraisal rights` and `s 256(d) the Secretary of State's record of the service is as the subsection requires`. Each restates a definition, a Court finding or an official's record that decides nothing the person would ask separately; each has its row in the coverage table.
10. **Names may collide at the merge.** Layer-1 record fields and `GIVEN` parameters are lowercase phrases such as `the effective date`; the keel's convention prefixes types and rules but not fields. If another piece defines a field of the same name on a record imported alongside mine, a seat file that imports both may need one of them renamed.
11. **The helper scripts are outside the deposit.** Layer-1 modules were generated from templates by `tools/build.py` so that every `-- src:N |` quote is copied from the raw text; the templates, the build script, the notes parts and the coverage checker are in `td/dg/dg7/tools/`, beside `deposit/`, and are not part of the deliverable. The generated `.l4` files are what the checks ran on.
