## Seat

**The seat** (from the brief and the keel): a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock in a Delaware corporation under the General Corporation Law (an incorporator, a director or officer, a stockholder including a minority stockholder), asking what the Law lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.
Why: the Law governs the corporation and the people around it, and Subchapter IX is where a corporation is merged into, consolidated with or converted into another entity, so the individual to whom it is applied is the stockholder whose shares are exchanged or cashed out, the director who must adopt the resolution and the agreement, the incorporator or officer who signs and files the certificates, and the minority stockholder against whom a 90 percent owner may merge the corporation.
The Secretary of State, the Court of Chancery and the Attorney General get no seat: the Secretary of State's duties (to notify, to mail the letter, to keep the record for five years) appear as what the person is owed or may require, and the plaintiff's duties on service appear as what the person must do.

**What the layer asks.**
121 questions in six `§` groups (§§ 263, 264, 265, 266, 267, 268), in the text's order, each `@export` with a question in the person's words and `@desc` on every input worded as that person would be asked.
Every question calls a layer-1 rule by name; the records the rules take (statements in an agreement or certificate, approvals, the class of stock, an entity) are built in the question's `WHERE` from plain inputs.
No rule is restated; no layer-1 module changes to suit the layer.

**Choices made for the layer, assumed and not ruled** (each in a commit that could be reverted alone):

1. **One class stands for "each class" in § 267(a)(1).** The person states the shares outstanding and the shares the parent owns of the class the parent owns the smallest share of, among the classes that would be entitled to vote on the merger; the layer builds a one-class list. If the parent reaches 90 percent of the class it owns least of, it reaches 90 percent of every class, so the answer is the same as for the full list, but the person is not asked for a list. Fork F19.
2. **An entity is chosen from one list** (`dg8 seat Entity the person describes`: fifteen descriptions), and the layer builds the core's `Kind of entity` and `Entity` from it. For a kind that is not a corporation, `where formed` is fixed at `this State`: no rule reads it. For a corporation, the kind of corporation (stock, nonstock) is fixed at `a stock corporation`: no rule reads it.
3. **The form of a combination** (§§ 263, 264) is asked as a choice of four (a merger into a surviving corporation of this State; a merger into a surviving entity that is not a corporation; a consolidation into a new resulting corporation of this State; a consolidation into a new resulting entity that is not a corporation).
4. **Counts of votes, not shares** (§ 266(b)): the questions ask for votes (core fork F5).
5. **"Stockholder" includes a member of a nonstock corporation** in §§ 263 to 266 and 268 (section 114(a)); section 114(b)(2) takes § 267 out of that reading, so § 267 is read as written (it speaks of shares of stock).
6. **The questions about the Secretary of State's letter and record** are asked from the side of the person who is owed them (a former stockholder who is now a partner, member or owner of the survivor, or the converted entity), and the questions about service from the side of the claimant.

Asserted (`dg8-tests-s263.l4`, `dg8-tests-s264.l4`, the approvals and certificate cases in `dg8-cases-seat.l4`).
2. **A stockholder who will become a general partner has a veto in a merger (§ 263(c)) and in a conversion (§ 266(b)), with no exception for a limited liability partnership.** § 267(a)(B), by contrast, bars terms that make a holder a general partner of a "partnership (other than a limited liability partnership or a limited liability limited partnership)", so there the holder can be made one of a limited liability partnership. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s263.l4` approvals; `dg8-tests-s266.l4` authorization; `dg8-tests-s267.l4` proviso (B)).
3. **Due notice of the § 266(b) meeting goes to every holder of stock, "whether voting or nonvoting"**, at the address on the corporation's records, "at least 20 days prior to the date of the meeting". Read inclusively, notice on the 20th day before is in time and on the 19th is not; across a month end the same count holds. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s266.l4` notice).
4. **The majority in § 266(b) is more than half of the outstanding stock entitled to vote, and the conversion needs no stockholder vote at all if no shares had been issued before the board adopted the resolution (§ 266(i)).** 50 votes of 100 does not carry it, 51 does; counts that cannot be a vote (no outstanding stock, more votes than there are, a negative number) are not covered. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s266.l4`).
5. **§ 266(k) has two dates and two edges.** A provision of the certificate of incorporation reaches a conversion only if the corporation was incorporated "before August 1, 2022" (1 August itself is out); a voting trust or other written agreement only if it was "in effect on or before August 1, 2022" (1 August itself is in), and, on the reading taken (F13), only for a corporation incorporated before that day. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s266.l4` and the cases in `dg8-cases-seat.l4`).
6. **§ 267(c) gives appraisal rights whenever the entity does not own all the stock immediately before the merger**, even when it owns 99 percent, while § 267(b) says § 262 otherwise does not apply. A minority stockholder in a short-form merger under § 267 therefore has the appraisal right that § 267(c) opens (subject to § 262's own conditions, which are piece dg7's); a stockholder of a wholly owned subsidiary has none. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s267.l4`).
7. **The 90 percent test of § 267(a)(1) is "at least" and is run class by class.** Exactly 90 percent is enough; 89 of 100 is not. A class whose corporation has the § 251(g)(7) provision, or that has no outstanding shares which "absent this subsection, would be entitled to vote", is left out; if no class is left, or the counts cannot be a holding, the test has nothing to measure and the answer is not covered. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s267.l4`).
8. **The Secretary of State may discard the record of a service after the fifth anniversary, not on it** (§ 263(d), § 264(d), § 266(c)(6): "longer than 5 years from receipt"); received on 29 February, five years on is 28 February. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s263.l4`, `dg8-tests-s266.l4`).
9. **A corporation of this State is not an "other entity" under § 265(a), but a corporation of any other State or country is** ("a foreign corporation"); a natural person is no entity at all. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s265.l4`).
10. **A converted corporation's existence dates from the other entity's first existence, not from its certificate of incorporation.** § 265(d) says so "notwithstanding § 106": a limited liability company formed in 2003 that converts in 2026 is a corporation since 2003. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s265.l4`, `dg8-cases-seat.l4`).
11. **§ 265(e) and § 266(e) protect liabilities incurred before the conversion and are silent on those incurred after.** § 266(e) adds the choice of law for matters arising before it. The after cases answer NOTHING. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s265.l4`, `dg8-tests-s266.l4`).
12. **§ 266(c) governs only a conversion to an entity of another jurisdiction.** What a Delaware corporation files to become a Delaware limited liability company or partnership is not in the section (Title 6, not among the inputs). Asserted that § 266(c) does not require a certificate of conversion for a Delaware target (`dg8-tests-s266.l4`, `dg8-cases-seat.l4`).
13. **§ 268(a) is a gate with an exclusion in two places.** It applies only to an agreement entered into under a provision of the subchapter "other than § 251(g)" that converts all of the constituent corporation's shares into cash, property, rights or securities "excluding stock of the surviving corporation". Where it applies the board may approve an agreement that is silent on the surviving corporation's certificate of incorporation, the named persons may adopt an amendment, and no alteration of the certificate is deemed an amendment to the agreement. Where it does not apply it says nothing (NOTHING), and for a person it does not name it says nothing either. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s268.l4`).
14. **§ 268(b): disclosure schedules are not part of the agreement for purposes of the title but have the effects the agreement provides**, unless the agreement expressly provides otherwise; then the subsection does not decide whether they are part of it. Asserted (`dg8-tests-s268.l4`).
15. **Typographical and structural oddities in the text**, recorded because a reviewer comparing the encoding with the source will meet them: § 266(l)(ii) reads "as the cause may be" (for "as the case may be") and "the terms and conditions of the conversions"; § 265(c)(3) ends with a full stop and (c)(4) follows it, so (c)(2)'s "and" no longer links the last two limbs; § 266(b)(1)-(4) is printed "[Repealed.]" in the middle of subsection (b); § 267(a) uses "(a)" and "(b)" as labels inside the subsection. None changes an answer.
16. **Section 267 is the one section of my range that section 114(b)(2) takes out of the stockholder-to-member reading** (§ 114(b)(2), src:202); sections 263-266 and 268 are not listed in § 114(b) or (c), so a member of a nonstock corporation is a "stockholder" there. Not asserted: it is a reading, recorded in fork F20.
17. **The five "facts" sentences** (§ 263(b), § 264(b), § 265(k), § 266(l), § 267(a)) each say that "facts" includes "the occurrence of any event, including a determination or action by any person or body", with the person varying (the corporation; the other entity or the converted corporation; the entity to which the corporation is being converted or the converting corporation; the entity). Each is encoded as a rule over four kinds of thing: an event, a determination by any person or body and an action by any person or body are facts (JUST TRUE); something that is neither is not covered (NOTHING), because "includes, but is not limited to" leaves the list open without making everything a fact (fork F26). The sentence before each, which allows terms to depend on facts ascertainable outside the document, is encoded as a condition (the manner must be clearly and expressly set forth). Asserted (all six test files, and four cases each in `dg8-cases-seat.l4`).

## What the stable binary does not accept

The binary is `1.6.98 (main, 7689b0d, 2026-10-19)`. Everything written is inside what `dialects.md` says `main` reads; the keel's table of 58 numbered probes is not repeated here. Found while writing:

- **`WHEN `ctor` WITH `field` THEN ...`** (naming the field of an enumeration constructor in a `CONSIDER` pattern) is rejected with `unexpected WITH`; bind the field positionally: ``WHEN `a corporation` kk THEN ...``. Not in `dialects.md`. Used in `s 265(a)`, `s 266(a)`, `s 267(d)(1)`.
- **``` `at least` 90% `of` b ```** mis-parses (the `%` takes ``` `of` b ``` as its argument and the error blames a missing definition of `of`); bracket the percentage: ``` `at least` (90%) `of` b ```. Used in `s 267(a)(1)`.
- **A nested `ELSE IF` on one line** under an `ELSE` is rejected with `incorrect indentation`; use `BRANCH`.
- **`IMPORT` finds only the modules beside it**, so the seat layer and the cases file import the layer-1 modules by file name (``IMPORT `dg8-s263-merger-with-partnerships` ``), and no layer-1 module imports another piece's module.
- **A name defined in two imported modules cannot be used**: every top-level name here starts `s 263(`..`s 268(` or `dg8 `, and every record field starts with its section locator (`s 263(b)(1) ...`), so the seat layer, which imports all the modules, sees no clash. Two modules may declare fields of the same name (probed and found to work), but fields were prefixed anyway so the merged folder is safe.
- **`YMD` comes from the core** (`dg-core.l4`), never from `daydate` (absent on this build); dates are compared with `AT LEAST`, `AT MOST`, `GREATER THAN`, `LESS THAN` and `EQUALS`, which work on dates made with it.
- **The seat's `@export` names and the question text** may contain parentheses, apostrophes and section marks; none was refused.
