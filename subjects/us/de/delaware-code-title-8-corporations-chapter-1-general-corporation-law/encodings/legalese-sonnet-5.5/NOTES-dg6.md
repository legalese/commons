## Seat

The seat is a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock in a Delaware corporation under the General Corporation Law: an incorporator, a director or officer, a stockholder including a minority stockholder. They ask what the Law lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them when corporations merge or consolidate.
Why this seat: Subchapter IX is addressed to the corporations, their boards and their officers, and the people it protects are the stockholders; the individual to whom it is applied is whoever stands in one of those places. A director asks whether the board must adopt a resolution, whether it may terminate or amend the agreement, what the agreement must state; an officer asks what the secretary must certify and what signing represents; a stockholder asks whether they are owed notice and by when, whether the vote carried, whether a vote was needed at all, whether they can have a copy of the agreement, whether they have appraisal rights, whether their old certificates and their standing to sue survive a holding-company merger, whether a personal liability survives; a plaintiff asks how to serve a foreign survivor and what that costs.
There is no seat for the Secretary of State or the Court of Chancery. What section 252(d) makes the Secretary of State do appears as what the foreign survivor is owed (the letter, the address, the way it is sent) and what a plaintiff may rely on (the record, kept five years from receipt). The Court of Chancery is not named in this range.
Only the individual's seat is written (the brief asks for one thin layer for the range). A second seat (the corporation, for instance) is not written, and nothing in layer 1 is shaped by the seat: no layer-1 rule was changed to suit it.
How the seat layer treats what a person cannot state as a fact: several questions ask a legal conclusion ("does the agreement comply with section 251?") because the parent question needs it; each such `@desc` says which other question works it out from facts. Where a layer-1 rule takes a list (the classes of a subsidiary's stock, the constituent corporations of an amendment, the tallies of a tender offer) the question asks about one item and says so (the class in which the parent's holding is smallest; your corporation and the other constituent corporation; one tally, to be asked again for each class). A field that no rule reads gets a fixed placeholder with a comment in the question's `WHERE`. One question, "In sections 251(d) and (e), what does \"agreement of merger\" mean when applied to a section 253 merger?", has `@export` and no inputs, so no `@desc`: its answer is a fixed construction rule of § 253(c). A fraction (a required vote) is entered as a numerator and a denominator, never as a decimal percentage (fork F25). The `§§` headings of the groups that arise only in a situation the person may not be in (251(f), (g), (h), 252, 253, 254) carry a `-- SITUATION:` comment saying so, so that a form can first ask whether it has happened.

## What the text turns out to say

1. No commencement or enactment date lies in lines 1058-1171 (the bracketed notes on the § 251 heading point to session-law application clauses that are not in the text), so no date is carried as a constant.
2. Section 251(f)'s exception "unless required by its certificate of incorporation" sits in the first sentence only. The second sentence (no stock issued) has no such exception. Asserted (`dg6-tests-s251f.l4`).
3. Section 251(f)(3): the 20% limit is "do not exceed", so exactly 20% of the common stock outstanding passes (200 of 1,000) and 201 fails. Asserted (`dg6-tests-s251f.l4`, `dg6-cases-seat.l4`).
4. Section 251(g) and (h) say "unless expressly required by its certificate of incorporation"; (f) says "unless required by its certificate". Asserted (`dg6-tests-s251f.l4`, `dg6-tests-s251h.l4`).
5. Section 251(h)'s size test is "held of record by more than 2,000 holders": 2,000 holders fails, 2,001 passes, and a listed class qualifies at any number. Asserted (`dg6-tests-s251h.l4`, `dg6-cases-seat.l4`).
6. Section 251(h)(3) tests the stock held after the offer against the vote that would have been needed "absent this subsection", for all the stock and for each class or series: under a "majority" requirement 500 of 1,000 votes is not enough and 501 is. Asserted (`dg6-tests-s251h.l4`, `dg6-cases-seat.l4`).
7. Section 251(h)(6)f. "received" loses the share if a certificate was canceled, or an uncertificated holding reduced by a sale, before the offer was consummated; the cancelled-certificate limb does not touch an uncertificated share and the sale limb does not touch a certificated one. Asserted (`dg6-tests-s251h.l4`).
8. Section 251(d) bars, once a constituent's stockholders have adopted, an amendment that alters what that constituent's holders receive, or alters terms adversely to a class or series of it; and, once any constituent's stockholders have adopted, one that alters the survivor's certificate. An amendment that alters only the consideration of a constituent whose stockholders have not yet voted is allowed (fork F8). Asserted (`dg6-tests-s251a.l4`, `dg6-cases-seat.l4`).

13. Section 252(d) gives a plaintiff a way to serve a foreign survivor, and puts three duties on the plaintiff (serve in duplicate, notify, pay the fee prescribed under § 391(a)(29)) and a liability on the survivor; the fee is taxed as costs only "if the plaintiff shall prevail". Asserted (`dg6-tests-s252.l4`, `dg6-cases-seat.l4`).

15. Section 253(a) does not say that the subsidiary's stockholders vote, or that they do not. The mechanism it gives is the parent's board resolution and the certificate of ownership and merger; the parent's own stockholders approve (by a majority after 20 days' notice) only where the parent is not the survivor; the protection of the subsidiary's minority is the terms the resolution must state and, under subsection (d), appraisal rights "as set forth in section 262". ## What the stable binary does not accept

Measured on this binary (1.6.98, `main`), only what I hit while writing; the dialect table in the keel's notes (probes 01 to 58) was read and its "write instead" column followed, and nothing in this piece uses a construct it lists as rejected (no `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED`, `BECAUSE`, section `GIVEN`, `TYPICALLY`, `SET OF`, `EVERY`, `RULES EFFECTIVE DATE`, `YMD` of my own).

- ``WHEN `a constructor` WITH k THEN ...`` is a parse error (`unexpected WITH`), although the writing skill's "WHEN Pat2 WITH field THEN" suggests it. The form that works binds the field by name after the constructor: ``WHEN `a corporation` k THEN ...`` (checked on the core's `Entity`).
- `length xs` is not defined (`I could not find a definition for the identifier length`); `null xs` is, and is what the empty-list guards use.
- A postfix `%` immediately before a mixfix keyword segment mis-parses: ``(`at least` 90% `of` b)`` gives "could not find a definition for the identifier of" and a type error on `%`. Write ``(`at least` (90%) `of` b)``.
- ``LIST `probe Class` OF 100, 90, `probe Class` OF 50, 45`` is an error ("expects 2 arguments, but you are applying it to 3"): build each record under a name, then `LIST a, b`.
- `l4 check` on every file of this piece prints only `Check succeeded.`; none prints `follows code on its line` or `is at the end of the file`, and none prints an unknown-annotation warning.
- Not tried, and so not claimed: lambdas over records (`GIVEN c YIELD ...`): I wrote named one-argument helper rules and passed them to `any`, `all` and `map` instead.
