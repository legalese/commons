# NOTES -- dg5: Delaware General Corporation Law (8 Del. C. ch. 1), Subchapter VII §§ 221-233 and Subchapter VIII §§ 241-246

This is piece `dg5` of the encoding of the General Corporation Law, written for the stable `l4` build 1.6.98 (sha256 `82ea44addf093ea4dd84c4e4416184920857ca9c762c5b31feed20541c20263e`).
It encodes lines 890-1057 of `de-code-t8-ch1-2026-10-01.txt`: notice (§§ 222, 229, 230, 232, 233), vacancies and contested elections (§§ 223, 225-227), consents and inspectors (§§ 228, 231), bondholders and records (§§ 221, 224), and amendment, retirement of stock, reduction of capital and restatement of the certificate (§§ 241-245; § 246 is `[Reserved.]`).

**The source.**
`de-code-t8-ch1-2026-10-01.txt` is a plain-text rendering, made by a converter script, of the Delaware Code Online (Delaware Division of Legislative Services, "through 85 Del. Laws, c. 550", fetched 2026-10-21).
It is not the official text, it is not marked up, and nothing in this folder has been verified against the official text.
Every `src:N` is a line of that file. The `srcrefs.py` gate checks every `-- src:N | ...` quotation in the `.l4` files against the line it names (result under `## What check.sh prints`).

**Method.**
Layer 1 is eight modules that follow the text's sections; each rule takes a record of facts or a few named inputs and carries `-- src:N | ...` quotation lines and an `@ref` with locators.
A decision the person would ask ("may", "must", "is") is a BOOLEAN predicate, not a DEONTIC value (the writing skill's source-patterns entries 5.1 and 5.2, on "may" and "must").
A judgment the text hands to the Court of Chancery is a conditional input ("if the Court determines ..."); a case the text does not answer is `MAYBE` with `NOTHING`, asserted.
Layer 2 is `dg5-seat.l4` (118 `@export` questions) and `dg5-cases-seat.l4` (212 assertions, at least one for every question).
Each layer-1 module has a test file whose expected values were written from the text before the code was run (`dg5-tests-*.l4`, 620 assertions).
One expected value was corrected while writing, and the correction was to my own arithmetic, not to the code: in `dg5-tests-amendment.l4`, 66 of 99 votes against "at least 2/3" (2/3 of 99 is exactly 66, so "at least" is reached; the first draft said not).

**Scope, stated as provisions.**
In: every section of the range, §§ 221-233 and 241-246, every subsection and paragraph, with the figures as the text states them (10 and 60 days; 30 days; 60 days; 1 week; 10 percent; 2,000 stockholders; $5,000).
Out (`inert`, with reasons in the coverage table): the 19 heading lines (13 section headings in Subchapter VII, 5 in Subchapter VIII, and the Subchapter VIII heading; pure titles), the 18 `History:` lines (citations), and the two bracketed application notes in the headings of §§ 228 and 232 (the session-law clauses are not in the text).
`repealed or blank in this text`: § 233(c), § 244(c), § 246.

**Dates.**
The range contains no date of enactment, no date of coming into force and no commencement rule: a search of lines 890-1057 outside the `History:` lines for four-digit years finds only the citation of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (lines 940 and 1022), and the only application clauses are the two bracketed notes in the headings of §§ 228 and 232, which point to session laws that are not in the text.
So no dated constant is defined and nothing is done about dates of commencement. Dates that rules use are facts the person states (a notice date, a meeting date, a filing date) and periods computed from them with the core's mixfix rules `the date` p `after` d and `the date` p `before` d.

## Seat

**The seat** is the one the brief fixes (`BRIEF.md`, layer 2): a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock in a Delaware corporation under the General Corporation Law: an incorporator, a director or officer, a stockholder including a minority stockholder.
That person asks what the Law lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.
Why: the brief says the Legalese website turns a seat layer into a question form (its Interact form asks the `@export` questions), and these are the people who read §§ 221-245 for themselves: a stockholder asking whether notice is owed to them and whether their class has a vote; a director asking how a vacancy is filled and whether the Court can remove them; an officer asking whether the corporation may act by consent and what the notice must say; an incorporator asking whether the certificate can still be amended.
The Secretary of State, the Court of Chancery and the Attorney General get no seat; their acts appear only as what they mean for the person ("may the Court order an election if I ask?", "do I have to give the Attorney General a copy?").
The registered agent is not an official, and one question is asked from its side (`As the registered agent, must I forward a copy of the application immediately (s 225)?`), as the mirror of the stockholder's question `Was the copy forwarded to me in the way the Law requires (s 225)?`, because § 225 puts a duty on the agent that the person in the seat is owed.
This is a reading of "the individual's seat" that goes one question beyond it (assumed, not ruled); deleting that one `@export` would change nothing else in the seat.

**Design choices taken** (the defaults ruled 2026-10-18 in `inputs/COMMONS-CLAUDE.md`, followed):
the text alone, with the instruments that set its numbers brought in (none are needed: every figure is stated in the range); the actors the text names; a judgment handed to someone else answered conditionally; the Law's own definitions worked out from facts (the three electronic-communication terms of § 232(d), "consent" in § 228(c), the "stockholders" deeming of § 221); borrowed terms asked as facts; one case is the text's natural unit (one meeting, one notice, one amendment, one consent); the Act's own terms with a plain gloss; the operative sections in scope, the machinery (here: none) out.

**What the person cannot state.**
The certificate and bylaws are asked as "does the certificate or the bylaws say otherwise?" and given to the layer-1 rule as the core's `Where a provision is made` (`in the certificate of incorporation` for yes, `in none of these` for no).
The kind of corporation is asked as "is it a stock corporation?" and "is it a charitable nonstock corporation?"; the seat maps a nonstock corporation that is not charitable to the core's `a nonprofit nonstock corporation that is not charitable`, and no rule of this range treats that kind differently from `a nonstock corporation with membership interests`.
A fraction of votes is asked as two whole numbers (a numerator and a denominator) so that "at least 2/3" is exact.

**Mirror cases** (the same facts asked from both sides of one relation, answers agreeing, each marked `MIRROR` in `dg5-cases-seat.l4`): § 222(b) (notice of a meeting: stockholder and secretary), § 222(c) (notice of an adjourned meeting), § 228(e) (prompt notice of action by consent), § 233(a) (one notice for a shared address), and § 242(b)(2) (the holder asks "do I vote as a class?", the board asks "which holders must?").

## Jural relations

Hohfeld's correlatives: a right (claim) of one party is the duty of the other; a privilege (liberty) is the other's no-right; a power is the other's liability (being subject to it); an immunity is the other's disability.
Each relation is encoded once, as one rule, named neutrally, and asked from both sides in layer 2 where both sides are in the seat.
An official's side (the Court, the Attorney General) is shown as what the person is owed or exposed to.

## What the stable binary does not accept

Measured on 1.6.98; each of these cost an error before it was avoided.

- **A genitive used as an argument of a mixfix rule must be in brackets.** `` `the date` p `after` a's `x` `` reads as `(... after a)'s x`; write `(a's `x`)`. The error names an unrelated token, not the genitive.
- **A typed empty list needs a rule with a `GIVETH`.** `EMPTY : LIST OF T` is a parse error (`unexpected :`); write a rule `GIVETH A LIST OF T` whose body is `EMPTY`.
- **A binding pattern in `CONSIDER` takes the constructor and the variable, not `WITH`.** `WHEN `someone else` s THEN`, not `WHEN `someone else` WITH s THEN`.
- **A continuation line of an application must be indented past the head of the call.** A call that starts mid-line and continues on the next line at a smaller indentation fails (`unexpected 0`); put the call in brackets on its own line and indent the continuation.
- **Nothing else from the dialect list was reached.** No `REFUSE`, `BECAUSE`, section `GIVEN`, `TYPICALLY`, set type, `EVERY`, `RULES EFFECTIVE DATE` or `YMD`-comparison appears in these files; `YMD` is the core's.
- **Names with parentheses and digits** (`` `s 242(b)(1) the amendment has been duly adopted` ``) work: every rule name in these files has them.

## Before this is worth relying on

- **No domain expert has read this against the text.** It is a first reading: a Delaware corporate lawyer should go through the fork register (F4, F5, F9, F10, F12 are the readings most likely to be disputed) and the `NOTHING` cases.
- **The text is a converter's rendering, not the official Code.** A bracketed note in a heading (§§ 228, 232) points to a session-law application clause that is not among the inputs; if a transitional rule applies to a notice or a consent given before that Act, these rules do not know it.
- **Two of the Law's judgments are inputs, not answers**: whether a failure to act was "within a reasonable time" (§ 226(a)(3)) and whether a director "did not act in good faith" (§ 225(c)). The Court decides them.
- **Cross-references are facts.** Everything the rules take from §§ 103, 104, 116, 154, 211-213, 215, 219, 220, 291, 352 and the federal securities statutes is a yes/no or a number the person states; a mistake there changes the answer and nothing checks it.
- **The seat asks one case at a time.** A list of consents, of classes entered on a vote, or of stockholders at a shared address is reduced to fixed slots (two classes in § 242(b)(1)) or to one person at a time (§ 233(a)); the layer-1 rules take lists, and the seat could be extended.
- **The seat's `@export` set is 118 questions**; changing the inputs of any of them is a breaking change for a deployment under the same name.
- **No independent test pass was run.** The tests and the seat cases were written by the same agent that wrote the rules, from the text, with expected values entered by hand before the code was run; a second reader would be more likely to find a reading that both share.
- **No second encoding of this range was compared.**
