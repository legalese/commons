## Seat

The seat is the one the brief fixes: a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock in a Delaware corporation under the General Corporation Law, that is, an incorporator, a director or officer, or a stockholder (a minority stockholder included).
They ask what the Law lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.
For ss 204 and 205 that person is the stockholder or director whose shares, or whose board's act, may be void or voidable for a failure of authorization, or who is asked to ratify it.
For ss 211 to 219 it is the stockholder (or the member of a nonstock corporation) who wants a meeting, a vote, a proxy, a record date or the list of stockholders, and the director who must run them.
For s 220 it is the stockholder, or the director, who wants to see the corporation's records.
The Court of Chancery, the Secretary of State and the Attorney General get no seat.
What the Court may do appears as what the person may ask it for and what the corporation, the directors and the stockholders are liable to.
A judgment the Law hands to someone else is a fact the person states, never guessed: what the Court has ordered, what the board has decided, whether the purpose is "reasonably related to a stockholder's interest as a stockholder" (s 220(a)(2)), whether an interest is "sufficient in law" to support an irrevocable proxy (s 212(e)), whether the board's notice was "prompt" (s 204(g)).
The seat decides only which questions layer 2 asks.
It leaves nothing out of layer 1: all 107 rows of the coverage table are encoded, forked or marked `inert` with a reason.
`dg4-seat.l4` asks 164 questions, each with `@export` and `@desc`, grouped under `§` headings in the text's order, and calls the layer-1 rules without restating any of them.
Where a layer-1 rule takes a record, a `MAYBE` or a list, the question builds it from plain answers; a field the rule never reads is given a fixed placeholder with a comment saying so (the `Placeholders:` comments in `dg4-seat.l4`).
The questions for ss 211 to 214 and 216 return `NOTHING` for a nonstock corporation where s 215(a) takes the provision out of it; a member of a nonstock corporation asks the questions grouped under `s 215`.
The questions on ss 204 and 205 take no kind of corporation: s 114(c)(3) leaves ss 204 and 205 applying to a nonprofit nonstock corporation read through s 114(a), and this piece writes them in terms of stock and shares only, so a member of a nonstock corporation who asks about them reads "membership interests" for "shares" by hand.
The 16 layer-1 rules named `s 2...` that `dg4-seat.l4` does not name are helpers (the later of two days, the size of the base a vote is counted against, the number of directors needed, and the like), each called by a rule the seat does name or by a test.
The worked cases in `dg4-cases-seat.l4` use four invented people: Alex Rivera, a minority stockholder of Acme Inc. (a stock corporation); Bea Chen, a director of Acme; Cy Okafor, one of the persons who acted as Acme's initial board; and Dee Park, a member of Park Commons, a nonprofit nonstock corporation.
Every one of the 164 questions has at least one case, and `dg4-cases-seat.l4` has 425 assertions written and 425 satisfied.

## Needs from the core

1. A business-day unit.
The core's `Time unit` has calendar days, weeks, months and years only (dg-core.l4, `Time unit`).
s 220(c) counts "5 business days", so I took the number of business days since the demand as an input (fork F-B5).
A `period in business days`, with a calendar of business days, would let that rule compute it.
2. A date with a time of day.
The stable build has no date-time type and the core has none, so ss 204(b)(1), (f), (h)(5) and (h)(6), which speak of times, return `NOTHING` when two events fall on one day (fork F-T).
A record type of a date and a time of day, with an order, would remove those cases.
3. A shared helper for the later of two days (and for the latest of several).
I wrote `s 204(h)(6) the later of two days` and `s 211(c) the later of two days` locally; the two have distinct names, so they do not collide at the merge, but one core rule would do.
4. The applicability of a section to a kind of corporation under s 114.
`dg4-s215-nonstock-members.l4` carries my reading of s 114(b)(2) and (c)(2) for ss 217, 218 and 219 (fork F-N); if dg1 encodes s 114, the lead should compare and keep one.

## What the text turns out to say

1. s 204(c) sends to the stockholders only a ratification "pursuant to paragraph (b)(1)"; a ratification of the election of the initial board under (b)(2) is not sent to them under (c), though (h)(6)a. speaks of the case where "no such vote of stockholders is required" after resolutions under "(b)(1) or (b)(2)". Asserted.
2. The 120 days of s 205(f) run from "the later of the validation effective time and the time notice ... is given" under s 204(g); where no (g) notice is required they run from the validation effective time; where a (g) notice is required and has not yet been given the period has not begun, and the rule for the last day returns `NOTHING` while the action is not barred. A person who was due a notice under (d) or (g) and was not given it is not barred at all. Asserted.
3. s 219(a) gives the stockholder a window of "10 days ending on the day before the meeting date": the first day is the tenth day before the meeting, the last is the day before it, and the text gives no right to examine the list on the meeting day itself. Asserted.
4. s 219(a)'s proviso makes the list reflect the stockholders "as of the tenth day before the meeting date" when the record date is less than 10 days before the meeting: a day earlier than the record date, so a person who became a record holder between the two is entitled to vote and is not on the list, and the section does not reconcile them; s 219(c) makes the stock ledger "the only evidence" of who may vote or examine. Asserted (the day).
5. s 215(d) lets a member of a nonstock corporation ask the Court of Chancery to order an election as soon as the election "shall not be held on the day designated by the bylaws", with no waiting period; s 211(c) makes a stockholder wait 30 days after the designated date, or 13 months after the latest of the organization, the last annual meeting and the last consent, and lets a director apply too. Asserted.
6. The floor of one third on a quorum is in s 216 ("in no event shall a quorum consist of less than 1/3 of the shares entitled to vote") and not in s 215(c), so a nonstock corporation's bylaws may set a quorum below one third of the members. Asserted.
7. s 220(f) reaches the functional equivalent of the director and officer questionnaires of (a)(1)i. only "in the case of a corporation that has a class of stock listed on a national securities exchange"; for an unlisted corporation that lacks them, (f) gives no order, while (a)(1)c., e. and g. are reached for every corporation. Asserted.
8. s 220(c) lets the stockholder apply when the corporation "refuses to permit an inspection" or "does not reply to the demand within 5 business days": a refusal needs no waiting. Asserted.
9. s 211(c) and s 215(d) say the shares (or persons) present at the Court-ordered meeting "shall constitute a quorum" "notwithstanding any provision of the certificate of incorporation", with no minimum; the text does not say what follows if none is present, and the rules return `NOTHING` for none. Asserted.
10. s 218(d) says the section "shall not be deemed to invalidate any voting or other agreement among stockholders or any irrevocable proxy which is not otherwise illegal"; it does not say what the section does to one that is otherwise illegal, and the rule returns `NOTHING`, not `FALSE`. Asserted.
11. s 220(b)(4)a. says the section does not affect a stockholder's right to seek discovery "if the stockholder is in litigation with the corporation"; for a stockholder who is not in litigation the text is silent, and the rule returns `NOTHING`. Asserted.
12. s 220(a)(1) puts the stock ledger and the list of stockholders outside "books and records"; s 220(c) gives the stockholder the stock ledger and the list on a lighter showing (that the person is a stockholder and made a compliant demand), after which the corporation bears the burden of proving an improper purpose, while for books and records the stockholder bears the burden of proving a proper purpose and must also meet (b)(2). Asserted.
13. The range holds no enactment, commencement or effective-date rule, so no date value is carried: the History lines cite session laws, the headings of ss 204, 205 and 220 carry bracketed notes ("For application of this section, see 80 Del. Laws, c. 40, § 16, and 81 Del. Laws, c. 354, § 16"; "see 80 Del. Laws, c. 40, § 16"; "see 85 Del. Laws, c. 6, § 3") that point to application clauses not among the inputs, and "the commencement of its existence" in s 204(i) is about a corporation's existence. Not asserted: a statement about the range.

What the source does not answer (each returns `NOTHING` or is an input): the counting convention for periods (F-D); the meaning of "business day" (F-B5); a tie for the last seat (F-L); the order of two events on one day (F-T); what "prompt" means (F-P); whether an otherwise illegal voting agreement survives s 218 (finding 10); the position of a stockholder not in litigation under s 220(b)(4)a. (finding 11); what a Court-ordered meeting does when nobody is present (finding 9); the vote "required for a specified action", which each other section states (cross-references).

## What the stable binary does not accept

Loud failures (the build says so):
- `JUST facts's amount` is rejected, with "You are trying to apply facts ... (which is not a function) to 1 argument"; write `JUST (facts's amount)`.
  The same holds for a genitive used as an argument of a mixfix call: parenthesise it, or the parse stops at `unexpected AT`.
- `WHEN x FOLLOWED BY y z THEN` is rejected ("I could not find a definition for the identifier y"): the pattern after `FOLLOWED BY` takes one name.
- A call whose argument is on the next line is rejected ("unexpected 5"); a mixfix such as `at least` ... `of`, or `after` with its argument on a new line, has to be on one line, or its arguments put in a `WHERE`.
- `DateTime`, `Date`-with-time and `Time` constructors are not defined ("I could not find a definition for the identifier DateTime"), so times of day cannot be written (fork F-T).

## Before this is worth relying on

- Read each rule beside the official text of 8 Del. C. ch. 1: the inputs are a converter's plain-text rendering, not the official text, and nothing in this folder has been checked against it.
- Have a Delaware lawyer read the forks in the order of their effect on an answer: F-B (the board's quorum on a ratification), F-E, F-C, F-Q, F-N, F-M, F-E2, then the counting convention F-D.
- The tests were written by the same hand as the rules (the brief provides no independent test author); an independent pass would find what a single author cannot, above all in ss 204 and 220, which carry most of the assertions.
- Every board's, officer's and Court's judgment is an input; the encoding does not predict what the Court of Chancery will do, and none of the case law on "proper purpose", "necessary and essential" or "compelling need" is in it (the text alone, as the brief pins).
- Times of day are not modelled (fork F-T); a same-day order of events is `NOTHING`.
- When the other pieces land, the inputs in the cross-reference table should be replaced by the rules that decide them (dg1: ss 103, 104, 108, 114, 116; dg2: subchapter II, s 122(18); dg3: ss 161, 203; dg5: ss 222, 224, 227 to 233), and my reading of s 114 compared with dg1's.
- The seat gives some records a fixed placeholder for a field the rule it calls never reads (see `## Seat`); a change to such a rule that reads the field would need the question to ask for it.
- ss 204 and 205 are written in terms of stock and shares; their application to a nonprofit nonstock corporation through s 114(a) is not translated.
