## Seat

**The seat** (ruled for the whole Law): a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock in a Delaware corporation under the General Corporation Law: an incorporator, a director or officer, a stockholder including a minority stockholder.
They ask what the Law lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.

**Why this seat for Subchapters X, XI and XII.**
The subchapters are about the end of a corporation's life and its afterlife: selling or mortgaging its assets, dissolving it, winding it up, putting it in the hands of a receiver or trustee, and reviving it.
Each of the three people in the seat is on the receiving end of different rules.
A stockholder asks whether a sale of all the assets needs their vote and how much notice they are owed, whether they can ask the Court of Chancery to wind up a 50-50 joint venture, when they may be paid a distribution, how much they can be made to pay a creditor of a dissolved corporation, whether they have appraisal rights in a merger ordered by a bankruptcy court, and whether they can call the meeting that revives a corporation.
A director or officer asks what vote dissolves the corporation, whether they are personally liable to claimants, what their corporation must publish and mail to claimants, whether they can be appointed a trustee, what a receiver or trustee must file, and what the corporation owes to revive.
An incorporator asks whether they can surrender the charter before shares are issued.
A creditor-employee in the seat asks whether they have a lien for wages (an officer does not).

**Officials get no seat.**
The Secretary of State, the Court of Chancery, the Register in Chancery, the Attorney General, a receiver or trustee and a bankruptcy court appear only as what is done to the person or what the person is owed: the Court "may" is always a plain input and is never guessed.

**How the layers are built.**
Layer 1 (`dg9-x-sale-of-assets`, `dg9-x-dissolution`, `dg9-x-winding-up`, `dg9-xi-receivers`, `dg9-xii-revival`) states each rule once, in the text's order, with a quotation and an `@ref` with `src:` locators on every rule and every type.
It takes no one's point of view.
Layer 2 (`dg9-seat.l4`, `dg9-cases-seat.l4`) asks the person's questions as plain inputs (BOOLEAN, NUMBER, DATE, one STRING for an address, or a choice declared in the core or in layer 1; never a `MAYBE`), builds the layer-1 inputs in `WHERE` (a party such as an applicant is built there from the person's answers about their role), and calls the layer-1 rules; it restates no rule.
It has 145 questions in five groups (ss 271-272, 273-277, 278-285, 291-303, 311-314) and 417 worked cases in `dg9-cases-seat.l4`.
A question whose layer-1 rule can answer "the text does not say" returns `MAYBE`, and a case asserts `NOTHING` for it; 32 of the 33 such questions have one, and the 33rd (`s 279 I may be appointed trustee or receiver of the dissolved corporation`) answers NOTHING only for offices the seat does not ask about.
Questions are grouped under `§` headings in the text's order.

**Provisions that get no layer-2 question**, each still encoded in layer 1 and in the coverage table: section 283 (which court hears an application the other sections already name), section 285 (a step between the Register in Chancery and the Secretary of State), section 297 (a step between the Court and its receiver), section 299 (substitution as party and abatement: a step between the receiver and the court) and section 301's second sentence.
The reason is the one the Guide gives: they decide nothing the person in the seat would ask.
Three more layer-1 rules have no question of their own: the vote in `s 311(a)(4) the vote that revokes the dissolution or restores the certificate` (it is used inside the authorization question), `s 275(i) the Secretary of State's record of a service sets forth what the subsection requires` (an official's record) and `s 312(j) the party who performs the act for the corporation` (it returns a party, not an answer).

## What the text turns out to say

Each finding is asserted by a test in the `dg9-tests-*.l4` files or a case in `dg9-cases-seat.l4` where it ends "Asserted."; the file is named in the sentence.

1. **A 50-50 deadlock has a clock, and either failure starts the Court's hand** (s 273(b)). If both stockholders have not filed the certificate of agreement on the plan within 3 months of the petition, the Court of Chancery may dissolve the corporation and appoint trustees or receivers, without waiting for the year; and if they filed it but not the certificate of completion within 1 year, it may then. A certificate of extension filed before the last day of a period lengthens it. `dg9-tests-x-dissolution.l4`. Asserted.
2. **Failing to file the certificate of dissolution does not keep a corporation alive** (s 275(f)). A corporation whose certificate of incorporation limits its existence to a date ceases to exist on that date whether or not it filed within the 90 days before it; the requirement to file remains; a certificate of good standing issued after the date has no force or effect. `dg9-tests-x-dissolution.l4`. Asserted.
3. **The 150-day limit can bind before the 90-day limit** (s 280(a)(3), (b)(2)). A rejection or an offer of security must be mailed within 90 days of receiving the claim and, in all events, at least 150 days before the end of the section 278 period: a claim received on 1 June 2027 with the period ending on 1 January 2028 can be rejected by 30 August 2027 on the first limit but only by 4 August 2027 on the second. `dg9-tests-x-winding-up.l4`. Asserted.
4. **Distribution to the stockholders waits to the 151st day** (s 281(a)), and the two ways of providing for later claims use different horizons: a corporation that follows section 280 provides for claims likely to arise within 5 years of dissolution (or a longer period, up to 10 years, that the Court determines: more than 10 years is not a period the text allows), while one that adopts a plan under section 281(b) provides for claims within 10 years. `dg9-tests-x-winding-up.l4`. Asserted.
5. **The stockholder's immunity under section 282(b) is for a distribution under section 281(a) only.** A stockholder paid under a plan of distribution (section 281(b)) has the caps of (a) and (c) but not the bar of (b) for a claim on which no action was begun in time. `dg9-tests-x-winding-up.l4`. Asserted.
6. **A stockholder's exposure is the lesser of two figures, and then capped again in aggregate** (s 282(a), (c)): the pro rata share of the claim or the amount distributed, whichever is less, on each claim, and all claims together never more than the amount distributed. `dg9-tests-x-winding-up.l4`. Asserted.
7. **An officer has no wage lien** (s 300). An employee in the regular employ of an insolvent corporation has a lien for up to 2 months' wages, ahead of every other debt; "the word employee shall not be construed to include any of the officers". `dg9-tests-xi-receivers.l4`. Asserted.
8. **No appraisal right in a merger ordered through the bankruptcy court** (s 303(b)): a corporation acting on a bankruptcy court's decrees may merge or consolidate and "no stockholder shall have any statutory right of appraisal". For the other listed actions the subsection says nothing about appraisal. `dg9-tests-xi-receivers.l4`. Asserted.
9. **A nonstock corporation is outside most of section 311** (s 311(f)): only subsections (b), (d) and (e) apply to it, so the time and vote rules of (a) and the franchise tax payment of (g) are not stated for it; its own manner is "analogous". `dg9-tests-xii-revival.l4`. Asserted.
10. **Revival has a five-year cliff in price** (s 312(g)): within 5 years the corporation pays the taxes, penalties and interest due when its certificate lapsed; after more than 5 years it pays instead 3 times the annual franchise tax for the year of revival, "in lieu". On the fifth anniversary the first rule still applies. `dg9-tests-xii-revival.l4`. Asserted.
11. **Revival can be authorized below a quorum, or by electing a board** (s 312(h)): a majority of the directors then in office, though less than a quorum, or the sole director; where none is available the stockholders elect a full board; and any officer or stockholder may call the special meeting on notice under section 222. `dg9-tests-xii-revival.l4`. Asserted.
12. **A corporation whose charter a court revoked or forfeited for abuse, misuse or nonuse cannot use section 312** (s 312(b), s 284). `dg9-tests-xii-revival.l4`. Asserted.
13. **A certificate provision requiring stockholder consent to a sale does not reach a collateral sale unless it says so, but only if it first took effect on or after 1 August 2023** (s 272(d)); for an earlier provision the subsection says nothing. `dg9-tests-x-sale-of-assets.l4`. Asserted.
14. **A sale to a wholly-owned, controlled subsidiary needs no stockholder resolution unless the certificate of incorporation says it does** (s 271(c)); a bylaw cannot add one. `dg9-tests-x-sale-of-assets.l4`. Asserted.
15. **The continuing life of a dissolved corporation never includes continuing its business** (s 278), and an action begun within the 3 years survives past them until the judgments are executed. `dg9-tests-x-winding-up.l4`. Asserted.
16. **Directors' protection is stated one way** (s 281(c)): directors of a corporation that complied with subsection (a) or (b) are not personally liable to the claimants; for one that did neither the subsection says nothing, so the model answers NOTHING and not "liable". `dg9-tests-x-winding-up.l4`. Asserted.
17. **A creditor who failed to prove a claim in time is not yet barred** (s 295): the bar comes by direction of the Court, so the case without a direction is not covered (NOTHING). `dg9-tests-xi-receivers.l4`. Asserted.
18. **Where the Law is silent about the effect of a failure it is not read as a bar**: sections 271(b), 275(e), 281(c), 282, 302(a) and 312(e) each have a NOTHING case, listed in the fork register (dg9-F15). Asserted in each test file.
19. **The range has no commencement rule and no date of enactment.** The only date it names is 1 August 2023, in section 272(d), carried as a constant with an `@ref`. `dg9-tests-x-sale-of-assets.l4`. Asserted.
20. **Section 295's "time fixed" is the Court's, so the encoding cannot compute whether a proof of claim is late**: it takes the last day as an input (rules of the Court of Chancery are not among the inputs).

## Before this is worth relying on

1. **No domain expert has read this against the official text.** The source is a converter script's plain-text rendering of the Delaware Code Online, not the official text, and nothing here is verified against the official text. Mark the encoding `draft`.
2. **It is one agent's reading, checked only by the agent's own tests.** The tests' expected values were written from the text before each run, and one real bug (`NOT a OR b`) was found by them, but there was no independent test author. An independent pass over the fork register (dg9-F1 to F23) is the first thing to do.
3. **Thirty-six rows of the cross-reference table are legal judgments or other pieces' outputs taken as inputs.** When the pieces are merged, the lead should unify the duplicates: section 103 compliance appears under seven names here; section 222 under two; the `the date the corporation expired by its own limitation or was dissolved` input and the section 278 end date are one fact asked by several rules.
4. **Chapter 5 of the Law (franchise taxes, exempt corporations), Title 6, the Federal Bankruptcy Code and the Rules of the Court of Chancery are not among the inputs.** Every figure and status that depends on them is a plain input; the encoding cannot say what franchise tax a corporation owes or when the Court of Chancery fixed the time for proofs of claim.
5. **The Court "may" throughout.** Every discretion of the Court of Chancery, the Secretary of State or an officer is a plain input or an explicit "the text puts the Court in a position to"; nothing here predicts a decision.
6. **The counting rules are forks.** Where a provision turns on the last day of a period (ss 271(a), 273(b), 275(f), 280, 281(a), 284(c), 292(b), 296, 302(a), 311(a), 312(g)) the reading is in the fork register; a different reading changes the answer on exactly the boundary day.
7. **Seat layer deployment:** changing a question's inputs is a breaking change for a deployed form. The seat's inputs should be reviewed with a person who has Legalese Cloud access before deployment; I did not deploy.
8. **Sections 283, 285, 297, 299 and 301 (second sentence) have no layer-2 question** by the Guide's test (they decide nothing the person in the seat asks), nor do three helper rules (the Seat section names them); a reviewer who disagrees can add one over the existing layer-1 rules without changing any.
