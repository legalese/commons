## Seat

The seat is the brief's: a person who incorporates, directs or holds stock in a Delaware corporation under the General Corporation Law (an incorporator, a director or officer, a stockholder including a minority stockholder), asking what the Law lets them do, what it requires of them, what they may require of the corporation, and what can be done to them.
The person reaches these two subchapters in four situations, and `dg11-seat.l4` groups its questions by them under `§` headings in the Law's order:
the person's non-United States entity is becoming a Delaware corporation (§ 388);
the person's non-United States entity is moving its domicile into Delaware because of an emergency (§ 389);
the person's Delaware corporation is moving out (§ 390);
the person is filing something with the Secretary of State or has been asked to pay a fee (§ 391), with the general sections after it (§§ 393 to 398).
The officials get no seat: the Secretary of State, the Attorney General, the State Bank Commissioner, the Director of the Division of Corporations, the General Assembly and the Court appear only as the bearers of a duty or a liability toward the person, or as the holders of a claim against the person (see the Jural relations table).
A judgment the Law hands to someone else is asked as a fact the person states and never guessed: the Secretary of State's waiver (§ 389(c)), the directors' determination (§ 389(i)), the management's judgment (§ 389(c)(4)f.), the credit and returned-check discretions (§ 391(e), (g)), and the officials' determination (§ 395(d)(3)).
Where the text gives the person a claim and also puts a duty on the person, the layer asks both sides.
Section 389(i) is asked as "May the directors withhold my dividend" (the holder) and "May I as a director withhold the amount" (the director).
Section 390(b) is asked as "Was the notice of the meeting given as required" (what the corporation owes me as a stockholder), "Must I approve the move myself" (my veto if I will become a general partner) and "Has the board adopted the resolution as required" (the director).
Section 390(b)(5) is asked as "How much must I pay the Secretary of State on service" and "Have I done what the plaintiff's duty on service requires" (the plaintiff), and as "To what address will the Secretary of State mail the letter" (the corporation).
Section 391 states some fees only as a ceiling ("up to $175"); the Secretary of State's schedule is not among the inputs, so those questions answer "the most I can be charged".
A question that answers `NOTHING` means the text of the Law does not settle that case.
The seat is recorded here because the Commons guide asks for it; the cases file `dg11-cases-seat.l4` has at least one case for every question, written as a person in the seat would state the facts.

## What the stable binary does not accept

I met no construct that the stable build 1.6.98 rejected while writing this piece.
The constructs the Commons guide and `dialects.md` list as rejected were avoided from the start, and this is what I wrote instead:
no `REFUSE`, `#ASSERT REFUSED` or `BECAUSE` (a case the text does not answer returns `NOTHING`, a `MAYBE`, and is asserted equal to `NOTHING`, or a constructor named for the case);
no section-level `GIVEN` (each rule carries its own `GIVEN`);
no `TYPICALLY`, no deontic `MUST`/`MAY` rules and no `PARTY` (every obligation is a yes/no fact or a number a rule computes; the party appears in the rule name and the comment);
no `SET OF`, `EVERY` or `WHOSE`;
every `NOT` inside a rule is bracketed, and a bare `#ASSERT NOT rule args` negates one whole call;
each `#ASSERT` is on one line;
`YMD` is the core's and is not redefined;
money is a `NUMBER` of United States dollars (the fraction of a cent in § 391(a)(1) is `(2 DIVIDED BY 5) TIMES 0.01`, exact);
the $100 unit is `FLOOR (x DIVIDED BY 100)`;
a record is built inside a question's `WHERE` with `WITH` from the plain inputs, so a question takes only plain inputs and the `@export` form can ask it;
a name defined in two imported modules cannot be used, so every top-level name here begins with its locator and no name is defined twice in this piece or in the core (a script checked it).

## Before this is worth relying on

1. The text is a converter's rendering of the Delaware Code Online, not the official text; every quotation here is a slice of that rendering (`srcrefs.py` checks it mechanically), and none of it has been compared with the official Code.
2. The bracketed application notes at §§ 388 and 390 (83 Del. Laws, c. 377, § 24; 84 Del. Laws, c. 98, § 18) point to clauses that are not in the text, so which filings and transfers the two sections reach is not answered here.
3. The dollar amounts of § 391 are those of the rendering (through 85 Del. Laws, c. 550); fee schedules change more often than other law, and the Secretary of State's schedule beneath the "up to" ceilings of (a)(11), (16), (24) and (h) is not among the inputs.
4. A lawyer should read every `fork` row and every `inert` row of the coverage table, and the Fork register, before the answers are used; the readings of F-391-5 (the dearer dissolution certificate) and F-390-4 (a printed "expected") are the most likely to be rendering slips.
5. The cross-references out of this piece are inputs, not computed: § 103 (dg1), § 102(a)(1) (dg1), subchapter II (dg2), §§ 321 and 371 (dg10), chapter 5, chapter 6, Title 29, the federal banking statutes and foreign law. The merge should either wire the dg1, dg2 and dg10 ones to the other pieces' rules or leave them as questions.
6. The seat layer's `its kind` placeholder (Needs from the core, item 1) has no effect on any rule here, but it is a placeholder.
7. I am the only author of the tests: every expected value is hand-computed from the text before the run, but there was no independent test author, so a misreading that I made in both the rule and the test would pass.
8. The seat's questions have not been run through the Legalese Interact form; they have `@export` and `@desc` and typecheck, and each has a case.
9. Facts about foreign law, federal statutes and Title 29 are the person's to state; nothing here checks them.
