## Register access

Reachable, but only after finding the right door. https://www.wyoleg.gov/StateStatutes/StatutesConstitution returns HTTP 200 to curl but is an Angular shell with no content in the HTML, so I loaded the LSO site in a real browser (the built-in browser pane, ordinary Chrome, no user-agent spoofing) and read its own navigation. That page offers three routes: 'Wyoming Statutes, Constitution, and Non-Codified Water Laws' -> the LSO's own Rocket NXT (Folio) browser at /NXT/gateway.dll; 'Wyoming Statutes-Files in Download Format' -> one PDF per title at https://wyoleg.gov/statutes/compress/title<NN>.pdf (zero-padded below 10: title01.pdf, title09.pdf); and 'Wyoming Statutes Annotated' -> an off-site LexisNexis-hosted edition at http://www.lexisnexis.com/hottopics/wystatutes/. The NXT browser builds its table of contents from an XML endpoint the page itself calls, gateway.dll?f=xmlcontents&command=getchildren&basepathid=<path>, which returns the infobase tree - title captions, chapter captions, article captions and section catchlines - as metadata. I used that endpoint with curl and an honest self-identifying user-agent ('canon-legislation-indexer/1.0 (metadata indexing; contact ...)'), about 25 requests spaced 1-2 seconds apart, plus about 6 browser page loads. Everything returned HTTP 200 first time. No 403, no 429, no bot challenge, no CAPTCHA, no rate limiting. https://wyoleg.gov/robots.txt returns a 404 page, so the site publishes no crawl rules. I read only the contents tree, never a section document, so no statute text was copied; the per-title PDFs were confirmed to exist by HEAD request only. Note for a later pass: the NXT root offers seven separate infobases (2026, 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022, 2021 and 2020 Wyoming Statutes); everything below is from the 2026 one.

## Topics with no legislation here

- **Leave/holiday pay**: No Wyoming statute. I walked the whole of Title 27 (Labor and Employment) on the register: its fifteen chapters are General Provisions, Labor and Statistics, Unemployment Compensation, Wages, Hours of Labor, Employment of Women and Children, Labor Unions, Employment Offices and Agencies, Fair Employment Practices, Collective Bargaining for Fire Fighters, Occupational Health and Safety, Worker's Compensation (two chapters), Displaced Worker Education and Training and Presumptive Disability - none of which creates any entitlement to annual, sick, family or holiday leave, paid or unpaid. Chapter 5 (Hours of Labor) is about overtime for state and county employees (27-5-101) and mine working days (27-5-102), with 27-5-103 to 27-5-107 repealed in 1979. Leave for state employees is set by the Wyoming Personnel Rules (regulation) rather than statute, and job-protected family leave comes from the federal FMLA (29 U.S.C. ch. 28). Nothing to list.
- **Termination/severance**: No Wyoming statute. Wyoming is an at-will employment state with no severance-pay statute and no mini-WARN mass-layoff notice statute; federal WARN (29 U.S.C. ss 2101-2109) does that work. Title 27, ch. 13 is 'Displaced Worker Education and Training' - retraining funding, not termination rights. The one statutory duty on termination is the timing of final wages, already covered by the Act listed under Payroll/wages (Wyo. Stat. Ann. ss 27-4-104 and 27-4-105, 'Payment of employee quitting or discharged and suit for wages'). Discriminatory discharge is dealt with by Title 27, ch. 9 (Fair Employment Practices), an anti-discrimination statute rather than a termination statute. Nothing to list.
- **Personal tax/withholding**: Wyoming levies no individual income tax, so there is no statute to list and no state wage withholding. This is not an assumption: on the register, Title 39 (Taxation and Revenue) ch. 7 'INCOME TAXES' contains exactly one section, '39-7-101. Repealed By Laws 1998, ch. 5, s 4.', and ch. 12 'INCOME TAX' contains exactly one section, '39-12-101. Preemption by state.' Wyoming's personal tax burden is sales and use tax (Title 39, ch. 15 and ch. 16) and property tax (ch. 13). Federal income tax withholding is 26 U.S.C. ch. 24.
- **Passenger compensation**: Federal, and largely preempted. Denied boarding, cancellation and delay compensation come from US DOT rules under 49 U.S.C. s 41712 (14 C.F.R. pt. 250, pt. 259), and the Airline Deregulation Act (49 U.S.C. s 41713) preempts state law on air-carrier prices, routes and services. Wyoming's Title 37 does carry carrier chapters - ch. 8 Motor Carriers, ch. 9 Railroads, ch. 11 Common Carriers by Air - but they are licensing and safety statutes and create no passenger compensation right; interstate motor-carrier and rail passenger matters are federal (49 U.S.C. subtits. IV-V). Nothing to list.
- **Customs/duties**: Federal, and constitutionally exclusive. U.S. Const. art. I, s 8, cl. 1 and s 10, cl. 2; the Tariff Act of 1930 and the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, 19 U.S.C. Wyoming has a Foreign Trade Zones chapter (Title 40, ch. 11) but it merely enables participation in the federal zone programme (19 U.S.C. s 81a et seq.) and levies nothing.
- **Work permits/immigration**: Federal. Immigration status and work authorisation are governed by the Immigration and Nationality Act, 8 U.S.C. ch. 12, with employer verification at 8 U.S.C. s 1324a. Wyoming has no work-permit statute; the only related state provision seen on the register is Title 42, ch. 12 (Citizens and Qualified Aliens Status Verification for Welfare), a benefits-eligibility check rather than a work permit.

## Statute text deposited 2026-10-20: state benefits and the taxes and reliefs that fall on individuals

Wyoming has **no personal income tax**, confirmed in the deposited text: Title 39, ch. 7 (Income Taxes) consists of "39-7-101. Repealed By Laws 1998, ch. 5, § 4.", and ch. 12 (Income Tax) only reserves the field to the state ("The state of Wyoming does hereby preempt for itself the field of imposing and levying income taxes ...", W.S. 39-12-101). So the sample is state benefits plus the property-tax exemptions and refunds an individual can claim. **How the pieces connect, as read in the deposited texts:**

- **The reliefs exclude each other.** An applicant for the elderly and disabled refund must say whether they have applied for or received a property tax exemption under W.S. 39-13-105, a property tax refund under 39-13-109(c)(v) or a credit under 39-13-109(d) for the same year (39-11-109(c)(ii)).
- **Benefits count as income.** For the elderly and disabled refund, "income" includes "public assistance payments, unemployment compensation, federal social security payments, veteran's benefits and disability payments" (39-11-109(c)(i)(C)), so the UI and POWER benefits in this set, and federal Social Security, reduce that refund.
- **Federal income tax returns as evidence.** Both refunds are verified by the applicant's federal income tax return (39-11-109(c); 39-13-109(c)(v)(B)). The federal Code is in `subjects/us/internal-revenue-code-of-1986`.
- **UI and federal law.** Ch. 3 defines "wage" and "employment" by reference to FUTA, 26 U.S.C. 3301-3311 (27-3-102 to 27-3-105). Claimants are told that "Benefits are subject to federal income tax" and may elect federal withholding (27-3-320). IRC s 85 is deposited in the IRC subject (`usc26-ch1-schB-ptI-III.txt`); FUTA (IRC ch. 23) is indexed there but not deposited.
- **State SSI supplement.** W.S. 42-2-103(d) entitles a person receiving federal SSI "with no other income during any one (1) calendar month" to a monthly state payment set in the budget, "not ... less than the required payment under applicable federal law". SSA Title XVI, incl. s 1616 (42 U.S.C. 1382e), is deposited in `subjects/us/social-security-act` (`usc42-ch7-schXVI.txt`). Art. 4 also borrows the SSI disability definition, 42 U.S.C. 1382c, for Medicaid trust and transfer rules (42-2-402).
- **TANF.** Ch. 2 refers to ss 402 and 408 of the Social Security Act as amended by P.L. 104-193 (the family-violence option, 42-2-103). Title IV-A is indexed in `subjects/us/social-security-act` but not deposited there.
- **SNAP** is federal (`subjects/us/food-and-nutrition-act-of-2008`); ch. 2 defines the state's SNAP benefit terms (42-2-102), and ch. 3 lets the department withhold SNAP overissuances from UI benefits (27-3-321).

**Terms.** Read 2026-10-19 (UTC). The Disclaimer page (https://wyoleg.gov/Legislature/disclaimer) is an Angular page; its text, read in the page's own script, says "The information obtained from this site is not the official record, nor intended to replace the official record, of the Wyoming Legislature." No copyright notice, licence or reuse restriction. wyoleg.gov has no robots.txt (404). The LexisNexis-hosted annotated edition (earlier note: "provided for non-commercial use") was not used; the owner's ruling of 20 Oct 2026 treats Legalese Commons as non-commercial in any case. Verdict: **clean**.

**Gaps.**
- The PDFs state no as-at date; the creation dates (title 27 and 42: 2026-05-08, after the 2026 budget session; title 39: 2026-10-14) are the best currency evidence.
- The POWER payment table in the text is labelled "for the year 2008"; current payment levels are set by rule. The state SSI payment amount is set in the budget. The UI statewide weekly wage is computed annually. Wyoming Administrative Rules (rules.wyo.gov) are not sourced.
- Conversion from PDF loses indentation; in a few places two enumerated items run together in one paragraph where the PDF gave no line break (e.g. 27-3-320(a)(iii)-(iv)).

## Administrative rules checked 2026-10-20

The question was where Wyoming sets the **amounts** a person gets: the POWER payment, the state SSI supplement, and the UI maximum weekly benefit.

**Added:** `wyoming-administrative-rules-049-0030-chapter-1-power-program` (new). It is the Department of Family Services' POWER rule, Chapter 1 (Reference 049.0030.1.01122018, in effect from 01/12/2018 and current on 2026-10-19), from the Secretary of State's Administrative Rules system, rules.wyo.gov. The text comes from the site's HTML rule viewer; the rule's PDF was checked against it and matches word for word apart from page headers. The file is `wy-rules-049-0030-ch1-power`, 112,364 characters.

**What the rule supplies, as read in the deposited text:**
- How a POWER payment is worked out: the shelter qualified and shelter disqualified maximum payment (s 9), the $600 earned income disregard (or $1,200 for a married couple with a child in common), the maximum payment test, and the earned income incentive at 50% of the maximum payment level.
- The $5,000 asset limit, and the asset and income treatment tables (ss 12-13).
- Sanctions and the 5-year limit.

**What the rule does not supply: the dollar maximum payment table.** The earlier note that "current payment levels are set by rule" was wrong. The levels come from the deposited statute: W.S. 42-2-103(e)(iii) has the 2008 table and adjusts it each year from July 1, 2009 by the Wyoming cost-of-living index. The current-year figures are not published as a rule. DFS's website gives only two examples: a family of three with no income "could receive" $781 under the POWER work program, and a caretaker relative with one child $259. That page carries "Copyright © 2026 Wyoming Department of Family Services" and no reuse grant, so it was read, not deposited (read 2026-10-19T18:42:39Z UTC).

**Terms.** rules.wyo.gov states no terms of use or copyright claim, has no robots.txt (404), and itself quotes *Doidge v. State Board of Charities and Reform* (Wyo. 1990): rules "have the force and effect of law". Verdict: clean, under the government-edicts doctrine.

**Not sourced:**
- **State SSI supplement.** No current DFS rule program for it was listed; W.S. 42-2-103(d) leaves the amount to the budget.
- **UI weekly maximum.** The current DWS Unemployment Insurance rules (program 053.0018, chapters 1-37, listed) do not set the maximum; W.S. 27-3-303 computes it from the statewide average weekly wage each year. The current figure is not sourced.
- **General Assistance.** Its rule chapter (049.0018) is listed as repealed.

**Remaining gaps:** the current-year POWER maximum payment table after the cost-of-living adjustment; the state SSI supplement amount; the UI weekly maximum figure.
